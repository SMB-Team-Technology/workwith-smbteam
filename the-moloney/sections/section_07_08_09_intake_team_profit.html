Intake Assessment

Bradley personally speaks with new leads, decides whether to take each case, and sends the fee agreement and first retainer request himself, with Smith AI answering calls and Lawmatics handling the paperwork. It works at today’s volume, but every step runs through one person. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: RED
"…when I'm the only one doing intakes and I'm the only one that's showing up in court and… filing… even something as simple as entries of appearance, you know, it's a lot."
The Problem
  • You personally screen each new lead by phone to decide whether you want them as a client
  • The back-and-forth with new leads is manual, and it is one of the most time-consuming parts of your week
  • Lawmatics is used only for intake, fee agreements, and the first retainer payment — a lot of platform for a solo practice
  • Smith AI answers calls, but qualification, follow-up, and sign-up all depend on your availability — while you are also the one in court
Blind Spot: Because lead volume is manageable today, the manual process stays invisible. It becomes the bottleneck the moment referrals, SEO, or ads send more leads — and growth then adds hours instead of freedom.
The Positive
  • Steady lead flow from referrals and organic Google means intake is not starved for volume
  • Calls are answered by an outsourced service, and fee agreements and first retainer payments already run through a system rather than by hand
The Gap
  • Every hour spent going back and forth with a new lead is an hour not spent in court, on filings, or building the firm
  • Leads who reach out while you are in court wait for your attention
  • More volume would mean more of your time at intake, not less
A firm that runs without staff needs the first conversation with a new client handled by a system, with your judgment applied where it matters most: the decision to take the case.

Team Assessment

The Moloney Law Firm is deliberately built to run with no employees: Bradley is the only attorney, the only intake person, the only one in court, and the only one filing, with Smith AI, a bookkeeper, an accountant, and an SEO vendor around him. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
"I wanted to set up the firm such that it would just be me. So everything else could be automated."
The Problem
  • With no staff, no one can cover intake, court, or filings if you are unavailable
  • Phone answering, bookkeeping, accounting, and SEO are each outsourced — and each is managed by you
  • The automation meant to replace staff is still being assembled, so routine work like discovery downloads is still done by hand
  • Tools overlap — Clio, Lawmatics, Dialpad, Make.com, and AI subscriptions — and Clio’s document storage in particular does not fit how you work
Blind Spot: You enjoy building the systems, and that is an asset. The risk is that building them becomes a second full-time job on top of court, intake, and filing, leaving no time to work on the firm itself.
The Positive
  • A clear, deliberate model: no staff, with systems doing the work — built by an owner with 16+ years of experience
  • You already use Claude, ChatGPT, and Make.com, so adopting AI is not the hurdle — focus and time are
The Gap
  • Checking for new discovery, downloading it, unzipping it, renaming files, and organizing them like a trial file takes your time every day
  • Entries of appearance and routine filings stay on your desk alone
  • Time spent assembling tools is time not spent on cases or on growing the firm
A firm with no staff only works if the automation layer carries the load an employee would — and that layer is the part worth building well.

Profit Plan Assessment

Revenue is roughly $250,000 today, with a goal near $350,000 next year and a longer-term hope to double it once the systems are built; no profit or margin figures were discussed on the call. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"By far my biggest expense is software."
The Problem
  • Software is the largest expense, spread across overlapping tools with their own subscriptions, paywalls, and pop-ups
  • Goals are stated as revenue targets; no take-home or profit target was discussed
  • Fee structure and per-case economics were not covered, so it is unclear which work earns the most for the time it takes
Blind Spot: With no payroll, software is where margin can leak. Every overlapping tool you retire adds directly to take-home — and, for a firm you may one day sell, to the profit a buyer would value.
The Positive
  • With no staff, the firm carries no payroll, which keeps the cost base lean
  • A bookkeeper, an accountant, and QuickBooks Online give the firm a base for clear financial reporting
The Gap
  • Without a profit target, progress toward $350,000 cannot be measured against what you actually keep
  • Overlapping tools quietly consume margin month after month
A firm worth owning — or selling — is one where profit is planned, not discovered.
