Intake Assessment

South Atlanta Family Law has a dedicated intake person and an answering service for evenings and weekends, but that service takes messages and does not book consultations. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
"They actually do not book for us, unfortunately."
The Problem
  • The answering service covers after five and weekends, but it does not book consultations — a caller waits until someone calls back
  • The firm's paid answering-service minutes are spent talking to callers who may never be a fit
  • One person handles intake in-house, so coverage depends on a single desk
  • Some clients sign and then cannot afford the fee later, which suggests ability to pay is not being qualified early enough
Blind Spot: You may not realize how many after-hours inquirers leave a message, wait until morning, and have already spoken with another family law firm by the time anyone calls back.
The Positive
  • There is a dedicated intake person, so intake is not falling entirely on the attorneys
  • After-hours coverage already exists — evenings until about 9 PM and weekends — so the foundation for faster follow-up is in place
The Gap
  • Every after-hours inquiry that waits for a callback is a consultation that may never be booked
  • Time and minutes spent on unqualified calls are time not spent on the clients the firm wants
  • Signing clients who cannot sustain the fee creates collection stress and weakens the firm's revenue quality
Booking and qualifying more of the right clients with the team you already have is the fastest path toward your goal without building another team.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • A "Request a Consultation" form appears above the fold on the homepage, in the top navigation, and in the footer
  • No live chat widget was detected on the homepage
  • A way for after-hours visitors to book a consultation directly, without waiting for a callback, was not observed

Team Assessment

The firm runs with about nine people — attorneys, a paralegal, staff, an intake person, and a salesperson — but growth is tied to building another attorney and paralegal team, and workflow automation has not stuck. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"We need to get more … cases, but then … if we get more cases, … I'd have to hire another, set up another attorney and paralegal team."
The Problem
  • A growing caseload is expected to require a second attorney and paralegal team rather than more output from the current one
  • Efforts to automate workflows in the firm's case management system have been a do-it-yourself attempt that has not worked out
  • Case management licensing for roughly nine users is a meaningful, recurring cost
  • AI integration with the case management system has been held back over client data concerns, so AI use is still limited to drafting letters, emails, and media content
The Positive
  • The team already has specialized roles — attorneys, a paralegal, intake, and sales
  • The owner is already using AI tools and has attended SMB Team bootcamps, so there is real openness to adopting the technology
The Gap
  • Each new case adds work without removing any manual steps, so growth lands directly on the owner's and team's time
  • Building a second team multiplies payroll and management load at the same moment revenue is still short of goal
  • Until repeatable tasks are handled by systems, the owner stays the bottleneck for firm improvements
Taking repeatable work off your team's plate is how you reach your goal without hiring a whole second team.

Profit Plan Assessment

The firm has not reached its $1.5 million revenue goal, and margins, cost per signed case, and profit planning were not part of the conversation. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"I was hoping to have made the 1.5 this year."
The Problem
  • The $1.5 million revenue goal has not been met
  • The ChatGPT ads test produced clicks with no tracked conversions, so the spend could not be tied to results
  • Budgets and results for the Meta and Google Ads channels were not shared, so cost per signed case is unclear
  • Case management licensing was described as expensive, and it scales with every person added to the team
The Positive
  • A bookkeeper maintains the firm's books in QuickBooks, so the financial records needed for a profit plan already exist
The Gap
  • Without cost per signed case by channel, the firm cannot tell which marketing dollars are actually producing revenue
  • Adding a second team before the numbers are clear raises fixed costs without a guaranteed return
  • A defined profit target would show how much of the $1.5 million goal the firm actually keeps
Growing revenue without growing payroll at the same pace is how more cases turn into more profit for you.
