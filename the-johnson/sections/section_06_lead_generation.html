Lead Generation

The Johnson Law Group has built its practice on referrals and word of mouth, which brings in about two new clients a month with no other steady source of cases. The firm's only attempt at paid advertising, a three-month Google Ads trial, ran over budget and mostly brought in cases that pay out months later — so the goal now is predictable, retainer-paying leads with spend that stays under control.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • The site runs on Wix and its footer still reads "© 2020" — a sign it has not been refreshed in years, which can erode trust with someone choosing a bankruptcy attorney
  • Mobile and desktop page-speed scores were not measured for this report; a live speed test should be run before any rebuild decision
  • The homepage leads with "Our Purpose" and a "Meet Our Attorneys" button — no phone number or free-consultation prompt appears above the fold
  • The contact form sits mid-page, and no blog, video, or city-specific pages for Greenbelt, Bowie, College Park, or other nearby communities were detected
Blind Spot: The site's practice-area list leads with truck accidents, mass torts, and pharmaceutical litigation, while the work you want more of is retainer-based bankruptcy. Visitors — and Google — are getting a different picture of the firm than the one you want to build.
The Positive
  • A dedicated attorneys page presents the firm's two listed attorneys, their bar admissions, and their experience
  • A "Get Started" navigation item and a contact form with a case-details field give visitors a way to reach out
The Gap
  • A visitor ready to hire a bankruptcy attorney does not see a clear "call now" or "free consultation" action right away
  • Without bankruptcy-focused pages for each service and community, the site gives search engines little reason to show it for the searches you want
  • Mixed messaging between mass-tort/injury work and bankruptcy makes it harder to attract the retainer-paying clients you are targeting
A website built around the cases you want is what turns more of your marketing spend into consistent retainer clients — without more hours from you.

Local SEO

The Problem
  • Three different phone numbers appear across the firm's website and online listings, which can confuse both prospects and Google about which is the real business line
  • No city-specific pages were found on the website, so the firm has little presence in searches for neighboring communities in Prince George's County
  • Google Business Profile details, review counts, and local map-pack rankings were not captured in this review and should be verified live — they are the first thing to check on the follow-up call
Blind Spot: A Yelp listing for the firm appeared in search results under a "Personal Injury Law" category rather than bankruptcy. Directory categories like this shape which searches a listing is matched to.
The Positive
  • The firm has listings on several directories — including Yahoo Local, MapQuest, and ReachAttorneys — and its Greenbelt street address is consistent across them
The Gap
  • Inconsistent phone numbers and categories weaken the local trust signals that help a firm show up when someone searches "bankruptcy lawyer near me"
  • Competitors with dedicated local pages are capturing searches the firm's single website never answers
Law Office of Donald L. Bell — located in the same Greenbelt building, the firm's website links to more than 40 city-specific Chapter 13 pages covering communities across Maryland, plus a prominent phone number and consultation button.
Showing up first for local bankruptcy searches is how the firm stops relying on a referral network that depends on one person's connections.

Google Ads

Ad Presence
  • You told us the firm ran Google Ads for about three months last year through a lead-generation vendor and has not continued since the trial ended
  • Whether the firm currently appears for bankruptcy, traffic, landlord-tenant, or consumer-protection searches was not checked in this review — it may not be configured to show for these terms, or may be getting outbid
Competitor Ad Landscape
  • Law Office of Donald L. Bell — built its website to support paid and organic search, with city-specific Chapter 13 pages and a "Schedule Your Consultation" call to action; live ad copy was not captured
  • Middleton Legal — leads with "Free consultation, no judgment, straight answers" and a Prince George's County landing page, messaging that fits bankruptcy searchers well; live ad copy was not captured
The Opportunity
  • Core bankruptcy searches such as "bankruptcy lawyer Greenbelt MD" and "Chapter 13 bankruptcy lawyer Greenbelt"
  • "Chapter 7 bankruptcy attorney Prince George's County", plus traffic-ticket and landlord-tenant searches in the same area
  • Urgency searches from people in crisis — such as stopping a foreclosure or wage garnishment — which tend to come from people ready to hire and pay a retainer
  • Neighboring communities such as College Park, Bowie, Laurel, and Hyattsville, where a Greenbelt-based competitor is already building reach
Blind Spot: The earlier trial mostly produced contingency injury cases rather than retainer-paying clients. That is typically a keyword and targeting issue — tight keyword control, blocking injury-related searches, and a firm monthly spend limit are what keep paid leads aligned with the cases you actually want.

Local Service Ads

LSA Presence
  • Whether the firm appears in Google Local Service Ads, and whether it is Google Screened, was not checked in this review
  • With no confirmed LSA presence, this is a likely opportunity gap for bankruptcy and consumer searches in Greenbelt and Prince George's County
Competitor LSA Landscape
  • Which local competitors hold Local Service Ads spots, and their review counts, were not verified in this review and should be checked live before the call
The Opportunity
  • Because Local Service Ads charge per lead, not per click, they offer a more predictable cost per call — useful for a firm that wants tight control after an over-budget trial
  • LSAs appear above everything else on Google — above paid ads, above the map pack, above all organic results — and you only pay when a real prospect calls you directly.
  • Google review volume and responsiveness influence LSA ranking, so building a steady stream of client reviews supports both LSAs and local search
Blind Spot: LSA leads are phone calls, so how quickly and how well each call is answered determines whether a paid lead becomes a retainer client.
