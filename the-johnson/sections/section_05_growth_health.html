Pillar Health Scorecard

Lead Generation

  • New clients come almost entirely from referrals and word of mouth — about two a month, with no other consistent source
  • Demand runs in waves: a few very busy months followed by a few quiet ones
  • A past Google Ads trial through a lead vendor ran well over its intended budget and mostly produced contingency cases rather than retainer-paying clients
A steady flow of retainer cases is what lets profit grow without the hours growing with it.

Intake

  • Intake is handled by the attorneys themselves and works at today's low volume
  • There is no dedicated intake staff, so a jump in lead volume would land directly on attorney time
  • The website lists three different phone numbers and offers no free-consultation prompt above the fold, which makes it harder for a ready-to-hire visitor to act
Intake that does not depend on an attorney picking up every call protects the time you want to keep for family.

Self-Managing Team

  • Three attorneys carry all of the casework, with no dedicated support staff
  • Decisions run through the founder, and the referral network that feeds the firm is tied to his personal connections
  • The next generation of attorneys wants to run the firm on its own, but there is no system yet that works without the founder's relationships
A firm that runs on systems rather than on every attorney's personal hours is what makes work-life balance realistic.

Profit

  • The goal is to grow toward roughly a million dollars a year, but there is no stated plan tying marketing spend to retainer revenue
  • Cash timing depends on when contingency matters pay out, which makes monthly revenue hard to predict
  • Time on lower-value matters competes with time for the larger cases the firm would rather focus on
Profit that is planned, rather than discovered after the fact, is how more income comes without more hours.
