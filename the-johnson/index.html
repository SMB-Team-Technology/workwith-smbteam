Here Is Where The Johnson Law Group LLC Stands Today

The Johnson Law Group is a three-attorney firm in Greenbelt, Maryland, handling bankruptcy, civil litigation, and consumer protection work. New clients come almost entirely from referrals and word of mouth — about two a month — and the owners describe the flow as running in hot and cold quarters. The most important finding is that the firm has no repeatable way to bring in retainer-paying cases, at the moment the founder's personal network is expected to step back.

Stage 6 is the stage that lets you grow profit without giving up your evenings and your family time — and it is exactly where we are taking you.

Where You Are Today

Stage 3: Solo Practitioner
  • Referrals and word of mouth are the only consistent source of new clients, averaging about two a month
  • Some quarters are very busy and the next ones go quiet, which makes planning hard
  • A past three-month Google Ads trial ran well over its intended budget and mostly produced contingency cases that pay out months later
  • The three attorneys carry the work themselves with no dedicated support staff, and the firm's referral network is tied to the founder's personal connections

Where You Need To Be

Stage 6: Law Firm Owner
  • The firm runs without you
  • Your income is no longer tied to your personal hours
  • Your time is yours to spend however you choose
  • This is where true freedom and true wealth become possible
The gap between a referral-driven practice and a firm that predictably adds six to ten retainer clients a month is closable — and it starts with a lead system built around the cases you actually want.

The Law Firm Growth Acceleration Model

We Help Law Firm Owners Get More Profit, More Personal Freedom, And Outstanding Results For Their Clients.

Most law firms try to grow by chasing more leads. But leads alone are not the problem — and leads alone are not the answer. Here is what actually works.

Lead Generation

Leads are to a law firm what gasoline is to a car. Without them, you go nowhere. But a steady, predictable flow of leads changes everything — you get to choose your cases instead of taking whatever comes in.

Intake

But all the leads in the world are worthless without a system to turn them into paying clients. Most firms are losing cases they already paid for simply because their follow-up is slow or inconsistent.

Self-Managing Team

Once clients are coming in, you need a team — otherwise you end up working 90-hour weeks and results suffer. A self-managing team is what gives you the freedom to step back.

Profit Plan

And once the team is in place, most owners look at their bank account and ask: where did all the money go? That is what a profit plan solves — clarity on what you earn, what you keep, and how to grow both.

All four have to work together. If one is missing, you cannot have more profit, more freedom, and outstanding results for your clients at the same time.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • The site runs on Wix and its footer still reads "© 2020" — a sign it has not been refreshed in years, which can erode trust with someone choosing a bankruptcy attorney
  • Mobile and desktop page-speed scores were not measured for this report; a live speed test should be run before any rebuild decision

  • The contact form sits mid-page, and no blog, video, or city-specific pages for Greenbelt, Bowie, College Park, or other nearby communities were detected
Blind Spot: The site's practice-area list leads with truck accidents, mass torts, and pharmaceutical litigation, while the work you want more of is retainer-based bankruptcy. Visitors — and Google — are getting a different picture of the firm than the one you want to build.
The Positive
  • A dedicated attorneys page presents the firm's two listed attorneys, their bar admissions, and their experience
  • A "Get Started" navigation item and a contact form with a case-details field give visitors a way to reach out
The Gap
  • A visitor ready to hire a bankruptcy attorney does not see a clear "call now" or "free consultation" action right away
  • Without bankruptcy-focused pages for each service and community, the site gives search engines little reason to show it for the searches you want
  • Mixed messaging between mass-tort/injury work and bankruptcy makes it harder to attract the retainer-paying clients you are targeting
A website built around the cases you want is what turns more of your marketing spend into consistent retainer clients — without more hours from you.

Intake Assessment

Intake at The Johnson Law Group is handled by the attorneys themselves, which works at today's volume but has no dedicated person or process behind it. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
"I think intake is fine for us to handle. It only would become an issue if the volume was so high that you can't… manage it by yourself."
The Problem
  • Attorneys personally handle intake, with no dedicated intake team member in place
  • No after-hours or overflow coverage was described, so calls that arrive while attorneys are in court or hearings depend on someone being free to answer
  • The website shows three different phone numbers and no phone number or free-consultation prompt above the fold
  • A defined follow-up process for prospects who do not sign right away was not described on the call
Blind Spot: Intake that feels fine at two new clients a month can break quickly once marketing brings in six to ten. Paid leads go cold fast, and the firm that responds first is usually the firm that gets hired.
The Positive
  • At current volume, attorneys speaking with every prospect directly is a strong personal touch
  • A family member who is not an attorney helps with technology and could be brought in to support intake as volume grows
The Gap
  • Every missed or slow-to-return call from a paid lead is a retainer case that can go to a competitor in the same building
  • Attorney time spent on intake calls is time not spent on the larger matters the firm wants to focus on
  • Without a repeatable intake process, new marketing leads will land on attorneys already stretched during busy stretches of hearings and depositions
A simple, repeatable intake process lets new retainer clients come in without costing you the evenings and family time you want to protect.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • No live chat was detected on the site, so visitors who do not want to fill out a form or call have no quick way to reach the firm
  • Three different phone numbers appear across the website and online listings, so prospects may reach a number that is not monitored the same way

Status: AMBER
"the goal for us, especially with cori just now becoming a member of the bar, is to sort of get the wheels turning so that we can carry it on our own without his assistance and his connections or whatever."

  • All casework and client contact rests on three attorneys, with no dedicated support or operations staff

  • No documented org structure, accountability system, or management layer was described on the call
  • The firm's referral flow depends on the founder's personal connections, which the next generation does not yet have a way to replace

Profit Plan Assessment

The firm's goal is to grow toward close to a million dollars a year, but it has not yet tied marketing spend or case mix to a clear profit plan. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"Increase in profit, but, like, not extracting too much time either."
The Problem
  • Profit appears as a goal rather than a tracked number — no profit plan or cost-per-case view was described on the call
  • The earlier Google Ads trial ran well over its intended budget, and its results arrived months later, so spend was hard to tie back to revenue
  • Contingency matters and Chapter 13 plans pay out over time, which makes monthly cash flow unpredictable
  • Time is spent on lower-level matters while the firm wants to focus on larger cases and a more automated practice area
Blind Spot: A marketing budget only pays off when it is aimed at the case types that produce retainers quickly. Tracking cost per signed retainer, not cost per lead, is what keeps spend from running away again.
The Positive
  • Clear flat-fee and retainer structures for bankruptcy work, with defined fees for Chapter 7 and Chapter 13 matters
  • A clear target of six to ten retainer cases a month that can be used to size spend and measure return
The Gap
  • Without a spend ceiling tied to retainer revenue, marketing can repeat the earlier over-budget experience
  • Without a view of which case types pay fastest and best, effort may go to work that does not move profit
  • Uneven cash timing makes it harder to plan hiring, tools, and personal income with confidence
A clear profit plan is how more income arrives without more hours — which is exactly what you said you want.
