Here Is Where Sibley Law Stands Today

Dee Sibley runs an estate planning firm in Rockledge, Florida, built on free community seminars that bring in roughly 12 to 15 leads a month and convert well once people book. The seminars have not been promoted for about four to five months and the paid ads stopped without anyone noticing, so the pipeline that was working is now unpredictable.

Stage 6 is the stage where you can "truly unplug" for a week and know the firm is running — and for you, the road there starts with a steady pipeline and processes your team can follow without you.

The Law Firm Growth Acceleration Model

We Help Law Firm Owners Get More Profit, More Personal Freedom, And Outstanding Results For Their Clients.

Most law firms try to grow by chasing more leads. But leads alone are not the problem — and leads alone are not the answer. Here is what actually works.

Lead Generation

Leads are to a law firm what gasoline is to a car. Without them, you go nowhere. But a steady, predictable flow of leads changes everything — you get to choose your cases instead of taking whatever comes in.

Intake

But all the leads in the world are worthless without a system to turn them into paying clients. Most firms are losing cases they already paid for simply because their follow-up is slow or inconsistent.

Self-Managing Team

Once clients are coming in, you need a team — otherwise you end up working 90-hour weeks and results suffer. A self-managing team is what gives you the freedom to step back.

Profit Plan

And once the team is in place, most owners look at their bank account and ask: where did all the money go? That is what a profit plan solves — clarity on what you earn, what you keep, and how to grow both.

All four have to work together. If one is missing, you cannot have more profit, more freedom, and outstanding results for your clients at the same time.

Intake Assessment

Sibley Law's seminars do the hard part — they get prospects in the room — but the step between "interested" and "signed" still depends on follow-up that is not consistent. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
"where I see the biggest gap would be with those coming to the seminar, … if they don't book with me right away, that follow-up."
The Problem
  • Seminar attendees who do not book right away depend on a follow-up that Dee herself identifies as the biggest gap
  • By Dee's own count, roughly eight of the 12 to 15 attendees book a session and about five of those become clients, so several warm prospects each month never make it to a consultation
  • A team member who is under two months in is learning a process that is not yet written down
  • The nurture flow is automated, but Dee sees the follow-up for attendees who do not book on the spot as the part that is still missing
Blind Spot: Every attendee who sat through a 75-minute seminar without booking is already a warm lead who gave you real attention — they are the cheapest prospects you will ever have, and right now they are the ones slipping away.
The Positive
  • Once people book, they convert well — about five of every eight booked sessions, by Dee's estimate
  • An automated nurture flow and website lead magnets already exist, so the follow-up tools are partly in place
The Gap
  • At an average flat fee of roughly $4,000 to $4,250, each additional attendee who signs is worth about that much — one more signed client a month would add roughly $48,000 to $51,000 a year (an illustration, not a projection)
  • Follow-up that depends on Dee's memory and calendar competes with every other thing she is carrying
  • Without a written intake process, each new hire has to learn it from scratch
A follow-up process that runs without Dee chasing it is one more piece of the firm she can step away from.

Profit Plan Assessment

Sibley Law runs lean and works with a CFO, but the firm's revenue swings from month to month and Dee does not yet know what share of it is profit. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"in working with my CFO, if I raise my revenue, then more of that will be profit because I am so streamlined."
The Problem
  • Revenue is on pace to close the year between $350,000 and $375,000, against a stated goal of $50,000 a month, or $600,000 a year
  • Some months reach the monthly goal and others are "just like minimal," so there is no consistency to plan around
  • Dee does not know her profit percentage, and she is not yet satisfied with her take-home because the firm is lean and revenue is uneven
  • The planned January hire adds payroll before the revenue is consistent, which is a concern Dee herself raised
Blind Spot: A lean firm is not the same as a profitable one. When the seminar calendar drives revenue, a quiet month leaves almost no cushion, so the firm can be efficient and still feel financially fragile.
The Positive
  • Dee already works with a CFO and has a clear revenue goal, which is the foundation of any profit plan
  • With a flat-fee model of roughly $4,000 to $4,250 per matter, every additional signed client is predictable revenue for a lean firm
The Gap
  • Without a known margin, the firm cannot say how much each extra signed client adds to take-home
  • Inconsistent months make it hard to commit to payroll, equipment, or a raise for the owner
  • The distance between the current $350,000 to $375,000 and the $600,000 goal cannot be closed without a steady pipeline
Consistent months and a known margin are what turn hard work into the stability Dee wants.
