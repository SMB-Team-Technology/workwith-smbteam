Here Is Where Sibley Law Stands Today

Dee Sibley runs an estate planning firm in Rockledge, Florida, built on free community seminars that bring in roughly 12 to 15 leads a month and convert well once people book. The seminars have not been promoted for about four to five months and the paid ads stopped without anyone noticing, so the pipeline that was working is now unpredictable.

Stage 6 is the stage where you can "truly unplug" for a week and know the firm is running — and for you, the road there starts with a steady pipeline and processes your team can follow without you.

Where You Are Today

Stage 4: Small Business Manager
  • Free estate planning seminars are the main lead source, but they have not been advertised for roughly four to five months
  • Revenue swings from months that hit your goal to months that are minimal, so there is no consistency to plan around
  • You work about 50 hours a week and still cannot fully disconnect, because "I'm the person"
  • A new team member is under two months in, another full-time hire is planned for January, and the processes behind them are still fragmented

Where You Need To Be

Stage 6: Law Firm Owner
  • The firm runs without you
  • Your income is no longer tied to your personal hours
  • Your time is yours to spend however you choose
  • This is where true freedom and true wealth become possible
The gap between a firm that needs you for every decision and one that keeps running while you are away is closeable — it starts with a pipeline that fills on purpose and a team that has clear processes to follow.
Overall Growth Stage Assessment
Sibley Law
Dee is operating at Stage 4 — a Small Business Manager with a working seminar-based lead source and a small team, but still heavily involved in day-to-day operations. The most urgent gap is a pipeline that depends on seminars Dee has not been promoting, with no paid channel behind it and a follow-up gap for attendees who do not book right away. Until that is fixed, reaching the steady $50,000 months she is aiming for — and the freedom to step away that comes with them — will stay out of reach.
