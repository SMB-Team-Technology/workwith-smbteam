Lead Generation

Sibley Law has a lead source that works — free "Estate Planning 101" seminars that bring in roughly 12 to 15 leads a month — but it has gone quiet, and the paid ads that used to support it stopped when the firm changed vendors. With no active paid channel and the seminars unpromoted for about four to five months, the pipeline now depends on whatever the website and word of mouth deliver.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • Search results show template and test-looking pages indexed under the main domain, including titles such as "HTML. Home" and "HTML.Estate Planing" with a spelling error
  • A legacy Wix version of the site still appears in search results alongside the current website, splitting the firm's web presence across several properties
  • The events page still lists "Estate Planning 101" sessions on October dates that have now passed, which signals an inactive firm to anyone who lands there
  • Mobile and desktop page-speed scores were not captured for this report and should be tested as part of the website review
Blind Spot: Test pages and an old Wix site showing up in Google can quietly dilute your main site's authority and send prospects to pages that do not represent the firm well — and none of it is visible from the site itself.
The Positive
  • Your phone number is the primary call to action at the top of the homepage, and consultation booking and discovery-call scheduling are also available
  • The site has an attorney bio, a blog, and a seminar registration option, which is a solid base to build on
The Gap
  • No geo-targeted pages for Melbourne, Viera, or other Brevard County communities were found from the homepage, so the site is not built to capture searches from the whole county
  • No video was observed on the homepage, which leaves the strongest tool for building trust with a warm seminar audience unused
  • Outdated event dates and stray indexed pages make a good firm look less active than it is
A website that stays current and works without Dee touching it is one less thing she has to carry herself.

Local SEO

The Problem
  • The firm appears under several names — "Sibley Law & Associates," "Sibley Law Associates," "The Legacy Lawyer at Sibley Law," and a personal-name variant — which makes it harder for Google to treat them as one business
  • Directory listings disagree on basic details: the entity suffix appears as both "P.A." and "PLLC," and one directory shows "Ste 400" while the website shows no suite number
  • Local map-pack rankings for estate planning searches in Rockledge, Melbourne, and Brevard County were not captured for this report and are a key item to verify before any local SEO work begins
The Positive
  • A Google listing for Sibley Law & Associates at 1682 S. Fiske Blvd. in Rockledge appears in search, and the reviews reported for it are favorable
The Gap
  • Inconsistent names, entity types, and suite numbers across directories weaken the local signals Google uses to decide who appears first
  • With elder law planned for 2027, there are no local search foundations yet for the new practice area
Estate Planning & Elder Law Center of Brevard — operates offices in both Indialantic and Viera and already presents estate planning and elder law together, the same combination Sibley Law plans to offer in 2027.
Clean, consistent local listings keep new clients finding the firm without Dee having to promote it every month.

Google Ads

Ad Presence
  • Live Google search results for estate planning terms were not captured for this report, so what the firm currently shows for these searches has not been verified
  • Per Dee, ads ran with a prior vendor on Facebook and some Google, and they stopped when the firm changed vendors; she has not advertised the seminars for about four to five months
  • Elder law, planned for 2027, has no ad foundation yet
Competitor Ad Landscape
  • Estate Planning & Elder Law Center of Brevard — promotes "Attend a Free Seminar" on its site with online registration, competing for the same seminar-minded audience; its Google ad activity was not captured
  • Romaine & Nugent — an established Rockledge estate planning firm; no seminar marketing was observed on its site and its Google ad activity was not captured
The Opportunity
  • Estate planning attorney Rockledge, estate planning lawyer Melbourne FL, and wills and trusts attorney Brevard County
  • Living trust, probate-avoidance, and "protect my family" searches from homeowners in Brevard County
  • Seminar-intent searches such as "estate planning seminar near me" to fill the Estate Planning 101 sessions again
  • Neighboring communities such as Viera, Cocoa, Titusville, and Merritt Island, and elder law and Medicaid planning terms ahead of the 2027 expansion
Blind Spot: When the firm changed vendors, the ads stopped and no one noticed — in Dee's words, "I thought my paid advertising was going on. And then I learned that it was not." Without someone watching the account, a channel can go dark for months.

Local Service Ads

LSA Presence
  • Whether Sibley Law appears in Local Service Ads for estate planning in Brevard County was not captured for this report
  • Google Screened status and any LSA review count were not verified, so this is framed as an opportunity to confirm rather than a finding
Competitor LSA Landscape
  • Estate Planning & Elder Law Center of Brevard, Romaine & Nugent, and Mommers & Colombo are the firms to benchmark first — their LSA positions and review counts were not captured for this report
The Opportunity
  • A pay-per-lead channel gives Sibley Law a source of consultations that does not depend on running a seminar that month
  • LSAs appear above everything else on Google — above paid ads, above the map pack, above all organic results — and you only pay when a real prospect calls you directly.
  • Because Dee does not have the capacity to run paid ads herself, a managed LSA channel is the lowest-effort way to add a second, trackable lead source

The Opportunity

  • Video ads featuring the attorney — builds trust before a prospect ever calls
  • Educational content ads — capture leads earlier in the decision process
  • Seminar registration ads and retargeting of attendees who did not book, which ties directly to the follow-up gap Dee described
