Intake Assessment

Intake volume has grown from 8–10 to 12–15 new clients a month, and the team absorbing it is new and still in training. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Staff handling a growing client flow are new and still training, so consistency in how leads are answered and followed up is not yet established
  • The website offers no contact form, call to action or other online way for a prospect to reach the firm
  • Two different phone numbers appear across directory listings, so some prospects may reach a line that is not monitored
  • No review-generation process is in place to turn satisfied clients into the social proof new prospects look for before they call
The Positive
  • Referral demand is strong enough to have lifted new clients from 8–10 to 12–15 a month with no marketing at all
  • The firm is investing in building a team rather than relying on Caroline alone to answer every call
The Gap
  • Every referral who cannot find a working website or contact path is a client the firm earned but may never sign
  • A team still in training without a defined process means conversion depends on who happens to pick up
  • Without reviews, even well-handled calls start from a trust deficit against competitors who show a visible review base
Intake that runs without Caroline is one of the clearest steps from attorney to CEO — and toward the 40-hour week.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • No contact form was found on the homepage, /contact or /about
  • No call to action appears anywhere on the site, so a visitor has no next step
  • No live chat or other way to engage a visitor outside office hours was observed

Team Assessment

Caroline personally reviews and signs all legal work, and her team is new, still training, and turns over every 4–5 months. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Every piece of legal work waits for Caroline's personal review and signature
  • Caroline works roughly 80 hours a week to keep up
  • Paralegals turn over on a 4–5 month cycle, so training starts over before it pays off
  • Another firm's caseload arrives by the end of October, adding volume to a team that is still in training
Blind Spot: The firm has held back on marketing because of capacity, which points to the review-and-sign process, not demand, as the real bottleneck. Fixing that process unlocks growth without adding hours.
The Positive
  • Caroline has named the goal clearly — moving from attorney to CEO with a self-managing team
  • Demand is already there: new clients per month have grown by roughly 50% without any marketing
The Gap
  • Because every file routes through Caroline, each new case adds to her hours instead of the team's
  • High turnover keeps the team at a training level that cannot yet carry work without her sign-off
  • The incoming caseload will widen the gap between what the team can deliver and what Caroline must personally review
A team that can review and deliver without Caroline's signature on every file is the path to a healthy 40-hour week and the Japan trip with her family.

Profit Plan Assessment

Revenue has grown from $400K toward a $650K goal, and Caroline now wants to reach $1M+ in the next 12 months, with $1.3M discussed as achievable. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Profit and margin were not discussed on the call, so there is no visible view of what each additional client contributes
  • The $1M+ goal is a large step from the $400K and $650K figures discussed, and no plan for what it will cost to get there was mentioned
  • Paralegal turnover every 4–5 months means recurring hiring and retraining time that is not being measured
  • No tracking of time or cost by case type, immigration versus estate planning, was mentioned
Blind Spot: With immigration at roughly 80% of the work and estate planning at 20%, it is hard to know where each hour of an 80-hour week earns the most without tracking time and cost per case type.
The Positive
  • Revenue has grown and new clients per month have climbed from 8–10 to 12–15
  • Caroline has set a specific, ambitious revenue target rather than drifting
The Gap
  • Without margin visibility, reaching $1M+ could simply mean more hours for the same person
  • The cost of turnover and retraining stays hidden, so it cannot be planned around
  • Growth through the incoming caseload will be hard to evaluate without a clear baseline
A clear profit plan is what turns the CEO transition and the Japan trip from aspirations into something the firm can fund.
