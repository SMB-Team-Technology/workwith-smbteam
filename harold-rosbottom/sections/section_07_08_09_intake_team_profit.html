Intake Assessment

Intake was not covered on the discovery call, so this assessment rests on what is visible from the outside: the website's contact points and the Google profile. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • How quickly calls and web forms are answered, and who answers them, is unknown, and it is the number that decides whether paid leads turn into signed cases
  • We could not tell whether the intake widget on the site covers evenings and weekends
  • It is not known whether every lead, from every channel, is tracked through to a signed case or a lost one
Blind Spot: Paid leads cost more than organic ones and are usually shopping several firms at once. The firm that responds first often wins the case, so a slow callback quietly raises your true cost per signed case.
The Positive
  • The homepage offers a case form, a "Tell us about your case" button and two click-to-call numbers (Dallas and Fort Worth)
  • Your Google profile lists the firm as open 24 hours, a strong signal for people searching after an accident
The Gap
  • Every paid lead that goes unanswered for even a few minutes is a lead you paid to generate and handed to a competitor
  • Without tracking from first contact to signed case, you cannot tell which channel is really paying for itself
  • At a higher volume of calls and forms, small response gaps add up to many missed cases
Answering every lead fast and tracking it to the end means each dollar you test on a new channel is measured fairly, so you can add budget only where it earns it.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • A case form sits on the homepage just below the headline, with a separate contact page as well, so the path to reaching you is short
  • An "Intaker" intake-software script is present in the page source. It is commonly used for chat and intake widgets, but we could not confirm a live chat is active on the site
  • The call button and form are in place, but we did not test response times or after-hours handling

Team Assessment

Team size, roles and how much of the day-to-day depends on the owner were not discussed on the call, so this rating is provisional. The website lists a lead attorney, an associate and an of-counsel attorney, which points to a small team. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • It is not known who owns marketing results day to day, or whether that ownership sits with the owner personally
  • Adding paid channels adds weekly work: reviewing results, answering the platforms' questions and feeding back on lead quality
  • Without a named owner, new channels tend to fall back on whoever has the most to lose from them
The Gap
  • If every new marketing channel needs the owner's attention, growth adds hours instead of freeing them
  • A defined weekly review lets the team run paid channels without constant owner involvement
  • Handing the day-to-day to an expert partner keeps the firm's leadership focused on cases and clients
Putting an expert and a clear owner behind each new channel lets the firm grow without that growth landing on you personally.

Profit Plan Assessment

Harold has a good feel for what organic leads cost, but paid channels have not yet been measured against that benchmark. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"We're well below that number currently in our organic work, but we understand that paid costs a little more."
The Problem
  • The cost of a signed case from paid channels is still unknown, because no paid channel is running to measure it
  • ChatGPT advertising in particular has little performance history, so early results will be a test and not a forecast
  • Overall margin and how it will change as paid spend grows were not part of the call
Blind Spot: Comparing paid to organic only works if both are measured the same way: cost per signed case, not cost per lead or per click. A cheaper lead that rarely signs can cost more than it appears.
The Positive
  • You already know your organic cost levels, and your plan is to "set a budget and try it to kind of see what the results are"
  • Starting at a budget and adding "if we have good success" is the discipline that keeps new spending from running ahead of the numbers
The Gap
  • Without a paid benchmark, there is no way to know which channel deserves more budget and which deserves less
  • Any budget increase is a guess until cost per signed case is measured by channel
  • A clear target cost per case, set before launch, turns a test into a decision
Measuring cost per signed case by channel lets you add budget only where the numbers earn it, so you grow without overpaying.
