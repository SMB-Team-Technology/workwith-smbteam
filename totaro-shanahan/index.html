Here Is Where Totaro & Shanahan Stands Today

Totaro & Shanahan has practiced bankruptcy law in Southern California for decades, built almost entirely on Chapter 11 work that arrives by referral from other attorneys. Michael Totaro wants to grow Chapter 7 volume from about two cases a month to 20–30 — but the firm has a single Google review, no digital advertising history, and did not appear in the local results for any bankruptcy search we tested in Los Angeles, Orange County, or San Diego.

Stage 6 is where Michael spends his time on the legal work he wants to do while someone else owns the marketing and the administration — and it is exactly where we are taking the firm.

Where You Are Today

Stage 3: Solo Practitioner
  • New Chapter 11 clients come through referrals from other attorneys — the firm has never run digital ads, and the radio campaign that once produced four or five calls a day is no longer running
  • Chapter 7 is the growth target, but the firm gets only about two a month today against the 20–30 a month Michael wants
  • A three-person team — two attorneys and one paralegal — leaves Michael carrying the casework, the administration, and any marketing decisions himself
  • Revenue is back to about $600,000 a year and rising, against a goal of $1.5 million next year, with only one Google review behind the firm's online reputation

Where You Need To Be

  • Your time is yours to spend however you choose
  • This is where true freedom and true wealth become possible

The Law Firm Growth Acceleration Model

We Help Law Firm Owners Get More Profit, More Personal Freedom, And Outstanding Results For Their Clients.

Most law firms try to grow by chasing more leads. But leads alone are not the problem — and leads alone are not the answer. Here is what actually works.

Lead Generation

Leads are to a law firm what gasoline is to a car. Without them, you go nowhere. But a steady, predictable flow of leads changes everything — you get to choose your cases instead of taking whatever comes in.

Intake

But all the leads in the world are worthless without a system to turn them into paying clients. Most firms are losing cases they already paid for simply because their follow-up is slow or inconsistent.

Self-Managing Team

Once clients are coming in, you need a team — otherwise you end up working 90-hour weeks and results suffer. A self-managing team is what gives you the freedom to step back.

Profit Plan

And once the team is in place, most owners look at their bank account and ask: where did all the money go? That is what a profit plan solves — clarity on what you earn, what you keep, and how to grow both.

All four have to work together. If one is missing, you cannot have more profit, more freedom, and outstanding results for your clients at the same time.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • The homepage headline, "Overcome Debt & Reclaim Your Financial Freedom," speaks to consumers in debt — Chapter 11, the work behind most of the firm's current revenue, appears only on an inner services page
  • PageSpeed scores: Mobile 88/100 — Desktop 98/100 (Lighthouse engine). Mobile largest-contentful-paint is 3.7 seconds, slower than Google's 2.5-second "good" threshold
  • Chapter 7 location pages exist for Los Angeles, Long Beach, Glendale, and Orange County, but there is no San Diego page — where Michael serves clients — and no Chapter 11 location pages anywhere
  • No video, no blog, and attorney credentials limited to a "Meet Our Team" link and an About page — little content to build trust with someone comparing several firms
Blind Spot: Attorneys who refer Chapter 11 matters will often look a firm up before sending a client. A homepage aimed at consumers in debt, with a single online review behind it, may undersell the specialty that earns those referrals.
The Positive
  • A modern, mobile-responsive design with clear "Get Free Consultation" calls to action at the top of the page
  • Fast desktop performance and a clean base to build on — this does not look like a site that needs to be rebuilt from scratch
The Gap
  • Chapter 7 searchers in San Diego, and Chapter 11 searchers anywhere, have no page written for them — nothing to rank in search and nothing to send paid traffic to
  • Without video or educational content, a visitor who finds the firm has little reason to choose it over a competitor showing hundreds of reviews
  • The Chapter 11 specialty, which carries the firm's revenue today, is under-presented to anyone who finds the site directly
A website that turns Chapter 7 searchers into consultations is the foundation that lets Michael hand marketing to someone else and get back to the legal work.
