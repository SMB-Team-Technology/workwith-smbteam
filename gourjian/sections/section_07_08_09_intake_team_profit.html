Intake Assessment

Gourjian Law has no defined intake process at all today — every inquiry, referral or otherwise, funnels through a single email address with no tracking in between. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • No contact form on the website — every inquiry has to be composed as an email to one address, with no structured way to capture who reached out or why
  • No CRM or lead tracking software identified — there is no way to know how many inquiries come in, how quickly they are answered, or how many go unanswered
  • With a "lean, top-heavy" attorney structure and minimal dedicated support staff, whoever checks that inbox is likely also billing hourly client work — inquiries compete directly with revenue-generating time
  • No defined follow-up sequence exists for a prospective client who reaches out but doesn't get an immediate response
Blind Spot: Because every new client currently arrives through a warm referral, it's easy to assume intake isn't a real risk. But as the firm's authority-building strategy starts working and cold inquiries increase, that single-inbox process will not hold up — and a referral who doesn't hear back promptly reflects on the referring relationship, not just the firm.
The Gap
  • There is currently no way to measure how many potential engagements Gourjian Law has already lost simply because nobody saw the email in time
  • A firm planning to add attorneys and generate $650K–$700K per hire needs an intake process built for more volume than one inbox can reliably handle
  • Every inquiry that slips through undermines the very referral reputation the firm has spent 21 years building
A defined intake process protects the referral relationships Varand has spent 21 years earning — so a hiring push built on more visibility doesn't get undercut by inquiries nobody sees in time.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • No contact form exists anywhere on the website — the sole above-the-fold CTA routes directly to a mailto link for one email address
  • No live chat is present on the site, which is appropriate for this sophisticated, relationship-driven buyer rather than a gap to fix
  • Because there is no form, there is also no way to route or tag inquiries by practice area (real estate vs. business litigation vs. general counsel) before someone manually reads the email

These findings are also referenced in the Website Audit section.

Team Assessment

Gourjian Law's growth plan is built entirely on hiring more attorneys — but there is no operational layer in place to support, market around, or systematize that hiring. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • A "lean, top-heavy structure" of attorneys with minimal dedicated support staff — the firm has legal talent but no operations, marketing, or business-development function
  • Growth strategy depends on hiring attorneys who can each generate $650K–$700K, but there is no repeatable system for attracting or onboarding them beyond word-of-mouth
  • All profit is currently taken as owner compensation rather than reinvested — a sign that major financial decisions still run through Varand personally
  • No dedicated marketing, ops, or intake role exists to carry the authority-building strategy discussed on the call once it's built
Blind Spot: A firm this profitable and this senior in talent can feel like it doesn't need "team" work in the way a smaller firm does — but the absence of anyone dedicated to growth operations means every new initiative, including the branded content play, will compete for Varand's own time unless someone owns it.
The Positive
  • The firm already has 8 named attorneys — real bench strength that most firms this profitable would still be building
  • A 21-year track record of successfully hiring and retaining senior legal talent shows the firm can attract strong people when it puts effort into it
The Gap
  • Every hour Varand or a senior attorney spends managing marketing, intake, or growth operations is an hour not spent on billable client work worth $650–$700/hour
  • Without an operational layer, the firm's capacity to grow is capped at whatever Varand personally has time to build and oversee
  • A hiring-driven growth plan without a systemized way to attract and onboard new attorneys will always grow in fits and starts rather than predictably
Building the operational systems around growth — not just adding more attorneys — is what eventually lets Varand's firm expand without every new hire and every new initiative running through him personally.

Profit Plan Assessment

Gourjian Law's financial picture is unusually strong for a firm SMB Team audits — the gap here isn't visibility, it's a plan for what the profit does next. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"60% profit margins" and "consistent 20%+ annual growth"
The Problem
  • All profit is currently taken as owner compensation — none of it has been formally allocated toward a growth or reinvestment plan
  • There is no stated marketing or growth budget today, even though the firm has the margin to support one
  • Growth is being funded reactively, one attorney hire at a time, rather than through a deliberate capital plan
  • Varand has flagged openness to reinvesting roughly 20% of revenue toward growth, but that plan does not exist yet in any concrete form
Blind Spot: A 60% margin can create a false sense that nothing needs to change — but every dollar sitting in owner comp instead of a growth plan is a dollar not compounding the firm's next stage of expansion.
The Positive
  • Gourjian Law knows its numbers with real precision — a 60% profit margin and 20%+ annual growth is a level of financial clarity most firms SMB Team audits do not have
  • Varand is already thinking in the right terms: he referenced a 20% of revenue reinvestment benchmark on the call, unprompted
The Gap
  • Without a formal plan, that willingness to reinvest ~$600K a year risks staying a good intention rather than becoming a funded growth strategy
  • Every year growth is funded ad hoc instead of planned is a year of compounding the firm could have captured instead
  • A firm this profitable has the resources to move first in its market — but only if that capital gets deployed intentionally
The firm already generates the capital to fund the exact hiring and visibility plan Varand described — the only thing missing is turning "I'm open to it" into a funded, running plan.
