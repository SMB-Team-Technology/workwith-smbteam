Lead Generation

Kopchian Law currently has no functioning website, no paid advertising, and no local SEO presence of any kind — every new client arrives through referrals and upsells to people Adam has already represented. Three named competitors are actively dominating the same immigration searches in Brooklyn and NYC with hundreds of reviews and live, content-rich websites.

Website Audit

"there is no website or active marketing."
The Problem
  • The domain resolves but serves no real firm content — the root page immediately redirects to a GoDaddy domain-parking placeholder page
  • PageSpeed scores: Not obtainable — no real website exists to score, and the PageSpeed Insights API returned a quota error (HTTP 429) on this pass regardless
  • No attorney bio exists on the firm's own domain — bio-style content only lives on third-party profiles (Avvo, Justia, LinkedIn, Lawyer.com) that the firm does not control
  • No contact form, no practice area pages, no case results, and no way for a prospect to reach the firm outside of already knowing Adam personally
Blind Spot: Because the domain technically resolves (HTTP 200), tools and prospects alike may assume Kopchian Law has an active web presence — in reality anyone who clicks through lands on a parking page with zero firm content, which is often worse for trust than having no domain at all.
The Gap
  • Every prospect who searches for an immigration attorney and does not already know Adam personally has nowhere to land — they go straight to a competitor's website instead
  • No owned platform means the firm cannot control its own story, bios, or case results — only third-party directories currently represent Adam online
  • A rebuilt, real website is a prerequisite for every other lead generation channel below — none of them work without a destination to send traffic to
A real website is the first physical asset that lets Kopchian Law generate new clients without Adam personally knowing them first — the foundation for the buffer he is trying to build.

Local SEO

The Problem
  • Google Business Profile shows a 1.0-star rating across only 2 reviews — both describe the attorney as not appearing to know the law and lacking confidence, a live and damaging reputation signal
  • Kopchian Law does not appear in the local 3-pack for "immigration lawyer Brooklyn NY," "green card lawyer Brooklyn NY," or "immigration attorney near me Brooklyn" — competitors occupy those results instead
  • Name, address, and phone number are inconsistent across three different directory identities: "Kopchian Law" at 195 Broadway, Brooklyn; "Kopchian Law" at 291 Broadway, NYC (per Avvo); and "Brown Law Practice PLLC" at a Long Island City address (per FindLaw) — a completely different firm name for the same attorney
Blind Spot: Google and directory algorithms weight Name/Address/Phone (NAP) consistency heavily — having three different identities live for the same attorney actively works against ranking and trust signals, independent of any website or ad spend issue. This is fixable at low cost and does not require a marketing budget to begin correcting.
The Gap
  • Every search a prospect runs for a nearby immigration attorney currently surfaces a competitor instead of Kopchian Law
  • A 1.0-star public rating is actively costing referrals credibility once a referred prospect checks Google before calling
Hargis Law Firm, PLLC — holds 100+ five-star Google reviews and an 11-consecutive-year Avvo Clients' Choice Award, with a dedicated webpage built specifically to showcase that review volume as an ongoing local SEO and trust asset.
Correcting the NAP inconsistency and rebuilding a review pipeline are the fastest, lowest-cost steps toward the referral flow Adam needs to stop relying on credit cards to cover expenses.

Google Ads

Ad Presence
  • Kopchian Law does not appear to be running Google search ads for immigration-related terms — consistent with having no live website to send paid traffic to
  • This is expected given the firm's current stage rather than a sign the firm chose not to advertise — there is currently no destination page a campaign could point to
Competitor Ad Landscape
  • Gilmer Law Firm, PLLC — runs a dedicated "Get your Green Card Now — Low Fee!" landing page, the kind of page typically built specifically for paid search traffic, suggesting active investment in this exact keyword space
  • Alena Shautsova Law Offices — maintains a content-rich, testimonial-heavy site (shautsova.com) that would convert paid traffic effectively if the firm is running ads to it
The Opportunity
  • "immigration lawyer Brooklyn NY" and "green card lawyer Brooklyn NY" — high-intent searches where Kopchian Law currently has zero visibility
  • "mandamus lawsuit USCIS delay" — a specific, high-value niche term tied directly to the mandamus actions Adam already offers as an upsell
  • Competitor-name conquest terms (e.g., searches for Hargis Law Firm or Alena Shautsova) to intercept prospects already comparing immigration attorneys
  • Neighboring-market terms (Queens, Long Island City, Jersey City immigration attorney) — geographies with no dedicated landing pages built yet by Kopchian Law
Blind Spot: Once a real website exists, immigration searches in NYC are highly competitive and typically require both Google Search ads and strong organic content to compete — running ads without fixing the underlying site and NAP issues first would waste spend.

Local Service Ads

LSA Presence
  • Kopchian Law does not appear to be running Local Service Ads or holding a Google Screened badge at this time
  • This is an open opportunity rather than a confirmed absence of interest — no LSA setup can exist without the underlying Google Business Profile and reputation issues addressed first
Competitor LSA Landscape
  • Direct live LSA placement data could not be confirmed in this research pass (no rendered top-of-SERP view available in this environment) — flagged as an open question rather than an asserted competitor list
The Opportunity
  • LSAs are pay-per-lead, not pay-per-click — a meaningful fit for a firm as budget-conscious as Kopchian Law right now
  • LSAs appear above everything else on Google — above paid ads, above the map pack, above all organic results — and you only pay when a real prospect calls you directly.
  • A rebuilt Google Business Profile with new reviews would directly qualify Kopchian Law for Google Screened status, unlocking this channel
