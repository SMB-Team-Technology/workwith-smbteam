Intake Assessment

Every intake call at Kopchian Law goes through Adam personally, with no defined process and no support to catch what he misses while buried in casework. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
"solo attorney is overwhelmed by casework."
The Problem
  • Adam personally handles every intake call himself — no delegation in place, no staff to catch overflow
  • No CRM or intake tracking system of any kind — referral leads are managed informally
  • No website means no after-hours coverage path at all — a referred prospect who calls outside business hours has nowhere to go but voicemail
  • No defined follow-up sequence for a prospect who does not sign immediately, at a time when Adam is already overwhelmed by casework
Blind Spot: Because every current lead is a referral, it is easy to assume intake works "well enough" — but a referred prospect who cannot get a timely response can still walk to Hargis Law Firm or Alena Shautsova instead, both of which have far larger reputations to fall back on.
The Gap
  • Every hour Adam spends on casework is an hour a new referral goes unanswered — in a solo practice, that gap costs a signed client, not just a delayed one
  • No system exists to measure how many referrals are being lost to slow response, so the true cost is currently invisible
  • Intake capacity, not lead volume, is the current ceiling on revenue — more referrals would not help until this is fixed
Fixing intake is the fastest way for Adam to convert more of what he already has coming in — without adding a single dollar of ad spend or risking the debt pressure he is already under.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • No contact form exists anywhere — the domain has no real page, only a parked placeholder
  • No chat or messaging option is present for a prospect who lands on the site expecting to reach the firm
  • Every channel a modern prospect expects (form, chat, click-to-call) is currently unavailable outside of a personal referral introduction

Team Assessment

Kopchian Law has no team at all — Adam is the entire firm, personally handling every case and every client interaction. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
"Growth is limited to referrals and upsells... there is no website or active marketing."
The Problem
  • No staff of any kind — Adam is solo across casework, intake, and every administrative function
  • No org structure or delegation exists to absorb work when Adam is unavailable
  • Adam previously enrolled in two business-coaching programs (New Law Business Model and HTM) and discontinued both due to lack of time and feeling overwhelmed — a direct symptom of having no one to share the load
  • Every new referral adds directly to Adam's personal workload with no outlet
Blind Spot: The fact that Adam has made it this far solo can mask how close to capacity he already is — the same overwhelm that ended two coaching programs will also limit how much new business the firm can safely take on without a team in place first.
The Gap
  • Every hour Adam spends on tasks a team member could handle is an hour he is not spending on the highest-value casework or on building the systems that would free him up
  • There is no path to taking meaningful time away from the firm right now — revenue stops the moment Adam stops working
  • Without a team, growth simply means more hours for Adam, not more freedom
Building even a small team is the step that finally moves Adam from being the player in every case to being the owner of a firm that can run without him in the room.

Profit Plan Assessment

Weekly revenue of $5,000–$6,000 is already falling short of covering Adam's own debt obligations, and business expenses are currently going onto credit cards. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: RED
"Weekly revenue of $5,000–$6,000 is insufficient to meet debt obligations and create a financial buffer, leading to business expenses being put on credit cards."
The Problem
  • Current weekly revenue is not enough to cover Adam's debt obligations, let alone build any financial cushion
  • Business expenses are being charged to credit cards — a sign of no working capital buffer
  • No financial visibility beyond weekly cash coming in — no tracked profit plan or margin picture
  • The stated revenue goal of $7,000–$8,000/week exists specifically to cover debt and create a buffer — not to fund expansion for its own sake
Blind Spot: Adding marketing spend before fixing this cash pressure would make the credit-card cycle worse, not better — which is exactly why the systems-first approach discussed on the call matters more here than in a typical growth engagement.
The Gap
  • Without a profit plan, Adam cannot tell which cases or activities are actually building the buffer he needs versus simply keeping the lights on
  • Continuing to fund the business on credit cards adds interest cost on top of an already tight margin
  • The $2,000–$3,000/week gap between current and goal revenue represents the buffer Adam is trying to build — currently unaddressed by any system
A real profit plan is what turns "enough revenue to survive the week" into the financial buffer Adam has been trying to build since before this call.
