Intake Assessment

At Duffy Law, calls booked through the website land on Kristen directly, and a single emotional call can pull her away from the rest of her day. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: RED
"I really want people to stop using my personal phone."
The Problem
  • Booked consultation calls are handled by you personally rather than screened by a team member first
  • Long-time clients text your personal phone, so client communication follows you into evenings and time off
  • The team member who handles phones and background tasks is underused while you take the calls yourself
  • There is no defined step that decides which calls need an attorney and which can be handled, scheduled or referred out by someone else
Blind Spot: The cost of a long call is not only the call. It is the review work waiting behind it, which is how a manageable day turns into working late.
The Positive
  • Prospects are already booking directly from your website, so the demand and the trust are there
  • You have a person in place for phones and administration, so intake does not need a new hire to start improving
The Gap
  • Every call you take is an hour not spent on billable work or on the review queue
  • With no screening step, matters you would rather refer out still consume your attention
  • You are turning business away because you cannot keep up, so each hour recovered from intake is capacity the firm can use
Moving intake off your desk is the quickest way to give yourself time back — and the first step toward a phone that stays quiet when you are away.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The homepage has no contact form; visitors are directed to a phone number or a Book Your Appointment button
  • Live chat was not detected on the site
  • No screening step was observed on the homepage to sort visitors by practice area before they book, which is why you have considered a consultation fee to filter calls

Team Assessment

Duffy Law has a team in place, but work product, hiring decisions and client communication still run through Kristen. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
"Yeah, I'm still very owner-dependent."
The Problem
  • Work product from your paralegal and legal assistant goes through you for review, and the review queue builds up on busy days
  • You want to hire an attorney but are not sure how to trust who you are hiring
  • You worry about spending two years training someone who then leaves
  • No true vacation since 2016 — the firm has not yet been tested without you
Blind Spot: The fear of training an associate who leaves is real, but the cost of not hiring is already being paid every week in your own hours. Without a defined role and a way to measure it, the same fear will also hold back any hire you do make.
The Positive
  • You already have a paralegal, a legal assistant, an administrator and an of-counsel attorney — the foundation of a team is there
  • You have a clear picture of the end state: you do roughly 20 percent of the work and delegate the other 80 percent to competent people
The Gap
  • Being the reviewer for everything caps the firm at the hours you personally have
  • Without clear roles for who can finalize which work, delegation stays informal and falls back to you
  • The hiring plan is stuck on trust and structure, which delays the one change that would free the most time
A firm that runs without you is what turns a week unplugged from a hope into something you can schedule.

Profit Plan Assessment

Duffy Law's revenue has grown steadily, but billing has fallen behind and there is no regular view of what the firm actually keeps. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"I'm so far behind in billing, so I'm getting caught up with that."
The Problem
  • Billing is behind, which delays revenue you have already earned
  • You described not recently checking whether the firm is profitable, so margins are unclear
  • Books were brought up to date with outside help for a few months, but there is no ongoing financial routine
  • Hiring decisions, including the associate you are considering, are being weighed without a clear profit model
Blind Spot: As a CPA, you read numbers better than most owners. The gap here is not skill but time — billing falls behind for the same reason everything else does: it all routes through you.
The Positive
  • Revenue has grown each year, with further growth expected this year
  • You already work out the overhead on of-counsel work to make sure each matter makes money — the right instinct, applied to one part of the firm
The Gap
  • Unbilled time is earned revenue that has not been invoiced, so catching up on billing is the fastest way to improve cash coming in
  • Without a monthly profit view, it is hard to tell whether adding an associate or more clients will increase take-home or only workload
  • You told us that even a little less money would be fine if the firm were sustainable — a profit plan is how you know the trade-off is safe
Knowing your numbers is what lets you decide to work less and still be confident the firm can pay for two weeks away.
