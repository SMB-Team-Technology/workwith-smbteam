Intake Assessment

A 24/7 answering service gives Dhanani Law after-hours phone coverage, but its conversion rate on higher volume leads has never been tested, and the website's own contact form is buried where most visitors will never find it. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: AMBER
"A 24/7 answering service handles calls, but its conversion rate on paid leads is unproven."
The Problem
  • Your website's contact form is buried in the footer — the primary path to booking a consultation is not visible without scrolling past a competing newsletter signup
  • A separate newsletter signup form mid-page distracts from the "Schedule a Consultation" call to action
  • The answering service's actual conversion rate on inbound leads has never been measured
  • No defined follow-up sequence exists for prospects who call but don't sign immediately
Blind Spot: You may not realize how many visitors reach your site intending to reach out, get distracted by the newsletter form, or simply can't find the contact form quickly, and leave without ever making contact — that's a loss that happens before the answering service is even involved.
The Positive
  • Having any after-hours coverage at all — via the answering service — already puts Dhanani Law ahead of solo firms with no coverage past business hours
The Gap
  • An unmeasured intake conversion rate means Rahim has no way to know how many referral leads are quietly being lost every month — revenue already earned through 25 years of reputation, walking away uncounted
  • A buried contact form actively works against every dollar spent building the website in the first place
  • Without a follow-up sequence, prospects who don't convert on the first call are simply gone
Intake is the one pillar that can improve without spending a single new dollar on lead generation — exactly the kind of foundational fix that should come before any ad spend, and directly supports the systems-first sequencing Rahim is looking for.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • Contact form is located in the footer only — not visible above the fold or reinforced elsewhere on the homepage
  • A competing newsletter signup form mid-page splits visitor attention away from the primary consultation request path
  • No live chat is present on the site — a lower-cost, incremental improvement worth considering once other intake fixes are in place

Team Assessment

Rahim is Dhanani Law Firm's only attorney and its only point of accountability — every case, every intake call, and every decision runs through him personally. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
"Past attempts to hire freelancers failed due to poor quality and lack of detail."
The Problem
  • Rahim is the sole attorney and the firm's sole bottleneck — nothing moves without him
  • Past freelance hiring attempts failed due to poor quality and lack of detail, leaving him hesitant to try again
  • No accountability structure or org chart exists — there is no one else to delegate to even if Rahim wanted to
  • The firm's growth is capped by his personal capacity, not by demand — referrals already outpace what one person can handle well
Blind Spot: The "chicken-and-egg" problem you named yourself is the real blind spot here — you can't justify hiring without more consistent volume, but you can't handle more volume without hiring. That loop doesn't break on its own; it takes a deliberate plan for both sides at once.
The Gap
  • Every hour Rahim spends on a task someone else could do is an hour he is not spending on strategy, or with his family — the direct opposite of the personal freedom he says he wants
  • A firm with no one else trained to run it has no value beyond Rahim's own presence, which works directly against building something "sellable"
  • The prior failed hire has created hesitation that, left unaddressed, will keep the bottleneck in place indefinitely
You told us the goal is a firm that runs without your constant presence — that outcome does not happen by generating more leads, it happens by building the team and delegation systems that let someone besides you carry the load.

Profit Plan Assessment

Dhanani Law Firm is pacing $150K-$200K/year against a stated $500K gross / $250K net initial target, but no margin, expense, or acquisition-cost data exists anywhere beyond that top-line figure. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • No financial visibility exists beyond top-line annual revenue (~$150K-$200K/year)
  • No tracking of margin, cost per case, or cost per referral source
  • The stated $500K gross / $250K net target has no defined path or cost model behind it yet
  • No way currently exists to know which practice area — family or business immigration — is more profitable per case
Blind Spot: Without basic financial tracking, it's possible to grow revenue and still not know whether the firm is actually keeping more of it — more clients could mean more work at the same or worse margin, not more profit.
The Gap
  • Without financial visibility, Rahim cannot know if the firm is on track toward the $250K net target or simply generating more top-line revenue with no more take-home
  • No acquisition-cost data means any future ad spend cannot be measured against actual return without building this tracking first
  • A firm with no margin visibility is harder to value and harder to eventually sell — directly at odds with the "sellable firm" goal
Building a lasting legacy starts with knowing the numbers behind the revenue — profit visibility is what turns "the firm is growing" into "the firm is worth more, and so is my time."
