Here Is Where Scarborough Law Stands Today

Xavier Scarborough runs Scarborough Law as a first-year solo practice in Houston, focused on estate planning (trusts, wills, and special needs trusts) with probate as a second practice area and a stated goal of serving high-net-worth families. New clients have come mainly from networking and strong referral partners, while Google Ads and Local Service Ads produced early clients and have since gone quiet. The most important finding: clients are signing at roughly $4,000 per trust plan, but the firm has no measured, always-on channel to bring in six to eight of them a month without taking up his own time.

Stage 6 is where demand outgrows what one person can handle and the firm is built to absorb it — exactly the direction this plan is designed to take you.

Where You Are Today

Stage 3: Solo Practitioner
  • Referral partners and three to four networking events a week drive most new clients today
  • Local Service Ads started with leads, then tailed off to zero in the past seven days
  • Xavier answers his own phones and personally handles drafting, notarizing, and witness logistics
  • Lead-to-client conversion has never been tracked, so what a signed client costs is not yet visible

Where You Need To Be

Stage 6: Law Firm Owner
  • The firm runs without you
  • Your income is no longer tied to your personal hours
  • Your time is yours to spend however you choose
  • This is where true freedom and true wealth become possible
The gap between three clients a month and six to eight is a system, not a talent problem — and building that system is how you grow into the firm you are aiming for.
Overall Growth Stage Assessment
Scarborough Law
Xavier is operating at Stage 3 — a Solo Practitioner whose referral network is strong but whose pipeline still depends on his own time at networking lunches. The single biggest limit right now is the absence of a measured, always-on lead channel: Local Service Ads that produced early leads have gone quiet, and there is no tracking to explain why. Until that changes, the goal of six to eight clients a month, roughly double the current pace, will keep depending on how many hours he can spend networking.
