Here Is Where Scarborough Law Stands Today

Xavier Scarborough runs Scarborough Law as a first-year solo practice in Houston, focused on estate planning (trusts, wills, and special needs trusts) with probate as a second practice area and a stated goal of serving high-net-worth families. New clients have come mainly from networking and strong referral partners, while Google Ads and Local Service Ads produced early clients and have since gone quiet. The most important finding: clients are signing at roughly $4,000 per trust plan, but the firm has no measured, always-on channel to bring in six to eight of them a month without taking up his own time.

Stage 6 is where demand outgrows what one person can handle and the firm is built to absorb it — exactly the direction this plan is designed to take you.

The Law Firm Growth Acceleration Model

We Help Law Firm Owners Get More Profit, More Personal Freedom, And Outstanding Results For Their Clients.

Most law firms try to grow by chasing more leads. But leads alone are not the problem — and leads alone are not the answer. Here is what actually works.

Lead Generation

Leads are to a law firm what gasoline is to a car. Without them, you go nowhere. But a steady, predictable flow of leads changes everything — you get to choose your cases instead of taking whatever comes in.

Intake

But all the leads in the world are worthless without a system to turn them into paying clients. Most firms are losing cases they already paid for simply because their follow-up is slow or inconsistent.

Self-Managing Team

Once clients are coming in, you need a team — otherwise you end up working 90-hour weeks and results suffer. A self-managing team is what gives you the freedom to step back.

Profit Plan

And once the team is in place, most owners look at their bank account and ask: where did all the money go? That is what a profit plan solves — clarity on what you earn, what you keep, and how to grow both.

All four have to work together. If one is missing, you cannot have more profit, more freedom, and outstanding results for your clients at the same time.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • The site was rebuilt very recently with ChatGPT, so it is still new and unproven — no client testimonials, reviews, or schema markup were identified on it
  • The domain, ScarboroughFamilyLawyer.com, reads as family law while the practice is estate planning and probate
  • No dedicated special needs trust page, and no pages aimed at Katy, Sugar Land, The Woodlands, or St. Louis
  • No video of the attorney and no live chat identified — the first impression is text only
Blind Spot: A name and domain that suggest family law can pull in the wrong searches and the wrong expectations — and the contact email uses a different domain (scarboroughpllc.com) than the website, which splits the firm's identity in two places.
The Positive
  • A clear attorney bio with Georgetown Law, U.S. Army service, and Texas and Missouri licenses builds credibility with high-net-worth families
  • A clear call to action (“Tell us how we can help”) and a dedicated intake page, plus a blog with Texas-specific trust and probate topics
The Gap
  • Paid traffic that lands on a site without trust signals or matching pages converts less of the money spent on ads
  • Special needs trusts are one of Xavier's services, yet searchers for that topic have no page to land on
  • Suburban Houston families searching close to home have no location-specific page to find
A site that matches the high-net-worth families you want to serve is what lets Google Ads turn clicks into the six to eight clients a month you are aiming for.

Intake Assessment

Every inquiry to Scarborough Law reaches Xavier directly — he answers his own phones and has not yet tracked how many inquiries become signed plans. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: RED
"I'm really confident in my process, you know, once they get through the door, … it's just showing them the door."
The Problem
  • You personally answer every call — with no staff, a call during a signing or a hearing goes unanswered
  • Conversion from inquiry to consultation to signed plan has never been tracked, so there is no way to see where families drop off
  • With Local Service Ads reporting zero leads in the past seven days, there is no record of whether calls were missed or never came in
  • No defined follow-up sequence was described for people who inquire but do not sign right away
Blind Spot: You may not realize how many people call a second firm when the first one does not pick up. Families dealing with a death or a major planning decision often contact several attorneys in one sitting and sign with whoever responds first.
The Positive
  • Your confidence in what happens once a family is in the door is a real asset — you record every meeting and handle the notary and witnesses yourself, so signings run smoothly
  • A dedicated intake screening page, reachable from several buttons on the site, gives every inquiry a clear starting point
The Gap
  • At roughly $4,000 per trust plan, each missed or slow-followed-up inquiry that would have signed costs the firm about a month of one client's fee
  • Without conversion numbers, a marketing campaign that looks like it is failing could really be an intake problem — and vice versa
  • Every inquiry that waits for Xavier to be free competes with firms that can answer immediately
Turning more of today's inquiries into signed plans is the fastest way to grow toward six to eight clients a month.

Team Assessment

Scarborough Law is a one-attorney practice: Xavier handles consultations, drafting, notarizing, and signings himself. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
"I'd rather have that problem than not enough, because I totally believe in that concept of getting yourself overworked and overlead, and then you'd be naturally forced to delegate."
The Problem
  • No staff — every call, draft, and signing runs through you
  • You recruit your own witnesses and hold your own notary license, so signing logistics also sit on your calendar
  • A virtual paralegal for drafting is something you have thought about, but no one is in place or trained yet
  • Three to four networking lunches a week keep referrals flowing, but they only work when you are there
Blind Spot: Waiting to be forced to delegate means delegating in a rush — during a month when calls are missed, drafts are late, and the first hire has to be found under pressure. Choosing the first hire before the volume arrives is faster and cheaper than learning it in the middle of overload.
The Positive
  • You already see that growth will require delegation and are open to it — many owners resist this step for years
  • You record all of your meetings, which creates raw material a future paralegal or associate could learn from
The Gap
  • Every added client adds hours to your week, because no one else can take any of the work
  • Time spent on drafting and logistics is time not spent on consultations and the networking that wins high-net-worth clients
  • There is no one to keep the firm moving during a vacation, an illness, or a long signing day
A first hire chosen on your timeline lets you keep growing fast without being buried by the growth.

Profit Plan Assessment

Scarborough Law is a first-year firm billing roughly $12,000 a month from about three flat-fee clients, with a goal of reaching six to eight a month. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"I would say I had about three clients per month. So that's probably 12K a month."
The Problem
  • Cost to win a client is not tracked by channel, so there is no way to compare Google Ads, Local Service Ads, events, and postcards against each other
  • Conversion rate from inquiry to signed plan has never been measured, which makes any budget a guess
  • A 2027 marketing budget of roughly $1,500 to $2,000 a month was on your mind — but it has not yet been set against a client target and what each client is worth
  • Postcards “work, they're just expensive,” yet there is no per-client number to confirm that
Blind Spot: You may not realize that a marketing budget that feels small can cost more per client than a larger, tracked one. Without a per-client number, it is impossible to tell whether a cheaper channel is truly cheaper.
The Positive
  • Flat-fee trust plans at roughly $4,000 and up give each new client strong value against the cost of winning them
  • The Wine and Wills event brought in two clients on about $1,500 of marketing — you already have one data point that marketing pays back
The Gap
  • Without cost-per-client numbers, money can flow to the channels that feel busy instead of the ones that sign clients
  • Without a client target tied to a marketing budget, it is hard to know how fast it is safe to grow
  • Fixed costs like software and binders are small, but unmeasured acquisition spending is where profit leaks in a young firm
Knowing what each client costs to win lets you grow as fast as you want to — and keep more of what you earn.
