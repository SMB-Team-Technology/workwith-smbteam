Lead Generation

Guymon Law has 16 years of credibility and two established Arizona offices, but almost none of that credibility currently shows up in local search, paid ads, or a review footprint big enough to compete with the Scottsdale firms it's up against. Without a lead generation system in place, the firm is relying on reputation alone in a market where named competitors are actively investing in visibility.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • The homepage contact form sits at the bottom of the page, not above the fold — real friction for a firm trying to capture leads fast
  • PageSpeed scores: Mobile NOT OBTAINED — Desktop NOT OBTAINED (public API quota was exhausted this session and the live report renders client-side; recommend pulling both numbers manually before the proposal call)
  • No live chat is present to capture visitors who won't fill out a form
  • The blog's most recent visible posts are dated 2021 — stale content is a missed SEO opportunity
The Positive
  • Dedicated Scottsdale and Chandler landing pages already exist — more localized SEO investment than many prospects this size have made
  • A full "Meet the Team" bio section and a video library are already in place, giving real credibility once more traffic arrives
The Gap
  • The below-the-fold contact form and missing live chat mean visitors likely leave before ever reaching out
  • A blog untouched since 2021 signals to Google — and to prospective clients — that the site isn't being actively maintained
  • Without current PageSpeed data confirmed, mobile visitors could be abandoning the site before it finishes loading — worth verifying regardless of which growth path is chosen
A faster, easier-to-convert website turns the traffic Guymon Law already has into more of the cases that fund the pay raises Amber wants to give her team.

Local SEO

The Problem
  • Guymon Law's live Google Business Profile star rating and review count could not be independently verified this session; the best available proxies (Yelp 14 reviews, Avvo 4.5/5.0 across 13 reviews) point to a review footprint an order of magnitude below Scottsdale competitors
  • The firm did not surface in any of the three local search tests run for family law, divorce, or child custody terms in Scottsdale or Chandler
  • A Birdeye-aggregated listing for "Law Office of Amber L. Guymon" at a Gilbert, AZ address (4.6★/71 reviews) does not match either published office — worth confirming directly with Alison
Blind Spot: A NAP inconsistency like the Gilbert, AZ listing found in this research can quietly suppress a firm's local ranking even when its primary listings are otherwise correct — this is worth resolving regardless of which service package is chosen.
The Positive
  • The firm already holds an A+ BBB rating (accredited since November 2020) and active profiles on Avvo, Justia, Super Lawyers, and LawInfo — a solid directory foundation to build on
The Gap
  • Every named competitor in this market has a review count in the hundreds — Guymon Law's proxied review count is nowhere close
  • Not surfacing in local search results means prospective clients searching for a family law attorney in Scottsdale or Chandler are finding competitors first
Arizona Law Group — 400+ Google reviews at 4.8★, directly overlapping Guymon Law's Scottsdale market and outranking it in every local search test run this session.
Closing the review and visibility gap with firms like Arizona Law Group and Owens & Perkins is what would let more of Guymon Law's own local searches turn into new cases — capacity that funds pay raises without adding headcount.

Blind Spot: Guymon Law already has geo-targeted landing pages for both Chandler and Scottsdale — most firms would need to build these from scratch before running geo-targeted ads. That work is already done.

Competitor LSA Landscape
  • Arizona Law Group — LSA participation not confirmed this session, but their 400+ Google reviews would make them a strong LSA candidate if participating
  • Owens & Perkins — LSA participation not confirmed this session, but their 240 Google reviews would make them a strong LSA candidate if participating
The Opportunity
  • With even a modest review count, Guymon Law could hold an LSA slot backed by a Google Screened badge that builds trust before a call ever happens
  • LSAs appear above everything else on Google — above paid ads, above the map pack, above all organic results — and you only pay when a real prospect calls you directly.
  • Family law LSA leads are billed per qualified call, not per click — a lower-risk way to test paid lead generation before committing to a full campaign
Blind Spot: LSA eligibility requires passing a background check and license verification — steps Guymon Law can complete before ever spending a dollar on ads.
