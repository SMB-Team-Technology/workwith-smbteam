Intake Assessment

Guymon Law's intake and conflict-checking process currently spans five or more disconnected systems, and the website's own contact form sits below the fold — a firm that already gets calls in the door still risks losing them before they convert. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Intake and conflict-checking span 5+ separate systems, per the discovery call — no single source of truth for a new client inquiry
  • The website's contact form is buried at the bottom of the homepage, not above the fold
  • No live chat is present to capture visitors who won't fill out a form
  • No after-hours coverage was confirmed — calls or form submissions after hours may sit until the next business day
Blind Spot: Every manual step in a 5-system intake process is a place a prospective client can fall through the cracks without anyone at the firm ever knowing a lead was lost.
The Positive
  • The contact form already captures name, phone, email, case description, and SMS opt-in — a reasonably complete form once its placement is fixed
  • Staff are already using Claude for email triage, which is a real head start on automating intake communication
The Gap
  • Every extra manual step between a prospect's first contact and a signed case is a chance to lose that case to a competitor who responds faster
  • With intake spread across 5+ systems, no one at the firm has full visibility into how many leads are actually being followed up on versus falling through
Streamlining intake into fewer, connected systems is exactly the kind of capacity gain that lets Amber's team handle more without adding headcount — and frees up the margin to pay them more.

Team Assessment

Amber built Guymon Law into a four-attorney, two-office firm, but she is still the one personally handling sensitive tasks like performance reviews and training-video creation. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"Use AI to increase staff capacity and pay raises, avoiding new hires."
The Problem
  • Amber personally records video tutorials and handles performance reviews rather than delegating them
  • The firm's existing AI use (Claude for email triage and paralegal drafting) is ad hoc, not yet standardized across the team
  • Staff outside of Amber are hesitant to use AI and prefer familiar manual processes, per the discovery call
  • Team size has stayed level while the firm looks to add capacity without new hires
  • No mention was made of a manager or COO-level role handling day-to-day operations independent of Amber
Blind Spot: Staff hesitation toward AI means a rollout that depends on the team adopting new tools themselves is likely to stall — the faster path is automation built and delivered by a dedicated outside expert, not one more system staff are asked to learn on their own.
The Positive
  • The firm already has real team depth — 4 attorneys, a Legal Paraprofessional, and support staff across two offices
  • Amber is already comfortable using AI tools personally, which is a head start most prospects don't have
The Gap
  • Every sensitive task Amber handles personally is time she can't spend on higher-value work or her own life outside the firm
  • Ad hoc AI adoption limits the capacity gains to whatever Amber and a few staff happen to try on their own, rather than a firm-wide system
Turning ad hoc AI use into a structured, firm-wide system is exactly what lets Guymon Law's existing team handle more work — and lets Amber pay them more for it, without hiring a single new person.

Profit Plan Assessment

Guymon Law's annual revenue was not stated on the call — the only figure available is a monthly range from the internal trigger record — and no case value, close rate, or profit target was discussed either. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • No annual revenue figure was stated directly on the call; the available range comes from an internal monthly estimate, not a number Amber gave
  • No revenue goal was stated — there is currently no target to plan capacity or pay raises against
  • Average case value and close rate were not discussed
  • No bookkeeping or financial advisory engagement was mentioned
Blind Spot: Without a stated revenue goal, it's hard to know how much of the capacity AI frees up should convert into pay raises versus reinvestment — that's a number worth pinning down before finalizing pay decisions.
The Gap
  • Without a stated revenue goal or close rate, it's hard to measure whether the capacity AI frees up is actually being converted into the pay raises Amber wants to give
  • No visibility into case value or profitability by case type makes it hard to know which part of the firm's caseload is actually driving profit
A clear profit plan is what turns "the team has more capacity now" into a specific, provable case for the raises Amber wants to give them.
