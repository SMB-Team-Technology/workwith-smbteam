Lead Generation

BW Law currently relies on Google Business Profile, referrals, and local networking for every new client — a real, working foundation, but one with no paid channel behind it to add volume on top. That single-source dependency is the quiet risk sitting underneath Sarah's plan to grow from ~$200k to $500k.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • The Wix-built site's footer displays "©2035" — an unedited template placeholder that is a small but visible trust signal issue for prospective clients doing due diligence
  • PageSpeed mobile/desktop scores could not be verified in this research pass (tooling limitation) — recommend running a live PageSpeed Insights check manually before finalizing any website recommendation
  • No dedicated attorney bio page content surfaces on the homepage — a "Team Profile" page exists, but bio depth was not confirmed
  • No blog or resource content exists beyond a placeholder-style "News & Resources" page link, and no video content is present
Blind Spot: The contact form is not visible on the homepage — contact information lives in the footer only, which means a visitor ready to act has to hunt for a way to reach the firm instead of finding one immediately.
The Positive
  • The homepage clearly signals the firm's bilingual Chinese/English identity and includes a visible "Request A Consultation" call-to-action above the fold
  • The site appears mobile responsive based on its nav and layout structure
The Gap
  • Every visitor who lands on the site and cannot immediately find a way to make contact is a lead that may go to a competitor with an easier path to reach out
  • Without a bio page that builds trust in Sarah's specific experience, the site is not doing as much selling as it could before a prospect ever picks up the phone
  • An uncorrected "©2035" footer is a small, free fix that currently undercuts the credibility the firm has otherwise earned
A website that sells and converts on its own is one less thing competing for Sarah's personal attention every week.

Local SEO

The Problem
  • BW Law's Google Business Profile shows the strongest star rating in its competitive set (5.0) but only 21 reviews — well behind two established local competitors
  • BW Law did not surface on page one of any of three generic local immigration searches performed for this market ("immigration attorney Gainesville FL," "immigration lawyer near Gainesville Florida," "visa attorney Gainesville Florida")
  • An address/suite number inconsistency exists across citations: the website and Avvo list "Ste 190," while Sunbiz and BCGSearch reference "Ste 307" — a NAP (name/address/phone) inconsistency that can quietly suppress local search trust signals
  • The firm is confirmed present on Avvo (with a "Not Yet Reviewed" / 0.0 rating despite 21 reviews elsewhere) and LegalRank.co, but could not be confirmed on Justia (403 blocked), and does not appear on FindLaw or Yelp
Blind Spot: An AI visibility report raised on the call found the site is not optimized for generic English-language AI search prompts — but this is a live, unresolved thread. Sarah pushed back that she has received clients from ChatGPT, including from non-Chinese searches, and both parties agreed the report's test prompts were likely too generic compared to what her real clients search for. Randy is following up with the exact prompts used before this should be treated as a settled finding.
The Positive
  • BW Law holds the highest star rating (tied at 5.0) of any firm in its local comparison set — a real trust advantage once review volume catches up
The Gap
  • Every prospect who searches generic immigration terms in Gainesville and doesn't see BW Law on page one is a prospect who never learns the firm exists
  • Missing directory presence on Yelp and FindLaw, plus an unresolved NAP inconsistency, are fixable gaps costing free visibility competitors are already capturing
dePaz Cabrera Immigration Law — a 10+ year practice with broad organic directory presence across Justia, Super Lawyers, LawInfo, and Facebook, consistently surfacing in places BW Law does not.
Closing these visibility gaps builds a second source of new clients that runs on its own — without adding a single hour to Sarah's week.

Google Ads

Ad Presence
  • BW Law does not appear to be running Google search ads for immigration-related terms in this market — consistent with the transcript, where paid marketing was confirmed as "None"
  • No paid search history exists to evaluate, which means there is no existing campaign data to build from — but also no bad habits to unwind
Competitor Ad Landscape
  • Named Gainesville-area immigration competitors — no competitor ad units (sponsored listings) surfaced in the searches performed for this market during this research pass; this does not confirm no one is running ads, only that none rendered in this session's tooling
The Opportunity
  • Practice-area + city terms: "immigration attorney Gainesville FL," "green card lawyer Gainesville," "H-1B visa attorney Gainesville"
  • Case-type specific terms: naturalization, deportation defense, family-based immigration, employment-based visas (O, P, R, L/E, H-1B, EB categories)
  • Non-obvious opportunity: bilingual-specific search terms capturing Mandarin-speaking prospects searching in English for a Chinese-speaking attorney — directly aligned with the firm's stated differentiator
  • Non-obvious opportunity: competitor-name and neighboring-city terms (Ocala, Lake City), where BW Law currently has no geo-targeted landing pages

Local Service Ads

LSA Presence

Competitor LSA Landscape
  • No Gainesville-local named competitor was identified holding an LSA or Google Screened badge in the searches performed for this practice area and market
The Opportunity

  • Pay-per-lead pricing means spend is tied directly to actual prospect contact, not clicks — a lower-risk way to test paid visibility alongside a coaching engagement
