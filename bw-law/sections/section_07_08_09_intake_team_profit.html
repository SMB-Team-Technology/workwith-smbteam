Intake Assessment

Intake volume is currently manageable for Sarah and her one part-time legal assistant, but the process runs on her personal availability rather than a defined system. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • No contact form is visible on the homepage — contact information lives in the footer only, adding friction between a ready prospect and a first conversation
  • Intake currently depends on Sarah and one part-time assistant, with no documented process for after-hours coverage
  • No CRM or intake-tracking system was mentioned on the call — there is no way to confirm leads are being consistently followed up with
  • As case volume grows toward the $500k goal, the current informal intake approach has no built-in room to scale
Blind Spot: You may not realize how many prospects visit the site after hours, can't easily find a way to reach the firm, and call a competitor before the next business day begins.
The Positive
  • Referral-based leads tend to arrive pre-qualified and trusting, which reduces some of the intake burden that a colder lead source would create
  • The firm's high star rating suggests intake conversations that do happen are landing well with prospects
The Gap
  • Every lead that reaches the site after hours with no clear path to contact is potential revenue lost toward the $500k goal — at no additional marketing cost to recover
  • Without a defined intake process, growth in lead volume will increase pressure on Sarah personally rather than being absorbed by a system
  • No visibility into current intake-to-signed-client conversion means there is no baseline to measure improvement against
A defined intake process is one more piece of "chief everything officer" work Sarah can hand off instead of carrying herself.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • No contact form appears on the homepage — a visitor has to scroll to the footer to find phone, email, or address information
  • No live chat is present on the site
  • The visible "Request A Consultation" button above the fold is a strong start, but its destination and follow-through were not independently confirmed in this research pass

Team Assessment

Sarah runs BW Law as its owner, lead attorney, and only real manager — personally reviewing every piece of contractor and partner-attorney work before it goes out. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
"Chief everything officer"
The Problem
  • Sarah personally reviews all contractor work — there is no delegated quality-control layer anywhere in the firm
  • The team is Sarah, one part-time legal assistant, and outside contractors and partner attorneys used only for overflow — no one has been hired into an ops, training, or management role
  • Sarah is working 40-50+ hours a week, much of it consumed by oversight rather than higher-value legal or growth work
  • There is no documented hiring or onboarding process, which means every new hire currently depends entirely on Sarah's personal time to bring up to speed
Blind Spot: Because Sarah has never experienced a team that runs without her constant review, it is easy to assume this level of personal oversight is simply what running a law firm requires. It is not — it is a symptom of not yet having a hiring and delegation system in place.
The Positive
  • Sarah is already using contractors and partner attorneys for overflow — the instinct to delegate exists, even without the system to support it yet
The Gap
  • Every hour Sarah spends personally reviewing contractor work is an hour she is not spending on the business development and networking she says she wants more time for
  • Without a repeatable hiring process, scaling the team to match $500k in revenue means scaling Sarah's oversight burden right along with it
  • The stress of being "chief everything officer" is a direct, stated cost of the current setup — not a hypothetical one
This is the exact gap standing between Sarah and the shift from Small Business Manager to Law Firm CEO that she described wanting on the call.

Profit Plan Assessment

BW Law grew to roughly $200k in revenue in 2025 with an expectation of higher numbers in 2026, and Sarah has set a clear $500k target — but no profit tracking or planning system was described on the call. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • The stated goal of scaling from ~$200k to $500k is a 2.5x jump with no operational or financial plan yet built to support it
  • No practice-area profitability data exists or was shared — case mix and margins by category remain unknown
  • Growth is currently being driven by referral volume rather than a deliberate plan tying revenue targets to hiring and capacity decisions
  • With Sarah personally handling oversight of all contractor work, more revenue today likely means more of Sarah's time, not more margin
Blind Spot: A revenue goal without a hiring and operations plan behind it can create a firm that generates more billings but not more actual take-home freedom or profit for Sarah personally.
The Positive
  • Sarah confirmed comfort with a realistic ~$2,000/month coaching investment — a positive sign of financial awareness and readiness to invest in the firm's growth
The Gap
  • Without a hiring and systems plan tied to the revenue goal, hitting $500k could simply mean 2.5x the current workload for Sarah rather than 2.5x the freedom
  • No stated visibility into which case types are most profitable makes it hard to know where growth capacity should be added first
A profit plan that grows alongside a hiring plan is what turns "more revenue" into "more freedom" — which is the outcome Sarah actually described wanting.
