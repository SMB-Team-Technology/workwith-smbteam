Intake Assessment

Every lead that reaches Prendamano Tax Resolution goes straight to Jennifer, with no intake team, no defined follow-up process, and a contact form buried a click off the homepage. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Jennifer personally handles every intake call — there is no delegation in place
  • No CRM or intake tracking software was mentioned — with lead volume already this low, any dropped lead is a real loss
  • No after-hours coverage — someone facing an IRS notice at 8pm has no way to reach the firm until the next business day
  • No defined follow-up sequence exists for leads who do not sign on the first call
Blind Spot: You may not realize how many people search for IRS help outside business hours, in a panic, right after opening a notice — and how many of them will call the next firm on the list if no one answers.
The Gap
  • At $10,000 per case, every missed or delayed follow-up is a $10,000 loss, not a minor inconvenience
  • With only one signed client so far, the firm cannot afford to lose any of the leads its $4,200/month in marketing spend is already generating
  • Every hour Jennifer spends on intake is an hour not spent on casework or the firm's own growth
A firm you can eventually sell needs an intake process that runs without you personally answering every call — right now, that process does not exist.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The homepage CTA links out to a separate contact page rather than presenting a form directly on the homepage — the request is buried one click deep
  • No live chat is present, so visitors browsing after hours have no way to start a conversation before calling
  • The contact page does include a standard name/email/phone/message form once reached — the form itself is functional, but visibility is the problem

These findings are also referenced in the Website Audit section.

Team Assessment

Prendamano Tax Resolution has no team beyond Jennifer herself — no associates, no intake staff, no marketing support. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
"financial autonomy and time freedom"
The Problem
  • Every function of the firm — casework, intake, marketing oversight — runs through one person
  • There is no accountability structure because there is currently no one else in the firm to hold accountable
  • The firm cannot take on more volume than Jennifer alone can personally process
  • Nothing in the firm's current operation would allow Jennifer to step away, even briefly, without work stopping
Blind Spot: After 26 years as a partner at another firm, doing everything yourself may feel normal — but it is the single biggest obstacle between where you are and the financial autonomy and time freedom you said you are building toward.
The Gap
  • A firm that depends entirely on one person is not a sellable asset — it is a job, no matter how much revenue it generates
  • Every hour Jennifer spends on marketing oversight and intake is an hour not spent on billable casework
  • Reaching the $50,000/month goal on personal bandwidth alone, with no team, is not mathematically sustainable
The multi-million-dollar, sellable firm you described 10 years out has to be built on more than your own two hands — that shift starts with getting marketing and intake off your plate first.

Profit Plan Assessment

Prendamano Tax Resolution is spending $4,200/month on marketing that, so far, has barely broken even on a single case. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: RED
"barely covered the ad spend"
The Problem
  • $4,200/month is committed to two outside vendors that have produced one signed client combined
  • The $3,500 upfront fee from that one client barely covered a single month of Facebook ad spend — a break-even outcome, not a profitable one
  • No annual revenue figure exists yet to plan margins or reinvestment against
  • There is no stated tracking of cost per client or return on marketing spend across either vendor
Blind Spot: You may not realize that "barely covering the ad spend" on one case means the firm made $0 in profit that month after marketing costs — not a small win, but a wash.
The Gap
  • At the current pace, the $50,000/month goal is not reachable on marketing that merely breaks even
  • Every month spent on underperforming vendors delays the sellable asset Jennifer is trying to build for retirement
  • Without a profit plan, more revenue could simply mean more hours worked for the same take-home pay
Funding your children's college and building something worth selling both depend on marketing spend that returns a profit — not one that quietly breaks even.
