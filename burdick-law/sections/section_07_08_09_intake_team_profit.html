Intake Assessment

Every case Burdick Law handles today reaches the firm through the court, not through a system built to capture and convert a private client. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • No formal intake process exists to catch and qualify a prospective private client today
  • Vanessa is the only person available to answer any inquiry, with no backup or overflow coverage
  • No after-hours coverage of any kind exists — a call outside business hours has nowhere to go
  • No defined follow-up sequence exists for someone who does not retain the firm on the first call
Blind Spot: Because every case today comes through court assignment, Burdick Law has never had to build an intake process — but the moment marketing generates the first private inquiry, there will be nothing in place to capture it, and that first missed call could be the highest-value case the firm sees all year.
The Positive
  • Vanessa's direct, personal involvement in every case today is itself a strength for the high-touch, high-trust conversations serious felony clients expect — the goal is to formalize that strength, not replace it
The Gap
  • Without an intake process in place before marketing launches, any future ad spend risks generating inquiries the firm cannot capture or convert
  • A missed or slow-answered call from a family member seeking a high-value private felony retainer is a lost case, not just a lost lead
  • No system exists to track which inquiries convert, making it impossible to know whether a future marketing investment is working
Building an intake process now — before spending a single ad dollar — is what makes sure every future private inquiry becomes exactly the kind of case Vanessa is trying to build her CEO-level practice around.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • No contact form exists because no website exists — there is no digital pathway for a prospective client to reach the firm outside of a phone call
  • No online scheduling or intake questionnaire exists to pre-qualify inquiries before Vanessa speaks with them
  • These gaps compound the broader intake problem: even if a private client found the firm today, there is no low-friction way for them to make first contact

Team Assessment

Vanessa is the only person at Burdick Law today — attorney, intake, and every administrative function all fall to her alone. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • No staff, associates, or support team of any kind exists today
  • Every task — casework, business development, billing, scheduling — depends entirely on Vanessa personally
  • No accountability structure or delegation exists, because there is no one to delegate to
  • Taking on higher-value private cases while still handling the current assigned-counsel caseload will not be sustainable without support
Blind Spot: Because the firm is only four months old, it may not yet feel urgent to build a team — but waiting until case volume and case value increase together makes hiring and training far harder to do well.
The Positive
  • Vanessa's hands-on involvement in every case today is exactly what has made 70–80 cases possible in four months — the goal now is to build the support system that lets that same intensity scale
The Gap
  • Every hour Vanessa spends on administrative work is an hour she is not spending on casework or building the private-client pipeline
  • Without any support staff, the firm's capacity is permanently capped at what one person can personally do
  • The self-managing firm Vanessa described building cannot exist while she remains the only person in it
The self-managing, CEO-level firm Vanessa described is, by definition, a firm that does not depend on her doing everything herself — team is the bridge between where she is today and where she wants to be.

Profit Plan Assessment

Compensation at Burdick Law today is per-case assigned-counsel reimbursement — low, inconsistent, and not tracked against any target. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: RED
"Payment is per-case, not monthly, and rates are low."
The Problem
  • No dollar figure exists yet for annual revenue — case volume (70–80 cases in 4 months) is tracked, but not case value or margin
  • Compensation today is per-case assigned-counsel reimbursement, not the private hourly or flat-fee structure the $700K–$800K goal requires
  • No system exists to distinguish gross revenue from net take-home
  • No case-value or cost-per-case tracking exists to know which case types are actually profitable
Blind Spot: Transitioning to private, high-value felony and homicide work will not automatically produce the $700,000–$800,000 profit goal — without a plan to track case value, cost per case, and margin along the way, it is possible to grow busier without growing more profitable.
The Positive
  • The $700,000–$800,000 profit target Vanessa set on the call is specific and ambitious — a real number to build a plan around, not a vague aspiration
The Gap
  • Without a private fee structure, every case taken today generates less than its potential value
  • Without financial visibility, there is no way to measure progress toward the profit goal — only case volume
  • No profit plan means growth could just mean more casework at the same low per-case pay, not more wealth
A clear profit plan — not just more cases — is what turns a busy caseload into the $700,000–$800,000 in annual profit Vanessa is building toward.
