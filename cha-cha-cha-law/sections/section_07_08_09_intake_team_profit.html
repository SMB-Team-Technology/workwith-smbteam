Intake Assessment

Intake at Cha Cha Cha Law is still largely manual work, and it is the first thing the team told us they would hand to AI if they could. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: AMBER
"I know AI is not there yet, but like if it could take over intake, that'd be, that'd be great."
The Problem
  • Intake is manual work that the team wants to hand off — it is the number-one item on their AI wish list
  • Lawmatics, the firm's CRM, is not connected to Claude, so AI cannot yet see or update intake records
  • An intake automation is being built inside a single Claude Pro account today, which limits how far it can scale across the team
  • Business hours are listed inconsistently — 24 hours on the Google profile and Monday–Friday 9–6 on FindLaw — so callers get mixed signals about when someone will answer
Blind Spot: An intake workflow that lives in one person's account depends on that person. A firm-managed, shared AI workspace removes that single point of failure.
The Positive
  • The firm already runs a legal CRM (Lawmatics) and holds intake calls over Zoom — a solid foundation to automate on
  • The Google profile shows 24-hour availability, which signals a firm that is ready to take calls
The Gap
  • Every manual intake step is time that two case managers and six legal assistants are not spending on active cases
  • Disconnected tools mean information is re-entered rather than flowing from first call to signed file
  • Without a defined, automated first-response process, speed to lead depends on who happens to be available
Intake that runs without Alex is a direct step toward a firm that does not need him in production.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The homepage offers a phone number only — no contact form is visible above the fold, with a separate Contact page for forms
  • No chat or messaging widget was detected on the homepage, so after-hours visitors have no way to start a conversation
  • Tracking is in place through Google Tag Manager, but no call-tracking vendor was detected, so it is harder to see which calls come from which channel

Team Assessment

Cha Cha Cha Law has a defined team of ten — two attorneys, two case managers, and six legal assistants — but the owner is still working cases hands-on and the operations director is working to move him out of production. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"Yeah, like a self-managing team. I try to get him out of production."
The Problem
  • Alex still works cases and enjoys it, which keeps him in production instead of on marketing and the business
  • Discovery drafts, complaint drafts, and demand letters consume significant staff time
  • Finding information across Gmail and Dropbox is slow, time-consuming work for the team
  • The team's tools — Lawmatics, Gmail, Dropbox, Zoom, Gusto, and Adobe — are not yet connected to AI, so efficiency gains depend on manual effort
Blind Spot: Enjoying some case work is not the problem — the goal is for Alex to work "a handful of cases" by choice, not because the firm cannot run without him.
The Positive
  • A clear structure is in place: attorneys, case managers, legal assistants, and an operations director driving efficiency
  • The approach is efficiency, not replacement — "I don't want to replace people" — which protects team morale and culture
The Gap
  • Each hour Alex spends in production is an hour not spent on marketing and the business, where he can grow the firm
  • Repetitive drafting and search work caps what ten people can handle without adding headcount
  • Without shared AI tools and documented workflows, knowledge stays in individual heads and inboxes
A self-managing team is the shortest path to Alex leading the business instead of every case.

Profit Plan Assessment

The firm has set clear revenue goals — $3M this year and $3.5M in 2027 — and is already scrutinizing costs, but the call did not surface margin or cost-per-case visibility. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"It's pretty much doing what EvenUp is doing, but at a significantly cheaper cost."
The Problem
  • Margin, cost per case, and cost to acquire a client were not discussed, so there is no visible financial picture to plan growth from
  • A paid demand-letter vendor is a recurring cost the team is already looking to replace
  • Revenue goals are defined, but the call did not cover how they connect to a profit target
  • Contingency-based work, including Lemon Law, makes cash flow less predictable, which raises the value of a clear profit plan
Blind Spot: Reaching $3.5M by adding headcount is the most expensive path. Efficiency gains from AI go straight to margin, which is how the same revenue produces more wealth.
The Positive
  • The firm knows its targets — $3M in 2026 and $3.5M in 2027 — and has decided it does not want to chase $5M
  • The team is cost-conscious, actively looking for a lower-cost alternative to an existing vendor
The Gap
  • Without cost-per-case and acquisition-cost tracking, growth decisions are made on feel rather than numbers
  • Tools and vendors added over time can quietly erode margin if they are not reviewed against results
  • A profit target set alongside the revenue goal gives every investment a clear return to measure against
Profit that is planned instead of discovered is what turns a bigger firm into more freedom for Alex.
