Pillar Health Scorecard

Lead Generation

  • Marketing is handled in-house through a mix of paid and organic efforts, with no lead-source breakdown the team can point to
  • The website is a genuine asset: an active blog, about 20 brand-specific Lemon Law pages, and Spanish and Korean city pages across Los Angeles County
  • Several nearby competitors market hundreds of Google reviews, so review volume is a gap worth verifying before the next call
A predictable lead flow is what lets Alex step toward the marketing and business side of the firm instead of the cases.

Intake

  • Intake is a stated priority for automation — "if it could take over intake, that'd be great"
  • Lawmatics, Gmail, Dropbox, and Zoom are not yet connected to the AI tools the team is starting to use
  • Business hours are listed inconsistently across directories, which can confuse prospects trying to reach the firm
Faster, more consistent intake means more of today's leads become signed cases without adding headcount.

Self-Managing Team

  • Ten people — two attorneys, two case managers, and six legal assistants — support the firm today
  • The stated goal is a self-managing team, with the owner out of production and focused on marketing and the business
  • Document search, discovery drafts, and complaint drafts consume time the team would rather spend on higher-value work
A team that runs on systems, not on Alex, is what finally gets him out of production.

Profit

  • The firm has set clear goals: $3M in 2026 and $3.5M in 2027, with no wish to chase $5M
  • A demand-letter vendor is being reconsidered in favor of a much cheaper, in-house AI approach
  • Margin and cost-of-acquisition visibility were not discussed, so this pillar is worth confirming together
Growing efficiently, without a bigger and more complex firm, is how the numbers translate into more freedom for the owner.
