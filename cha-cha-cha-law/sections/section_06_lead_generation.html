Lead Generation

Cha Cha Cha Law handles its marketing in-house through a mix of paid and organic efforts, supported by a strong trilingual website with an active blog. What is not yet visible is a measured, predictable system showing which channels produce signed cases — while the owner's time is still going to production instead of growth.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • The homepage leads with a phone number only — no contact form is visible above the fold, which loses visitors who prefer not to call
  • PageSpeed scores: Mobile 79/100 — Desktop 100/100
  • No video appears on the homepage, so prospects cannot see or hear the attorneys before they call
  • Trust claims such as "150+ Five-Star Reviews" and "$10M+ recovered" appear without a visible source link a skeptical prospect could check
Blind Spot: The firm appears under two names — "Cha Cha Cha Law" on the website and Facebook, and "Law Offices of Alex Cha & Associates" on FindLaw and Avvo. Prospects and Google may treat these as different businesses, splitting trust signals.
The Positive
  • The site is mobile responsive with an active blog, a bio for Alex Cha, and desktop speed that scores a perfect 100
  • About 20 brand-specific Lemon Law pages plus English, Spanish, and Korean city pages give the firm unusually deep local coverage
The Gap
  • A mobile score of 79 means many phone visitors wait longer than they should for the page to load
  • Without a form or video above the fold, visitors who are not ready to call have no easy next step
  • Strong content is not yet paired with a clear conversion path on every page
A website that converts on its own is one more thing Alex does not have to personally oversee.

Local SEO

The Problem
  • Several nearby competitors market hundreds of Google reviews; the firm's live Google review count and recency should be confirmed against them
  • Local 3-pack visibility for searches like "los angeles personal injury lawyer" and "los angeles lemon law attorney" was not confirmed and should be checked live
  • FindLaw lists Personal Injury only (no Lemon Law) and shows Monday–Friday 9–6 hours, while the Google profile shows 24-hour availability
Blind Spot: Inconsistent business names and hours across directories are a ranking signal problem, not just a cosmetic one — Google rewards listings that match everywhere.
The Positive
  • The Google Business Profile was reviewed on the call and looked well optimized, with strong reviews and 24-hour availability
The Gap
  • Directory name and hours mismatches dilute local trust signals
  • Lemon Law is under-represented on directories even though the website has about 20 dedicated Lemon Law pages
Law Offices of John C. Ye — a Korean-speaking Los Angeles personal injury firm that markets a very large Google review base, competing for the same Koreatown and Korean-language searches.
Stronger local visibility brings qualified cases to the team without Alex having to chase them.

Google Ads

Ad Presence
  • Paid ads are part of the firm's mix per the call, but the platform, budget, and results were not specified
  • Live ad copy for Personal Injury and Lemon Law searches was not observed in this review, so it should be checked before assuming how the firm shows up
Competitor Ad Landscape
  • West Coast Trial Lawyers — a downtown Los Angeles personal injury firm; live ad copy was not observed, and it is the first firm to benchmark for Personal Injury.
  • Seven Law Group, APC — a Lemon Law firm that markets several hundred reviews; live ad copy was not observed, and it is the first firm to benchmark for Lemon Law.
The Opportunity
  • Personal Injury: "los angeles personal injury lawyer," "car accident lawyer koreatown," and rideshare and truck accident terms
  • Lemon Law: "los angeles lemon law attorney" and brand-specific searches such as Tesla, Toyota, and Hyundai lemon law
  • Korean-language and Spanish-language search terms, where the firm already has dedicated site pages and fewer competitors bid
  • Neighboring LA County cities the firm already has landing pages for, such as Glendale, Pasadena, Torrance, and Long Beach
Blind Spot: The firm already has Spanish and Korean landing pages across dozens of cities — ads pointed at those pages can reach communities competitors are not speaking to.

Local Service Ads

LSA Presence
  • Local Services Ads presence and Google Screened status were not observed in this review
  • This is an opportunity gap to confirm, not a confirmed absence
Competitor LSA Landscape
  • West Coast Trial Lawyers — LSA presence and review counts were not observed; benchmark first for Personal Injury.
  • El Dabe Ritter Trial Lawyers — LSA presence and review counts were not observed; a second downtown Los Angeles benchmark.
The Opportunity
  • For Personal Injury, LSAs put the firm in front of people actively searching for an accident lawyer in Los Angeles right now
  • LSAs appear above everything else on Google — above paid ads, above the map pack, above all organic results — and you only pay when a real prospect calls you directly.
  • A strong, recent review base is what earns the Google Screened badge and the top LSA positions, so review growth and LSAs work together
Blind Spot: Calls from LSAs are billed per lead, so response speed matters — which ties directly to how quickly intake can answer.
