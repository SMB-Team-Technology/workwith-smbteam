Here Is Where Cha Cha Cha Law Stands Today

Cha Cha Cha Law is a downtown Los Angeles personal injury firm with Lemon Law on the side, led by attorney Alex Cha and run day to day by a 10-person team that includes two attorneys, two case managers, and six legal assistants. The firm is in growth mode with a 2026 goal of $3M and a 2027 goal of $3.5M — and the single most important finding is that the owner is still in production while the team lacks the systems to run without him.

Stage 6 is where the firm runs as a self-managing team and Alex is out of production and focused on the business — which is exactly where we want to take you.

The Law Firm Growth Acceleration Model

We Help Law Firm Owners Get More Profit, More Personal Freedom, And Outstanding Results For Their Clients.

Most law firms try to grow by chasing more leads. But leads alone are not the problem — and leads alone are not the answer. Here is what actually works.

Lead Generation

Leads are to a law firm what gasoline is to a car. Without them, you go nowhere. But a steady, predictable flow of leads changes everything — you get to choose your cases instead of taking whatever comes in.

Intake

But all the leads in the world are worthless without a system to turn them into paying clients. Most firms are losing cases they already paid for simply because their follow-up is slow or inconsistent.

Self-Managing Team

Once clients are coming in, you need a team — otherwise you end up working 90-hour weeks and results suffer. A self-managing team is what gives you the freedom to step back.

Profit Plan

And once the team is in place, most owners look at their bank account and ask: where did all the money go? That is what a profit plan solves — clarity on what you earn, what you keep, and how to grow both.

All four have to work together. If one is missing, you cannot have more profit, more freedom, and outstanding results for your clients at the same time.

Intake Assessment

Intake at Cha Cha Cha Law is still largely manual work, and it is the first thing the team told us they would hand to AI if they could. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: AMBER
"I know AI is not there yet, but like if it could take over intake, that'd be, that'd be great."
The Problem
  • Intake is manual work that the team wants to hand off — it is the number-one item on their AI wish list
  • Lawmatics, the firm's CRM, is not connected to Claude, so AI cannot yet see or update intake records
  • An intake automation is being built inside a single Claude Pro account today, which limits how far it can scale across the team
  • Business hours are listed inconsistently — 24 hours on the Google profile and Monday–Friday 9–6 on FindLaw — so callers get mixed signals about when someone will answer
Blind Spot: An intake workflow that lives in one person's account depends on that person. A firm-managed, shared AI workspace removes that single point of failure.
The Positive
  • The firm already runs a legal CRM (Lawmatics) and holds intake calls over Zoom — a solid foundation to automate on
  • The Google profile shows 24-hour availability, which signals a firm that is ready to take calls
The Gap
  • Every manual intake step is time that two case managers and six legal assistants are not spending on active cases
  • Disconnected tools mean information is re-entered rather than flowing from first call to signed file
  • Without a defined, automated first-response process, speed to lead depends on who happens to be available
Intake that runs without Alex is a direct step toward a firm that does not need him in production.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The homepage offers a phone number only — no contact form is visible above the fold, with a separate Contact page for forms
  • No chat or messaging widget was detected on the homepage, so after-hours visitors have no way to start a conversation
  • Tracking is in place through Google Tag Manager, but no call-tracking vendor was detected, so it is harder to see which calls come from which channel

These findings are also referenced in the Website Audit section.
