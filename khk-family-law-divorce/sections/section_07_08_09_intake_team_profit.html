Intake Assessment

Intake was not a focus of the discovery call, so this assessment reflects what is visible from the outside and the results KHK shared: about 20 signed cases a month against a goal of 40. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • The share of inquiries that become signed clients was not discussed, so there is no known baseline for conversion
  • Evaluation requests can arrive by phone, a contact form, a Calendly link, or the separate consult.khk.law site, and how each path is tracked and followed up was not covered
  • The contact form sits at the bottom of the homepage and includes CAPTCHA and text-message consent checkboxes before a prospect can submit
  • After-hours handling and follow-up timing for new inquiries were not discussed
Blind Spot: Doubling lead volume from 20 to 40 signed cases a month only works if intake can answer, qualify, and follow up on twice as many inquiries. Without knowing today's conversion rate, extra marketing dollars could pay for leads that never get fully worked.
The Positive
  • A "Get A Free Evaluation" button, the phone number, and "Se Habla Español" appear above the fold on the homepage
  • Calendly self-booking and a dedicated consult landing page already give prospects more than one way to start
The Gap
  • At roughly 20 signed cases against a target of 40, even a modest lift in lead-to-client conversion closes part of the gap before any new ad dollars are spent
  • Without source-level tracking, the firm cannot tell which channel produces its highest-value divorce and custody cases
  • The move from QuickBooks to Practice Panther is an opportunity to track every inquiry from first contact to signed case
A tracked, repeatable intake process is part of the firm that runs without Matthew in the middle of it.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The contact form (first and last name, phone, email, and service inquiry) is in the footer at the bottom of the homepage
  • The "Get A Free Evaluation" button leads to a separate contact page rather than directly to a form or calendar
  • Live chat was not observed in the pages reviewed

Team Assessment

KHK already has a sizable team — seven attorneys, nine support staff, a business manager, and an in-house marketing coordinator — and Matthew plans to move into a CEO role. The handoff is planned but not yet built into systems. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Matthew is still leading the firm day to day while preparing to move to a CEO role
  • The leadership plan depends on one person: a top associate who is expected to become a 50% equity partner within three years
  • There are no clear financial KPIs for the team beyond basic billing expectations
  • Revenue is projected to roughly triple in one year, so structure and accountability must scale as fast as the caseload
Blind Spot: Naming a successor starts the clock, but it does not make the firm self-managing. A CEO transition works when scorecards, meeting rhythms, and clear decision rights let someone else run the firm — otherwise Matthew remains its operating system, which is the very burnout pattern he studies.
The Positive
  • A business manager and a marketing coordinator are already in-house, giving the firm real operating roles to build on
  • Matthew has a clear succession intention and a named associate path to equity, which gives the transition a defined starting point
The Gap
  • Without role-level scorecards, Matthew stays involved in decisions that a CEO should not need to make
  • A single-successor plan with no supporting metrics leaves the three-year equity timeline resting on judgment rather than data
  • Growth at this pace without management structure adds work for Matthew before it adds freedom
A self-managing team is what turns Matthew's CEO role from a title into time he can invest in his mission to combat attorney burnout.

Profit Plan Assessment

KHK's revenue is projected to grow from $700K in 2025 to more than $2M in 2026, but net profit is only about 15% (roughly $300K) — well short of the 20–30% net profit the firm is targeting on its way to $10M. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • P&L statements are not currently used to make strategic decisions
  • There is no profitability analysis by case type or by individual attorney
  • No clear financial KPIs exist beyond basic billing expectations
  • Financial systems are in transition from QuickBooks to Practice Panther, and reporting has not been built around the new setup
Blind Spot: At 15% net, the firm keeps about $300K on more than $2M of revenue. Reaching $10M at 20–30% net would mean $2M–$3M in profit, and the path there depends on knowing which cases and attorneys produce margin — information KHK does not yet have.
The Positive
  • The firm is profitable while revenue is projected to nearly triple in a single year
  • Matthew has already identified the visibility problem and is exploring a fractional CFO partnership to solve it
The Gap
  • Without profit-by-case-type and by-attorney reporting, marketing and hiring decisions are made on revenue alone rather than on what each dollar earns
  • At $2M of revenue, moving from 15% to a 20–30% net margin would add roughly $100K–$300K a year in profit
  • The planned 50% equity partner path requires financials clear enough to value and share ownership confidently
Knowing the numbers is what lets Matthew hand the firm to a partner and step back, confident that growth is building wealth and not just workload.
