Lead Generation

KHK's marketing, at $5K–$10K a month with a focus on Local Service Ads, produces about 20 signed cases a month — half of the 40-case goal — and is not aimed at the high-net-worth and contentious divorce cases the firm wants to win in Cook, Lake, and DuPage counties. The firm has the attorney credentials, the content, and the bilingual reach to compete for those cases; what is missing is a targeted system that puts them in front of the right prospects.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • PageSpeed scores: Mobile 26/100 (Lighthouse lab test) — the main content takes 19.7 seconds to load and the first content appears at 8.7 seconds
  • The contact form sits at the bottom of the homepage, so a visitor has to scroll the entire page to reach it; the "Get A Free Evaluation" button sends visitors to a separate contact page
  • County-level pages for Lake and DuPage counties were not confirmed, even though the firm is targeting both
Blind Spot: A staging copy of the website appears to be indexed by Google. Duplicate versions of a site can split ranking signals and put an unfinished copy in front of prospects searching for a divorce attorney.
The Positive
  • Individual profile pages exist for all seven attorneys, and a Spanish-language toggle and "Se Habla Español" are shown site-wide
  • Nine practice-area pages (including high-net-worth, LGBTQ+, gray, military, and collaborative divorce), neighborhood landing pages, and a blog with posts dated this month give search engines plenty to work with
The Gap
  • A slow mobile experience means prospects searching for a divorce attorney from their phone are likely to hit "back" and call the next firm on the results page
  • Slow load times can also make every Google Ads and Local Service Ads click less valuable, because paid traffic lands on the same slow pages
  • With the form buried at the bottom, high-intent visitors have to work to reach the firm
A website that converts the traffic KHK already earns is the first step toward 40 signed cases a month — and toward a firm that grows without Matthew carrying every piece of it.

Local SEO

The Problem
  • Yelp lists the firm under its old name, "Kulek, Hakimi, and Katz," and the firm's Facebook page still uses "Kulek, Hakimi, & Katz" — inconsistent names across directories weaken local search signals
  • Local map-pack placement for divorce, high-net-worth divorce, and child custody searches was not confirmed in this review and should be benchmarked before any campaign launches
  • The newest reviews shown in the firm's own testimonial widget are several months old, which suggests review velocity may have slowed
Blind Spot: The firm's brand changed with the merger, but some of the places prospects check — Yelp and Facebook — still show the old name. A prospect who finds both names may wonder whether they are looking at the same firm.
The Positive
  • KHK has a Google Business Profile and displays strong client reviews on its own site, giving it a foundation to build on
The Gap
  • Competitors that appear to carry a much larger review base have an advantage in map results and in trust for high-stakes divorce decisions
  • Neighborhood and suburb pages exist, but Lake and DuPage county coverage was not confirmed — leaving two of the three target counties unaddressed
Merel Family Law — publishes a dedicated Chicago high-net-worth divorce page and appears to hold a considerably larger review base than KHK, positioning it directly in the segment KHK wants to win.
Local visibility is how high-value families find KHK without Matthew having to find them one referral at a time.

Google Ads

Ad Presence
  • Google search ads for divorce / family law in Chicago were not confirmed in this review — on the call, the firm's paid marketing was described as focused on Local Service Ads rather than targeted campaigns for high-value cases
  • Search ads for high-net-worth divorce and child custody were also not confirmed; KHK may not be configured to appear for these terms, or may be getting outbid by competitors
Competitor Ad Landscape
  • Merel Family Law — a dedicated Chicago high-net-worth divorce page signals active targeting of high-asset prospects (ad activity not verified)
  • STG Divorce Law — a high-net-worth practice page and a downtown-plus-suburban footprint (ad activity not verified)
The Opportunity
  • Obvious searches: "divorce lawyer Chicago," "family law attorney Chicago," and "child custody lawyer Cook County"
  • High-value searches: "high net worth divorce lawyer Chicago," "high asset divorce attorney," and business-owner and stock-option divorce terms that match the topics KHK already writes about
  • Non-obvious: Lake County and DuPage County searches, plus neighborhood and suburb terms such as Lincoln Park, Gold Coast, Evanston, and Skokie
  • Non-obvious: Spanish-language searches, since the site already offers a Spanish toggle and "Se Habla Español"
Blind Spot: Local Service Ads cannot be pointed at a specific case size. Reaching high-net-worth prospects means pairing LSAs with search campaigns that target the detailed queries those prospects actually type.

Local Service Ads

LSA Presence
  • On the call, Local Service Ads were described as the firm's main paid channel, working alongside a $5K–$10K monthly marketing budget to produce about 20 signed cases a month
  • The firm's Google Screened status and LSA review count were not confirmed in this review
Competitor LSA Landscape
  • Merel Family Law and Arami Law — both appear to carry a considerably larger review base than KHK, which matters because Google weighs reviews when ranking Local Service Ads (LSA placement not verified)
  • STG Divorce Law — a sizable review base and a downtown-plus-suburban footprint make it a benchmark for LSA competition (LSA placement not verified)
The Opportunity
  • Build the review flow that supports LSA ranking, since review volume, rating, and responsiveness all influence where the firm appears
  • LSAs appear above everything else on Google — above paid ads, above the map pack, above all organic results — and you only pay when a real prospect calls you directly.
  • Track which LSA calls become signed cases, so the budget moves toward the leads that produce the highest-value matters
Blind Spot: LSA leads are charged per lead regardless of case value. A volume of 20 signed cases a month is a good base, but without tracking case value by lead source, the firm cannot tell whether LSA dollars are bringing in $5K cases or $50K cases.

Facebook & Instagram Ads

Ad Presence
  • Active Facebook and Instagram ads for KHK were not confirmed in this review
  • The firm's Facebook page still uses the old "Kulek, Hakimi, & Katz" name, which would carry into any ad that runs from it
Competitor Ad Landscape
  • Competitor Meta ad activity in the Chicago family-law market was not verified in this review; a competitor ad-library baseline is a recommended first step before launching
The Opportunity
  • Retargeting — website visitors who did not convert are leaving with no follow-up
  • Video ads featuring the attorney — builds trust before a prospect ever calls
  • Educational content ads — capture leads earlier in the decision process
  • Matthew's combination of decades of family law experience, an MBA, and a psychotherapist's insight, already featured in the homepage headline, is a differentiated message for video and educational ads aimed at high-net-worth and contentious-divorce prospects in Cook, Lake, and DuPage counties
