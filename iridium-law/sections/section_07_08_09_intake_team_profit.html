Intake Assessment

Intake is already automated with an answering service and online booking — a real head start for a firm this new — but there is no step yet to qualify leads before they reach the calendar. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • No formal client-qualification step exists to screen out "red flag" cases before they land on the calendar
  • The current process was built to absorb whatever word-of-mouth and ARAG referrals happen to arrive — not to prioritize higher-value cases as volume grows
  • No tracking or reporting on intake performance was described — there is no visibility into how many leads convert versus fall away
  • At today's low lead volume, every unqualified consult booked is a disproportionately large loss of Hadeel's own time
Blind Spot: The intake process that works fine at today's referral volume will not hold once GBP verification starts sending leads at a materially higher rate — the qualification gap gets more expensive, not less, as marketing succeeds.
The Positive
  • An answering service and online booking are already in place — most firms this new are still handling every call personally
  • This is a real foundation to build a qualification step onto, not a system that needs to be built from scratch
The Gap
  • Every unqualified consult booked is time Hadeel spends personally that could go to a case that actually converts
  • Without a defined follow-up sequence, there is no way to know how many of today's referrals are lost simply to slow response
  • As paid lead volume increases, an unscreened process risks converting new marketing spend into wasted attorney hours instead of signed clients
Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing. Every hour of casework you get back this way is an hour closer to being present for your daughter instead of buried in the file room.

Team Assessment

Hadeel is Iridium Law's only attorney — every case, every client relationship, and every decision runs through her personally, alongside one paralegal. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Hadeel is the firm's only attorney — every case runs through her directly, with one paralegal for support
  • There is no accountability structure or second attorney yet to absorb any part of the caseload
  • The 3-5 year plan calls for two more associate attorneys, but no hiring timeline or delegation plan exists yet
  • The firm cannot function, even for a single day, without Hadeel directly involved in every active matter
Blind Spot: The current profit growth is actually making this harder to fix, not easier — the busier the firm gets on referrals alone, the less time there is left to build the team and systems that let you step back.
The Positive
  • Doubling monthly profit since February on referrals alone shows real skill — the caseload just needs a team built underneath it to become sustainable
The Gap
  • Every hour Hadeel spends on casework today is an hour she isn't spending building the systems that get her out of casework tomorrow
  • Without delegation, marketing success itself becomes the next bottleneck — more leads simply means more of Hadeel's own time consumed
  • The goal of being present for her 13-year-old daughter stays tied to her billable hours until a team exists that can run cases without her
Until Iridium Law has a team that runs without you in the middle of every case, the freedom to be present for your daughter will keep waiting on your calendar.

Profit Plan Assessment

Iridium Law has grown monthly profit from $10-15K to roughly $30K since launch, tracked closely against expenses held under $3K/month — real numbers, but no formal plan yet for what happens to margin as revenue scales. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"$30k/mo profit currently, up from $10-15k/mo" — expenses under $3k/mo (paralegal, case management, co-work space)
The Problem
  • Revenue itself has never been calculated as an annual figure — only monthly profit and rough expenses are tracked
  • No stated marketing budget or ad spend exists yet, so there is no visibility into cost per client or return on any future investment
  • Profit is being discovered month to month rather than planned against a target, in Hadeel's own "rollercoaster" framing
  • The initial retainer was already lowered from a $10-15K standard to $7,500 "to improve affordability" — a pricing decision made without a stated margin analysis behind it
Blind Spot: As the firm grows toward a $1.5M, 6-person structure, today's level of financial visibility — profit and expenses only, no formal reporting — will not scale with it. The bigger the firm, the more expensive an unplanned surprise becomes.
The Positive
  • Hadeel already tracks and can state her numbers precisely — $30K/month profit, under $3K/month in expenses — real financial awareness most brand-new solo firms don't have yet
The Gap
  • Without a profit plan, growth in revenue does not guarantee growth in what Hadeel actually keeps
  • No visibility into cost per case type makes it impossible to know today whether a discounted ARAG referral is more or less profitable than a full-price client
  • The $1.5M revenue goal has no supporting margin target yet — hitting the top-line number without a profit plan could mean more work at the same take-home pay
A profit plan turns "we had a good month" into a number you can count on — the stability that lets you plan your time around your daughter instead of around whichever month the rollercoaster is up.
