Intake Assessment

Leads reach Riggs Law Firm through several channels, but Dan, his paralegal and his receptionist handle them ad hoc, and follow-up tends to end within a day or two if someone does not sign at the consult. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
"I'm seeing these leads kind of slip through the cracks and we don't have the time to keep on them the way we probably should."
The Problem
  • There is no defined intake process, so whoever picks up the call or message, whether Dan, the paralegal or the receptionist, handles it their own way
  • If a prospect does not sign the retainer on the day of the consult, follow-up usually stops within a day or two
  • Practice Panther is set up for clients once they retain, but it is not yet used to track leads or schedule follow-up, so no one can see which leads are still open
  • You told us you are so busy that it is hard to find the time to build the process yourself
Blind Spot: You pay roughly $100 for one vendor's leads and roughly $40 for another's. Each lead that goes unfollowed is a cost you have already paid, with nothing to show for it.
The Positive
  • You said the firm is decent at getting people in for consults, so the hardest part, getting prospects to engage, is already working
  • You said that once someone becomes a client, things go well, so the firm delivers once a case is signed
The Gap
  • By your own estimate, you may be losing up to 20% of potential clients, possibly as many as 10 a month. This is not a measured figure, but it points to meaningful revenue walking away
  • Many of those leads were already paid for through Google Ads, lead vendors or the effort behind a referral
  • Every additional signed case closes the gap between about 25 filings a month and the 50-case firm you described
Capturing the leads you already have is the fastest way to build the leading bankruptcy firm in the valley without waiting on new demand.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • No contact form appears on the homepage, where the main calls to action are a free assessment scheduler and a phone number
  • A separate Contact Us page exists, but visitors must find it first
  • Visitors who do not book a consult have no evident automated follow-up, so interest that does not turn into a booking goes cold

Team Assessment

Dan is the only attorney, with a paralegal and a receptionist who joined in October 2026, so the team is just forming and depends on Dan for direction. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"It is anywhere from me to my paralegal, to my receptionist."
The Problem
  • Dan is the only attorney, so every consult and filing runs through him
  • No one owns lead follow-up, so it depends on whoever has a free moment
  • A newly hired receptionist is still learning the firm, and there is no written process to train from
  • Dan has little time to build the structure the team needs because he is handling the day-to-day himself
Blind Spot: Growing from about 25 to 50 cases a month without defined roles will put more on Dan's plate, not less. The easiest time to set up the team is now, while the firm is small.
The Positive
  • Dan has already added a paralegal and a receptionist, which means he is investing in support before the case count grows
  • Practice Panther is fully set up, so the team already has a system to build on
The Gap
  • Time spent chasing leads and handling intake is time Dan cannot spend on the legal work he missed doing
  • Without defined roles, a larger case load can make the firm busier without making it easier to run
  • A trained team that follows a clear process lets Dan step back from daily follow-up
A team with clear roles is how Riggs Law Firm grows into the firm in the valley while Dan stays focused on the work he started the firm to do.

Profit Plan Assessment

Riggs Law Firm is new, and Dan did not name a revenue or profit target on the call, only a goal of reaching 50 cases a month. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"I'm charging $2,000 for a seven, but I take on all the costs with the seven."
The Problem
  • The growth goal is stated in cases per month, with no dollar revenue or profit target alongside it
  • Fees are flat, and on Chapter 7 the firm absorbs the filing and credit-report costs, so every case adds cost the fee has to cover
  • Leads come from Google Ads, two per-lead vendors, referrals and organic efforts, and the split between them was unclear, so what each source costs per signed case is hard to see
  • Without lead tracking in Practice Panther, there is no simple way to connect marketing spend to signed cases
The Positive
  • Flat fees are simple and clear, which makes each case's revenue easy to forecast
  • Dan raised the question of how total cost grows over time before committing, a sign he plans ahead
The Gap
  • Without a dollar target, it is hard to tell whether reaching 50 cases a month is the right target for the firm Dan wants to build
  • Without cost per signed case by lead source, spend can keep going to sources that work less well
  • A simple plan for revenue, costs and lead spend turns growth into something Dan chooses rather than something that happens to him
Knowing the numbers behind each case makes it possible to grow Riggs Law Firm on purpose rather than by chance.
