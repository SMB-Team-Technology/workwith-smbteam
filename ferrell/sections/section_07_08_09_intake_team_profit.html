Intake Assessment

Every call, form submission, and referral conversation at Ferrell Law Firm is currently handled personally by Andrew, with no defined process behind it. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Andrew personally handles every intake call and conversation — no delegation exists at any point in the process
  • No CRM or intake tracking system — leads move through the process with no defined structure
  • No after-hours coverage — the website's contact form is the only channel available once the day ends
  • Contact form is buried at the bottom of the homepage, not visible without scrolling past the entire page
Blind Spot: You may not realize how many prospects visit the site after hours, find no clear way to reach you quickly, and call one of the well-reviewed competitors like McShane & Brady or Foster Wallace before you ever see the inquiry.
The Gap
  • With a 7-case cap already reached through referrals alone, any new lead volume from marketing will hit the same bottleneck without a delegated intake process
  • A buried contact form and no after-hours coverage likely costs the firm inquiries it never even sees
  • Every minute spent on intake is a minute not spent on the case work only Andrew can do — or on family, investments, and ministry
Fixing intake is the fastest path to more free time, because it converts the leads you already have without adding a single hour to your week.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • No live chat is present on the site, removing a channel for capturing prospects who want an immediate response

Team Assessment

Andrew is the entire team at Ferrell Law Firm — attorney, intake, and administrator all at once, which is what caps the practice at 7 cases despite working only 10-15 hours a week. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
"Blockers: Financial uncertainty and finding the right fit" — Andrew, describing what has kept him from hiring a paralegal-level assistant.
The Problem
  • No staff exists beyond Andrew himself — every function of the firm runs through one person
  • A paralegal-level hire has been discussed as the path to scaling from 7 to 20 cases, but is on hold
  • The stated blockers to hiring are financial uncertainty and finding the right fit — both tied to not yet having proven, predictable lead flow
  • No accountability structure or delegation exists because there is no one to delegate to yet
Blind Spot: The plan to hire only after lead flow is proven is reasonable, but it also means the firm's growth ceiling stays exactly where it is today until that lead flow exists — the two problems have to be solved in the right order.
The Gap
  • Every hour Andrew spends on intake and admin is an hour not spent practicing law or with family, investments, and ministry
  • The 7-case cap is a hard ceiling on revenue and profit until a first hire is made
  • Without predictable lead flow first, the financial case for hiring stays uncertain — which is exactly what has stalled the decision so far
Breaking the solo bottleneck is the single step that turns "more time for family, investments, and ministry" from a goal into a plan with a start date.

Profit Plan Assessment

Ferrell Law Firm's annual revenue has swung between $100K and $700K year to year, with 2026 currently projected at $600K-$650K — but Andrew's stated goal is a specific $250K-$350K in consistent annual net profit, not simply more top-line revenue. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Annual revenue has ranged from $100K to $700K historically, with no consistency year over year
  • The stated goal is a specific net profit figure ($250K-$350K/year), which is a different target than revenue growth and requires its own plan
  • No stated system for tracking cost per case or converting referral-driven revenue into predictable profit
  • Growth plans (predictable leads, hiring an assistant, scaling to 20 cases) currently exist as a sequence with no financial plan connecting the steps
Blind Spot: Chasing more cases without a profit plan could mean Andrew works harder to reach the same inconsistent outcome — volume alone does not guarantee the specific $250K-$350K net profit target he has already named.
The Gap
  • Without a profit plan, it is unclear how many cases at what value are actually required to hit the $250K-$350K net profit goal
  • Revenue swings this wide make it difficult to responsibly plan the paralegal-level hire that unlocks more capacity
  • Growth without a profit target risks producing more work and more stress without more of the freedom Andrew is actually after
A clear profit plan is what turns "more revenue" into the specific, dependable number that funds the time back for family, investments, and ministry.
