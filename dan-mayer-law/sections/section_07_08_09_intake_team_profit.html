Intake Assessment

Intake at Dan Mayer Law runs on a contract intake coordinator, a legal assistant, and a lot of manual reminders and follow-up. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
"When aggregate, that's a lot of time."
The Problem
  • Reminders and follow-up are handled manually, and the time adds up across the week
  • Proactive status updates to your roughly 150 practice clients are not going out as consistently as you would like
  • Contact tracking lives in Monday.com, whose built-in AI features you feel you are not using to their potential
  • No automated follow-up sequence for new referrals or inquiries was described on the call
Blind Spot: With five to ten referrals a month, a single slow or missed reply is a meaningful share of your monthly new business.
The Positive
  • You already have intake support in place, so there is a process to improve rather than build from nothing
  • Your engagement agreement already includes an AI disclaimer, and you have already identified the platform security your clients' data requires
The Gap
  • Manual follow-up takes hours from you and your team that could go to billable legal work
  • Inconsistent client updates leave practices wondering where their matters stand, which weakens the relationships that drive referrals
  • Without an automated process, intake capacity can only grow by adding salary
Automating intake and follow-up gives you your time back without adding payroll.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The contact form sits on a separate Contact page, not on the homepage
  • The only action above the fold is "Learn More," so there is no direct call or consultation option for a ready prospect
  • No live chat is present on the site, and contact details differ across listings, so a prospect may reach a different address than the one on your website

These findings are also referenced in the Website Audit section.

Team Assessment

Dan Mayer Law runs with three people: Dan, a legal assistant, and a contract intake coordinator, serving roughly 150 practices. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"there's a lot of things pulling on me in different directions."
The Problem
  • Dan sits at the center of the work, pulled in many directions with a long to-do list
  • There is little time in the day to learn and set up AI, so it remains "another thing on my list to do"
  • Your hiring stance is "eat what you kill," so added capacity has to pay for itself rather than sit idle
  • Client communication and content distribution depend on people finding spare time, and content has been paused since the summer
The Positive
  • A lean team with a clear standard for who gets added is a strong base for adding AI staff instead of payroll
  • You already use Claude, Copilot, Spellbook, and Monday.com, so the team is not starting from zero on technology
The Gap
  • Every task that waits on Dan costs him time that could go to legal work or to his own priorities
  • Without someone to lead AI adoption, the tools you already pay for stay under-used
  • A firm that serves 150 practices needs systems that scale without scaling salaries
The goal is a team, human and AI, that carries more of the load so you can focus on the legal work only you can do.

Profit Plan Assessment

Dan says this year is going well after a slightly softer prior year, but profit targets and margins did not come up on the call. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"I really don't want to have to pay them a salary for them staying around and doing nothing."
The Problem
  • No profit target or margin goal was described, so growth is not yet tied to a number
  • Revenue dipped slightly last year, and without tracking it is hard to see what drove the swing
  • With five to ten referrals a month as the only source, revenue depends on others sending business
  • Payroll is the cost most exposed as the firm grows, because it is fixed whether or not the work is there
Blind Spot: You may not realize how much value is sitting in tools you already pay for, such as the Monday.com AI credits you feel are under-used.
The Positive
  • Dan describes this year as going well, which is a good base to build a profit plan on
  • A lean model built on contract help and a small team keeps fixed costs low
The Gap
  • Without a profit plan, it is hard to know how much automation spend is worth making
  • Software and AI tools bought piecemeal can add cost without adding capacity
  • Time spent on repeatable tasks is time not spent on higher-value legal work
The aim is for added capacity to show up in your take-home, not in added payroll.
