Intake Assessment

Intake was identified on the discovery call as the number one success factor for Chase Injury Law, and today it is covered from 5 AM to 7 PM Florida time by a small team whose owners work remotely from Portugal. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Live coverage ends at 7 PM Florida time, so evening and overnight inquiries have no one to answer them
  • No intake solution has been selected yet — AI, a virtual assistant, and an answering service are all still being weighed
  • Referrals close at 82% because they arrive already trusting you; online leads are strangers and need a faster, more structured response to convert
Blind Spot: You may not realize how many injured workers search and call after work hours, reach no one, and move on to the next firm — especially when competitors such as Rosenberg & Rosenberg advertise 24/7 availability.
The Positive
  • An 82% close rate on referrals shows that when the firm talks to a prospect, it wins the case
  • Prospects can already reach the firm by phone, text, or email, with a call button above the fold on the homepage
The Gap
  • Each signed case represents roughly $4,375 in firm fees, so even one missed after-hours inquiry a month is real revenue left on the table (an illustration, not a measured loss)
  • Paid advertising brings in more first-time callers, which raises the cost of every unanswered call
  • Without a defined follow-up process, the cost of each signed case from advertising stays unknown
Intake that works around the clock is what lets Charles run the business instead of being tied to the phone.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The homepage offers a "Start Your Free Consultation" button and a click-to-call number above the fold
  • The dedicated contact page offers Call Now, Text Now, and Email Us buttons; we did not observe an embedded contact form there or on the homepage
  • No live chat was detected, and no intake hours are advertised on the site

Team Assessment

Chase Injury Law is a two-person team — Michelle handles the legal work and Charles handles business operations and marketing — with an associate hire planned once the firm reaches 75–100 cases. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Legal work, operations, marketing, and intake all sit with two people, with no dedicated support roles yet
  • The hiring trigger of 75–100 cases depends on a steady flow of new cases that the firm does not yet have
  • Marketing knowledge is not yet in-house — Charles wants to learn it so the firm is not dependent on an agency
  • Support from a mentor goes beyond a standard once-a-month meeting, and how that will be provided is still being assessed
Blind Spot: The firm that learns marketing first is the firm that can hire ahead of growth. Waiting until you are overloaded to hire an associate puts both the new cases and your own time at risk.
The Positive
  • Michelle and Charles divide the work along clear lines: legal on one side, operations and marketing on the other
  • Attorney time per case is under 10 hours, which leaves room to take on more cases before an associate is required
  • The plan to hire an associate at a specific case count gives the team a concrete milestone to build toward
The Gap
  • Without documented marketing and intake processes, every new case adds work for the same two people
  • If marketing stays dependent on an outside agency, Charles cannot step back from managing it
  • Without a plan for who handles overflow, growth would put pressure on the owners' time, not relieve it
A team that can absorb 8–10 new cases a month without breaking is what lets you run the business instead of just working in it.

Profit Plan Assessment

Chase Injury Law knows its per-case numbers — about $4,375 in firm fees on a 25% contingency, with under 10 attorney hours per case — but its marketing has not run long enough to show what a signed case really costs. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Paid advertising has run for one week, so cost per lead and cost per signed case are still assumptions, not measured results
  • The goal is expressed as cases per month, with no dollar target to measure marketing spend against
  • Contingency fees are collected as cases resolve, so money spent on marketing today comes back later — which makes tracking cost per case more important
Blind Spot: The 82% referral close rate can make online leads look disappointing at first. Online leads typically close at a lower rate, so the right comparison is cost per signed case, not cost per lead.
The Positive
  • The firm has already planned a monthly marketing budget and understands the cost-per-lead-to-signed-case math behind it
  • Wanting ad spend to be paid directly to the platforms, with SMB Team charging only a management fee, reflects a healthy focus on transparency
The Gap
  • Without call tracking and signed-case tracking from day one, there is no way to know which channels earn their budget
  • Without a dollar target, it is hard to tell when to scale spend and when to hold
  • Owning the marketing assets and data is what lets the firm reinvest profit with confidence instead of guessing
Knowing exactly what each signed case costs is what lets you grow the firm on your terms, with marketing assets you own.
