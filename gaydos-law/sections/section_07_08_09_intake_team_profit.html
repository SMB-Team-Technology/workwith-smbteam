Intake Assessment

Gaydos Law has a dedicated reception and intake-paralegal role handling incoming calls and records, but there is no CRM or lead tracking system behind it, and no defined after-hours coverage. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • No CRM or intake tracking software exists, so there is no visibility into where leads come from or where they stall out
  • No after-hours coverage was described — inquiries that come in outside business hours have no defined path to a response
  • Referrals — the firm's only real lead source — are not tracked in any structured way, making it impossible to see which relationships are actually producing new clients
  • There is no defined follow-up sequence for prospects who do not retain the firm on first contact
Blind Spot: With a below-the-fold contact form and no after-hours coverage, prospects who reach out to Gaydos Law outside business hours may be calling a competitor before the office opens the next morning.
The Positive
  • A dedicated reception and intake-paralegal role already exists — the firm is not starting from zero, it is starting from a real person handling real intake work who simply lacks a system behind her
The Gap
  • Every untracked referral is a client relationship the firm cannot measure, follow up on, or learn from — a real cost given that referrals are the only channel the firm currently relies on
  • Without a follow-up sequence, prospects who do not sign on the first call are simply lost rather than nurtured toward a decision
  • After-hours gaps mean the firm's namesake reputation is doing the work of attracting a prospect, only for a slow response to hand that prospect to someone else
Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Team Assessment

Gaydos Law has a real eight-person team, including two associate attorneys, an of-counsel partner, and a 30-year office manager who functions as a genuine operational anchor — but there is no formal leadership structure, and Joseph remains the central figure the firm still runs through. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"I'll be working until I'm dead."
The Problem
  • No formal leadership or accountability structure exists beyond the informal role the office manager has grown into over three decades
  • Joseph remains the central figure the firm operates around, even with associate attorneys and of-counsel support already in place
  • There is no stated plan for succession, even though his daughter is considering law and could be a future successor
  • The firm's growth engine — referrals, marketing decisions, and vendor relationships — still runs through the owner rather than a defined team process
Blind Spot: Because your office manager has quietly picked up so much operational weight over 30 years, it is easy to mistake her informal role for a real leadership structure — until she is unavailable, and everything routes back to you anyway.
The Positive
  • Your office manager's 30-year tenure shows the firm is already capable of real, trusted delegation — the foundation for a self-managing structure already exists in practice, even without the formal title
  • Having associate attorneys and an of-counsel partner already handling casework by practice area means the firm is not a true one-person operation — it has real capacity to build a leadership layer on top of
The Gap
  • Without a formal structure, informal delegation can disappear the moment your office manager is unavailable — leaving you exactly where you started
  • Every decision that still requires you personally is time you are not spending with family, or on the succession plan you have already started thinking about
  • A firm that cannot run without its owner cannot be handed to a successor — including a daughter who may want to carry the name forward
Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Profit Plan Assessment

Gaydos Law generates approximately $1M in annual gross revenue, but the firm has no visibility into margins, cost of client acquisition, or where money is actually being made across its three practice areas. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • No margin tracking exists across personal injury, family law, or estate planning — revenue is discussed as a single plateaued figure rather than analyzed by practice area
  • There is no visibility into cost of client acquisition, so past marketing spend has never been evaluated against what it actually returned
  • Annual revenue has been described as sitting around $1M gross, but with no distinction made between gross and net or take-home figures
  • Growth has been framed as "making a bunch more money" rather than a specific, planned revenue or profit target
Blind Spot: Without knowing acquisition cost by practice area, it is impossible to know whether the referral-driven personal injury cases, the steady estate planning volume, or family law work is actually the most profitable use of the team's time — growth decisions are currently being made blind.
The Gap
  • Without financial visibility, it is impossible to know whether the firm is actually building toward the freedom Joseph wants, or simply staying at the same plateau with more effort
  • Marketing and vendor decisions have historically been made without a clear read on return, which is part of why past ad spend increases did not produce proportional results
  • A firm that does not track its own numbers cannot hand a clean, well-understood business to a successor
Real freedom is not just having more revenue — it is knowing exactly what the firm earns, what it keeps, and that the plan behind it will still work when you are not the one running it.
