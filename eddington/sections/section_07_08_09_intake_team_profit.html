Intake Assessment

Eddington Law Firm already has dedicated intake people in place, but the process around them is in motion: new staff are still being trained and the intake platform is changing. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • A phone-routing problem trapped callers in a transfer loop for roughly four months, so some callers likely hung up before reaching a person
  • Two intake team members, one in the office and one remote and Spanish-speaking, are still being trained
  • The firm is moving to a new intake platform at the same time, so processes are being rebuilt while calls keep coming in
  • No written intake process or follow-up routine was described on the call
Blind Spot: A caller who hits a routing loop rarely calls back. They move to the next firm on the list, so the cost of a phone problem never shows up as a complaint — only as cases you never knew you lost.
The Positive
  • Intake is already delegated to dedicated team members instead of resting on the attorneys
  • The new platform vendor trains the staff directly, which lowers the training load on the owners
The Gap
  • Every call lost to a routing problem is a case the firm already paid to attract
  • New staff working without a documented process learn from whoever is nearest, which usually means an owner
  • A platform switch without a defined follow-up routine can leave leads waiting during the transition
Intake that runs on a written process is one more thing the owners no longer have to watch.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The contact form sits near the bottom of the page rather than above the fold, so only visitors who scroll will find it
  • The "Call for a Free Consultation" link above the fold is a clear path for visitors who want to phone right away
  • Two different phone numbers appear on the site, so it is worth confirming that both reach the same intake process

Team Assessment

Eddington Law Firm has a team of about seven and plans to add more case managers and two associates, but the daily running of the firm still sits with Wes and Adam. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
"it's like basically every day we're having to touch stuff and fix stuff and be involved in stuff."
The Problem
  • Both owners are involved in every single case, so the firm's capacity is capped by two people's hours
  • Technology problems and staff questions come to the owners, and a single failure can cost an hour of an attorney's day
  • Hiring, onboarding, and SOPs are not yet built out, even though more case managers and two associates are on the way
  • Past operations help was informal, so there was no roadmap and no regular, objective view of what was getting done
Blind Spot: Because you are both skilled at jumping in, the firm has never needed systems to run without you. That is why adding associates and case managers will not lighten the load by itself — each new person needs the same direction you give today.
The Positive
  • A newly promoted office manager is well placed to own hiring, onboarding, and SOPs with the right support
  • You already know what you want from a partner: a roadmap and a regular, accountable meeting rhythm
The Gap
  • Time spent fixing tech and answering daily staff questions is time not spent growing the firm and signing cases
  • Every new hire adds supervision to the owners' plates until onboarding is documented and owned by someone else
  • If both owners are out at once, there is no one accountable for keeping daily operations moving
The goal is a firm where, if one of you or both of you are away, the whole place is not going to go crumbling.

Profit Plan Assessment

Profit planning and financial reporting were not covered on the call, so we cannot yet say how clearly Eddington Law Firm sees what it keeps. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • No profit plan, budget, or regular financial reporting rhythm was described on the call
  • Revenue and a revenue goal were not stated, so there is no target to plan against yet
  • Hiring plans, a technology switch, and a marketing relaunch are all coming, with no profit plan described to guide how much to spend and when
Blind Spot: A new associate adds payroll months before the cases they bring in start paying back. Without a plan that accounts for that gap, growth can feel like more work for the same take-home.
The Positive
  • The firm told us it has capital ready to put behind marketing once the new website launches
The Gap
  • Spending decisions made without a clear view of margin are guesses, however well intended
  • Without a target, there is no way to tell whether a new hire or a marketing push is paying off
  • The owners are the only ones making these calls, which adds financial decisions to an already full plate
A plan for profit means the growth you are working toward leaves you with more time and more wealth, not just more cases.
