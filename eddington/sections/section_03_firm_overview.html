Here Is Where Eddington Law Firm Stands Today

Eddington Law Firm is a Denver personal injury practice led by attorneys Wes Eddington and Adam Gleason, with a team of about seven handling roughly 10 cases a month. The firm is in the middle of a rebrand, a new website, and a move to a new practice management platform — and the most important finding is that the two owners still touch nearly everything, so every new hire, associate, and marketing push will land on the same two people.

Stage 6 is where Wes and Adam can both be away and know the whole place is not going to go crumbling — and it is exactly where we are taking you.

Where You Are Today

Stage 4: Small Business Manager
  • Both attorneys are involved in every single case and touch daily operational and technology issues themselves
  • A team of about seven is in place, with an office manager just promoted and more hires on the way, but no one yet owns hiring, onboarding, and SOPs end to end
  • Marketing is paused while a new website and brand are built, so the firm is running on momentum until the relaunch
  • A major technology switch is underway, and there is no one to own integrations and day-to-day tech issues

Where You Need To Be

Stage 6: Law Firm Owner
  • The firm runs without you
  • Your income is no longer tied to your personal hours
  • Your time is yours to spend however you choose
  • This is where true freedom and true wealth become possible
The gap between a firm that needs both owners every day and one that keeps running when they are not there is a roadmap, an operating rhythm, and someone accountable for the daily work — and that is exactly what this plan puts in place.
Overall Growth Stage Assessment
Eddington Law Firm
Eddington Law Firm is operating at Stage 4 — a real team and real case volume, but with the two owners still acting as the operating system for the firm. The most urgent gap is operational ownership: daily staff questions, technology problems, hiring, and onboarding all route back to Wes and Adam. Until that changes, the goals you described — growing, adding associates, and relaunching marketing — will add more weight to the same two people instead of giving them time back.
