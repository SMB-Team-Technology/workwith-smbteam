Intake Assessment

No intake process, CRM, or after-hours coverage was described on the discovery call — the website's main contact paths sit mid-page and in the footer rather than above the fold, and there is no live chat to catch visitors searching in an urgent moment. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • No defined intake process, CRM, or lead-tracking system was mentioned on the call
  • Website contact form and consultation CTA are not visible above the fold — the only immediate option on load is a mobile call/text icon
  • No live chat means after-hours visitors searching for a criminal defense attorney in a moment of urgency have no way to engage before the next business day
  • No stated follow-up sequence for prospects who inquire but do not sign immediately
Blind Spot: Criminal defense inquiries often happen at the worst possible moment — after an arrest, late at night, on a weekend. Every hour without a way to respond is an hour a prospect has to call the next firm on the list instead.
The Gap
  • Leads already being generated through the website and reputation are being lost to slower or less visible follow-up — revenue the firm has already paid to attract
  • Without a tracked intake process, there is no way to know what percentage of inquiries are actually converting into signed cases
  • The stated goal of raising average case value to $20,000–$30,000 depends on capturing and converting higher-value inquiries — something an ad-hoc intake process cannot reliably do
A defined intake system is what makes sure the premium cases you're trying to attract don't slip away before Murat or Ryan ever gets on the phone.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The most prominent CTA links ("contact us today," the online contact form) sit mid-page and in the footer rather than immediately visible on load
  • No live chat widget is present anywhere on the site
  • The mobile header does include a call icon and a "Text Us" option — a partial after-hours capture path, but not a substitute for live chat or a visible form

These findings are also referenced in the Website Audit section.

Team Assessment

Murat and Ryan run a team of six — themselves plus four staff members — but the firm's own growth plan is built around hiring an associate specifically to relieve the partners, and the partners are personally targeting the highest-margin casework themselves. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"Target $800/hr for partners by focusing on high-margin work like clerk's hearings ($4k fee for 2 hrs work = $2k/hr)."
The Problem
  • Growth capacity is planned around hiring one new associate — but no management or leadership layer beyond the two partners was described
  • Partners are personally handling the highest-margin work (clerk's hearings) rather than that work being distributed and managed across a scaled team
  • No stated system exists for delegating client-facing casework so the firm can run without Murat or Ryan directly involved
  • Team of four support staff (Shah, Victor, Sandra, Felicia Gordon) exists, but their roles relative to intake, casework, or operations were not detailed on the call
Blind Spot: Hiring an associate adds case capacity, but it does not by itself create the leadership structure needed for the firm to run without the two of you — those are two different problems, and only one of them is currently being solved for.
The Gap
  • Every hour Murat or Ryan spends on casework that could be delegated is an hour not spent on the firm's brand, strategy, or the freedom the call's goals point toward
  • Without a management layer, adding an associate creates more work to personally oversee — not necessarily more time back
  • The firm's capacity ceiling stays tied to two people no matter how much lead generation improves
Real freedom comes from the firm running without you in the room — not just from having one more attorney on staff.

Profit Plan Assessment

Murat and Ryan have already identified the per-hour economics of at least one high-margin case type, but no firm-wide profit plan, close rate, or annual revenue figure was discussed on the call. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"Target $800/hr for partners by focusing on high-margin work like clerk's hearings ($4k fee for 2 hrs work = $2k/hr)."
The Problem
  • No annual revenue figure, close rate, or client acquisition cost was quoted on the call — decisions are being made without full financial visibility
  • Average case value sits at ~$15,000 today against a stated goal of $20,000–$30,000, with no stated plan yet for closing that gap beyond hoped-for marketing results
  • Profitability awareness exists for one case type (clerk's hearings) but was not described as extending across the firm's full practice area mix
  • No mention of tracking which practice areas or case types are actually the most profitable to prioritize
Blind Spot: Knowing that clerk's hearings pay $2,000/hr is valuable — but without the same math run across every case type the firm takes, it's possible time is still going to work that pays far less per hour than it could.
The Gap
  • Without a documented close rate or acquisition cost, it is impossible to know which marketing dollar is actually paying off
  • Growth in case volume without a profit plan risks adding revenue without adding real take-home wealth for the partners
  • The $800/hr target stated on the call has no tracking mechanism in place to confirm it is being hit
A real profit plan turns "we should be making $800/hr" into a number you can actually see land in the bank every month.
