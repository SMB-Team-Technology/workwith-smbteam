Intake Assessment

Miiko takes the first conversation with every prospective client herself, supported by Clio, Dropbox, and an assistant for part of the follow-up. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • You personally take the first conversation with every prospective client, so intake competes with court and casework for your time
  • Follow-up on consultations that do not sign right away is inconsistent
  • Intake automation through Clio and Dropbox is only partial, so some steps still depend on manual effort
  • Consultation requests are split between a booking page and a separate Contact page, rather than one clear path
Blind Spot: Referred clients arrive already trusting you. Leads that come from search and advertising do not — they compare several Fresno firms, and the first one to respond professionally often wins. The consultation process that works for warm referrals will need a faster, more structured response once marketing starts to bring in new people.
The Positive
  • Clio is already in place, with an online consultation booking page connected to the website
  • You already have help at the front of the process, which is a base to build a repeatable system on
The Gap
  • Every consultation that goes unanswered or un-followed-up is a prospect who may sign with another Fresno firm
  • As lead volume grows, a process that depends on you personally will become the bottleneck
  • Without a consistent follow-up routine, the cost of each lead the firm earns goes up
A consistent intake process is what lets you be free from the first conversation on every case and focus on the work you are better at.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The contact form lives on a separate Contact page rather than on the homepage
  • The homepage offers a mailing-list sign-up and a "Schedule a Consultation" button that sends visitors to an outside booking page
  • Prospects who are not yet ready to book a call have no easy, low-pressure way to reach out from the homepage

These findings are also referenced in the Website Audit section.

Team Assessment

Miiko is the attorney on every case, supported by one assistant and contract help, with no associates or back-office roles in place yet. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
"It should be scaled to the level that I'm not needed in every case."
The Problem
  • You are the attorney on every case, and the firm cannot take on more work than your own calendar allows
  • No associate attorneys are in place yet, even though two are part of the vision
  • No back-office or accounting role exists yet, so those tasks fall to you or to contract help
  • The hiring plan is not yet tied to a revenue figure that would fund payroll
Blind Spot: Hiring associates before new cases arrive predictably shifts risk onto you — payroll starts immediately, but case flow still depends on referrals. The order matters: a reliable lead flow first, then the team to serve it.
The Positive
  • You have a clear picture of the firm you want — a couple of attorneys so you can focus on different things
  • You are already investing in building out processes and have a hiring plan in view for the coming months
The Gap
  • As long as every case runs through you, taking real time away from the firm is not possible
  • With a full schedule and no one to hand cases to, extra demand cannot be turned into extra revenue
  • Without documented steps for intake and case handling, a new hire has nothing to follow
A firm built so you are not needed in every case is what gives you the space and freedom you described.

Profit Plan Assessment

Miiko has a clear sense of her average monthly revenue, but it moves noticeably from one month to the next, and there is no bookkeeper or defined revenue target yet to support associate hires. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • No bookkeeper is in place yet — you are still exploring that
  • Monthly revenue swings from month to month, which makes forward planning difficult
  • The annual revenue needed to support two associate attorneys and limited staff has not been defined
  • With flat-fee criminal defense work, revenue depends on a steady stream of new cases each month
Blind Spot: Adding associates is a fixed monthly cost. Without knowing how many new cases a month are needed to cover it, it is hard to tell when hiring is safe — and when marketing spend is worth it.
The Positive
  • You are asking the right question — what level of income the firm needs to generate to afford associate attorneys
The Gap
  • Without financial visibility, every marketing and hiring decision is made on instinct rather than numbers
  • Uneven months make it harder to commit to payroll or to a consistent marketing investment
  • No defined target means there is no clear point at which the firm can step back from day-to-day casework
Knowing the number that funds your associates is what turns the freedom you want from a hope into a plan.
