Lead Generation

Oakwood Legal Group has never run a paid lead generation campaign in either Personal Injury or Employment Law — the ChatGPT Ads account set up on this call is the firm's first step into paid marketing, and it is currently pre-launch with no budget or results yet. Meanwhile, the firm does not appear in a single local search tested for either practice area, leaving two growing departments dependent entirely on reputation and referrals.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • PageSpeed scores could not be independently retrieved this session — the testing tool was rate-limited on every attempt. A live speed check is recommended before paid traffic is directed to the site
  • Attorney names appear on the homepage, but no dedicated attorney bio pages were found — a missed trust-building opportunity in a market this competitive
  • The contact form is buried in a dedicated section near the bottom of the homepage rather than surfaced above the fold
  • No dedicated, geo-targeted landing pages exist for the surrounding markets the firm says it serves — Riverside, San Bernardino, Orange County, San Diego, and the Bay Area
Blind Spot: Levitate maintains the site and makes edits when asked, but by Mike's own description does not actively market it. That means nobody is currently responsible for turning site visitors into signed clients — a gap that only gets more expensive once paid traffic starts arriving.
The Positive
  • The site is mobile responsive with a clear phone number in the hero section, and a blog with roughly a dozen practice-relevant articles is already in place as a content foundation
The Gap
  • A buried contact form means visitors who are ready to reach out have to work to find how — some will simply call a competitor instead
  • No attorney bios means prospects comparing firms have nothing personal to connect with before they pick up the phone
  • Without geo-targeted pages, the firm cannot compete for searches in the very markets it claims to serve
A website that buries its own contact form cannot support the market-dominating position Mike wants Oakwood to hold.

Local SEO

The Problem
  • Oakwood did not surface in any of the local searches tested for either Personal Injury or Employment Law — consistent with Mike's own description that Maps visibility is "working horribly"
  • Until this call, the Google Business Profile had no Employment Attorney category, no registered DBA, and a keyword-stuffed business name — all of which can suppress ranking or trigger a suspension
  • Oakwood carries a noticeably smaller review footprint than several established Personal Injury competitors in this market
Blind Spot: Three different address variants for the firm appear across live directory listings (the current office plus two legacy/duplicate listings). That NAP inconsistency quietly splits review signal and can confuse Google's local ranking algorithm — a technical problem invisible from the website itself.
The Positive
  • The firm is already listed on FindLaw, Super Lawyers (2 attorneys selected), and BBB — a real foundation of directory credibility to build on once the NAP and category issues are cleaned up
The Gap
  • Every month spent invisible in the local pack is a month of referral-quality search traffic going to a competitor instead
  • The new Employment Law department has no category-specific visibility at all, undercutting the very momentum Mike says it already has
Setyan Law & Custis Law, P.C. — both carry sharply-focused, keyword-matched Employment Law positioning, exactly the category specificity Oakwood's GBP lacked until this call.
You cannot dominate a local market you are invisible in — fixing that is the first step toward the category-owning position Mike described.
