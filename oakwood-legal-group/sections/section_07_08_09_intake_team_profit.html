Intake Assessment

Oakwood's intake process was not discussed in detail on this call, but the website findings raise a real concern: a buried contact form and no live chat option, at the exact moment the firm is about to turn on paid campaigns for the first time. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • The homepage contact form is buried in a dedicated section near the bottom of the page — not visible without scrolling
  • No live chat option exists to capture visitors who won't call or wait for a form response
  • No intake process, follow-up cadence, or after-hours coverage was discussed on this call — meaning there is no confirmed plan for the volume increase paid Employment Law campaigns would create
  • The firm has never had to stress-test intake against paid lead volume before, since every lead to date has come from referrals or organic search
Blind Spot: You may not realize that once Employment Law ads go live, the leads will start arriving faster and less "pre-qualified" by reputation than referrals are — if intake isn't ready, the ad spend itself becomes the risk, not the solution.
The Gap
  • A buried contact form means some visitors who are ready to reach out simply give up and call a competitor instead — every one of those is a lead already paid for and lost
  • Without a defined follow-up sequence, new Employment Law leads generated by paid campaigns risk going cold before anyone calls them back
  • No after-hours coverage means calls outside business hours may go unanswered in a market where competitors are just as reachable
Dominating a market category means capturing every lead that search visibility produces — not losing them at the exact moment they raise their hand.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The contact form is positioned near the bottom of the homepage rather than above the fold, requiring visitors to scroll past the full page before finding it
  • No live chat option is present anywhere on the site
  • The form itself captures the right fields (name, email, phone, legal area, case details) — the placement, not the design, is the problem

Team Assessment

Mike is personally involved in tasks that a self-managing firm delegates — on this very call, he was the one editing Google Business Profile categories and business details rather than a marketing team member. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Mike personally handled GBP category edits, business name review, and profile corrections live during a call that was originally scheduled for something else entirely
  • No dedicated marketing or operations role was mentioned for either the Personal Injury or the newer Employment Law department
  • The firm's current website vendor, Levitate, only makes edits when asked — there is no one proactively managing the firm's digital presence day to day
  • Team size and org structure were not detailed on the call, consistent with an owner still closely involved in operational decisions rather than delegating them
Blind Spot: The fact that Mike is comfortable doing marketing tasks himself is actually the blind spot — it means he has never experienced what it feels like to have a team executing this work daily while he focuses only on cases and strategy.
The Positive
  • The Employment Law department launch shows Mike is willing to invest in growth beyond his core practice — that same appetite is what a self-managing team could execute on without requiring his direct time
The Gap
  • Every hour Mike spends on GBP categories or vendor management is an hour not spent on cases or strategy — and it does not scale as the firm grows
  • Without a team executing marketing daily, "dominating local market categories" will always be limited by how much time Mike personally has to give it
  • A newer department like Employment Law needs consistent operational attention to keep its early momentum — attention that currently competes with Mike's core PI caseload
Dominating a category takes a team executing every day — not an owner squeezing marketing fixes into whatever time is left over.

Profit Plan Assessment

This call did not include a discussion of margins, cost per acquisition, or financial visibility for either practice area — understandable given the call's original purpose, but a real gap as the firm prepares to spend real dollars on paid marketing for the first time. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • No financial visibility, margin data, or cost-per-acquisition benchmarks were discussed on this call for either Personal Injury or Employment Law
  • The firm is investing in growing a new department without a stated framework for measuring what that growth is actually worth
  • No tracking exists yet to measure return once paid ad spend begins — a new cost center with no baseline to measure it against
  • No revenue goal was stated, meaning there is currently no defined target for what "dominating the market" needs to produce financially
Blind Spot: You may not realize that without a cost-per-acquisition baseline in place before ad spend starts, it will be difficult to tell whether the new Employment Law campaigns are actually profitable or simply generating activity.
The Gap
  • Without a profit plan, new marketing spend is a leap of faith rather than a measured investment
  • No visibility into case profitability means growth decisions — like how hard to push the Employment Law department — are being made without the numbers to back them
  • A firm dominating its market categories needs to know its numbers well enough to reinvest confidently — that foundation is not in place yet
Dominating the market only builds real wealth if the growth converts to profit Mike can actually see and measure.
