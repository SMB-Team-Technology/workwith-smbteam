Intake Assessment

Prospects at Current Shift Legal move through an online form, a conflict check, and several touch points before they ever speak with a person, and everything after the 30-minute intake call — scoping, engagement letter, onboarding — is done by hand. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
"I would like for the whole intake process to be automatized in a way that feels like there's a human engaged in, but that there's no human involved in intake, outside of the intake call that we offer at 30 minutes."
The Problem
  • A form submission, conflict check, and follow-up create "several touch points" before a prospect sees a person
  • Scoping, engagement letter drafting, and onboarding after the intake call are all manual
  • Scheduling and rescheduling are handled by hand
  • Client notes and intake information live in more than one place, including a lightweight Trello board
Blind Spot: A referred prospect already trusts the firm, and every manual step before a person responds is a chance for that trust to cool or for another firm to get the first conversation.
The Positive
  • A screening form and a defined 30-minute intake call already help the firm choose the clients it works with
  • Referral clients arrive with trust built in, which shortens the path to a signed engagement
The Gap
  • The current process is a long way from the stated goal of no human involvement outside the intake call
  • Engagement letters drafted by hand slow the path from first call to signed work
  • Time spent moving prospects through forms and emails is time not spent on the newsletters, case studies, and follow-ups the team wants to produce
Automating intake gives your team back the hours it takes to grow your margin without growing your headcount.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The homepage has no contact form — prospective clients are sent to a separate intake form that screens for fit
  • The general "Get in Touch" form on the contact page also handles speaking, media, and hiring inquiries through a subject dropdown, so client inquiries share a path with other requests
  • No live chat was detected on the site

Team Assessment

Current Shift Legal runs lean — two owners, a mix of mid-level and senior attorneys, and a small operations and client-experience team — and the team is feeling the load. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"I get the sense that our attorneys all feel like they are stretched to their limit, which feels to me like it's in direct contrast to their only doing 40% billable work."
The Problem
  • One role covers executive assistance, client experience, and marketing and business development — each large enough to be a full-time job — which leaves the work reactive rather than thoughtful
  • Attorneys feel stretched to their limit while only about 40% of their time is billable, with the rest going to email catch-ups, internal meetings, and admin
  • There is no process to make sure matters get regular follow-up, and many transactional matters carry no outside deadline, so quiet matters can drift
  • Legal advice given by email is not consolidated anywhere, so the firm loses learning and training value from work already done
Blind Spot: Feeling stretched at 40% billable suggests the constraint is not attorney capacity — it is how attorney time is being spent. Hiring more people would add cost without fixing that.
The Positive
  • A fully remote team already works across Colorado, Ohio, and the Philippines, so the firm is comfortable running on shared tools and systems
  • Leadership would rather give the team a tool than hire another assistant — an openness to technology that makes change realistic
The Gap
  • Reactive work crowds out the proactive work the team wants to do — newsletters, follow-ups, and case studies
  • Without a follow-up process, the status of each matter lives in individual attorneys' heads
  • Until admin is automated, any growth in matters lands on the same few people
A team whose time goes to creative, strategic work instead of admin is the team that can grow without adding headcount.

Profit Plan Assessment

Attorneys at Current Shift Legal bill about 40% of their time, and the firm is shifting from hourly to fixed fees while still working out that process. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"I don't want us to … be hiring more people … without increasing profit margin. I don't need to have 20 attorneys to grow."
The Problem
  • Roughly 39–45% of attorney time is billable; the rest goes to non-billable and admin work
  • Financial dashboards are semi-manual — data is pulled from separate billing and accounting tools and plugged into an Excel report
  • A lot of the work coming in is not translating into billable hours
  • The move from hourly to fixed fees is under way, but the firm is still working out how to scope and price it
Blind Spot: Under fixed fees, every unbilled hour comes straight out of margin. If time tracking does not show where non-billable hours go, fixed-fee pricing becomes a guess.
The Positive
  • Time is tracked in a dedicated billing system, with accounting software and an outside accounting firm supporting the books
  • Leadership is already asking about margin, not just revenue — the right question to be asking
The Gap
  • Manual reporting takes time away from analysis, so decisions about pricing and capacity rest on a partial picture
  • At roughly 40% billable, adding people adds payroll without adding a proportionate amount of billable work
  • Raising billable utilization is a way to add profit without adding clients or headcount — but only if the firm can see and act on where time goes
The goal is a firm where profit is planned, margin grows, and you never need 20 attorneys to get there.
