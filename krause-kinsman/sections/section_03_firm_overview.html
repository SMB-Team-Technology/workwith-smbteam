Here Is Where Krause & Kinsman Law Firm Stands Today

Krause & Kinsman is an established Kansas City mass tort firm with a mature referral network of 130+ co-counsel partnerships, a 100,000+ client base, and more than $700M recovered historically — a firm that does not need to be introduced to its core market. The single most important finding from this research: the firm is launching a brand-new practice vertical, Index Universal Life (IUL) insurance litigation targeting fraudulent MLM sales tactics, with zero social media or content presence to support it, while at least two national competitors are already building case volume in that exact space.

Stage 6 is where the firm's next vertical runs on systems instead of Robert and Adam's personal time — the same protection of bandwidth that made the AI-avatar approach the right fit in the first place.

Where You Are Today

Stage 5: Law Firm CEO
  • The existing mass tort practice runs on a mature referral network of 130+ co-counsel firms and a strong reputation (~4.7 stars, 400-700+ reviews across platforms) — a real, working lead-gen system for the core business
  • For the new IUL vertical, the firm is starting from zero: no social media presence, no video content, no case-acquisition engine of any kind
  • National competitors (RP Legal LLC, Stoltmann Law) are already building organic content and case volume around the exact same MLM/IUL fraud narrative Krause & Kinsman wants to pursue
  • The firm's 100,000+ existing mass tort clients generate a steady stream of inbound status-update calls that currently consume staff time, with no automated system in place to handle them

Where You Need To Be

Closing the gap on the IUL vertical means giving Robert and Adam a content engine that runs itself — so launching a new practice area doesn't mean trading away the time they already built the existing firm to protect.
Overall Growth Stage Assessment
Krause & Kinsman Law Firm
Krause & Kinsman operates as a mature Stage 5 firm in its core mass tort practice — multiple referral channels, a strong reputation, and an in-house AI call center already handling volume. But the new IUL vertical the firm wants to launch has none of that infrastructure yet: no social presence, no content library, and no system for turning attention into cases. The gap isn't credibility or case-acquisition experience — it's that the exact channel this vertical requires, social and video content, is the one channel the firm has never had to build, and named national competitors are already building it faster.
