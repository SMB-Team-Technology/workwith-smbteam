Intake Assessment

Smith.ai screens and hot-transfers qualified callers to Karen and Juliana, who schedule the firm's $250 consultation — a real process exists, but it's unreliable and has a demographic blind spot that's capping growth. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Smith.ai is seen as unreliable, with poor Spanish-language filtering and frequent dropped calls
  • Non-Hispanic callers — for example from Pakistan or Iran — often disengage upon hearing a Spanish accent from the intake team, closing off growth outside the firm's core market
  • Average case value isn't tracked, so intake specialists have no clear sense of which consults are worth prioritizing
  • No after-hours coverage confirmed beyond Smith.ai's own reliability gaps
Blind Spot: The intake bias isn't a training issue Ricky can fix by asking Karen and Juliana to "sound less accented" — it's a structural mismatch between the team built for the Hispanic market and the growth he wants outside it. That likely needs either a dedicated non-Hispanic-facing intake path or a deliberate decision to double down on the Hispanic market instead.
The Positive
  • The core Hispanic market conversion is genuinely strong — shared language builds trust and drives high retention, per the transcript
  • A real, staffed hot-transfer process is already in place rather than voicemail or a generic call queue
The Gap
  • Every non-Hispanic caller who disengages on accent alone is a paid-for lead (via the $5K/mo SEO and Smith.ai spend) walking straight to a competitor
  • Dropped Smith.ai calls mean some fraction of the firm's existing lead flow never even reaches Karen or Juliana
  • Without a tracked average case value, it's impossible to know which consults are worth the most follow-up effort
Every lead lost to a dropped call or an accent-driven hang-up is time Ricky spent generating a lead his team then let go — the opposite of the freedom more revenue is supposed to buy him.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The "Talk with Ricky" contact form sits above the fold and repeats in the footer — strong placement
  • No live chat on the site — after-hours visitors who don't want to call or fill out a form have no way to engage
  • No confirmation of what happens to a form submission after hours, given Smith.ai's own reliability gaps on the phone side

These findings are also referenced in the Website Audit section.

Team Assessment

Ricky runs an 8-person team spread across Charlotte, South America, and Manassas — but he is still the one pulled into daily minutiae because staff don't follow the SOPs he's already documented. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
"Only 2 of 8 staff are considered 'A-players.'"
The Problem
  • Ricky is the firm's bottleneck — pulled into daily minutiae by staff who fail to follow documented SOPs
  • A high-quality, A-or-F performance standard clashes with a local talent pool accustomed to lower standards, driving high turnover
  • Checklist sign-offs exist but deadlines are still missed — the documentation isn't the gap, enforcement is
  • Remote structure across three locations (Charlotte, South America, Manassas) makes informal, in-person accountability difficult to fall back on
Blind Spot: Documented SOPs feel like a solved problem because they exist on paper — but a process nobody follows produces the same outcome as no process at all, just with more false confidence.
The Positive
  • A hiring plan is already underway — an associate attorney to take over court work and free Ricky for business-level activities
  • 2 of 8 staff are already performing at an A-player level, giving the firm a real foundation to build a coaching and accountability system around
The Gap
  • Every hour Ricky spends re-doing or chasing down work that should have followed an existing SOP is an hour he isn't spending on business-level growth
  • High turnover from an unmet A-or-F standard means the firm keeps re-training instead of compounding institutional knowledge
  • Hiring an associate attorney solves the court-work bottleneck but does nothing for the SOP-compliance bottleneck underneath it
Until the team runs itself without Ricky enforcing every SOP personally, no amount of revenue growth converts into the time and freedom he's actually after.

Profit Plan Assessment

No profit, margin, or cost data came up on the call at all — the conversation centered entirely on retainer volume (15–16/mo, with a goal of 30/mo) with revenue described only as "stuck below $1M for years." Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • No financial visibility discussed on the call — no numbers on margin, cost per case, or overhead relative to revenue
  • Average case value isn't tracked — only the $250 consultation fee is known
  • Revenue has been flat below $1M for years despite a full 8-person team and steady lead flow, suggesting a cost or capacity issue as much as a lead issue
  • No stated target for what "doubling retainers" should do to take-home profit, as opposed to just gross activity
Blind Spot: Doubling retainers from 15–16 to 30 a month sounds like the goal, but without knowing average case value and true cost per case, there's no way to know whether that actually doubles what Ricky takes home — or just doubles the workload.
The Gap
  • A firm that's been flat for years without financial visibility is optimizing for activity, not profit — the two aren't the same thing
  • Without a tracked average case value, ad spend and marketing ROI decisions down the road will be guesses instead of math
  • A team of 8 with only 2 A-players likely has real, uncounted turnover and re-training costs eating into margin right now
Ricky can't know if growth is buying him freedom or just more overhead until he can see the numbers behind the retainer count.
