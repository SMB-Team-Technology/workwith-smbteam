Intake Assessment

Slow, inconsistent internal follow-up at Salam & Associates is estimated to be losing at least 5 cases a month — roughly $600,000 a year — before a single new marketing dollar is ever spent. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Intake currently runs through internal staff with slow, inconsistent follow-up and no defined escalation process
  • No after-hours coverage exists today — calls that come in outside business hours have no dedicated handling
  • The firm is in the process of transitioning intake to Alert Communications, a third-party intake service, but this is not yet complete
  • No AI-based instant lead routing is in place to make sure a new inquiry reaches someone immediately
Blind Spot: A lead lost to slow follow-up looks identical, from the outside, to a lead that was simply never interested — which makes this leak easy to underestimate until it is measured directly, as it was on the call (5+ cases/month, ~$600K/year).
The Gap
  • At least 5 cases a month lost to slow follow-up — approximately $600,000 a year in revenue the firm has already paid to generate but never converts
  • Case volume has fallen from roughly 400/year to roughly 175/year, and broken intake is a direct contributor to that decline, not a coincidence alongside it
  • Every case lost to slow follow-up also lowers the return on any future marketing spend, since new leads would flow into the same broken process
Fixing intake is the fastest way to put real money back in the firm without adding a single dollar of ad spend — and it is the first proof point that the firm can function without Fatima catching every ball herself.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The website's contact form is buried near the footer rather than visible above the fold, adding friction at the exact moment a prospect is ready to reach out
  • No live chat is present on the site today
  • Phone numbers are visible above the fold in the header, which is a strength — but a slow internal handoff after that call still risks losing the lead

Team Assessment

Fatima Salam personally handles roughly 75% of the firm's work today and has said herself that she is not a strong mentor to the rest of the team. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
"Make the firm self-managing so Fatima Salam can retire from daily operations and run a new NGO."
The Problem
  • Fatima estimates she personally handles about 75% of the firm's casework
  • Fatima has acknowledged being a poor mentor, with no structured coaching or accountability system for the rest of the team
  • No leadership layer exists between Fatima and day-to-day case files — gaps in the team's performance fall directly back on her
  • The proposed fix discussed on the call is full-team coaching led by Peter Schultz, alongside a Fractional COO alternative that was also raised
Blind Spot: Because Fatima has always been the one who steps in when something falls through, the team has never been forced to develop the accountability structure that would let it catch its own mistakes — the fix is not simply "hire more people," it is building the coaching and structure that makes the existing team self-sufficient.
The Gap
  • Every hour Fatima spends on casework the team should own is an hour she cannot spend building the NGO she wants to run next
  • A team that cannot function without her personal involvement caps how much the firm can grow, since her time is the bottleneck on every file
  • Without an accountability structure, the same intake and case-management failures documented elsewhere in this audit will keep recurring
A team that runs itself — coached, accountable, and no longer dependent on Fatima catching every mistake — is the direct precondition for the retirement and NGO work she says she actually wants.

Profit Plan Assessment

Revenue at Salam & Associates is projected to fall from $4M last year to roughly $2M this year — a decline driven directly by the intake and team issues documented above, not by a shortage of demand for the firm's services. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Revenue is projected to fall from $4M/year to approximately $2M/year this year — nearly a 50% decline
  • Case volume has fallen from roughly 400/year to roughly 175/year over the same period
  • Profit has become fundamentally unpredictable rather than planned, a direct downstream effect of the intake leak and team bottleneck, not a separate financial-management problem
  • The firm's stated 20% revenue growth goal (to ~$2.4M) was flagged on the call by the SMB Team rep as likely a conservative target relative to what the firm's own website suggests is achievable
Blind Spot: A revenue decline this steep can look like a demand problem from the outside, but the intake leak alone (~$600K/year) accounts for a meaningful share of it — meaning a large piece of the fix is operational, not a matter of finding more clients.
The Gap
  • Roughly $600,000/year in revenue is being left on the table through slow intake follow-up alone, before any team or marketing factors are even considered
  • Without a stable, predictable revenue base, it is difficult to plan the eventual transition that would let Fatima step back and run her NGO
  • A revenue swing of this size makes it hard to responsibly commit to new spending of any kind — which is exactly why this roadmap starts with the lowest-risk, highest-return fixes first
Stabilizing and rebuilding revenue through fixed operations — not through new marketing spend on a leaking system — is what turns "retire and run the NGO" from a hope into a plan with a real timeline.
