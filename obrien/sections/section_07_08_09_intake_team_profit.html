Intake Assessment

Your staff already handle intake and screening in-house, and the challenge you described is the number of calls coming in rather than what happens to them. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: GREEN
"they know how to do the intake, they know how to screen. I guess that's not a problem for us here."
The Problem
  • Call volume has dropped, so a capable intake team has fewer opportunities to turn calls into signed cases
  • We found no call-tracking vendor on your website, so there is no way yet to see which marketing source each call came from
  • The only contact form we found sits in the website footer, which gives visitors who prefer not to phone few obvious ways to reach you
  • Your goal of at least 10 new cases a month will mean noticeably more calls than the team handles today, and that process has not been tested at higher volume
Blind Spot: You described pay-per-case lead services as first come, first served. The same speed-to-lead principle applies to every call generated by the new campaigns: the firm that answers first usually wins the case, so response time becomes a number worth watching.
The Positive
  • Trained staff already handle intake and screening, so you are not the one answering every call
  • After-hours and weekend coverage is in place, which is a real advantage for accident cases that do not wait for business hours
  • Some of your staff speak Spanish, which positions the firm well for Spanish-language campaigns
The Gap
  • Without lead-source tracking, you cannot connect marketing spend to signed cases, which is the proof you told us you need before committing to ads
  • A team that is ready for more volume is waiting on calls that are not coming in
  • Visitors who do not want to call have to hunt for the contact form instead of reaching you immediately
A steady stream of qualified calls reaching a team that already knows how to screen them is the shortest path to the full pipeline you want going into 2027.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The contact form on your website (first name, last name, email, message) is in the footer and not near the top of the page
  • A "Free Case Evaluation" button and a click-to-call phone number do appear at the top of the homepage, which is a good start
  • No live chat tool and no call-tracking tool were found on the site

These findings are also referenced in the Website Audit section.

Team Assessment

You are the firm's only attorney, supported by a five-person team that covers office management, case management, legal assistance, and secretarial work. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Every decision that needs an attorney, from case strategy to litigation, runs through you alone
  • No one on the team owns lead generation, so the firm has no one watching where new cases come from
  • When referrals slowed over the last couple of years, nothing in the firm's structure caught the drop before the caseload fell to about 60
  • No one is yet positioned to track and report which marketing efforts actually produce signed cases
The Positive
  • An office manager and case managers are already in place to keep matters moving day to day
  • Your staff handle intake and screening without you, and some are Spanish-speaking
The Gap
  • Without someone owning the pipeline, new-case flow depends on you remembering to look at it, which is time taken from practicing law
  • Reporting on which channels bring in cases is not part of anyone's role today
  • If volume rises toward your goal, the structure for tracking and handling it needs to be planned before it is needed
With someone else watching the pipeline, you can practice law knowing the next case is already on its way instead of wondering where the clients went.

Profit Plan Assessment

You told us the firm is doing well financially today, and no margin figures were shared that would let us assess profitability further. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"we do great where we are financially."
The Problem
  • Income depends on cases already in the pipeline, and as they settle, the caseload has come down to about 60 with nothing yet refilling it
  • Case sizes vary enormously, from settlements as low as $10,000 to policy-limit cases of $1 million, so a single average cannot guide marketing spend
  • With no paid channels tracked to date, the cost to sign a case is unknown, and you told us you want to know an investment will work out before you make it
  • You have said budget is not the barrier, which makes the real question return per dollar rather than how much can be spent
Blind Spot: A low policy-limit case and a high-limit case can cost about the same to attract but pay very differently. Tracking cost and fee by case type shows which kinds of cases deserve more of your marketing dollars.
The Positive
  • By your account, the firm is financially comfortable today, which gives you room to invest in a plan carefully rather than out of urgency
  • Your contingency fee structure means growth in new cases translates directly into growth in fees
The Gap
  • Without cost-per-signed-case numbers, ad spend is a leap of faith rather than a planned investment
  • Without tracking by case type, the firm cannot tell which marketing dollars produce the most valuable cases
  • If the caseload keeps shrinking and nothing refills it, today's comfortable position gets harder to protect
Knowing what each new case costs to win turns marketing into a measured decision and protects the secure, comfortable firm you have built.
