Here Is Where Hernandez Law Stands Today

Tatiana Hernandez has spent over a decade building Hernandez Law into a trusted employment law practice for Los Angeles's Spanish-speaking workforce, handling wrongful termination, wage and hour, discrimination, sexual harassment, and retaliation cases from a single Beverly Hills office. She has a small team in place and a Spanish-language ad campaign that has run for years — but with referrals slowing and revenue pacing at roughly half of last year's level, the firm has no organic visibility, no unified bilingual web presence, and an owner who still personally absorbs every intake and litigation bottleneck.

Stage 6 is the stage where the firm generates and converts cases without every decision routing through you first — giving you back the bandwidth that discovery-heavy litigation weeks currently consume.

Where You Are Today

STAGE 4: SMALL BUSINESS MANAGER
  • Spanish-language Google Ads have run for 2–3 years, but lead quality is declining and the campaign is managed by an outside agency
  • A once-strong attorney referral network has slowed materially, pulling 2026 revenue pace down to roughly half of last year's
  • Everything still routes through Tatiana personally, causing delays and missed opportunities during discovery-heavy litigation weeks
  • No organic or SEO presence exists — the firm does not surface in local search results where named competitors consistently appear

Where You Need To Be

Stage 6: Law Firm Owner
  • The firm runs without you
  • Your income is no longer tied to your personal hours
  • Your time is yours to spend however you choose
  • This is where true freedom and true wealth become possible
The gap between where Hernandez Law is today and Stage 6 comes down to visibility and bandwidth — closing it means building the organic and local presence the firm has never had, so referrals slowing down no longer means revenue slowing down, and so fewer decisions require you personally.
Overall Growth Stage Assessment
Hernandez Law
Hernandez Law is operating at Stage 4 — a Small Business Manager with a real team and an existing paid lead source, but still personally carrying the intake and case-management load. The single biggest gap holding the firm back is lead generation diversification: with referrals slowing and paid search quality declining, and zero organic, local SEO, or bilingual web presence to fall back on, the firm has no cushion against the exact slowdown it is now experiencing. Until that changes, the 2026 pace toward roughly $500K — down from near $1M the two years prior — will keep pulling in the wrong direction, and Tatiana's stated goal of more consistent case volume without more of her own bandwidth will remain out of reach.
