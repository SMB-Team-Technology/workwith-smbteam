The Law Firm Growth Acceleration Model

We Help Law Firm Owners Get More Profit, More Personal Freedom, And Outstanding Results For Their Clients.

Most law firms try to grow by chasing more leads. But leads alone are not the problem — and leads alone are not the answer. Here is what actually works.

Lead Generation

Leads are to a law firm what gasoline is to a car. Without them, you go nowhere. But a steady, predictable flow of leads changes everything — you get to choose your cases instead of taking whatever comes in.

Intake

But all the leads in the world are worthless without a system to turn them into paying clients. Most firms are losing cases they already paid for simply because their follow-up is slow or inconsistent.

Self-Managing Team

Once clients are coming in, you need a team — otherwise you end up working 90-hour weeks and results suffer. A self-managing team is what gives you the freedom to step back.

Profit Plan

And once the team is in place, most owners look at their bank account and ask: where did all the money go? That is what a profit plan solves — clarity on what you earn, what you keep, and how to grow both.

All four have to work together. If one is missing, you cannot have more profit, more freedom, and outstanding results for your clients at the same time.

Website Audit

“They just made it, and that was it.”
The Problem
  • The site is new this year but was built once by a third party; no blog, video or ongoing content has been added since
  • PageSpeed scores (Lighthouse test of the live site): Mobile 65/100 — Desktop 97/100
  • No pages for Jefferson Parish, Orleans Parish, Gretna, Metairie, Marrero or other nearby communities
  • The only call to action above the fold is a “Learn More” button to the About page; the consultation form sits mid-page
Blind Spot: Paying a vendor to host a website is not the same as someone growing it. Search engines reward sites that keep adding pages and content for the areas and case types people search, and a site that was built once and left alone gives them little new to show.
The Positive
  • A clean, modern 2026 design with an attorney headshot and bio covering personal injury, wrongful death and workers' compensation
  • Strong desktop speed (97/100) and a responsive build give a solid base to grow from
The Gap
  • Without pages for each parish and city, search engines have no reason to show the site to people searching for an injury lawyer in those communities
  • With no blog, video or guides, there is little for search engines to rank beyond the firm's name, and little to build trust before a prospect calls
  • A mobile score of 65 leaves room to speed up the experience for visitors on their phones, where many injury searches happen
A website that brings in cases instead of acting as a digital business card is the first step toward the constant flow of cases that makes hiring help possible.

Intake Assessment

As a solo attorney, Jonathan handles every inquiry himself and does not track how many come in, which works for referrals from people he knows but will not hold up once strangers start calling. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: RED
"One time I… tried a service. Answer phone calls, but the volume wasn't there to pay for that service at that time."
The Problem
  • With no staff, you personally handle every call, consultation and follow-up
  • The phone-answering service you tried was dropped, and no replacement was mentioned
  • Inquiries are not counted, so there is no view of how many come in or how many sign
  • The website's contact form sits mid-page, with no call or consultation prompt above the fold
Blind Spot: Referrals convert because, as you put it, "it's people I know." Leads from search or ads arrive without that built-in trust and expect a fast response, so an intake process that works for referrals may not hold up for strangers.
The Positive
  • Referral inquiries convert well because they come from people who already trust you
  • You have already recognized the value of phone coverage and tested a solution, and your LawInfo listing offers a free consultation
The Gap
  • Every inquiry that goes unanswered or slowly answered can go to another firm, and at a typical case of roughly $5,000 to $8,000 by your own estimate, even one lost case matters
  • Reaching the five or so additional cases a month you want requires an intake process built for more volume than referrals alone
  • No defined follow-up process was described for inquiries who do not sign right away
A simple, reliable intake process lets new inquiries become signed clients without every call depending on you, and moves you toward the steady caseload that makes a first hire possible.

Profit Plan Assessment

Jonathan has not set a revenue goal or a marketing budget, and the practice does not track where cases come from, so marketing spend cannot yet be tied to a return. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"I don't have the best bookkeeper, I will say that."
The Problem
  • No revenue goal has been set; you said you did not have any expectation for a number
  • No marketing budget has been set; you said you had not considered one
  • Case sources are not tracked, so cost per case cannot be calculated
  • Bookkeeping is handled by an outside bookkeeper you describe as not the best
Blind Spot: You cannot measure the return on marketing you cannot trace. Until every inquiry is tied to a source, it is impossible to say which dollars create cases and which only create views.
The Positive

The Gap

  • Without cost per case, there is no way to know what a new case is worth paying to win
