Intake Assessment

As a solo attorney, Jonathan handles every inquiry himself and does not track how many come in, which works for referrals from people he knows but will not hold up once strangers start calling. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: RED
"One time I… tried a service. Answer phone calls, but the volume wasn't there to pay for that service at that time."
The Problem
  • With no staff, you personally handle every call, consultation and follow-up
  • The phone-answering service you tried was dropped, and no replacement was mentioned
  • Inquiries are not counted, so there is no view of how many come in or how many sign
  • The website's contact form sits mid-page, with no call or consultation prompt above the fold
Blind Spot: Referrals convert because, as you put it, "it's people I know." Leads from search or ads arrive without that built-in trust and expect a fast response, so an intake process that works for referrals may not hold up for strangers.
The Positive
  • Referral inquiries convert well because they come from people who already trust you
  • You have already recognized the value of phone coverage and tested a solution, and your LawInfo listing offers a free consultation
The Gap
  • Every inquiry that goes unanswered or slowly answered can go to another firm, and at a typical case of roughly $5,000 to $8,000 by your own estimate, even one lost case matters
  • Reaching the five or so additional cases a month you want requires an intake process built for more volume than referrals alone
  • No defined follow-up process was described for inquiries who do not sign right away
A simple, reliable intake process lets new inquiries become signed clients without every call depending on you, and moves you toward the steady caseload that makes a first hire possible.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The contact form sits mid-page in a “Book a consultation” section (name, email, subject, message), not at the top of the page
  • The only button above the fold leads to the About page, with no call or consultation prompt
  • No live chat is on the site, so a visitor with a question has no immediate way to reach the firm beyond the form

These findings are also referenced in the Website Audit section.

Team Assessment

Jonathan is the entire team: he practices alone with no paralegal or other attorney, and bookkeeping is the only function referred out. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
"I would have to have a constant flow of cases."
The Problem
  • No paralegal, associate or support staff, so every case, client and task runs through you
  • No hiring plan or revenue target for adding help has been set; you said you had not considered what revenue you would need to hire
  • Bookkeeping is the only task handed off, to an outside bookkeeper you describe as not the best
  • Case flow is not yet steady enough to support a hire, and you have said steady flow has to come first
Blind Spot: Hiring is usually seen as the result of growth, but it is also what makes growth possible. A solo attorney who is also the marketer, intake desk and bookkeeper has no hours left to build the case flow he is waiting for.
The Positive
  • You are clear about the order of operations: steady case flow first, then help
  • Seven or eight years as a solo practitioner means you already know how to run a practice on your own
The Gap
  • Every hour spent on admin, marketing or intake is an hour not spent on casework
  • You could take on a handful more cases a month alone for a while, but without help the owner becomes the ceiling on growth
  • Without a plan, the first hire stays a someday item instead of a milestone
A constant flow of cases is what makes the first hire possible, and the first hire is what starts to take the weight of doing everything alone off you.

Profit Plan Assessment

Jonathan has not set a revenue goal or a marketing budget, and the practice does not track where cases come from, so marketing spend cannot yet be tied to a return. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"I don't have the best bookkeeper, I will say that."
The Problem
  • No revenue goal has been set; you said you did not have any expectation for a number
  • No marketing budget has been set; you said you had not considered one
  • Case sources are not tracked, so cost per case cannot be calculated
  • Bookkeeping is handled by an outside bookkeeper you describe as not the best
Blind Spot: You cannot measure the return on marketing you cannot trace. Until every inquiry is tied to a source, it is impossible to say which dollars create cases and which only create views.
The Positive
  • By your own estimate, cases average roughly $5,000 to $8,000, so each additional signed case moves the numbers meaningfully
  • An outside bookkeeper is already in place, which gives you a foundation to build financial visibility on
The Gap
  • Without a target, there is no way to decide how much to invest to reach it
  • Without cost per case, there is no way to know what a new case is worth paying to win
  • Cleaner, more regular financial reporting would support better decisions on marketing and the first hire
A simple profit plan turns the improvement in your bottom line that you are looking for from a hope into a number you can plan around.
