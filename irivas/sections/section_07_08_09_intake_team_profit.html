Intake Assessment

As a solo PI practice, Israel personally fields and manages nearly every incoming case himself, with contract attorneys taking on overflow casework rather than intake. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • You personally handle intake and casework — no dedicated intake team or process exists
  • No CRM or intake tracking software was identified for a firm generating $1.5–2M a year
  • No after-hours coverage was identified — calls after hours likely go unanswered while named competitors keep working
  • No contact form is visible on the homepage — the only path to reach the firm online routes through a separate contact page
Blind Spot: You may not realize how many prospects hit that separate contact page, get no immediate response, and call one of the three named competitors in this report before you ever see the message.
The Positive
  • Every prospective client currently gets direct access to the attorney himself — a level of personal attention some larger firms cannot match
The Gap
  • Every case that goes to voicemail after hours or gets stuck on a hard-to-find contact page is revenue walking to a competitor
  • Without a tracking system, there is no way to know how many leads are being lost to slow or missed follow-up
  • Since referrals are the only channel today, a slow intake process compounds the lead-generation gap instead of offsetting it
Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Team Assessment

Beyond contract attorneys who handle overflow casework, Israel has no dedicated staff — every decision, every case, and every client conversation currently runs through him. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • You are the only attorney with an independent client base; contract attorneys take on overflow casework, not administrative or operational responsibility
  • No office manager, marketing lead, or dedicated intake staff was identified — every decision and every case currently routes through you
  • There is no org structure beyond you and the contract attorneys you bring in for casework volume
  • The firm cannot yet operate for a day, let alone a week, without your direct involvement
Blind Spot: The fact that the firm still runs today is actually the blind spot — it means you have never had to find out what breaks when you step away, because you never have.
The Positive
  • Bringing in contract attorneys to handle overflow casework is already a first step toward delegation — the instinct to hand off work is already there
The Gap
  • Owner dependency at this level means every hour spent generating more revenue is an hour you are not spending on the personal time you said you want back
  • Doubling revenue without adding real delegated staff means doubling the workload on the one person the firm cannot function without — you
  • Without an operational structure in place, the growth SMB Team is about to help create will hit the same ceiling your current growth already has
Until delegation exists beyond casework, doubling revenue will only double the hours you have to work — not the freedom you are after.

Profit Plan Assessment

Rivas Law Firm generates $1.5–2M in gross revenue and roughly $500K net, but $60,000 of that flows into two billboards a year with no way to measure what they return. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"$3.3k/month budget is insufficient for a competitive PI market"
The Problem
  • You know your top-line numbers — $1.5–2M gross, roughly $500K net — but there is no formal profit plan tying marketing spend to case value
  • $60,000/year in billboard spend produced roughly 5 cases last year, with no visibility into cost-per-case or return on that spend
  • The stated $100K marketing budget is mostly nominal: $60K is already committed to billboards, leaving only about $40K/year (~$3,300/month) for anything digital — not enough for a competitive PI market
  • There is no tracking system connecting marketing dollars spent to cases won, so every marketing decision is being made without data
Blind Spot: You may not realize that $60,000 a year on billboards works out to roughly $12,000 per case last year — money that could instead be funding trackable digital campaigns with a clear return.
The Positive
  • You already know your revenue and net numbers clearly — more financial visibility than many solo practices have at this stage
The Gap
  • Without cost-per-case tracking, there is no way to know whether the $100K "marketing budget" is being spent where it actually produces cases
  • A budget that is 60% committed to billboards before a single digital dollar is spent will not support the revenue goal you have set
  • Without a profit plan, doubling revenue could just as easily double expenses without doubling what you actually take home
Redirecting the $60,000 a year currently spent on billboards into trackable digital channels is the fastest path to funding the growth you have already said you want.
