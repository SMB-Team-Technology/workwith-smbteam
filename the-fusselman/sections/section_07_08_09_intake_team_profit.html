Intake Assessment

At The Fusselman Law Firm, intake means taking in a new file full of documents and turning it into petitions, damages calculations, and a record of who was involved, and today that work is done largely by hand. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
"If AI could take all those documents and turn them into actionable product that I could use to create petitions and affidavits … it would save me weeks of time every year."
The Problem
  • New-file documents are reviewed and turned into petitions and affidavits by hand, with damages and the names and roles of the people involved pulled together manually
  • Forms go out with errors that have to be caught and corrected
  • There is no automatic screening for files that are too small or have too many parties, so every placement gets attention before anyone knows if it fits
  • Records are saved to both FileVine and a backed-up office server, which means the same file is handled twice
Blind Spot: Files can range from about $20,000 to $600,000 while the work to open and prepare them is similar. Without front-end screening, a small file can use as much of Christopher's time as a large one before anyone realizes it.
The Positive
  • FileVine and QuickBooks Online are already integrated, which gives any automation a solid base to build on
  • Saving to both systems protects bar record-keeping if either one fails, a requirement any new process should keep
The Gap
  • Weeks of attorney time each year go to preparing documents instead of negotiating, depositions, and trial work
  • Manual preparation makes errors more likely and slows the start of each file
  • Without screening rules, time goes to files the firm would rather not take
Moving document preparation off Christopher's desk is the fastest way to get his time back for the work he wants to do.

Team Assessment

The Fusselman Law Firm runs with a small team of six including Christopher, but the structure that should keep work moving lives in a binder, and the follow-through still depends on him. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"I feel like if I automated better, that would have already happened without needing a human being to say, go and do it."
The Problem
  • Procedures are kept in a binder and are flexible by design, so over time they drift away from the steps that matter most and have to be pulled back
  • Post-judgment steps, from perfecting the lien to the writ of execution, do not start until someone decides to start them
  • No KPIs are tracked, so there is no simple way to see how the team is performing
  • The business runs through Christopher, and that includes working weekends
Blind Spot: Flexible procedures feel like a strength because they adapt to each file. The hidden cost is that every drift has to be noticed and corrected by the owner, which makes Christopher the firm's quality-control system.
The Positive
  • The firm has an established office manager and a small, stable core team to build structure around
  • Christopher is open to bringing in outside expertise to build the structure and automation the firm needs
The Gap
  • Because key steps wait on a person, Christopher cannot step away without work stalling
  • Without KPIs, problems are found by feel rather than by numbers
  • Weekend work shows that the firm still relies on the owner's hours to keep moving
A team supported by clear process and automation is what lets the firm work for Christopher instead of Christopher working for the firm.

Profit Plan Assessment

Revenue at The Fusselman Law Firm has held between roughly $550,000 and $650,000 for five to six years, and the firm has no KPIs showing which files and processes earn their keep. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"But it's the little ones that kill me."
The Problem
  • Revenue has plateaued for five to six years despite the firm's strong reputation
  • Files range from about $20,000 to $600,000 in size, but the work required is not very different, so small files take a large share of time
  • Payment reporting is duplicated across systems, adding manual work to the financial picture
  • No KPIs are in place to show where time and money are going
Blind Spot: When small and large files take similar effort, adding more files can mean more work without more profit. Choosing which files to take, and moving the rest off the books, can raise take-home faster than adding volume.
The Positive
  • QuickBooks Online has been put in place and integrated with FileVine, a real foundation for clearer reporting
  • Christopher already wants to focus on the files he prefers and move away from the ones that do not fit
The Gap
  • Without KPIs, it is hard to tell which files and steps are worth the time they take
  • Duplicate reporting steps use hours that could go to higher-value work
  • A flat revenue line leaves the firm's value tied to Christopher's personal hours
Profit that is planned rather than discovered is what lets Christopher decide how much of his time the firm really needs.
