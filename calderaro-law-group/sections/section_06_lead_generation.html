Lead Generation

Calderaro Law Group has built a real practice serving overseas business owners pursuing E2, EB5, L, and O visas — but every one of those clients has arrived through referrals, with no paid advertising, no SEO strategy, and no predictable system behind it. The one paid test the firm ever ran was a sub-$500/month Instagram campaign that produced a single client before being cancelled.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • No dedicated attorney bio or credentials page — Renata is referenced only inside a client testimonial on the homepage, a gap for a buyer making a high-stakes, high-trust visa decision
  • PageSpeed scores: not verified live this session — the PageSpeed Insights API returned a quota-exhausted error on repeated attempts. Confirm real mobile/desktop scores at pagespeed.web.dev before finalizing this figure with the client
  • Primary on-site conversion path is a WhatsApp click-to-chat button — no native contact form observed above the fold
  • Blog exists but last-post freshness could not be confirmed with available tooling this session
Blind Spot: The site's primary call-to-action and the firm's actual after-hours client communication channel are the same tool — WhatsApp. If paid traffic increases lead volume, that single channel becomes a bottleneck for both new prospects and existing business clients at the same time.
The Positive
  • The site is fully Spanish-first ("Creamos Estrategias de Inmigración"), matching the language and framing of its actual target buyer — overseas Latin American business owners
  • Two clear calls-to-action ("Ver más" and "Empecemos Ahora") appear above the fold
The Gap
  • No attorney bio means paid traffic would land with no built-in credibility signal for a buyer about to wire a large retainer from overseas
  • A single conversion channel (WhatsApp) risks losing prospects who want a form or call option, and risks congestion as lead volume grows
  • No visible case studies, video, or documented track record to reinforce the firm's real 90-95% approval rate
A site built for the right audience with no way to prove credibility to that audience is running at half its potential — closing that gap is part of what makes the $3.6M target reachable.

Local SEO

The Problem

  • Calderaro Law Group was not observed in organic or aggregator results for "E2 visa lawyer Miami," "EB5 attorney Miami," or "business immigration lawyer Miami"
  • Multiple, meaningfully different addresses and phone numbers surface across Google, directories, and aggregators for what appears to be the same firm — a real NAP (name/address/phone) inconsistency
Blind Spot: The NAP inconsistency is not just a ranking issue — it can raise legitimacy questions for a first-time overseas prospect trying to verify the firm is real before wiring a large retainer.
The Positive
  • Attorney Sandra Renata Calderaro holds a verifiable 4.4/5.0 rating from 33 reviews on Avvo individually — a real, positive trust signal, distinct from the firm's own Google Business Profile rating
The Gap
  • Zero visibility in organic or aggregator results for the firm's core practice-area search terms
  • An inconsistent NAP profile likely suppresses whatever local ranking signal the firm has already earned
Kurzban Kurzban Tetzeli & Pratt, P.A. — holds 115 verified reviews at 4.7★ and surfaces consistently for the same business immigration search terms this firm does not appear in.
Fixing NAP consistency and building a real review presence costs far less than ad spend — and it is exactly the kind of trust signal an overseas buyer checks before committing.
