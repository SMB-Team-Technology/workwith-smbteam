Intake Assessment

With about 60 new estate planning matters and roughly 4 new probate matters arriving each month, Arch Legacy Firm's intake process is where capacity is won or lost. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Attorneys lead every initial client session, which keeps your most expensive people on the front end of every new matter
  • After-hours calls are answered by a voice AI service the firm considers subpar in quality, so a first impression is being made by a tool you do not trust
  • Intake and case information lives across GoHighLevel and DecisionVault, so recap emails, file organization, and task handoffs depend on people moving information between systems
  • Marketing and intake analytics reporting is not yet automated, which makes it hard to see what is converting
Blind Spot: Every handoff that relies on a person — from a consultation recap to a document request — is also a chance for a matter to stall. With about 60 new matters a month, even small delays add up to real lost capacity.
The Positive
  • A defined process already exists: attorneys run the initial session, client coordinators manage the rest, and paralegals draft the documents
  • The firm already has after-hours coverage in place and is actively looking to improve it, which is a stronger starting point than having none
The Gap
  • Attorney time spent on first sessions and administrative follow-up is time not available for the business and tax planning work the firm wants to add
  • Manual recaps and handoffs add hours of coordinator work to every matter, which is how headcount keeps climbing
  • A weak after-hours experience can cost the firm clients who call outside business hours and move on to the next firm
A smoother, more automated intake experience is how you handle the next 60 matters a month without hiring for each one.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The homepage "Schedule A Consultation" button leads to a separate scheduling flow rather than a form visible above the fold
  • The homepage and Contact Us page display different phone numbers, which can send callers to the wrong line and muddy call tracking
  • No live chat was observed on the site, so after-hours website visitors rely on the scheduling button and the phone number

Team Assessment

Arch Legacy Firm has built an 18-person team with a COO in place and plans for five estate planning attorneys next year, yet the owner describes being out of bandwidth and tired of hiring. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"I'm tired of hiring employees… implement systems… so that we can quit hiring for a hot minute."
The Problem
  • Growth has been met by adding people, and five estate planning attorneys are projected for next year
  • The owner is out of bandwidth, which is the stated reason business and tax planning have not launched
  • Repeatable work — file organization, recap emails, and task handoffs — is still done by team members instead of by systems
  • The firm's operating knowledge is not yet captured in a searchable AI knowledge base built from its own SOPs, so answers still depend on asking the right person
Blind Spot: A third office next year will multiply coordination work. If the firm scales by copying today's staffing model, each new location adds its own layer of hiring, training, and management on top of what already exists.
The Positive
  • A COO in place and a clear coordinator-and-paralegal structure give the firm real leadership depth to build on
  • The firm already uses Claude across the organization, so the team is not starting from zero on AI adoption
The Gap
  • Every new hire adds recruiting, training, and management time to an owner who has none to spare
  • Without systems doing the repeatable work, team members stay busy on tasks that do not need a person — a heavy load for a team the owner wants to protect
  • Expansion into business and tax planning stays on hold until capacity opens up
A team that runs on systems is how you protect the work-life balance you want for your people while the firm keeps growing.

Profit Plan Assessment

Arch Legacy Firm is on pace for about $3 million in revenue on a flat-fee model, but the cost of delivering each matter depends heavily on how many people touch it. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"We're leaving a lot of money on the table by not pushing forward into business and tax… I'm out of bandwidth."
The Problem
  • The only labor-cost figure shared on the call was an estimate from before recent team and price changes, so current margin per matter is not clearly defined
  • Flat fees with no trust accounting keep billing simple, but the main levers for margin are fewer hours per matter or more matters per team member
  • Hiring is the firm's default way to add capacity, which raises fixed costs ahead of revenue
  • Revenue growth into business and tax planning is stalled by capacity, not by demand
Blind Spot: Every hour a team member spends on a task that could be automated is a cost the firm pays on every single matter, and with about 60 new matters a month that cost compounds quickly.
The Positive
  • Flat-fee pricing gives clients certainty and gives the firm predictable revenue per matter
  • The firm has already adjusted its team and pricing, which shows an active focus on margin
The Gap
  • Without a current cost-per-matter number, it is hard to know how much each new hire actually adds to or takes from profit
  • Reducing the hours per matter is the most direct path to higher profit on a flat-fee model
  • Launching business and tax planning on the current staffing model would add cost faster than it adds profit
Strong, sustainable profit comes from doing more with the team you already have — that is what funds the next vertical without more hiring.
