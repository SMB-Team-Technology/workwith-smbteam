Here Is Where Arch Legacy Firm Stands Today

Arch Legacy Firm is a trust-focused estate planning firm serving Athens, Oconee County, and the Lake Oconee area of Georgia, with a growing uncontested probate practice. With roughly 18 team members, two offices, about 60 new estate planning matters a month, and revenue on pace for about $3 million this year, the firm has real momentum — and the most important finding is that its growth is now limited not by demand but by hiring and owner bandwidth.

Stage 6 is where Arch Legacy Firm can expand into business and tax planning without hiring its way there — a firm that runs on systems so your team keeps its balance, and it is exactly where we are taking you.

Where You Are Today

Stage 4: Small Business Manager
  • Roughly 60 new estate planning matters a month are carried by an 18-person team, and growth has historically been met by adding headcount
  • Attorneys lead every initial client session while coordinators and paralegals carry the rest — repeatable work is still done by people
  • After-hours calls go to a voice AI service the firm views as subpar, and intake and case information is split across more than one system
  • Business and tax planning is a known opportunity the firm cannot pursue yet because the owner is out of bandwidth

Where You Need To Be

Stage 6: Law Firm Owner
  • The firm runs without you
  • Your income is no longer tied to your personal hours
  • Your time is yours to spend however you choose
  • This is where true freedom and true wealth become possible
The gap between where you are and where you want to be is a systems gap, not a demand gap — and closing it is how you pause the constant hiring and give your team its time back.
Overall Growth Stage Assessment

The Law Firm Growth Acceleration Model

We Help Law Firm Owners Get More Profit, More Personal Freedom, And Outstanding Results For Their Clients.

Most law firms try to grow by chasing more leads. But leads alone are not the problem — and leads alone are not the answer. Here is what actually works.

Lead Generation

Leads are to a law firm what gasoline is to a car. Without them, you go nowhere. But a steady, predictable flow of leads changes everything — you get to choose your cases instead of taking whatever comes in.

Intake

But all the leads in the world are worthless without a system to turn them into paying clients. Most firms are losing cases they already paid for simply because their follow-up is slow or inconsistent.

Self-Managing Team

Once clients are coming in, you need a team — otherwise you end up working 90-hour weeks and results suffer. A self-managing team is what gives you the freedom to step back.

Profit Plan

And once the team is in place, most owners look at their bank account and ask: where did all the money go? That is what a profit plan solves — clarity on what you earn, what you keep, and how to grow both.

All four have to work together. If one is missing, you cannot have more profit, more freedom, and outstanding results for your clients at the same time.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • The homepage "Schedule A Consultation" button leads to a separate scheduling flow — there is no contact form visible above the fold on the homepage
  • Two different phone numbers appear on the firm's own site: (706) 702-5749 on the homepage and (706) 352-9060 on the Contact Us page
  • Page-speed performance has not yet been benchmarked for this report — a live mobile and desktop PageSpeed test is the recommended first step before any site changes
  • A dedicated Athens page exists, but no dedicated Greensboro or Lake Oconee page was found in our searches despite a second office in that area
Blind Spot: Two phone numbers on the same website can split call tracking, confuse Google about which number is correct, and make it harder to know which marketing efforts are actually producing calls.
The Positive
  • The site looks modern and professional, is mobile responsive, and carries a 2026 copyright date
  • A featured video, "How NOT to Leave a Legacy," and a blog updated roughly weekly through September 2026 build trust and keep the site fresh for search engines
The Gap
  • Every extra click between a ready-to-call visitor and a booked consultation costs the firm matters it has already paid to attract
  • Inconsistent phone numbers weaken local search signals and make lead tracking unreliable
  • Without location pages for the Lake Oconee and Greensboro office, the firm is relying on one Athens page to represent three service areas — and a third office is coming next year
A website that books consultations with fewer touches from your team is one more way to grow without adding headcount.

Intake Assessment

With about 60 new estate planning matters and roughly 4 new probate matters arriving each month, Arch Legacy Firm's intake process is where capacity is won or lost. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Attorneys lead every initial client session, which keeps your most expensive people on the front end of every new matter
  • After-hours calls are answered by a voice AI service the firm considers subpar in quality, so a first impression is being made by a tool you do not trust

  • The firm already has after-hours coverage in place and is actively looking to improve it, which is a stronger starting point than having none
The Gap
  • Attorney time spent on first sessions and administrative follow-up is time not available for the business and tax planning work the firm wants to add
  • Manual recaps and handoffs add hours of coordinator work to every matter, which is how headcount keeps climbing
  • A weak after-hours experience can cost the firm clients who call outside business hours and move on to the next firm

Profit Plan Assessment

Arch Legacy Firm is on pace for about $3 million in revenue on a flat-fee model, but the cost of delivering each matter depends heavily on how many people touch it. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"We're leaving a lot of money on the table by not pushing forward into business and tax… I'm out of bandwidth."
The Problem
  • The only labor-cost figure shared on the call was an estimate from before recent team and price changes, so current margin per matter is not clearly defined
  • Flat fees with no trust accounting keep billing simple, but the main levers for margin are fewer hours per matter or more matters per team member
  • Hiring is the firm's default way to add capacity, which raises fixed costs ahead of revenue
  • Revenue growth into business and tax planning is stalled by capacity, not by demand
Blind Spot: Every hour a team member spends on a task that could be automated is a cost the firm pays on every single matter, and with about 60 new matters a month that cost compounds quickly.
The Positive
  • Flat-fee pricing gives clients certainty and gives the firm predictable revenue per matter
  • The firm has already adjusted its team and pricing, which shows an active focus on margin
The Gap
  • Without a current cost-per-matter number, it is hard to know how much each new hire actually adds to or takes from profit
  • Reducing the hours per matter is the most direct path to higher profit on a flat-fee model
  • Launching business and tax planning on the current staffing model would add cost faster than it adds profit
Strong, sustainable profit comes from doing more with the team you already have — that is what funds the next vertical without more hiring.
