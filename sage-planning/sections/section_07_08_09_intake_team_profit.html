Intake Assessment

Sage's intake is split between a dedicated receptionist/intake specialist and Charles himself, but there is no formal process behind either — especially for the seminar leads that make up a meaningful share of the pipeline. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • No formal lead-nurturing or follow-up sequence exists for monthly seminar attendees, so interested prospects who don't sign on the spot are not being systematically re-engaged
  • No contact form on the homepage — the easiest, lowest-effort path to reach the firm doesn't exist yet
  • Referral and financial-advisor-network leads are not tracked in any documented system, so there is no way to see where warm leads stall before becoming clients
  • No after-hours coverage confirmed — calls outside business hours have no described path to a live response
Blind Spot: You may not realize how many seminar attendees leave interested, get busy, and never hear from Sage again simply because no one followed up — those are not lost prospects, they are lost sales that already cost time and money to generate.
The Positive
  • A dedicated receptionist/intake specialist role already exists — intake is not resting entirely on Charles, which gives Sage a real starting point to build a defined process on
The Gap
  • Every seminar attendee who goes cold is revenue Sage already spent time and money to generate
  • Referral leads that aren't tracked can't be measured, so it's impossible to know the real conversion rate from "referred" to "signed"
  • The 5% profit set-aside Charles wants to grow can only grow if more of the leads already coming in convert — not by finding new ones
Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • No contact form on the homepage — visitors must call or navigate to a separate contact page, adding friction to the lowest-effort path to convert a warm visitor into a lead
  • No live chat or equivalent real-time channel exists on the site today
  • The site's own "Schedule a free planning call" CTA depends entirely on a phone call or a follow-up click — there is no instant, no-commitment way to raise a hand

Team Assessment

Charles is running Sage as both its CEO and its primary attorney — every hour spent on strategy is an hour not billing, and every hour billing is an hour strategy waits. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Charles is simultaneously CEO and primary attorney — there is no one else setting strategy, evaluating marketing channels, or reviewing pricing
  • The 6-person team (2 attorneys, a paralegal, an office manager, a marketing specialist, and a receptionist/intake specialist) has defined functional roles but no documented SOPs or role scorecards tying that structure together
  • No one besides Charles is currently equipped to make higher-level business decisions if he is unavailable
  • The firm has no fractional or dedicated operations leadership — Charles has explicitly identified himself as the firm's core growth bottleneck
Blind Spot: Being used to carrying both jobs at once may be masking how much upside is being left on the table — every hour Charles spends on tasks someone else could own is an hour not spent on the strategic work only he can do, like pricing, positioning, or building out the probate practice he wants.
The Positive
  • Sage already has 6 team members with defined functional roles — paralegal, office manager, marketing specialist, and receptionist/intake specialist — more structure than many firms this age have built
The Gap
  • As long as Charles personally makes every strategic call, the firm's pace of growth is capped at what one person can push through
  • Team members without documented SOPs or scorecards can't be held accountable to outcomes, only tasks
  • Charles cannot take meaningful time away from the firm without every strategic decision stalling with him
Delegating the CEO function is the fastest way to get Charles the 50/50 CEO-attorney split he says he wants — and the only way the firm keeps growing without him personally growing right along with every task.

Profit Plan Assessment

Sage currently sets aside 5% of revenue with a stated goal to increase that, but pricing on both core services was inherited from Charles's prior family-law practice rather than built around what the firm delivers today. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"Currently 5% of revenue is set aside; the goal is to increase this."
The Problem
  • Only 5% of revenue is currently set aside, with no stated visibility into cost per case, client acquisition cost, or margin by service line
  • Pricing on both core services (~$2,500 average estate plan, $700–$800 average business formation) was inherited from Charles's prior family-law practice rather than rebuilt around Sage's current specialized value
  • No documented plan exists yet for turning the firm's stated higher-margin opportunities — annual compliance packages, an estate administration/probate practice — into actual revenue
  • Financial management and metrics analysis (acquisition cost, profitability by service line) has been identified as a need but has not yet been built
Blind Spot: Your two core services are still priced the way they were at your old family-law practice — pricing built for a different specialty, in a different market, under a different brand.
The Positive
  • The firm has already generated $160,000 in revenue in under four months with zero paid marketing spend — real proof that demand exists before a single new dollar is invested
The Gap
  • Without cost-per-case or acquisition-cost visibility, Charles can't tell which service line is actually the most profitable to grow
  • Pricing left unexamined means every case closed at old family-law rates leaves money on the table that a repriced service line would capture
  • The stated goal to increase the 5% set-aside has no plan behind it yet — a goal without a mechanism is just a wish
A $10M sale is built on margin, not just top-line revenue — building a real profit plan now is how the 5% becomes 15%, and how growth actually turns into wealth Charles can eventually cash out.
