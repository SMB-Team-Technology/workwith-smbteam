Intake Assessment

Every case evaluation and fee negotiation at Kamaluddin Law goes through Nahla personally, with no delegated intake process in place. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: RED
The Problem

  • The website's case evaluation form is mid-page, not visible above the fold, delaying the moment a ready prospect can act
  • No defined follow-up sequence was described for leads who do not sign immediately after their evaluation
  • No stated after-hours coverage — case evaluation depends entirely on Nahla's personal availability
Blind Spot: Every hour Nahla spends personally negotiating a fee is an hour she is not spending training the team she says she wants to build.
The Gap
  • Every case evaluation Nahla handles personally is time she cannot spend on higher-level firm leadership
  • Leads who don't convert on the first call have no described system pulling them back in
  • Case evaluation and fee negotiation both being fully manual caps how many new clients the firm can even process at once
You cannot lead the CEO-level firm you described while you are still the one negotiating every fee yourself.

Team Assessment

Nahla is currently the entire operational backbone of Kamaluddin Law — every case evaluation, fee negotiation, and piece of casework runs through her directly, with no delegation in place. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
"Primary Goal: Build a legacy firm with a larger team of attorneys, moving Nahla into a CEO role focused on leadership and training."
The Problem
  • No delegation exists for case evaluation, fee negotiation, or day-to-day casework — all of it currently depends on Nahla alone
  • No accountability structure or defined roles exist beyond Nahla; a second call attendee was named with no stated role
  • No system was described for training or onboarding future attorneys, despite that being the explicit long-term goal

Blind Spot: Because you have never had anyone else evaluate a case or negotiate a fee, it may not be obvious how much of your day-to-day time this actually consumes — until you start measuring it against building the team you say you want.

  • Without a defined delegation plan, adding attorneys later means retraining from scratch instead of plugging into existing systems
  • The firm's growth ceiling is currently set by Nahla's personal capacity, not by market demand
The legacy firm you described — with a larger team of attorneys and you in a true CEO role — starts with building the delegation systems that let someone else run a case from evaluation through resolution.

Profit Plan Assessment

Kamaluddin Law has grown to just under $1.2M in annual revenue, but fees are negotiated case-by-case with no stated standard structure, and gross revenue has not been distinguished from take-home profit. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Fee structure is negotiated case-by-case rather than following a stated standard model, making revenue harder to forecast
  • Gross revenue and net take-home have not been distinguished — a common blind spot when the owner is also the primary biller and negotiator
  • No stated visibility into acquisition cost or profitability by practice area (immigration vs. personal injury)
  • No profitability claims were made on the call — profit visibility appears to be an open question rather than a solved one
Blind Spot: Without knowing the acquisition cost and margin difference between immigration and personal injury cases, it is difficult to know which practice area is actually building the wealth behind the legacy firm you want to create.
The Gap
  • Without a standard fee model, every negotiation is a new guess instead of a repeatable, defensible number
  • Without knowing net margin by practice area, growth could mean more revenue without more profit
Building a legacy firm means knowing exactly what it is worth — not just how much revenue it generates.
