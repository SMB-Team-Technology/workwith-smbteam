Intake Assessment

Calls to Oregon Legacy are answered by an outsourced virtual receptionist and then wait on a paralegal callback, a process that the discovery call described as inefficient and that results in roughly a dozen missed calls a month. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Calls are answered by an outsourced virtual receptionist and then wait for a paralegal to call back — a hand-off with a built-in delay
  • Roughly a dozen calls a month are being missed, per our discovery call
  • No defined intake process, script, or follow-up sequence is in place, and no intake KPIs are being tracked yet
  • A small number of public reviews on directory sites mention difficulty getting calls and emails returned — the same gap the discovery call identified
Blind Spot: A missed call rarely leaves a voicemail and waits. A family facing a guardianship or probate decision is likely to call the next firm on the list, and in Bend there are several established ones.
The Positive
  • An answering service is already in place, so the foundation for responsiveness exists — the work is in what happens after the call is answered
  • Intake and process improvement are already on Lin's 12-month goal list, so the problem has been identified
The Gap
  • Roughly a dozen missed calls a month is a steady stream of prospective estate planning and elder law clients going to other firms
  • Time spent on callbacks and hand-offs is time not spent on billable legal work
  • Without a documented process, the quality of intake depends on who happens to be available that day
Every call that is answered and followed up on promptly is a client the firm has already earned the chance to win — without Lin working a single extra hour.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • No embedded contact form was found — the only online paths to reach the firm are a "schedule a free consultation" booking link and a Contact Us page
  • No live chat is present, so a visitor with a quick question outside of business hours has no immediate way to engage
  • With only two navigation items, a visitor cannot self-select into the specific need they have, such as probate or Medicaid planning, before reaching out

Team Assessment

Oregon Legacy is Lin, one paralegal, and an outsourced virtual receptionist, with Lin personally handling the legal work and a large share of the administrative work as well. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Lin handles both legal work and low-value administrative work, including about an hour a day editing Word templates
  • The team is one paralegal and a virtual receptionist service — there are no other roles to hand work to
  • No written SOPs, KPIs, or accountability measures exist yet to let anyone else run a process without Lin
  • Past hiring experiences have made Lin cautious about adding people before the systems are in place
Blind Spot: Waiting to hire until the systems exist is the right instinct. Most hiring problems start when a new person is added to a firm with no written processes, so the foundation has to come first.
The Positive
  • Lin is already planning to build systems, SOPs, and KPIs before hiring, which is the right order of operations
  • A paralegal and a receptionist service are already in place, so there is a team to build on
The Gap
  • The daily hour spent on template editing adds up to roughly a month of working days each year that is not spent on clients or on the business
  • With processes living in Lin's head, the firm cannot run for a day without her, let alone take a week off
  • A future team of three to four attorneys cannot be built on a firm that has no playbook to train them with
A team that runs from written systems is what turns Lin from the person who does everything into the owner who leads the firm.

Profit Plan Assessment

Oregon Legacy brings in about $260,000 a year after 12 years in practice, but no financial KPIs or reporting rhythm were described, and billing is one of the systems still to be built. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • No financial KPIs or regular reporting rhythm were described on the discovery call
  • Billing is one of the systems on the 12-month goal list, which suggests it is not yet running as smoothly as it could
  • Unbilled administrative time, such as daily template editing, comes straight out of the owner's take-home
  • The long-term goal of take-home income above $200,000 a year needs a clear view of what the firm keeps, not just what it bills
Blind Spot: A missed call never shows up on a profit-and-loss statement. It does not look like an expense — it looks like revenue that never happened.
The Positive
  • Twelve years in practice and about $260,000 in annual revenue show an established book of work to build on
  • KPIs and billing are already on the 12-month goal list, so the need for financial visibility is recognized
The Gap
  • Without KPIs, there is no early warning when intake, billing, or workload slip
  • Without a profit plan, the long-term take-home goal is a hope rather than a target
  • Revenue lost to missed calls and unbilled time is invisible until someone measures it
Knowing the numbers is what lets Lin plan her income instead of discovering it at year-end.
