Lead Generation

Oregon Legacy's online presence is a two-page website with no practice-area pages, and the firm's name did not surface in any of the nine Bend-area estate planning, elder law, and probate searches we ran. The discovery call did not identify where new clients come from today, so the findings below show what is possible once the operational foundation is in place to handle the leads.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • The site's footer points to a 2023 update, and it does not appear to have been refreshed since
  • Navigation has only two items, Home and Contact Us — estate planning, probate, guardianship, Medicaid planning, and special needs trusts all sit as sections on one long homepage rather than on their own pages
  • No blog, no video, and no city-specific pages for Redmond, Sisters, Prineville, or La Pine were found
  • There is no embedded contact form — the only paths to reach the firm are a consultation-booking link and the Contact Us page
Blind Spot: Google can only rank pages that exist. With five different services sharing a single homepage, the firm gives Google just one page to rank for all of them.
The Positive
  • A clear "schedule a free consultation" button sits above the fold, so a visitor who is ready to act knows what to do
  • Lin's bio lists real credentials, including her J.D. from Northwestern School of Law at Lewis & Clark College and active membership in the Estate and Elder Law Sections of the Oregon State Bar
The Gap
  • A prospect searching for Medicaid planning or probate help in Bend has no dedicated page to land on, so competitors with focused pages get the first look
  • Visitors who are not ready to book a consultation have no other way to reach out, and no content to build confidence in the meantime
  • A thin site for a five-area practice reinforces the same lack of systems the discovery call described
A website that works as a front door lets prospects book themselves — so the firm can grow without adding to Lin's daily workload.

Local SEO

The Problem
  • Across nine searches (three each for estate planning, elder law, and probate in Bend), the firm's name did not surface in the organic results reviewed — the map pack itself could not be rendered, so absence from the local 3-pack is not confirmed
  • The firm's review base on public listings such as Birdeye, Avvo, and Yelp is very small compared with the competitors that recur in these searches
  • No Justia or FindLaw listing was found for the firm, and no city-specific pages exist for the surrounding communities
The Positive
  • The firm's name, address, and category are consistent across Avvo, Yelp, Birdeye, and other directories — a clean base to build on
The Gap
  • Prospects who search for elder law, Medicaid planning, or probate in Bend are finding other firms first, so the firm's expertise never gets a chance to be considered
  • A thin review base makes it harder to win trust against firms with far deeper review histories
Two Spruce Law — appeared in nearly every search we ran with a dedicated estate planning and probate focus and a deep, consistently positive review base, which is exactly what both Google and prospects reward.
Being found for the right searches means the right clients come to the firm — instead of Lin having to chase them one referral at a time.

  • Elder law and Medicaid planning: No firm ads were observed for elder law, Medicaid planning, or guardianship terms.
  • Probate: No firm ads were observed for probate or trust administration terms.
Competitor Ad Landscape
  • Two Spruce Law — recurs across estate planning and probate searches; paid-ad activity could not be confirmed with available tools.
  • Bryant, Lovlien & Jarvis — recurs across all three practice areas searched; paid-ad activity could not be confirmed with available tools.
The Opportunity
  • High-intent searches such as "estate planning attorney Bend" and "elder law attorney Bend," where a prospect is already looking to hire
  • "Probate attorney Bend" and "trust administration attorney Bend," where the need is urgent and the prospect is ready to call
  • Medicaid planning and guardianship/conservatorship searches, where families are often under time pressure and comparing very few firms
  • Neighboring communities — Redmond, Sisters, Prineville, and La Pine — where the firm has no local presence today
Blind Spot: Ads only pay off when calls are answered. With roughly a dozen calls a month already being missed, fixing intake first means any future advertising converts instead of leaking.

The Opportunity
