Here Is Where Oregon Legacy Stands Today

Lin Ratcliffe has run Oregon Legacy as a solo estate planning and elder law practice in Bend for 12 years, handling everything from probate and guardianship to Medicaid planning and special needs trusts. The firm brings in about $260,000 a year, but the work still runs through Lin personally and through an intake process that, as discussed on our discovery call, misses roughly a dozen calls a month. The most important finding is that the firm does not need more leads first — it needs a foundation of systems, SOPs, and KPIs so that the leads it already receives are captured and the firm stops depending on one person.

Stage 6 is where the firm runs predictably without you holding every thread — and it is exactly where we are taking you, one system at a time.

Where You Are Today

Stage 3: Solo Practitioner
  • You are the only attorney and handle both the legal work and the low-value administrative work, including about an hour a day editing Word templates
  • Incoming calls go to an outsourced virtual receptionist and then wait on a paralegal callback, and roughly a dozen calls a month are missed
  • There are no written SOPs or KPIs yet — building them is your stated 12-month goal
  • Past hiring experiences have made you cautious about adding staff before the systems are in place

Where You Need To Be

Stage 6: Law Firm Owner
  • The firm runs without you
  • Your income is no longer tied to your personal hours
  • Your time is yours to spend however you choose
  • This is where true freedom and true wealth become possible
The gap between a firm that depends on you for everything and one that runs on its own is closed by process, not by working harder — and that is exactly the plan laid out in this report.

Oregon Legacy
