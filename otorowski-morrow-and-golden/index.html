Here Is Where Otorowski Morrow and Golden Stands Today

Chris Otorowski has spent more than 40 years building a Seattle and Bainbridge Island medical malpractice practice, with a secondary book of trucking, auto, and product injury cases. The firm turns away roughly 800 cases a year and accepts only 10 to 15, and its online presence has not kept pace with that reputation: a 1.0-star Google profile with 2 reviews, and no appearance in any of the Seattle map searches we checked.

Stage 6 is where the firm no longer sifts through hundreds of unwanted calls, the right cases find you as search moves to tools like ChatGPT, and a steady stream of the work you actually want is built into how the firm runs.

Where You Are Today

Stage 3: Solo Practitioner
  • New cases come from "some referrals" and word of mouth, plus an SEO and some Super Lawyers advertising that Chris is still working to understand
  • Five calls a day come in for medical malpractice and "most of them are junk" — about 800 a year are rejected
  • Every intake summary is routed to Chris and the other attorneys for review, and the intake database was built in-house about 20 years ago
  • A newly appointed office manager is still getting up to speed, and Chris says some marketing information is "not visible" to him

Where You Need To Be

Stage 6: Law Firm Owner
  • The firm runs without you
  • Your income is no longer tied to your personal hours
  • Your time is yours to spend however you choose
  • This is where true freedom and true wealth become possible
The gap between where the firm is and where you want it to be is mostly a visibility and lead-quality gap, not a quality-of-work gap — and closing it is what lets you spend less time screening and more time on the cases worth taking.

The Law Firm Growth Acceleration Model

We Help Law Firm Owners Get More Profit, More Personal Freedom, And Outstanding Results For Their Clients.

Most law firms try to grow by chasing more leads. But leads alone are not the problem — and leads alone are not the answer. Here is what actually works.

Lead Generation

Leads are to a law firm what gasoline is to a car. Without them, you go nowhere. But a steady, predictable flow of leads changes everything — you get to choose your cases instead of taking whatever comes in.

Intake

But all the leads in the world are worthless without a system to turn them into paying clients. Most firms are losing cases they already paid for simply because their follow-up is slow or inconsistent.

Self-Managing Team

Once clients are coming in, you need a team — otherwise you end up working 90-hour weeks and results suffer. A self-managing team is what gives you the freedom to step back.

Profit Plan

And once the team is in place, most owners look at their bank account and ask: where did all the money go? That is what a profit plan solves — clarity on what you earn, what you keep, and how to grow both.

All four have to work together. If one is missing, you cannot have more profit, more freedom, and outstanding results for your clients at the same time.

Intake Assessment

One intake coordinator fields every call, writes up the caller's story, and routes it to the attorneys — and the firm still turns away roughly 800 cases a year to accept a handful. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
"We get five calls every day. Every day, a malpractice call. And most of them are junk."
The Problem
  • About 800 cases a year are rejected and only 10 to 15 accepted — roughly a 2% acceptance rate — so most of the screening effort ends in a "no"
  • Every call goes through a single intake coordinator, and the summaries then circulate to you and the other attorneys for review
  • Intake details are typed into a FileMaker Pro database you wrote about 20 years ago, and you told us you would rather dictate straight into the software than transfer information by hand
  • The website does not tell callers which cases the firm does not take — dental malpractice, black mold, and minor-damage claims all still generate calls
Blind Spot: A low acceptance rate is not only an intake problem — it is a signal about who is being attracted. When the website and advertising do not screen out the wrong callers, every one of them still costs the firm time on the phone.
The Positive
  • A dedicated coordinator listens to every caller's story, and you can rule out a case in about 60 seconds when damages are clearly too small
  • Selective case acceptance protects the firm from spending heavily on cases that will not justify the cost
The Gap
  • Attorney time spent reviewing summaries for cases that are clearly rejected is time not spent on the cases the firm accepts
  • Information that has to be retyped or transferred between notes and the database slows the process and invites errors
  • Any improvement in lead quality would raise accepted-case flow without adding to the screening load
Spending less time sifting through unwanted calls is how you free up attention for the higher-quality cases you have said you want.
