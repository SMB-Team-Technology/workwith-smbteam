Intake Assessment

Every call to Law Offices of Jason L. Abelove is answered by Jason personally, with no CRM and no defined intake process behind him. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Jason personally handles every intake call himself — no delegation in place
  • No CRM or intake tracking software of any kind — the firm operates "pen and paper"
  • No live chat and no after-hours coverage — visitors who arrive outside business hours have no way to reach anyone
  • No defined follow-up sequence for leads who do not sign immediately
Blind Spot: Because Jason personally answers every call, it is easy to assume nothing is being lost — but there is no system tracking who called, who never called back, or who went to a competitor after hours.
The Gap
  • Every hour Jason spends on intake is an hour he is not spending on casework or the criminal defense caseload his new hire needs — what it costs in lost revenue is invisible precisely because nothing is tracked
  • Referral leads that call after hours or do not get an immediate response have nowhere to go but voicemail
  • Without a CRM, there is no way to know how many leads the firm is already generating but failing to convert
Fixing intake is the fastest path to more cases without spending another dollar on marketing — and the closest thing to free money available to a firm built to be sold.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • Contact form is located mid-page under a "Set Up a Free Meeting" section — not visible above the fold
  • No live chat present on the site to capture visitors before they leave
  • No CTA-driven intake path for the criminal defense practice, since the page itself does not exist

Team Assessment

The team at Law Offices of Jason L. Abelove is Jason plus one newly-hired attorney, with no dedicated intake, marketing, or ops staff. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
"costing $13k/mo for half-time work"
The Problem
  • The entire team is two people — Jason and one attorney hired specifically for criminal defense
  • The new attorney is working half-time and is currently unprofitable due to insufficient case volume
  • There is no dedicated intake, marketing, or ops team member of any kind
  • Every operational decision still runs through Jason directly
Blind Spot: Hiring the right attorney was the easy part — without a system generating criminal defense demand, that attorney cannot become a profitable team member no matter how skilled they are.
The Gap
  • A firm this dependent on one person cannot be the sellable asset Jason described wanting — buyers pay for systems, not for the owner's personal hours
  • The new hire is currently a cost center rather than a growth lever, which delays rather than accelerates the path to a firm that runs without Jason
  • No one besides Jason is positioned to take on leadership responsibility as the firm grows
Turning your newest hire into a self-sufficient, profitable team member is the single biggest step toward the sellable firm — and the personal time back — that you are building toward.

Profit Plan Assessment

Law Offices of Jason L. Abelove runs at roughly $1.2M-$1.4M in annual revenue with strong margins on paper, but that has not translated into disciplined cash management. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"high personal spending has maxed out a credit line"
The Problem
  • Revenue and margin are known at a high level (~$1.2M-$1.4M/year, ~50% margin) but that visibility has not prevented a maxed-out personal credit line
  • The new criminal defense hire is a direct, ongoing drag on profit — $13,000/month for half-time work with insufficient volume to break even
  • There is no formal profit plan connecting practice-area performance (employment law vs. criminal defense) to what actually gets kept
  • No system exists to know acquisition cost or which practice area is actually driving profit versus revenue
Blind Spot: Strong top-line margins can mask a real cash-flow problem — a maxed-out credit line alongside a healthy P&L usually means money is leaving faster than it is being planned for, not that the firm is unprofitable.
The Gap
  • Without a profit plan, Jason cannot see whether the criminal defense expansion is on track to pay for itself or quietly eroding the margin his exit valuation depends on
  • A 1.25x-revenue exit target requires provable, predictable profit — not just healthy revenue — and that proof does not exist today
  • Personal cash-flow strain compounds the pressure to grow the top line without necessarily protecting what is kept
A real profit plan is what protects the valuation you are building toward — a buyer pays for predictable profit, not for revenue alone.
