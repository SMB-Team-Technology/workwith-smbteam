Here Is Where Caress Law Stands Today

Caress Law is a boutique Estate Planning firm in Portland, Oregon, built by Tammi M. Caress over 13 years into a firm generating $2.2M-$2.7M in annual revenue across Estate Planning, Probate & Trust Administration, and Business & Tax Planning. The firm has a small team and an external fractional COO in place, but that COO lacks law-firm-specific experience — leaving Tammi as the operational fallback for systems and processes the firm needs to run without her. That single gap is the most important finding from this research: real revenue, a real niche reputation, and no leadership structure yet capable of carrying it forward.

Stage 6 is the stage where Caress Law finally runs itself — giving Tammi the time back that thirteen years of hands-on ownership have not allowed, and it is exactly where this plan is taking her.

Where You Are Today

STAGE 4: SMALL BUSINESS MANAGER
  • Tammi remains the operational fallback for all systems and processes across the firm
  • The current fractional COO lacks law-firm-specific experience, so foundational work like building attorney KPIs still falls back to Tammi
  • Core case work — the 27-step probate workflow, monthly bill review, mail processing — is still handled manually rather than through automation
  • Probate and trust administration are billed hourly today, with flat fees identified as a goal but not yet enabled by process or automation

Where You Need To Be

Stage 6: Law Firm Owner
  • The firm runs without you
  • Your income is no longer tied to your personal hours
  • Your time is yours to spend however you choose
  • This is where true freedom and true wealth become possible
The gap between where Caress Law is today and Stage 6 is not a revenue problem — it is a leadership and automation problem, and closing it is what finally gives Tammi her time back.
Overall Growth Stage Assessment
Caress Law
Caress Law is operating at Stage 4 — Small Business Manager: there is a real team and a delegated operations function on paper, but neither is yet capable of carrying the firm without Tammi. The single most urgent limiting factor is that Tammi remains the operational fallback for all systems and processes, unable to step back because her current fractional COO does not have the law-firm-specific experience to run operations independently. With a stated 3-year goal of $7.6M against current revenue of $2.2M-$2.7M, that gap will not close through more hours from Tammi — it requires qualified operational leadership and automation built specifically around this firm's workflows.
