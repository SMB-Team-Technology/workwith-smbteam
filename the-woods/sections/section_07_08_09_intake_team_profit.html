Intake Assessment

Intake is where The Woods Law Firm is strongest on paper — you close better than half of the cases that come in — but how new leads are answered and followed up was not reviewed, and that matters as ad volume grows. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
"So, yes, the conversion is not our problem."
The Problem
  • Who answers new-lead calls, how fast they respond, and whether after-hours calls are covered were not covered on the call and should be confirmed before volume scales
  • Callers asked how they found the firm mostly answer "the internet," which does not show which campaign or county produced the case
  • ChatGPT ads are serving with no visibility into impressions or leads, so any inquiries from them cannot be tied back to the spend
  • The website contact form sits lower on the homepage rather than above the fold, so visitors who prefer not to call have a harder path to reach you
Blind Spot: A close rate measured on the cases that come in does not capture calls that never reach the intake step — as ad volume grows, a missed or slow-answered call is a lost case you never see.
The Positive
  • You close better than 50% of the cases that come in, and better than 80% of those who have the money to retain you
  • You track your cases closely and said a monthly signed-case list for ad attribution is easy for the firm to produce
The Gap
  • At about $5,000 per case, every additional ad lead that goes unanswered or unsigned is a meaningful amount of lost fees
  • Without source-level tracking, the firm cannot tell which county or campaign is producing signed cases
  • Adding roughly 60 cases in flow puts more pressure on response time and follow-up than the firm has needed so far
Protecting every lead you pay for is the fastest way to turn ad spend into collected cash.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • A click-to-call button sits above the fold on the homepage, and a text option appears on the mobile view
  • The contact form (name, email, phone, case details) sits in the lower-middle of the homepage, not above the fold
  • No live chat or other after-hours capture was detected on the site, so late-night visitors have only the phone and the form

These findings are also referenced in the Website Audit section.

Team Assessment

As the lead trial attorney you are in court much of the time, while three staffed offices and rising volume add to what the team carries and to the marketing you currently manage yourself. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"I literally just got to focus on lead flow to keep all these people fed right now."
The Problem
  • You are the trial attorney and, in your words, always in court — your calendar is the firm's biggest constraint
  • You have been managing Google LSA and PPC yourself while also trying cases
  • Three staffed offices plus satellites in Myrtle Beach and York mean more locations to coordinate; who owns operations and marketing across them was not discussed
Blind Spot: When growth runs through your calendar, more cases can quietly mean more load on you rather than more freedom — marketing is the easiest piece to hand off first.
The Positive
  • Three staffed offices already give the firm a footprint in its key markets
  • You are open to a dedicated marketing manager and want to stay closely involved in the first calls
The Gap
  • Every hour spent adjusting ad campaigns is an hour not spent in court or with clients
  • Without a clear owner for marketing, results depend on how much attention you can spare in a given week
  • Growth in cases without a management layer lands on you first
Handing marketing to a dedicated team keeps you focused on trials and on the cash they generate.

Profit Plan Assessment

You describe the firm as healthy, with about $2 million a year in criminal work, but ad spending has been set through trial and error and the new investment has to come back. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"I want to be playing with house money, not my money, which I've been doing forever."
The Problem
  • Ad spend has been set through tests at $3,000, $5,000, and $10,000 rather than against a target cost per signed case
  • Self-managed PPC has run $38–$79 per click, but a cost per lead and per signed case has not been established
  • ChatGPT ads are serving with no impression or attribution data, so that spend cannot be tied to cases
  • You framed the new engagement as roughly a $130,000 investment that has to be earned back, but no payback target or timeline has been set
Blind Spot: In small markets, spend per county hits diminishing returns — in your words, "if we spend $3,000 per county, then you exhaust all of the leads in that area" — so profit depends on how many counties you open, not only how much you spend in one.
The Positive
  • You describe the business as healthy and said, "I'm making money. I'm okay."
  • You already track every case, which makes true cost-per-signed-case reporting possible from day one
The Gap
  • Without a cost per signed case, there is no way to know which spending is working and which is repeating a test
  • You want dollars weighted toward the actual ads and landing pages rather than management, which makes reporting on where each dollar goes essential
  • Scaling from one concentrated county to several without a county-level profit view risks spreading spend too thin
Playing with house money means knowing, county by county, which ad dollars come back as collected fees.
