Lead Generation

The Woods Law Firm is already buying leads — Google LSA and PPC are running, a Meta vendor has been hired, and ChatGPT ads are serving — yet you describe lead flow as the problem, and nothing yet turns that spend into a predictable stream of new DUI, domestic violence, and drug cases. The opportunity is less about spending more and more about concentrating spend by county and sending every click to a page built to convert.

Website Audit

"My site is trash from 2019, man."
The Problem
  • The live site is a 2019 build — the footer reads © 2019 and the newest blog post dates to September 2019
  • PageSpeed scores: Mobile 50/100 — Desktop 88/100
  • Most pages target all of South Carolina; the only city pages are Greenville, Columbia, and Beaufort — there are none for Spartanburg, York, or Myrtle Beach
  • The contact form sits lower on the homepage, the page title repeats the firm name twice, and there is no video
Blind Spot: A second domain (woodslaw-firm.com) appears in search results with similar "Why Choose the Woods Law Firm" content. It is unclear whether it is an old duplicate or another business — worth checking before the new site launches, because duplicate content can dilute rankings.
The Positive
  • A click-to-call button sits above the fold, so a mobile visitor can reach you in one tap
  • Strong trust signals are already in place — NACDL and ABA logos, an Expertise.com badge, and media logos — plus a base of about 31 pages to build on
The Gap
  • A mobile score of 50 means paid clicks land on a slower experience, which raises the cost of every signed case
  • With no Spartanburg, York, or Myrtle Beach pages, ad clicks in those counties land on statewide pages instead of a page that speaks to that market
  • A stale blog and no video give searchers and Google little evidence of a currently active firm
A site built to convert the clicks you already pay for is the fastest way to turn existing ad spend into signed cases.

Local SEO

The Problem
  • On a Greer-centered "DUI lawyer" map grid shown on the call, the firm appears in about 9% of the area versus about 65% for Dayne Phillips, 53% for The Bateman Law Firm, and 43% for Touma Law Group
  • A web search for "DUI lawyer Spartanburg SC" did not return the firm at all — in the county you call your sweet spot
  • Listing details are inconsistent: two phone numbers for the Greer office, one directory naming it the "Greenville Office," and suite formatting that varies by directory
Blind Spot: Map rankings are local to where the searcher stands — strength around the Greer office does little for a searcher in Spartanburg, where you report your highest number of closings.
The Positive
  • Three live Google listings (Greer, Columbia, Beaufort) and an established reputation are assets that a newer entrant like Touma Law Group does not yet have
The Gap
  • Searchers picking a DUI lawyer from the map results see competitors first in the counties that matter most to you
  • Listing, category, photo, and posting details were not reviewable in this research and should be verified live and tightened for each office
Dayne Phillips (South Carolina Criminal Law) — covers about 65% of the Greer-centered DUI map grid with offices across Greenville, Spartanburg, and Columbia, versus about 9% for your firm.
Showing up where a DUI arrestee is searching is the most direct way to put more people through your door right now.

Google Ads

Ad Presence
  • You confirmed the firm is running Google PPC today, self-managed at roughly $3,000 a month; live ad copy could not be captured in this research
  • Self-managed cost has run $38–$79 per click, and your 2026 tests showed the concentrated, one-county $3,000 campaign outperformed larger spreads
Competitor Ad Landscape
  • Jeffcoat Firm — you described it as blanketing the paid results ("blankets everything"); ad copy not captured
  • The Bateman Law Firm and Dayne Phillips — both named by you as active paid advertisers, with Bateman running "a bunch" of DUI-focused ads; Joshua Schultz also "does a lot"
The Opportunity
  • DUI, domestic violence, and drug possession searches by county — Spartanburg, Greer/Greenville, Columbia (Richland), York, Myrtle Beach (Horry), and Beaufort
  • Dedicated landing pages for DUI, domestic violence, drugs, and general criminal defense so each click matches the search that triggered it
  • Spartanburg: you said volume is higher and leads cost less there than in Greenville, which you called oversaturated
  • Negative keyword cleanup and county-by-county budget caps, so spend stops leaking into clicks that never become cases
Blind Spot: A $38–$79 click is only the first number — what matters is cost per signed case, and without a monthly match-back of signed cases to campaigns there is no way to see which county or campaign is paying for itself.

Local Service Ads

LSA Presence
  • You have an active LSA account today and find it more productive than paying per click, since you pay per call
  • Google Screened status and LSA review count were not observable in this research and should be confirmed in the account audit
Competitor LSA Landscape
  • Jeffcoat Firm, Dayne Phillips, and The Bateman Law Firm — all named by you as competing in LSA; their review counts were not verified
  • Joshua Schultz, FR Law, and David Price — also named by you as LSA competitors; review counts were not verified
The Opportunity
  • Because LSA is pay-per-call, it is the cleanest channel to turn directly into booked consultations — an immediate account audit is a quick win
  • LSAs appear above everything else on Google — above paid ads, above the map pack, above all organic results — and you only pay when a real prospect calls you directly.
  • Google Screened status and review volume drive LSA placement, so confirming both and keeping reviews flowing is the first step to ranking higher
Blind Spot: Google changed LSA budgeting this month, which can let a monthly budget run out in the first week or two — pacing spend weekly keeps the account visible all month.
