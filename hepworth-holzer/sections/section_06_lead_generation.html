Lead Generation

Hepworth Holzer generates most of its cases through referrals from a strong professional network, backed by a self-managed $4,000/month Google and Microsoft Ads campaign on hyper-specific local keywords. The firm just ran its first self-directed ChatGPT ad test — a smart, early move into a channel almost no local competitor has claimed yet, but one that needs sharper targeting to convert.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • PageSpeed scores could not be verified live during this research pass (API rate-limited on repeated attempts) — recommend a manual check before finalizing creative, since site speed directly affects paid ad conversion cost
  • No live chat observed on the site — for a firm testing new lead channels, that is one more point where an after-hours visitor can leave without converting
  • Blog is active and current (posts Aug 6, Aug 4, and July 30, 2026), but content has not yet been structured for AI answer engines (ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews) — the exact channel the firm is trying to win in
The Positive
  • Professionally built and actively maintained site (2026 copyright, named developer credit), mobile responsive, with a clear above-the-fold CTA ("Free Case Evaluation") and phone number in the header
  • All six attorneys have individual bios and photos, and the firm has already built out dedicated city/case-type landing pages (Boise Truck Accident Lawyers, Boise Wrongful Death Attorneys, and others) — a level of page depth most Boise competitors do not match
The Gap
  • Strong bones, but the content is not yet built for the AI-search channel Kurt wants to win — meaning the site itself isn't reinforcing the ChatGPT ad strategy
  • Unverified site speed is a live risk once paid spend increases — slow load times raise cost per lead on every paid channel
The site the current partners built is strong enough to carry the next generation — it just isn't structured yet for the AI-search channel that will define who wins Boise PI over the next few years.
