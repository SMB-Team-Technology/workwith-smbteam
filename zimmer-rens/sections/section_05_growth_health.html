Pillar Health Scorecard

Lead Generation

  • New client flow still runs almost entirely on organic referrals, repeat clients, and the Wisconsin State Bar Referral Service
  • A low-spend video ad campaign launched about a month ago, but there is no proven paid system behind it yet
  • No visible marketing footprint at all for the four practice areas the firm is expanding into — estate planning, criminal defense, PI, and first-party bad faith insurance
Every month without a real lead generation system is a month the four new practice areas grow slower than the office space and hires behind them.

Intake

  • Follow-up on leads that do come in is timely, but there is no system in place to maximize how many of them convert
  • Leads route through a call queue to a receptionist/paralegal and then remote assistants for matter creation — a reasonable process during business hours
  • No after-hours coverage — a critical gap since most criminal defense and PI leads come in outside 9-5, exactly when the firm currently has no one answering
Every after-hours lead that goes unanswered in criminal defense or PI is a case — and the revenue behind it — going to whichever competitor picks up the phone first.

Self-Managing Team

  • 16-person team already in place — 9 attorneys, 3 remote assistants, 1 office manager, and 3 paralegals — giving Zimmer & Rens real structure most firms this size don't have
  • A deliberate, working hiring pipeline: recruiting pre-law students as paralegals with a plan to bring them back as associates after graduation
  • Team is being asked to run four new practice areas and a second office in La Crosse on top of the existing workload, with no additional operational or marketing systems built yet to support that
A team this structured can carry a multi-office, multi-practice-area firm — but only once the systems behind lead flow and intake are built to match the ambition.

Profit

  • The firm already retains a fractional CFO — real financial visibility most growing firms don't have at this stage
  • That CFO has independently confirmed marketing spend sits significantly below the roughly 20% of revenue benchmark firms in growth mode typically need
  • A prior $100K quote from a generalist AI/operations consultant was judged too high relative to the value offered — pricing discipline is already part of how this firm evaluates investment
Closing the gap between current marketing spend and the benchmark the firm's own CFO has flagged is the fastest lever to fund estate planning, criminal defense, PI, and the La Crosse office at the pace Paul and Taylor want.
