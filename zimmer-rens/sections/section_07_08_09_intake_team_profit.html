Intake Assessment

Zimmer & Rens has a working intake process during business hours — a call queue into a receptionist/paralegal, then remote assistants for matter creation — but there is no after-hours coverage at all, and no system in place to maximize how many of the leads that do come in actually convert. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • No after-hours coverage — calls that come in nights and weekends have nowhere to go
  • Most leads for criminal defense and PI — the two practice areas the firm is prioritizing first — come in outside standard 9-5 business hours, which is exactly when the firm has no one answering
  • Follow-up on daytime leads is timely, but there is no defined process to maximize how many of those leads actually convert to signed clients
  • Intake currently runs through a multi-step handoff — call queue to receptionist/paralegal, then remote assistants for matter creation — with no stated tracking system to catch leads that stall between those steps
Blind Spot: You may not realize how many criminal defense and PI leads are calling after 5pm, getting no answer, and calling the next firm on the list before your team opens the next morning.
The Positive
  • Follow-up on leads that are received is timely — the foundation of a good intake process is already there, it just needs to run around the clock
  • The multi-step handoff (queue → receptionist/paralegal → remote assistant) shows real delegation is already happening rather than one person handling everything personally
The Gap
  • Every after-hours criminal defense or PI call that goes unanswered is a signed case — and the revenue behind it — going to a competitor instead
  • Without a defined conversion process, a meaningful share of daytime leads that do connect with the firm are still being lost to indecision or lack of follow-through
  • Two new consumer-facing practice areas are being launched on top of an intake process that was not built with after-hours consumer urgency in mind
Every new practice area Zimmer & Rens adds without after-hours intake coverage adds risk, not just opportunity — closing that gap protects the very expansion Paul and Taylor are investing in.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The contact form is reachable in one click via "Contact us" links throughout the site, but is not a native form embedded directly on the homepage above the fold
  • No live chat is present — a visitor with an urgent criminal defense or PI question has no immediate way to start a conversation outside of calling or filling out the contact form
  • The homepage video featuring Paul Zimmer and Taylor Rens is a strong trust-building asset that could be paired with a more prominent, above-the-fold contact path

Team Assessment

Zimmer & Rens already has a real team structure — 9 attorneys, 3 remote assistants, 1 office manager, and 3 paralegals — but that team is now being asked to stand up four new practice areas and a second office with no additional systems in place to support the load. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Four new practice areas (estate planning, criminal defense, PI, first-party bad faith insurance) and a new La Crosse office are launching on top of the existing team's workload, without dedicated marketing or intake systems built to support them yet
  • The firm's hiring pipeline — recruiting pre-law students as paralegals with a plan to bring them back as associates post-graduation — is a smart long-term strategy, but it takes time to mature and does not solve today's immediate lead flow or after-hours gaps
  • No stated leadership layer below Paul Zimmer and Taylor Rens dedicated specifically to marketing or growth operations — the partners are the ones driving this expansion personally
  • Team's capacity is being stretched across more practice areas and a new location at the same time, without a corresponding increase in the systems that generate and convert new business
The Positive
  • A 16-person team with real role specialization (attorneys, remote assistants, office manager, paralegals) is a genuine structural advantage most firms this size don't have
  • The pre-law-student-to-associate hiring pipeline is a proven, working strategy that reduces long-term hiring risk and cost
The Gap
  • Without dedicated marketing and after-hours intake systems, Paul and Taylor will remain the ones personally driving growth in each new practice area rather than stepping back
  • Growing headcount and locations without growing lead generation and intake systems at the same pace means more overhead before more revenue — the opposite of what the expansion is meant to achieve
A 16-person team is exactly the foundation a self-managing, multi-office firm is built on — but it only gets there once the systems around lead flow and intake catch up to the team's capacity.

Profit Plan Assessment

Zimmer & Rens already retains a fractional CFO who tracks real benchmarks — including confirming that marketing spend sits significantly below the roughly 20% of revenue that firms in active growth mode typically need. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Marketing investment is confirmed — by the firm's own fractional CFO — to sit well below the ~20% of revenue benchmark needed to fund four new practice areas and a second office
  • A prior $100K quote from a generalist AI/operations consultant was judged too high — a sign the firm is evaluating growth investments carefully, but also a sign it has not yet found the right-sized partner for its AI and automation needs
  • No stated profit target or margin goal tied specifically to the estate planning, criminal defense, PI, or first-party bad faith insurance expansion
  • Current annual revenue and revenue goal were not stated on the call — without that number confirmed, it is difficult to know exactly how much marketing and operations investment the firm can support
Blind Spot: Having a fractional CFO who can flag that marketing spend is below benchmark is a real advantage — but a benchmark alone does not tell you which of the four new practice areas will actually be profitable first. Without practice-area-level cost and revenue tracking, the firm is expanding four ways at once without knowing which bet to lean into hardest.
The Positive
  • A fractional CFO relationship already in place is more financial visibility than most firms this size have — the firm knows it has a marketing gap instead of guessing
The Gap
  • Knowing marketing spend is below benchmark is not the same as having a funded plan to close that gap — without one, the four-practice-area expansion will be slower and more expensive than it needs to be
  • Without confirmed revenue and revenue-goal figures, it is not yet possible to size exactly how much ad spend and marketing investment the firm can support without over-extending
The firm's own CFO has already done the hard part — naming the gap. Closing it is what turns "marketing spend below benchmark" into estate planning, criminal defense, PI, and La Crosse all growing profitably at the same time.
