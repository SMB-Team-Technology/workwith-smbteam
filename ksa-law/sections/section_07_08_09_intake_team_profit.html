Intake Assessment

KSA Law has no formal proposal or business-development process beyond Kelly personally handling every prospective client conversation — and the firm has explicitly flagged its own close rate on fractional GC proposals as something it does not know how to improve. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Kelly personally handles every prospective client conversation and proposal — no team member is trained to support business development
  • The firm has explicitly identified its own close rate on fractional GC proposals as unknown and something it doesn't know how to improve
  • No proposal template, pricing framework, or follow-up cadence was mentioned for prospects who don't sign immediately
  • With litigation already at capacity, KSA Law risks turning away new work without a defined process for evaluating and prioritizing incoming proposals
Blind Spot: Because KSA Law's engagements are large, VC-fund-scale proposals rather than high-volume consumer cases, a single missed or slow-to-close proposal represents a far bigger revenue swing than a missed individual lead — which raises the stakes on getting a repeatable proposal process in place.
The Positive
  • Kelly is already closing enough proposals to have grown the firm from solo to 7 people in 4 months — proof the underlying offer and referral relationships work, even without a formal process
The Gap
  • Every proposal that stalls or doesn't close is lost revenue on the path to $5M ARR, with no system to learn from a "no" and improve the next pitch
  • Without a benchmarked close rate, Kelly has no way to know whether the bottleneck is pricing, positioning, timing, or something else entirely
  • A defined proposal process could free Kelly from needing to personally close every deal — a prerequisite for the team growth she says she wants to protect
Fixing the proposal process doesn't require a single new referral — it means closing more of the fractional GC business Kelly is already being offered, which is the fastest, lowest-cost path toward $5M ARR.

Team Assessment

KSA Law's team has grown from Kelly alone to 7 people in just four months — but Kelly still owns every business-development conversation, and now also carries the demands of running Sandberg Phoenix's Kansas City office at the same time. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"The litigation team is at capacity, preventing pursuit of new work."
The Problem
  • The litigation team, added only recently via a Senior Counsel hire, is already at capacity — preventing the firm from pursuing new litigation work
  • Kelly simultaneously serves as Managing Shareholder of Sandberg Phoenix's Kansas City office (26 attorneys, 17 staff) while founding and running KSA Law — a dual leadership load worth confirming directly as a bandwidth constraint
  • No stated org structure, manager, or delegated leader beneath Kelly for business development, proposal strategy, or firm operations
  • The team grew from 1 to 7 in four months with no stated plan for how the next phase of hiring will be structured or managed
Blind Spot: Kelly's stated personal motivation is ensuring her team's satisfaction and career growth — but a team that is already at capacity in litigation, with no manager layer beneath Kelly, is more likely to burn out than grow the way she wants for them.
The Positive
  • The firm has already proven it can scale headcount fast — 1 to 7 people in 4 months — when the right opportunity presented itself
The Gap
  • With litigation at capacity and no delegated BD leader, growth is capped by Kelly's personal hours, not by market demand
  • The dual role at Sandberg Phoenix compounds the bandwidth problem, leaving less time for both the highest-value fractional GC work and the team development Kelly says she cares about
  • Without a management layer, every new hire adds more for Kelly to personally oversee rather than freeing her time
Until Kelly has leaders beneath her handling litigation capacity, proposal support, and day-to-day operations, she cannot deliver on the career growth she wants for her team — or step back from the two full-time leadership roles she is currently carrying.

Profit Plan Assessment

KSA Law is not yet profitable — Kelly has stated a firm goal of break-even by October 2026 and profitability by year-end 2026 — and in the meantime, slow-paying clients are forcing her to personally cover the gap. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: RED
"Strain from slow-paying clients (30–45 day cycles) requires Kelly to capitalize expenses."
The Problem
  • Cash flow strain from 30–45 day client payment cycles is requiring Kelly to personally capitalize firm expenses
  • The firm's own stated goal is break-even by October 2026, with profitability targeted only by year-end 2026 — meaning KSA Law is running at or below break-even today
  • No stated visibility into which type of engagement (fractional GC retainer, project-based, or litigation) is actually the most profitable per hour of attorney time
  • The transcript's ambiguity between a "$3M to $5M ARR" strategic goal and Kelly's individually stated $40k–$60k/month practice revenue suggests even the firm's current top-line number may not be fully reconciled internally
Blind Spot: The revenue figures used to scope this proposal are built on Kelly's individually stated monthly numbers, since the transcript's ARR goal was ambiguous about whether it reflects the whole firm today — worth confirming directly with Kelly, since it affects everything from pricing to hiring plans.
The Positive
  • The firm has a specific, dated target — break-even October 2026, profitability by year-end 2026 — rather than an open-ended goal, giving a profit plan a real starting point
The Gap
  • Without financial visibility into engagement-level profitability, Kelly cannot tell whether growing revenue is actually growing what she keeps
  • Continuing to personally capitalize the firm's expenses puts Kelly's own financial position at risk in service of the firm's growth
  • Hitting the $5M ARR goal without a profit plan in place risks simply scaling the same cash flow strain to a much larger size
Kelly cannot stop personally bankrolling KSA Law's growth, and her team cannot get the stability she wants for them, until there is real financial visibility behind the push toward $5M ARR.
