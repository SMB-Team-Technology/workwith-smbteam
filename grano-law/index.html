Here Is Where Grano Law Stands Today

Marc Grano runs Grano Law Offices, P.C. from Las Vegas, New Mexico — a boutique criminal defense and personal injury practice built around his DWI specialty, with a five-person team that includes two contract attorneys. After years of steady flow from his website, blog and Google ads, DWI inquiries have recently slowed to a trickle — and the cause is not yet visible to him.

Stage 6 is where the right cases arrive on a steady rhythm without you managing the machinery behind them — and it is exactly where we want to take you, while keeping the boutique practice you have built.

Where You Are Today

Stage 4: Small Business Manager
  • Leads come from the website, blog, Local Service Ads and Google search ads — but DWI inquiries have slowed to a trickle over the last couple of months
  • The ad accounts are run by outside vendors and are not something you review on a regular basis
  • A lean team — two support staff and two contract attorneys — with you as the DWI specialist at the center of every case
  • A deliberate boutique model: roughly 35 to 50 active matters, kept at 60–70% capacity so the firm can flex for trials without breaking

Where You Need To Be

Stage 6: Law Firm Owner
  • The firm runs without you
  • Your income is no longer tied to your personal hours
  • Your time is yours to spend however you choose
  • This is where true freedom and true wealth become possible
The gap between a steady-but-fragile pipeline and a firm that reliably brings you quality cases is smaller than it feels — it comes down to visibility, coverage and the right local focus, and that is what this plan is built around.

The Law Firm Growth Acceleration Model

We Help Law Firm Owners Get More Profit, More Personal Freedom, And Outstanding Results For Their Clients.

Most law firms try to grow by chasing more leads. But leads alone are not the problem — and leads alone are not the answer. Here is what actually works.

Lead Generation

Leads are to a law firm what gasoline is to a car. Without them, you go nowhere. But a steady, predictable flow of leads changes everything — you get to choose your cases instead of taking whatever comes in.

Intake

But all the leads in the world are worthless without a system to turn them into paying clients. Most firms are losing cases they already paid for simply because their follow-up is slow or inconsistent.

Self-Managing Team

Once clients are coming in, you need a team — otherwise you end up working 90-hour weeks and results suffer. A self-managing team is what gives you the freedom to step back.

Profit Plan

And once the team is in place, most owners look at their bank account and ask: where did all the money go? That is what a profit plan solves — clarity on what you earn, what you keep, and how to grow both.

All four have to work together. If one is missing, you cannot have more profit, more freedom, and outstanding results for your clients at the same time.

Website Audit

"I do feel like we've been doing blog posts. I had her completely revamp the homepage."
The Problem
  • The homepage title leads with the firm name and has no city, county or state, and DWI does not appear in it — so Google gets little signal about where you practice or what you are best at
  • PageSpeed scores: Mobile 80/100 — Desktop 97/100 (lab test run October 16, 2026). Desktop is excellent; mobile has room to improve for people searching from their phones
  • The Santa Fe DWI/DUI page has the city in its title and headline, but neither contains “lawyer” or “attorney” — the words people actually type when they are looking to hire
  • The homepage headline is the firm name alone, with the practice areas placed in a secondary line beneath it
Blind Spot: A refreshed homepage and regular blogging are real investments, but they are only as valuable as the search signals attached to them. If the page titles and headlines do not carry the city and “DWI attorney” wording, new content can be published for months without pulling in the searchers in Santa Fe and Rio Rancho that you most want.
The Positive
  • Modern, mobile-responsive design with a clear “Request Your Free Case Strategy Session” call-to-action and phone number above the fold
  • Deep practice-area coverage — 18 personal injury pages, 7 criminal defense pages and 6 DWI/DUI pages, including MVD license revocation hearings
  • Dedicated area pages for Santa Fe, Taos, Santa Rosa, Raton, Mora, Española, Rio Rancho and Bernalillo
  • A blog published every 10 to 14 days, alternating between criminal and personal injury topics, and a strong attorney bio with 25+ years of experience, Army JAG service and advanced DWI training
The Gap
  • Without city-plus-attorney wording in titles and headlines, competitors with that structure are better positioned to win the Santa Fe and Rio Rancho searches you want
  • Strong content that is not tied to local search terms leaves the best DWI prospects finding other firms first
  • Mobile speed at 80 leaves some phone searchers — most legal searches — waiting longer than they should
The website already shows the depth of a true DWI specialist — the next step is making sure the right searchers in the right cities can find it.

Team Assessment

Grano Law is a lean boutique — Marc, two support staff and two contract attorneys — and that focus is part of its strength. It also means the firm’s expertise, its flex capacity for trials and its oversight of marketing all run through one person. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"it's me. I have two support staff. And then I have two other attorneys that are on contract."
The Problem
  • The DWI expertise that defines the firm sits with one attorney, and the firm’s capacity to absorb a trial is you
  • No one on the team was described as owning marketing results, so lead flow is effectively unwatched between your own check-ins
  • The marketing vendors work independently and the ad accounts are not reviewed on a regular basis, leaving no single point of accountability for results
  • Adding people has not been a priority, so there is no built-out layer between you and the day-to-day case flow
Blind Spot: Holding the firm at 60–70% capacity is a smart way to protect quality — but it also means the firm’s shock absorber is your own time. A slow lead month and a heavy trial month can hit at once, and nobody else is positioned to buffer either one.
The Positive
  • A clear operating philosophy: a boutique caseload with enough margin to flex for trials without breaking
  • Contract attorneys already handle criminal writing and personal injury writing and interrogatories, which relieves pressure on your time
The Gap
  • Marketing performance depends on your attention and that of outside vendors, rather than a simple reporting rhythm that tells you the numbers each month
  • A single point of expertise and flexibility makes any unexpected demand on your time a risk to the whole firm
  • Without a defined weekly rhythm for reviewing leads, intake and marketing, a slowdown like this one is spotted late
A firm that can flex for a trial — and keep its pipeline healthy — without everything resting on you protects both the quality you stand for and the time you want to keep.
