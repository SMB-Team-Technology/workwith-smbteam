Intake Assessment

Grano Law advertises a phone line that is “available 24/7/365,” but the public listings and ads that send callers to it tell a different story after 5 p.m., and message leads are switched off. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
"That's a double-edged sword, by the way."
The Problem
  • Local Service Ads stop running at 5 p.m. and the Google Business Profile shows 9-to-5 hours, while the website promises a 24/7/365 line — the places people click from do not match the coverage you advertise
  • Message leads are turned off on Local Service Ads, so every inquiry depends on a live phone call; Marc’s concern about them is fair, but the answer is a response process rather than a switch left off
  • We do not yet have a view of how many inquiries arrive, how quickly they are answered, or how many become signed cases
  • The ad accounts are not something you look at on a regular basis, so missed calls or slow follow-up would not show up in any report you see
Blind Spot: Someone facing a DWI charge does not search only between 9 and 5. When the ad is dark and the Google profile is “closed,” the person who would have called you simply calls the next firm that shows up.
The Positive
  • A clear “Request Your Free Case Strategy Session” offer and a phone number above the fold on the website
  • A deliberately small caseload of roughly 35 to 50 matters means every new client gets real attention once they reach you
The Gap
  • Paid clicks and calls after 5 p.m. may be going unanswered — leads you already pay for that never reach a consultation
  • A slowdown in DWI inquiries is much harder to diagnose without call and lead tracking from first contact to signed case
  • With message leads off, a prospect who prefers to write instead of call has no way to reach you from the ad
A consistent stream of quality cases only helps if every call and message is answered quickly and turned into a consultation — and that is how the spigot stays open.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The primary website call-to-action — “Request Your Free Case Strategy Session” — appears above the fold with a tap-to-call phone number shown as “Available 24/7/365”
  • A “Free Strategy Session” link sits in the main navigation, but the exact placement and behavior of the contact form was not confirmed in our review
  • No live chat was evident on the pages we reviewed, so after-hours visitors who prefer not to call have no immediate way to reach the firm

These findings are also referenced in the Website Audit section.

Team Assessment

Grano Law is a lean boutique — Marc, two support staff and two contract attorneys — and that focus is part of its strength. It also means the firm’s expertise, its flex capacity for trials and its oversight of marketing all run through one person. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"it's me. I have two support staff. And then I have two other attorneys that are on contract."
The Problem
  • The DWI expertise that defines the firm sits with one attorney, and the firm’s capacity to absorb a trial is you
  • No one on the team was described as owning marketing results, so lead flow is effectively unwatched between your own check-ins
  • The marketing vendors work independently and the ad accounts are not reviewed on a regular basis, leaving no single point of accountability for results
  • Adding people has not been a priority, so there is no built-out layer between you and the day-to-day case flow
Blind Spot: Holding the firm at 60–70% capacity is a smart way to protect quality — but it also means the firm’s shock absorber is your own time. A slow lead month and a heavy trial month can hit at once, and nobody else is positioned to buffer either one.
The Positive
  • A clear operating philosophy: a boutique caseload with enough margin to flex for trials without breaking
  • Contract attorneys already handle criminal writing and personal injury writing and interrogatories, which relieves pressure on your time
The Gap
  • Marketing performance depends on your attention and that of outside vendors, rather than a simple reporting rhythm that tells you the numbers each month
  • A single point of expertise and flexibility makes any unexpected demand on your time a risk to the whole firm
  • Without a defined weekly rhythm for reviewing leads, intake and marketing, a slowdown like this one is spotted late
A firm that can flex for a trial — and keep its pipeline healthy — without everything resting on you protects both the quality you stand for and the time you want to keep.

Profit Plan Assessment

Grano Law’s typical revenue is steady at roughly $600K to $800K, and last year’s higher total came from a single large personal injury settlement rather than a new baseline. With a steady core and a lumpy top line, a drop in DWI inquiries matters more than it first appears. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Settlement-driven personal injury revenue makes year-to-year totals hard to compare, so a slow stretch in steady DWI work is easy to miss
  • Ad spend is not tied to a visible cost per signed case, so it is hard to know which dollars are producing cases and which are not
  • No profit target or margin tracking came up on the call, so “enough” is defined by capacity rather than by a number
  • Ads that stop at 5 p.m., paired with a profile that does not match them, risk paying for attention that never turns into a consultation
Blind Spot: Without a cost-per-signed-case figure, you cannot tell whether a slow stretch is a lead problem, a conversion problem or an ad-account problem — and each one has a very different fix.
The Positive
  • A steady revenue base that has been, in your words, consistent over time
  • A boutique model with built-in capacity margin gives the firm room to absorb a trial without overextending
The Gap
  • Marketing dollars are working without a scoreboard, so spending decisions rely on feel rather than numbers
  • When a large settlement can distort a year’s revenue, only a tracked baseline shows whether the firm’s steady work is growing, flat or shrinking
  • Not knowing what a signed DWI case costs to acquire makes it hard to protect margin as lead flow returns
Knowing what each marketing dollar returns lets you keep the firm the size you want — profitable, steady and in your control.
