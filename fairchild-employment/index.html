Here Is Where Fairchild Employment Law, P.C. Stands Today

Fairchild Employment Law is a San Diego plaintiff-side employment firm led by Jillian Fairchild, handling discrimination, harassment, retaliation, and wrongful termination cases on contingency with an associate attorney, a paralegal, and a virtual intake assistant. Leads are coming in, and the most important finding is that lead flow is not what is holding the firm back — the missing pieces are the operating systems behind those leads: KPIs, intake that runs without the owner, and a repeatable way to move the right cases toward settlement.

Stage 6 is where the firm runs without you having to hold every piece of it — so you can sleep at night, pay every bill without worry, and be present in your free time.

The Law Firm Growth Acceleration Model

We Help Law Firm Owners Get More Profit, More Personal Freedom, And Outstanding Results For Their Clients.

Most law firms try to grow by chasing more leads. But leads alone are not the problem — and leads alone are not the answer. Here is what actually works.

Lead Generation

Leads are to a law firm what gasoline is to a car. Without them, you go nowhere. But a steady, predictable flow of leads changes everything — you get to choose your cases instead of taking whatever comes in.

Intake

But all the leads in the world are worthless without a system to turn them into paying clients. Most firms are losing cases they already paid for simply because their follow-up is slow or inconsistent.

Self-Managing Team

Once clients are coming in, you need a team — otherwise you end up working 90-hour weeks and results suffer. A self-managing team is what gives you the freedom to step back.

Profit Plan

And once the team is in place, most owners look at their bank account and ask: where did all the money go? That is what a profit plan solves — clarity on what you earn, what you keep, and how to grow both.

All four have to work together. If one is missing, you cannot have more profit, more freedom, and outstanding results for your clients at the same time.

Fairchild Employment Law already has marketing and SEO in place, and Jillian told us she does not have a problem with leads coming in. The opportunities below are about capturing a larger share of San Diego employment-law searches over time, not about fixing a broken lead engine.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • Lighthouse performance scores (a Google-developed speed test): Mobile 74/100 — Desktop 84/100. Mobile is solid but leaves room to improve, and most prospects searching after a workplace incident are on a phone
  • No video was observed on the homepage — prospects in a sensitive situation often want to see and hear the attorney before they call
  • No city-specific landing pages were observed on the homepage for the surrounding communities the firm serves
  • Blog activity is real but irregular, with long gaps between posts
The Positive
  • A clear above-the-fold call to action — "Schedule a Case Evaluation" with a phone number — and contact forms at the top and bottom of the page
  • Attorney profiles for Jillian and her associate, plus a testimonials page that includes client reviews
  • Recent blog posts on pregnancy-related workplace questions speak directly to what prospects are searching for
The Gap
  • Without video or city pages, the site relies on text alone to build the trust a prospect needs before calling
  • Irregular publishing gives search engines fewer fresh signals than a steady cadence would
  • Mobile speed improvements would help more of the visitors who are already finding the site
A website that builds trust on its own is one less thing that depends on you personally.

Intake Assessment

Jillian has added a virtual intake assistant, but she told us she still needs to be involved in every step, and the firm does not yet have a consistent way to spot and prioritize the strongest cases early. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
"I want… intake to… run more smoothly without me having to be involved in every step of the process."
The Problem
  • You are still involved in every step of the intake process, so intake slows down when you are in court, in meetings, or off the clock
  • There is not enough sense of urgency in following up, and no consistent way to recognize the strongest cases early
  • No after-hours answering is in place — the plan is to add it eventually, but today evening and weekend calls have no live answer
  • KPIs are not tracked, because you told us you do not have the time to build them
Blind Spot: In a contingency practice, a case that does not settle quickly can take 12 to 18 months to resolve, so every case that is accepted without being properly evaluated at intake can cost the firm a year of work. Better case selection at the front door protects both your time and your cash flow.
The Positive
  • A virtual intake assistant has been in place for roughly three months and you described her as doing an excellent job
  • You told us the leads are "halfway decent" once you start working them — the raw material is there
The Gap
  • When intake depends on you, leads wait — and prospects in a workplace dispute often contact more than one firm
  • Without a defined way to prioritize cases, time can go to cases that are not the best fit while stronger ones wait
  • Without intake numbers, there is no way to see where leads are being lost
Intake that runs smoothly without you is what gives you your evenings back and lets you stop worrying about the business while you are at home.

Profit Plan Assessment

On a contingency model, income arrives when cases settle, which makes timing and case selection the keys to a predictable firm — and Jillian told us the firm does not currently seem to be making money. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: RED
"I mean, also just be able to sleep at night, being able to pay all the bills without having to worry about it."
The Problem
  • The firm is not currently producing the profit and owner pay you want, and the gap is a source of daily stress
  • Costs such as payroll and case expenses like mediations come due on a fixed schedule, while contingency income arrives on the timing of settlements
  • There is no KPI or forecasting system to show when income is expected, or how many strong cases are needed to cover the firm's costs
  • Owner compensation is below the level you want to be earning
Blind Spot: By your own review of recent settlements, the average attorney's fee per settled case is around $53,000. At that size, a handful of well-chosen, well-worked cases changes the entire financial picture — which means the lever is case selection and speed, not necessarily more leads.
The Positive
  • Each settled case carries significant fee value, and you have about 30 active cases in the pipeline
  • You have signed several new cases in recent weeks, which shows the firm can convert when cases come in
The Gap
  • Without a profit plan, you find out how the month went after it is over instead of steering it in advance
  • Without KPIs, it is hard to see which activities move cases to settlement faster
  • Until cash flow is predictable, higher owner pay, bonuses, and raises for the team stay out of reach
A clear profit plan is what turns the firm from a source of worry into a source of the income and peace of mind you described.
