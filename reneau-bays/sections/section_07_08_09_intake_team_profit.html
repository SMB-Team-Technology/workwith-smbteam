Intake Assessment

The firm fields roughly 5 calls a day, but nearly a quarter of the last 356 inbound calls went straight to voicemail unanswered, with no after-hours coverage and no CRM-integrated follow-up process yet in place. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • No CRM or intake tracking software is in place yet — Smith.ai and Clio integration are still just a planned "intro call," not a live system
  • 82 of the last ~356 inbound calls (roughly 23%) went unanswered to voicemail over the last two months
  • No after-hours or weekend coverage exists today — every call after hours goes straight to voicemail
  • RingCentral's ongoing post-merger phone transition is actively described as "an ops nightmare," adding friction on top of an already unstructured intake process
Blind Spot: You may not realize how many of those 82 missed calls were people calling a competitor next, rather than waiting for a callback.
The Positive
  • Courtney, Roger's senior paralegal, is described as an "A-player" already handling meaningful operational load
  • Average handle time of 3:11 on answered calls suggests the calls that do get through are being handled thoughtfully, not rushed
The Gap
  • At roughly 82 missed calls over two months, even a modest close rate on those alone represents real signed cases going to a competitor every single month
  • Every missed after-hours call is a prospect who may already be talking to Lai & Turner or Cannon & Associates by the time Reneau Bays calls back
  • Without a defined follow-up sequence, leads who don't sign on the first call have no structured path back into the pipeline
Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The homepage relies on a phone number and email address rather than a visible contact form — prospects must navigate to a separate /contact page to submit a written inquiry
  • No live chat is present on the site today
  • For prospects who visit after hours specifically to avoid a phone call, there is currently no way to reach the firm without waiting until the next business day

Team Assessment

Roger and Danny both still personally carry the bulk of casework under an "eat what you kill" structure, with Courtney and Madison providing support but no associates yet in place. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"Eat what you kill" — how Roger and Danny described their current partner compensation structure.
The Problem
  • Both partners still personally handle their own casework and client relationships — there is no associate layer yet to absorb overflow work
  • The firm is evaluating adding a Licensed Legal Intern and 1 short-term plus 2-3 long-term associates, but none of that hiring has happened yet
  • Danny's own stated goal is to move from litigating to managing over 3-5 years (50% out of court in 3 years, 75-90% in 5 years) — but there is no team in place yet to hand that casework to
  • Beyond Courtney and Madison, there is no formal leadership or management structure — decisions and casework still run through the two partners directly
Blind Spot: The "eat what you kill" structure that got you here is the same structure that will make it hardest to bring on and retain the associates you both want to add — it rewards individual production, not team building.
The Positive
  • Courtney is already described as an "A-player" handling real operational responsibility, and Madison provides additional team support — the foundation for delegation already exists
  • Danny has already articulated a specific, phased timeline (3-5 years) for shifting out of casework and into management — a level of planning many owners never reach
The Gap
  • Every case Roger and Danny personally handle is time they cannot spend mediating, managing, or with family — the exact trade Danny is trying to make
  • Without associates in place, any growth in leads from better marketing has nowhere to go except onto the same two partners' plates
  • There is no bench in place yet to make the 3-5 year "50% then 75-90% out of court" timeline realistic without a deliberate hiring and training plan
Team is the one lever that turns "more revenue" into "more time for Danny's kids and Roger's next chapter" — not the other way around.

Profit Plan Assessment

Rates are moving up across the board — Danny from $300 to $350/hr, Roger at $350/hr, Courtney potentially higher — but neither partner has a clear plan connecting those increases to actual take-home income. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Combined firm revenue is estimated at $700K–$900K/year, but that figure comes from Dan Bryant's estimate (concurred by the prospect) — not from the firm's own tracked financials
  • Danny's personal income goal of $250K/year (up from $160K–$180K) is clear, but there is no stated plan connecting rate increases or case volume to actually hitting that number
  • The "eat what you kill" shared-expense structure ties each partner's income directly to their own hours billed — the opposite of the profit planning both partners say they want
  • Retainers are moving from roughly $4K to $5K, but there is no visibility into which practice areas (family law, probate, or legacy bankruptcy) are actually driving the firm's profit
Blind Spot: Raising your hourly rate doesn't automatically raise your take-home — without knowing your true cost per case, higher rates can just mean working the same hours for slightly more revenue and the same profit.
The Positive
  • Both partners are already actively raising rates and increasing Danny's retainer structure — proactive moves most firms wait too long to make
  • Danny has already set a specific, numeric personal income target ($250K) rather than a vague "make more money" goal — that clarity makes a real profit plan possible
The Gap
  • Without knowing true profit by practice area, neither partner can tell whether growing probate, family law, or legacy bankruptcy work actually gets Danny closer to $250K faster
  • The "eat what you kill" structure was built for two partners — it has no defined path for how associate-generated revenue gets shared once hiring starts
  • Every month without a profit plan is a month of guessing whether rate increases are actually moving Danny toward his $250K target or just covering rising costs
A real profit plan is what turns "combined firm revenue of $700K–$900K" into Danny's actual $250K — and gives Roger the financial clarity to bring on associates without guessing.
