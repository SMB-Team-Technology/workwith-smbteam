Intake Assessment

Lindsey and Christopher currently split whatever intake happens without a defined process, and August's numbers show the cost of that: call volume actually rose while the close rate fell to roughly 7%, well below the 15-25% benchmark SMB Team sees at this stage. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • July converted roughly 22-33% of calls (9 calls → 2-3 clients); August fell to about 7% (~15 calls → 1 client) — a sharp drop in the same month call volume increased
  • No dedicated intake team member — whichever of the firm's two people is available fields each call
  • No stated follow-up process exists for leads who don't sign on the first call
  • The site advertises a 24/7 answering service, but there is no visibility into how quickly after-hours messages actually get followed up with — a likely factor in the drop-off
Blind Spot: You may not realize how much of the drop in close rate is happening after the phone connects, not before it — more calls came in during August, yet fewer of them became clients.
The Positive
  • Call volume is not the problem — the firm generated more inquiries in August than July; the drop-off is happening after the phone connects, which is fixable without spending another marketing dollar
The Gap
  • At August's ~7% close rate versus July's ~22-33%, the same call volume at July's conversion rate would mean roughly 3-5 clients instead of 1 — without a single additional dollar spent on lead generation
  • Every unconverted call is a case that never happens and revenue that never moves the firm toward hiring an associate
Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • No contact form exists anywhere on the site — every inquiry has to go through a phone call
  • No live chat is present
  • The firm's phone-first, single-number setup means anyone who would rather message first, especially after hours, has no way to do that today

Team Assessment

Lucero, PC's team is just two people — Christopher handling the private caseload and Lindsey on the operations side — with no associate attorney yet in place. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"hire an associate within 24-36 months to delegate court appearances and create a more resilient firm"
The Problem
  • No associate attorney — Christopher personally handles every private case himself
  • Hiring an associate is tied to first hitting a revenue target (5-6 private cases/mo) the firm hasn't reached yet, creating a chicken-and-egg dependency
  • No delegation structure exists for casework, intake, or marketing decisions beyond Lindsey's operations role
  • The only stated timeline is a general 24-36 month window for the associate hire, not a concrete milestone plan
Blind Spot: The associate hire is framed as something that happens after revenue grows — but without a lead generation system that actually works, the revenue growth that's supposed to fund that hire may keep slipping further out.
The Positive
  • Lindsey is already handling the operations side of the business, giving the firm a real starting point for delegation that a true one-person practice would not have
The Gap
  • Every hour Christopher spends personally handling casework is an hour not spent on the strategic work — marketing decisions, hiring, business development — that would actually grow the firm
  • Without a second attorney, Christopher cannot take meaningful time away from the firm without putting his caseload at risk
Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Profit Plan Assessment

Lucero, PC has never calculated an annual revenue figure, and average case value is an estimate rather than a number Christopher tracks directly — while real dollars are confirmed going out to an unprofitable PPC campaign. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: RED
"cover Lindsey's salary and enable hiring an associate"
The Problem
  • Annual revenue has never been calculated or stated — only monthly case-volume figures
  • Average private case value (~$7,500) is a calculated inference, not a number Christopher tracks per case
  • The firm confirmed its PPC campaign is "unprofitable," while separately paying $400/mo for SEO work it isn't receiving
  • No profit plan exists — the stated goal is a case-volume target, not a margin or take-home target
Blind Spot: Christopher may be measuring success in cases closed rather than dollars kept — without knowing the real cost and return of each lead source, it's hard to tell which growth is actually profitable.
The Positive
  • The firm already has a clear, specific target — 5-6 private cases a month, an additional ~$22.5k/mo — which gives a real number to build a profit plan around, rather than a vague "grow the business" goal
The Gap
  • Without knowing the true cost per lead and per case, it's impossible to tell whether the current $5,800/mo marketing spend is a good investment at any volume
  • Growth pursued without a profit plan risks adding casework and hours without adding real take-home income
Until Christopher can see the real cost and return behind each case, he can't be sure that hitting the growth goal actually gets Lindsey her salary and funds the associate hire he wants.
