Intake Assessment

With Christopher and Lindsey handling every call directly and a close rate that fell from roughly 22-33% in July to about 7% in August, intake is the single fastest place to recover lost revenue. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Christopher and Lindsey handle every intake call personally — no dedicated intake staff or process
  • No CRM or intake tracking software was described — nothing to show where leads fall through
  • No after-hours coverage was described — the site's "Call 24/7" promise has no confirmed staffing behind it
  • No defined follow-up sequence exists for leads who do not sign immediately, and close rate has fallen accordingly (22-33% in July to ~7% in August)
Blind Spot: The falling close rate — not a lack of leads — is likely the real reason 5-6 private cases/month feels out of reach; closing August's 15 calls at July's rate instead would already have moved the needle toward that goal.
The Positive
  • July's close rate (roughly 22-33%) was in line with SMB Team's 15-25% benchmark, proving the fundamentals are not broken — something changed between July and August
The Gap
  • At 15 calls in August, converting at the 15-25% benchmark instead of ~7% would have produced 2-4 clients instead of 1 — real revenue left on the table in a single month
  • No intake system exists to catch the difference between a bad month and a fixable process gap
  • Every unconverted lead is money already spent — via PPC or organic effort — with nothing to show for it
Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • No web contact form exists anywhere on the site — intake depends entirely on inbound phone calls
  • No live chat is present, so anyone visiting after hours or hesitant to call has no alternative way to reach the firm
  • The "Schedule a Case Evaluation" section near the footer is the closest thing to a form, but it is not a functioning contact form and sits below the fold

Team Assessment

Christopher is the only attorney in the building, and every private case and every court appearance runs through him personally. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Christopher is the only attorney — every case, every court appearance, and every intake call depends on him directly
  • No associate exists yet; hiring one is the stated 24-36 month goal, not the current reality
  • Lindsey supports intake and operations, but there is no additional staff or delegation structure in place to absorb growth
  • No org structure or accountability system was described beyond the two of them
Blind Spot: The fact that you and Lindsey have made this work so far is actually the risk — it means the firm has never had to build the delegation structure that would let it run without Christopher physically present in court every day.
The Positive
  • Christopher and Lindsey have already built enough of a caseload and reputation, without any additional team, to be actively planning an associate hire — proof the demand exists, just not yet the structure to support it
The Gap
  • Every day Christopher is in court is a day he cannot also build the systems — marketing, intake, hiring — that would let the firm scale
  • Without an associate, private-case capacity is permanently capped at what one attorney can personally handle
  • The stated 24-36 month timeline to hire an associate depends entirely on private-case revenue growing first, which depends on fixing lead gen and intake now
The freedom Christopher described — a firm that runs without him needing to be in court every day — starts with adding the associate this growth is building toward, not after.

Profit Plan Assessment

Lucero, PC tracks growth in private case counts, not profit margins — no annual revenue or take-home figure came up on the call, only the monthly case-count goal. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • No annual revenue or take-home figure was discussed or tracked on the call — only monthly case counts
  • The firm continues paying Convert It $5,800/mo combined despite it being described as unprofitable
  • Growth goals are stated purely as case counts (2-3 to 5-6/mo) with no attached margin or take-home target
  • No fee structure (contingency, hourly, or flat) was described, making it unclear how case value translates to real profit
Blind Spot: Doubling private cases without first fixing the unprofitable PPC spend could mean growing revenue while margins get worse, not better — more cases funding the same leaking bucket.
The Positive
  • The $22.5k/mo revenue goal was tied to a specific purpose — covering Lindsey's salary and funding an associate hire — showing the firm already thinks about growth in terms of what it needs to fund, not just top-line growth
The Gap
  • Without a profit plan, Christopher cannot know whether hitting 5-6 private cases/mo actually produces enough net profit to fund an associate's salary
  • Continued spend on an admittedly unprofitable PPC channel is happening with no clear accounting of the total drag on margin
  • No visibility into per-case profitability makes it impossible to know whether private or court-appointed cases are the better use of Christopher's time
Knowing the real return on every dollar spent is what lets Christopher confidently fund the associate hire and build the resilient, freedom-giving firm he described.
