Intake Assessment

Intake at Eileen Donovan Law is lean and personal: phones and client flow run through a client services director with no dedicated receptionist, and Eileen filters out poor-fit callers herself. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
"…I have a great client services director… I'd like for her to continue with that, and not do a lot of the other stuff that I'm dumping on her, and have the remote person do more of that, and have AI do what can be done."
The Problem
  • There is no dedicated receptionist — phones and first contact fall to a client services director who is already carrying other work
  • You personally decide which callers get a call back, so screening depends on your judgment and your time rather than a defined process
  • Your near-total close rate comes from referrals, which arrive pre-sold — the intake process has not yet been tested on cold leads from search and ads
  • A remote hire for phones is under consideration but not yet in place, so coverage today is whoever is available
Blind Spot: A referral arrives ready to hire you; a stranger from Google is comparing three firms in ten minutes. A close rate "shy of 100%" on referrals will not carry over automatically — response speed and a confident first call decide whether paid and search leads turn into clients.
The Positive
  • A close rate you describe as "shy of 100%" shows that when a prospect reaches you, you win the matter
  • A client services director you trust to manage client flow gives the firm a real foundation to build intake around
The Gap
  • Every missed or delayed call from a search-driven lead is a lost matter — at your average plan value of $5,000, a few lost calls a month adds up quickly
  • Time your team spends on phones and admin is time not spent on clients, and time you do not get back
  • Without a plan for cold leads, new marketing would be pouring prospects into a first call the firm has not yet built for them
Intake that runs without you is how the time you have already paid for finally comes back.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • There is no contact form on the homepage; the form lives on a separate Contact page that visitors have to find
  • No phone number appears on the homepage, so a visitor on a phone cannot tap to call
  • A clear "Schedule an Appointment" button sits above the fold — a good start, but the path after the click has to be short and obvious

Team Assessment

Eileen leads a small, capable team — two staff and a drafter — but she is still the person who absorbs what the team cannot, which is why the time she has invested in building the firm has not come back to her. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"I've spent the money to get my time back and I don't have it back."
The Problem
  • The team is small and there is no operating layer between you and the day-to-day, so overflow work lands on you
  • Client flow leans heavily on one key team member, who is also absorbing other admin tasks
  • There is no dedicated admin or phone role yet, and no defined split between what people handle and what technology can handle
  • No one yet owns operations as a role, so you remain the default manager of the firm's day-to-day
Blind Spot: Adding a remote hire without clear, repeatable processes can simply move the overflow somewhere else. The groundwork is deciding which tasks belong to people, which can be handled by AI, and which can be removed altogether.
The Positive
  • You already have a client services director you trust, and a lean team with low overhead
  • You are open to delegating — a remote hire and AI support are both on the table — which is exactly the mindset a self-managing firm needs
The Gap
  • Each phone call and admin task you absorb is time away from client work, and time you do not get to spend as you choose
  • Your goal of growing beyond six to eight matters a month puts more volume on the same few people
  • The longer-term opportunity to take over files from retiring peers needs a team that can absorb volume without routing it through you
A team that carries the day-to-day is what turns a firm you run into a firm that runs for you.

Profit Plan Assessment

Eileen has made deliberate financial choices — keeping owner pay modest and cash in the operating account — and is already revisiting her fees for January 1. What is missing is a clear view of what each new client costs to win and what the firm needs to net to deliver the life she wants. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"This is not a poor me. This is a, that was my business model before, and I need to switch it up."
The Problem
  • Owner pay was set low on purpose, and you have said the model behind it needs to change as you "scale and operate differently"
  • Fees are being rebuilt for January 1; with a close rate this high, pricing is worth testing before more volume is added
  • Because every client today is a referral, there is no baseline for what it costs to acquire a new matter through marketing
  • A recurring-revenue membership plan has a roadmap but has not yet been implemented, so each client relationship is a one-time engagement
Blind Spot: With most costs fixed — your software and office — each additional matter reaches the bottom line faster than in most firms. That is only an advantage if cost per acquired matter is tracked from the first day ads start.
The Positive
  • Your financial choices have been intentional, and you have cash in the operating account to invest from
  • A low fixed-cost base and a near-total close rate mean new matters can be very profitable once a steady source of them exists
The Gap
  • Without an acquisition-cost baseline, marketing budgets get set by guesswork instead of math
  • Without recurring revenue, income resets each month and depends on the next batch of referrals
  • Your take-home and the firm's profit are still tied to six to eight matters a month, which is the ceiling a referral-only model sets
A profit plan you design — instead of one you discover — is what lets you scale and operate differently on your own terms.
