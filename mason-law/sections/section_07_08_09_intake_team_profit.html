Intake Assessment

Mason Law's intake has real strengths — a paid initial consultation and a client care team — but no one answers the phone after hours, even though the firm's ads and listings are live around the clock. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
"I agree that we could possibly do some things there that are better."
The Problem
  • There is no after-hours call answering today, and the firm's earlier answering service was dropped once the firm grew
  • Ads run at all times, but the website lists business hours of Monday to Friday, 8:30 AM to 5:00 PM, with weekends closed
  • How many calls are being missed — abandoned, very short or sent to voicemail — has not yet been measured
Blind Spot: A person searching for an estate planning or probate attorney at 9 PM — often right after a death in the family — is likely to call the first firm that answers. Competitors including The Harris Law Firm, Wiley Reynolds and Peakstone Law Group show "Open 24 hours" on Google.
The Positive
  • A paid initial consultation screens out low-intent callers, and leads from Google and ads close at what the owner calls an extremely high rate
  • The client care team is strong enough to staff satellite offices, which gives the firm a ready-made base for new locations
  • The owner is open to improving after-hours coverage and plans to review 90 days of phone-system data to size the gap
The Gap
  • Every after-hours call from a paid ad or Local Services Ad that goes unanswered is a lead the firm paid for and handed to a 24-hour competitor
  • The more offices the firm opens, the more calls arrive outside any single office's hours
  • Without call data, the cost of missed calls stays invisible and cannot be weighed against the cost of covering them
When every call is answered the Mason Law way, clients meet the brand — not any one person — from the first ring.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The hero of the homepage carries a "Schedule A Consultation" button and a tap-to-call phone number — a strong first step for mobile visitors
  • The contact form (name, phone, email, message) sits lower on the homepage under "Contact Mason Law to Start Your Estate Plan," so a visitor has to scroll to find it
  • There is no chat widget on the site, and no way to reach a person from it outside the Monday–Friday office hours shown on the page

These findings are also referenced in the Website Audit section.

Team Assessment

Mason Law has already brought marketing in-house under a Director of Marketing and has a client care team that can staff new offices, but key decisions are still concentrated around its founder at the exact moment the firm is adding locations. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"I don't want to brand myself or an attorney. I want to brand us."
The Problem
  • Major decisions are shared between the owner and one other leader, with no wider leadership layer visible yet
  • A third office lease is signed and a fourth location is being considered — each one adds a place that needs consistent leadership
  • The goal of ten locations raises the question of who runs the offices day to day once they are open
  • A team is still being built around marketing, with additional hires for video, rather than a finished structure
Blind Spot: The reason a firm can say "it's our brand, not mine" is not a slogan — it is a set of documented processes and leaders who can run an office without the founder in the room. Building that layer is the work still ahead.
The Positive
  • A Director of Marketing leads an in-house team, and the owner wants to give that leader more people to build with
  • The client care team can staff satellite offices, so new locations do not start from scratch
  • The firm has an operating track record — Castle Rock grew into a million-dollar branch in 15 months
The Gap
  • Without a leadership layer, every new office can add decisions that route back to the founder instead of taking them off his plate
  • A brand-first firm needs the same standards in every office, which takes written processes and trained leaders — not just good people
  • The owner's goal that nothing points back to him is only reachable if the firm can run when he is not the one deciding
A firm that is truly "us" is one where leaders and systems carry every office — and the founder's name is no longer what holds it together.

Profit Plan Assessment

Paul knows the economics of a single new office cold, but expanding toward ten locations turns that office-level math into a firm-wide profit question that did not come up on the call. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"Literally, one to two clients a month pays for itself."
The Problem
  • The office-level payback math is clear, but a firm-wide view of profit by location was not discussed on the call
  • Each new office carries a build-out and a monthly lease before the first client arrives, and a third lease is already signed
  • The expansion goal of ten locations multiplies those commitments without a stated financial model for the whole network
  • Marketing runs in-house and the marketing budget was not discussed, so the cost to sign a client by channel is not yet part of the picture
Blind Spot: A payback of one to two clients a month is a strong rule of thumb for one office. As locations multiply, shared costs — marketing, client care and leadership time — are what decide how much profit actually reaches the owner.
The Positive
  • The owner knows the cost and the payback of a new satellite office, and has a clear rule of thumb for when it pays for itself
  • Castle Rock proved the model — it grew into a million-dollar branch in 15 months
The Gap
  • Without a profit view by office, it is hard to decide where the fourth, fifth or tenth location earns its place before a lease is signed
  • Without cost per signed client by channel, in-house marketing spend cannot be weighed against what each office returns
  • Growth measured only in locations can hide whether each new office adds to what the owner keeps
Ten offices only build the firm Paul wants if each one adds profit, not just another door to open.
