Lead Generation

Cooper & Cooper has built a strong digital foundation: a recently redesigned website, an actively updated blog, and a paid advertising program run through a marketing vendor. The conversation centered on making sure every marketing dollar turns into retained clients, and on how the firm shows up in AI search, where you already check your own visibility.

Website Audit

"We just, we just redid our website."
The Problem
  • The contact form sits in the footer under "Get In Touch," not above the fold, so a visitor who is not ready to call has no quick way to reach out
  • Two different phone numbers appear on the site, one on the main consultation button and another in the header, which makes it harder to see which marketing source produced a call
  • Mobile responsiveness and page speed were not verified in this review, so they should be confirmed on the new site
Blind Spot: A newly redesigned site can look excellent and still not be wired to show which channel produced each call and form fill. Without that, ad spend can grow while it stays unclear what is actually working.
The Positive
  • Individual bio pages for each attorney build trust before a prospect ever calls
  • More than 20 service pages, plus county and city pages across Clay, Duval, St. Johns, and Nassau counties, give the site a strong local content base
  • The blog is actively updated, with a new post as recent as early November, and includes video content
  • A clear "Schedule a Free Consultation" button and phone link appear above the fold
The Gap
  • Visitors who prefer to write instead of call have only a footer form, which can cost consultations the site already earned
  • Split phone numbers make call attribution harder, which weakens every decision about where to put the next marketing dollar
  • A redesign is the moment rankings are most at risk, so confirming that search and AI-answer visibility held up on the new site matters before adding more spend
A website that converts every visitor and tells you exactly where they came from is what lets growth keep compounding without you chasing it.

Local SEO

The Problem
  • Google review totals could not be confirmed against live Google data, and the figures on the firm's own site and in third-party listings do not match, so the firm's true review standing needs to be verified and kept consistent
  • Some directory listings appear to show a different street number and suite than the address on the firm's website, which can dilute local ranking signals
  • Local map-pack positions for core family law and probate searches were not captured in this review, so the firm's current standing against nearby competitors is still unmeasured
Blind Spot: Probate is the practice area you are growing, but it is also the one where established Orange Park firms are already visible in local listings. Winning it takes dedicated probate content and review momentum, not just family law strength carried over.
The Positive
  • A Google profile appears to be established, and listings exist on Avvo, Justia, Yelp, and MapQuest
  • Location pages for Clay County and nearby cities already target the geographies you serve
The Gap
  • Inconsistent name, address, and phone details across directories make it harder for Google to trust and rank one clear business listing
  • Without a benchmark of local-pack position by practice area, there is no way to measure whether marketing is closing the gap on local competitors

Owning the local map results in Fleming Island and Orange Park is how the firm keeps getting found while the founders focus on running it.

Google Ads

Ad Presence
  • On the call, the firm described paying a marketing vendor on top of its ad spend, with Google paid search discussed as the channel producing most retained clients
  • Live ad headlines and descriptions for family law and probate searches were not captured in this review, so ad copy and keyword coverage should be audited at onboarding
Competitor Ad Landscape
  • Kenny Leigh & Associates — a Fleming Island family law competitor whose ad activity should be benchmarked first, since it targets the same local divorce and custody searches
  • Sermons Law, PLLC — an Orange Park probate competitor whose ad presence should be benchmarked as probate becomes a larger share of your work
The Opportunity
  • Divorce, child custody, and family law searches in Fleming Island, Orange Park, and Clay County, matched to dedicated landing pages
  • Probate and guardianship searches in Orange Park and Clay County as that practice grows
  • Neighboring-area searches such as Green Cove Springs, Middleburg, Palatka, St. Johns County, and Jacksonville, where the site already has location content
  • Urgency-driven searches such as contested divorce and emergency custody, which signal a prospect who is ready to hire now
Blind Spot: When calls and form fills from each campaign are not tracked separately, spend can rise while retained clients per month stay flat. Tying each campaign to signed cases, not just leads, is the first thing to verify.

Local Service Ads

LSA Presence

  • Lead quality from LSA was described as mixed, which makes how leads are screened and scored a key question for this channel
Competitor LSA Landscape
  • Kenny Leigh & Associates — LSA placement and review base were not captured in this review and should be checked for family law searches in Fleming Island
  • Berg Bryant Elder Law Group, PLLC — LSA placement was not captured in this review and should be checked for probate and elder law searches in Orange Park
The Opportunity
  • A strong, verified review base is the lever that lifts LSA ranking and trust, so review generation and LSA performance should be managed together
  • LSAs appear above everything else on Google — above paid ads, above the map pack, above all organic results — and you only pay when a real prospect calls you directly.
  • Disputing low-quality leads and tuning the service areas and practice categories can turn LSA from a volume channel into a retained-client channel
