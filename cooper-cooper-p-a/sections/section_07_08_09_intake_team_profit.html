Intake Assessment

Cooper & Cooper has an intake process in place, but the consultation itself still leans on a founder, because prospects ask for an attorney by name. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
"when I do the consults, nine times out of ten, people are like, I want you to be my attorney… I'm taking more of the cases… And then I'm back into the case work."
The Problem
  • Consultations still route to a founder because prospects ask for an attorney by name, which pulls that founder back into casework
  • A non-attorney sales role is one of your biggest goals, but there is no proven playbook for hiring, training, or measuring one yet
  • The call described roughly two to three of every ten scheduled consultations being retained, against a goal of five in ten
  • Intake and sales are not yet a defined process a trained team member can own from first call to signed retainer
Blind Spot: Prospects asking for an attorney by name is a compliment and a bottleneck at the same time. As long as the firm's reputation rests on one person's consult, every new lead adds to that person's calendar instead of to the firm's capacity.
The Positive
  • You recognize the problem and have named the non-attorney sales role as a priority before it becomes a crisis
  • Prospects clearly trust the firm, so the consultation is a strong close when it happens
The Gap
  • Every consultation that does not convert is marketing spend that never became a case, so the move from two or three in ten toward five in ten is the fastest return available
  • Founder time spent closing consultations is time not spent on the casework and leadership only the two of you can do
  • Without a trained sales process, adding more leads adds more consultations for a founder to take, not more retained clients per founder hour
A trained intake and sales process is how Charlean gets out of the consult room and back to the life she described, without the firm losing a single client.

Team Assessment

Cooper & Cooper has grown to about eleven people, including six attorneys, with another attorney joining, but the two founders still sit at the center of both the casework and the business. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"right now where we are at, we're not really at a place where I feel like our system is really broken yet, but I know that that's coming… I want to be ahead of the game there so that way I don't get bogged down when it happens"
The Problem
  • One founder manages the litigation team while still carrying a personal caseload, so management and casework compete for the same hours
  • The other founder runs the business side and is the one handling the operational decisions that come with each new hire and each new space
  • The firm is adding attorneys and expanding its practice areas, and the call did not point to a documented operating system for how work, training, and accountability flow
  • The non-attorney sales role you want does not exist yet, so consultation work cannot move off the founders' calendars
Blind Spot: The firm is not broken, which is exactly why this is the right time to build. Systems put in place before the strain arrives cost far less than systems built while both founders are already stretched.
The Positive
  • A team of about eleven, with six attorneys and a support staff, is a real foundation for a firm that can run without constant founder involvement
  • You are thinking about structure before problems force the issue, which is rare in a fast-growing firm
The Gap
  • Each new attorney adds capacity, but without defined management layers it also adds more people looking to a founder for answers
  • Founders who manage and litigate at the same time have no protected time to work on the firm instead of in it
  • Expanding across Northeast Florida and beyond is not possible while every new location and attorney depends on the same two people
A self-managing team is what lets the firm keep growing while you spend your time on the life you are building it for.

Profit Plan Assessment

Cooper & Cooper is growing quickly, adding attorneys, space, and equipment, but the call did not surface a profit target or a clear view of what each retained client returns on marketing spend. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"when I see the numbers going up, when I see, okay, I'm having to get a new office every five seconds, I'm having to get more, you know, I'm adding computers… that's the stuff that drives me."
The Problem
  • The growth you described shows up as headcount, space, and equipment, which are all costs, and no profit target came up on the call to balance them
  • No cost per retained client by channel, and no marketing return benchmark, came up on the call
  • The right test for marketing is whether retained clients per month rise, and that test needs numbers behind it
  • Any change of marketing partner has to protect cash flow, since you want business to stay consistent through a switchover
Blind Spot: A transition between marketing providers can create a short gap before new campaigns reach full strength. Planning that transition around cash flow, instead of hoping it goes smoothly, protects the firm during the change.
The Positive
  • Momentum is real: the firm is hiring attorneys and expanding its footprint at a pace most young firms never reach
  • You are asking hard questions about proof and return before committing, which is the mindset a profit plan is built on
The Gap
  • Without a profit target, it is hard to tell whether the next hire, office, or ad dollar is adding take-home or only adding volume
  • Without cost per retained client by channel, marketing decisions rest on activity instead of on what actually produces signed cases
  • Knowing the numbers is what turns "the numbers going up" into wealth the two of you can actually enjoy
A clear profit plan is how growth becomes proof of success you can see, and freedom you can feel.
