Here Is Where Shandon Phan Law Stands Today

Shandon Phan is the only attorney at a Houston estate planning practice that serves middle-class immigrant families, with the Vietnamese community at its core, and he has just relaunched the firm online under a new brand. The most important finding: his clients have always trusted him personally, but he has no system that turns that trust into a steady flow of new clients without him personally handling every conversation.

Stage 6 is where the firm carries institutional value beyond your personal name and runs on systems instead of your own low-level work — and for you, the road there starts with a dependable intake process and one streamlined set of tools.

Where You Are Today

Stage 3: Solo Practitioner
  • You are the only attorney, supported by a small team and outside vendors, and the firm converts best when you personally engage each lead
  • Referrals and your Vietnamese radio authority built the client base, but the radio show has been paused and Google Ads have produced only a handful of leads
  • Practice management, drafting, and marketing tools are spread across several vendors, and your automation is not yet functional
  • Every new hire takes six months to two years to train, so growth has depended on your own hours, and you have already scaled the old office down

Where You Need To Be

Stage 6: Law Firm Owner
  • The firm runs without you
  • Your income is no longer tied to your personal hours
  • Your time is yours to spend however you choose
  • This is where true freedom and true wealth become possible
The gap between a practice built on your personal name and a firm that can last without it is closeable, and it starts with a plan to put intake and your tools on a system rather than on your own time.
Overall Growth Stage Assessment
Shandon Phan Law
Shandon Phan Law is a Stage 3 Solo Practitioner: one attorney with a loyal community following, a brand-new website, and a small team that is still being built. The most urgent gap is intake, because leads convert well when Shandon engages them directly and much less well when they are handled by staff or vendors without a defined process. Until that is fixed, adding more marketing will not move the firm toward the system-driven, lasting firm he described wanting to build.
