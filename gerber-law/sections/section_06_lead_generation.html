Lead Generation

Gerber Law has built a 29-year reputation across Venice and Sarasota almost entirely on referrals — 85-90% of new cases, per Maria's own numbers — while two decades of paid advertising across multiple vendors produced no measurable link between spend and case volume. The firm already owns an extensive SEO foundation, but thin review volume across its two offices is the single biggest thing standing between that investment and a page-one ranking.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • No video content appears on the homepage — video testimonials exist on a separate /reviews/ page, but a first-time visitor doesn't see them without digging
  • PageSpeed scores: Mobile not observed — Desktop not observed (Google's public PageSpeed API was rate-limited on every attempt during this research session; a manual live check is recommended before your next conversation)
  • The site's practice-area pages aren't yet structured for AI-search answer engines (Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT-style results) — the exact channel Maria flagged as reshaping how clients find firms
Blind Spot: Because Maria's own strategic instinct is that "AI search is displacing websites," the real blind spot isn't the website itself — it's that the site's content isn't yet structured in the clear, direct-answer format AI Overviews and ChatGPT-style search pull from. A great site that isn't AI-readable is still invisible in the exact channel she's most worried about.
The Positive
  • Fully mobile-responsive with a modern, actively maintained design — no rebuild is needed here
  • An active, current blog (six posts published August 13, 2026 alone) plus 30+ existing geo-targeted and practice-area landing pages spanning Venice, Sarasota, Bradenton, Port Charlotte, Punta Gorda, Lakewood Ranch, Wellen Park, and Nokomis — a real head start most 29-year-old practices don't have
The Gap
  • A well-built site with no authority behind it still won't outrank a newer competitor with a stronger review presence — the site isn't the constraint, the reputation signal feeding it is
  • Every month spent without AI-search-optimized content is a month ceded to whichever tri-county competitor structures their content for AI Overviews first
  • The Port Charlotte wrongful death page exists but didn't surface in this session's search testing — proof that page coverage alone doesn't guarantee visibility
A modern website built on 29 years of earned trust should be winning the tri-county market outright — closing the authority gap is what turns that trust into the $3M firm Maria is building.

Local SEO

The Problem
  • Gerber Law's own review volume — while unconfirmed pending a live Google check — appears thin for a practice with 29 years of tenure, split across two separate office listings (Venice and Sarasota) that dilute the signal further
  • The firm did not surface in organic search results for "wrongful death attorney Port Charlotte FL" despite having a dedicated landing page for that exact market — a visibility gap in a high-value case type
  • Reviews are split across two separate Yelp profiles in addition to the two office listings, further fragmenting the review signal each individual listing can build
Blind Spot: Splitting review volume across two offices doesn't just look thinner online — it actively works against Gerber Law in Google's local ranking algorithm, which favors listings with higher review counts and velocity. A unified push toward the review threshold identified on your discovery call would compound the benefit across both offices, not just add to it.
The Positive

The Gap
  • Far fewer reviews than several named competitors show in this market — directionally significant, though exact figures should be verified live before your next conversation
  • Extensive geo-page coverage exists but isn't yet converting into local pack visibility for every practice area and city it targets
David Harris Law — a hyper-local Venice competitor whose review base appears meaningfully larger and higher-rated than Gerber Law's current standing, competing directly for the same home-market searches.
Owning the tri-county conversation Maria wants to own starts with closing the one gap standing between her existing SEO investment and page-one visibility: reviews.

Local Service Ads

LSA Presence
  • No LSA / Google Screened presence was observed for Gerber Law — the live LSA unit itself could not be rendered this session, so this should be read as an open opportunity, not a confirmed absence
Competitor LSA Landscape
  • Goldman Babboni Fernandez Murphy & Walsh and David Harris Law — LSA presence could not be confirmed or ruled out this session; a live spot-check is recommended before your next conversation
The Opportunity
  • LSA is pay-per-lead, not pay-per-click — a materially lower-risk way to test paid acquisition than traditional search ads, which fits the budget caution raised on your discovery call
  • LSAs appear above everything else on Google — above paid ads, above the map pack, above all organic results — and you only pay when a real prospect calls you directly
  • Because LSA ranking rewards review volume and responsiveness, closing the review gap identified in Local SEO would directly strengthen any future LSA presence — the two initiatives compound
Blind Spot: Google Screened status functions as a trust badge prospects see before ads even load — for a firm whose entire brand is trust built over 29 years, this is a natural extension of Gerber Law's existing strength, not a new positioning to build from scratch.
