Here Is Where Truce Resolutions Stands Today

Tamara Arnold launched Truce Resolutions in Solvang, California about a year ago and now focuses on flat-fee estate planning under her Life & Legacy Planning brand, which grew out of her start in mediation. Every client so far has come from her own presentations, referrals and word of mouth — she has spent nothing on marketing, and by her own account she signs the prospects who sit down with her, but too few of them are walking through the door.

Stage 6 is the stage where you step in and out of the firm — leading the presentations and the high-value client meetings while your team handles everything else — and it is exactly where we are taking you.

Where You Are Today

Stage 3: Solo Practitioner
  • Presentations, referrals and word of mouth are the only sources of new clients — no repeatable system is bringing in consultations
  • No money has been spent on paid marketing, and Google Ads and Local Service Ads have not been tried
  • The team is effectively Tamara alone, with one part-time helper just beginning to be trained
  • Revenue is on track for roughly $200K–$250K this year, with a goal of $250K–$300K next year

Where You Need To Be

Stage 6: Law Firm Owner
  • The firm runs without you
  • Your income is no longer tied to your personal hours
  • Your time is yours to spend however you choose
  • This is where true freedom and true wealth become possible
The gap between where you are and a firm that runs without you is not as wide as it feels — it starts with a steady flow of the right people through the door and a plan that makes a small budget count.

The Law Firm Growth Acceleration Model

We Help Law Firm Owners Get More Profit, More Personal Freedom, And Outstanding Results For Their Clients.

Most law firms try to grow by chasing more leads. But leads alone are not the problem — and leads alone are not the answer. Here is what actually works.

Lead Generation

Leads are to a law firm what gasoline is to a car. Without them, you go nowhere. But a steady, predictable flow of leads changes everything — you get to choose your cases instead of taking whatever comes in.

Intake

But all the leads in the world are worthless without a system to turn them into paying clients. Most firms are losing cases they already paid for simply because their follow-up is slow or inconsistent.

Self-Managing Team

Once clients are coming in, you need a team — otherwise you end up working 90-hour weeks and results suffer. A self-managing team is what gives you the freedom to step back.

Profit Plan

And once the team is in place, most owners look at their bank account and ask: where did all the money go? That is what a profit plan solves — clarity on what you earn, what you keep, and how to grow both.

All four have to work together. If one is missing, you cannot have more profit, more freedom, and outstanding results for your clients at the same time.

Website Audit

"I don't want to spend more money on my website, to be honest."
The Problem
  • The homepage headline and its two top buttons lean on life-transition and Family Conflict & Divorce language, while estate planning is the firm's actual focus
  • Lighthouse performance scores: Mobile 48/100 — Desktop 97/100. On mobile, the main content takes about 9.1 seconds to load
  • No "book a consultation" call to action was observed above the fold, and the contact form sits mid-page
  • No pages aimed at Solvang or the Santa Ynez Valley were observed in the site navigation
Blind Spot: Your firm name was created when you were only doing mediation. Search engines and AI tools reading the site may see a mediation and divorce firm rather than an estate planning firm.
The Positive
  • Desktop performance is excellent, and the site already includes an attorney bio, an embedded video and a blog
  • Tamara can maintain the site herself in GoHighLevel, so improvements can be tweaks rather than a rebuild
The Gap
  • A mobile load time over nine seconds means visitors on phones can leave before they read a word
  • Without a clear consultation button, an interested visitor has to hunt for the next step
  • Without local pages, the site gives Google little reason to connect it to Solvang estate planning searches
A few targeted tweaks — not a rebuild — can make the website work harder so your presentations and referrals turn into booked consultations.

Intake Assessment

Tamara is the intake process today: consultations, follow-up and the close all run through her, with a part-time helper just starting to reach out to clients afterward. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: RED
"When they're in front of me, I convert… It's just getting them in the door."
The Problem
  • Intake and follow-up depend on you personally — a part-time helper is only beginning to reach out to clients after meetings
  • No after-hours inquiry coverage was observed, and the AI phone agent you tried was turned off because it was not accurate
  • The website has no "book a consultation" button above the fold, and its contact form sits mid-page
  • Presentations and workshops create interest, but a defined path from attendee to booked consultation was not described on the call
Blind Spot: With a close rate this strong, intake is not your constraint — volume is. That also means every missed call or unanswered web inquiry costs you a client you would likely have signed.
The Positive
  • By your own account, prospects who sit down with you convert
  • A flat-fee model gives families a clear price up front, which makes the decision easier
The Gap
  • At 3–4 new clients a month and roughly $6,000 per flat-fee client, each missed or slow-to-answer inquiry can cost about $6,000
  • Reaching $250K takes about 6–7 clients a month, so booking more consultations matters more than improving a close rate that is already high
  • Until the process is repeatable, intake stays tied to your calendar and limits your ability to step away
A simple intake process means the next consultation is booked and followed up whether you are in the room or not.

Team Assessment

Truce Resolutions is a one-attorney firm with a first part-time helper in training, and Tamara's goal is a team of three to five. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
"I can step in and out and just do the presentations and the meetings with high value clients and then offload everything else to staff."
The Problem
  • Tamara is effectively the whole firm: attorney, presenter, marketer, client contact and manager
  • The first team member is part-time and just beginning to be trained
  • Nobody owns marketing — a marketing VA has been considered, but no one is in place
  • Staff costs have to follow client volume, so the lead flow gap and the team gap are linked
Blind Spot: Feeling like you can never turn it off is a structure problem, not a discipline problem — and it tends to get worse as client volume grows unless roles are built first.
The Positive
  • A clear vision of the team you want: junior associates, a paralegal and a caseworker
  • You have already begun delegating client follow-up and are deliberately sizing the firm — a seven-figure practice without building an empire
The Gap
  • Every presentation, consultation and follow-up still needs you, which caps how many families the firm can serve
  • Marketing, intake and client work all compete for the same calendar
  • Until roles and hand-offs are defined, a first hire may add management work instead of freedom
A team that handles the work behind the meetings is what lets you be the face of the firm without being the whole firm.

Profit Plan Assessment

Truce Resolutions is projecting roughly $200K–$250K in flat-fee estate planning revenue in its first full year, with $250K–$300K targeted next year and a seven-figure firm as the longer-term goal. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"I can probably throw $2,000 to $3,000 a month, I think."
The Problem
  • The marketing budget is limited, so there is little room for trial and error
  • With no marketing run yet, there is no cost-per-client data to guide where the first dollars should go
  • Revenue rides on 3–4 new clients a month, so a few signings either way swing the whole month
  • Existing monthly commitments already take a large share of the budget available for growth
Blind Spot: At roughly $6,000 per flat-fee client, one extra signed client a month adds about $72,000 a year — so a modest, well-tracked marketing budget can pay back quickly if it brings in even a few more consultations.
The Positive
  • Flat-fee pricing with an average of about $6,000 per client makes revenue per client predictable
  • A clear multi-year revenue path is already defined, from $250K–$300K next year toward a seven-figure firm
The Gap
  • Hitting $250K takes roughly 6–7 new clients a month, about double today's pace
  • Without a plan tied to cost per client, a small budget risks being spread across tactics that do not move the number
  • Net profit and acquisition cost have not yet been measured, so growth decisions rest on instinct
A profit plan gives you the numbers to decide, on your own terms, whether to keep growing or ease off the gas.
