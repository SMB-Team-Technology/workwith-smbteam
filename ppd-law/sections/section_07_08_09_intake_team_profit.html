Intake Assessment

New files arrive at PPD Law at an inconsistent 3–8 a month, and current capacity is already full — so the challenge is handling each inquiry without everything flowing through you. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • New files arrive in an uneven 3–8 a month, which makes it hard to plan staffing and time
  • With capacity full, every new inquiry competes with existing client work for the same hours
  • No documented intake process — who answers, how inquiries are screened, how follow-up happens — was described on the call
  • Published office hours are Monday to Friday, 9 to 5, with weekends closed, and no after-hours way to reach the firm was observed
Blind Spot: When a firm is at full capacity, the inquiries that slip through are rarely the ones you notice — they are the immigration or business prospects who call once, get no quick answer, and move on to the next firm.
The Positive
  • Demand is healthy — the firm is at full capacity, which means the market already trusts PPD Law
  • Client reviews praise responsiveness and communication, with comments like "Communication was clear and they took the time to answer all my questions"
The Gap
  • Without a repeatable intake process, extra demand lands on the owner by default — which makes growth to $1M more work rather than more freedom
  • A strong client-experience reputation depends on quick, clear first contact, and that is hard to protect at full capacity
  • Inconsistent inflow combined with full capacity makes it difficult to decide which matters to take and which to refer
A defined intake process means new matters are handled without you, so growth stops costing you your time.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The homepage "Get Started" button leads to a separate Contact page — a clear first step, though not a form a visitor can complete on the homepage itself
  • The contact form asks for first name, last name, email, phone, a services dropdown, and a message, which gives the team useful context but adds steps for a mobile visitor
  • No live chat was observed on the site, and the website lists office hours only on weekdays

Team Assessment

PPD Law runs on Premmal plus three support staff, and the firm is entirely dependent on that one person — if you took a vacation, operations would stop. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • The firm is entirely dependent on you — operations would stop if you took a vacation
  • Training newer staff consumes roughly 20% of your day — about one full workday every week
  • Trained staff are hard to find, so hiring means recent graduates with less experience who need hands-on development
  • The roles of the three support staff and who owns decisions when you are unavailable were not defined on the call
Blind Spot: A heavy training load is usually a sign that the work lives in your head rather than in written systems — fix that and newer team members can produce solid first drafts on their own, and training stops consuming a day a week.
The Positive
  • You have a team in place and have already named the shift you want to make — from Small Business Manager to Wealth Multiplier
  • You are willing to hire and develop newer talent, which is the right long-term pipeline once systems exist to support it
The Gap
  • Roughly a day a week spent training is time not spent on higher-value work or on the other ventures you want to pursue
  • A firm that stops when the owner steps away cannot be sold, scaled, or even paused without losing momentum
  • Doubling to $1M with the same dependency would double the load on you, not the freedom you are working toward
A team that can run without you is what turns a busy practice into the firm that gives you your time back.

Profit Plan Assessment

PPD Law is generating roughly $500K a year and has set a clear goal of reaching $1M by 2027. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • A profit plan or margin target for reaching $1M was not described on the call
  • Fees mix flat-fee work with hourly billing, so it is hard to know which matters earn the most per hour of your time without tracking it
  • About 20% of your day goes to training — a cost that is invisible on a financial statement but real in lost capacity
  • Growth plans are currently built on revenue, with no visibility described into what each additional dollar actually leaves for the owner
Blind Spot: When work is billed at a flat fee, any time spent correcting or redoing work comes straight out of your margin — which makes a well-trained team and consistent systems a profit lever, not just an operations goal.
The Positive
  • A clear, specific revenue goal — $1M by 2027 — gives the firm something concrete to plan against
  • A mixed fee structure gives the firm predictable flat-fee revenue alongside hourly work on complex matters
The Gap
  • Without a profit plan, doubling revenue can add staff, complexity, and hours without a matching increase in what the owner keeps
  • Flat-fee work is only as profitable as the time it takes, so unmanaged training and rework quietly erode returns
  • You want to grow the firm without giving up sustainable work hours — and that requires knowing the numbers before scaling
A profit plan makes the extra revenue buy you time and wealth, not just a bigger workload.
