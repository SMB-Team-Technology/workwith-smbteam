Intake Assessment

With no live chat, a below-the-fold contact form, and every incoming lead defaulting to Angel personally, intake is the fastest fix available before another marketing dollar is spent. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Attorney personally handles every incoming lead across two offices — no delegation in place
  • Above-fold conversion path is phone-call only; the contact form sits below the fold
  • No live chat to capture visitors who browse after hours or won't call a stranger about a will
  • No defined follow-up sequence was described for leads who don't sign immediately
Blind Spot: You may not realize how many people are researching estate planning at 9pm after putting the kids to bed — exactly when your only above-fold option is a phone call to an office that's closed.
The Positive
  • Every lead who does reach Angel gets attorney-level attention immediately, not a call center screen — a real trust advantage once volume grows enough to need a system behind it
The Gap
  • Every after-hours visitor who won't call is revenue this firm never collects
  • Without a defined intake process, leads from any new marketing engagement will hit the same bottleneck limiting leads today
  • Two offices funneling into one person doesn't scale — the busier the pipeline gets, the more leads slip through
Fixing intake before layering on new marketing spend is what makes the shift from operations to CEO possible sooner, not later.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • No live chat widget is present on the site

Team Assessment

Team size and structure were not detailed on the call beyond Angel running both offices herself, and the firm just added a second location with no additional staff described to support it. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • No dedicated ops, marketing, or intake role was mentioned on the call
  • Angel is the operational hub for both the established Georgia office and the brand-new Alabama office
  • No leadership layer exists yet to run day-to-day work while Angel steps back
  • Scaling into a second state without added staff increases the load on a structure that already runs through one person
Blind Spot: The fact that you've made it to $250K largely running things yourself is actually the risk — it's proof the firm depends on you being everywhere at once, and that dependency gets more expensive, not less, the more it grows.
The Positive
  • Angel has already successfully expanded to a second office, showing the ambition and execution to grow beyond a single-location practice
The Gap
  • Every hour Angel spends on tasks a team member could own is an hour not spent on the CEO-level work she said she wants
  • Without a team, growth to $500K just means more of the same personal load, not more freedom
  • The new Alabama office will compete for the same limited attention as the established Georgia office
Building a team that can run daily operations is what turns "growing the firm" into "gaining a life back" — which is the whole point of the goal you described.

Profit Plan Assessment

No profitability figures, margins, or cost-per-acquisition numbers came up on the call, even as the firm runs two pay-per-lead vendors and a new marketing retainer at the same time. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • No profitability or margin figures were discussed on the call
  • No stated visibility into cost per acquisition across the pay-per-lead platforms already in use (Legal Match, Unbundled Attorney/Access Legal)
  • No mention of how overhead is tracked or split now that a second office in Alabama is live
  • The growth goal ($250K to $500K) was framed purely as a revenue target, with no profit or margin target attached
Blind Spot: Doubling revenue without knowing your margin by lead source could mean doubling your busiest, least profitable work — growth that feels like success but doesn't pay you any more.
The Positive
  • Angel has already set a specific, measurable revenue goal ($500K next year) — a clearer target than most firms at this stage define for themselves
The Gap
  • Without knowing which lead source or practice area is actually profitable, new spend could go to the wrong place
  • Two offices sharing overhead with no per-location visibility makes it hard to know if the Alabama expansion is paying for itself
  • Revenue growth without a profit plan just means more risk carried at a larger scale
A real profit plan is what makes the CEO transition sustainable — because freedom funded by margin doesn't disappear the moment growth slows.
