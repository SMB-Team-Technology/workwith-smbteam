Intake Assessment

Josh personally fields every intake call across eight practice areas with only one paralegal for support — a structure that already caps how much new business the firm can safely take on. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Every intake call, regardless of practice area, goes to Josh personally — no delegation, no defined process
  • No CRM or intake tracking system was mentioned — with only one paralegal, follow-up on non-immediate signers is at risk of falling through the cracks
  • No after-hours coverage — a live chat gap only compounds slow response after 5pm
  • The firm's stated 96% close rate lives in Josh's head, not in a documented, transferable process
Blind Spot: Every hour spent on intake is an hour not spent interviewing the attorneys Josh needs to hire — the two priorities are directly competing for the same time.
The Positive
  • Josh's personal close rate of 96% shows the underlying sales skill is exceptional — the gap is capacity, not capability
  • A case intake portal link already exists above the fold on the homepage — a genuine strength worth building on
The Gap
  • Every hour Josh spends on intake is an hour not spent on the business development that is supposed to become 70-80% of his time — what it costs in lost momentum toward the $1M goal
  • Without a documented process, the 96% close rate cannot be replicated by any attorney the firm hires — capping growth at exactly the ceiling Josh is trying to break through
Building a team that can close at anywhere near Josh's rate is what actually lets him step back from casework and into the business-builder role he wants.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • Contact form appears twice (mid-page, footer) but is not above the fold on first load
  • No live chat is present — after-hours visitors have no way to get an immediate response
  • A case intake portal link exists above the fold, which is a genuine strength worth building on

Team Assessment

Mountain State Attorneys is Josh Kotter and one paralegal — there is no management layer, no delegation structure, and no hiring blueprint yet, even as Josh actively interviews new attorneys. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • No org structure beyond Josh and one paralegal — every decision and every case ultimately routes through him
  • No documented hiring or onboarding blueprint exists yet, even though Josh is actively interviewing attorneys as a top priority
  • The website implies multiple attorneys handle separate practice areas, but the firm has no attorneys beyond Josh — a gap between brand and reality that needs resolving before scaling
  • No stated plan yet for how a future team will be managed day-to-day once hired
Blind Spot: Interviewing attorneys without a hiring and onboarding blueprint in place risks bringing people into a firm with no system to ramp them — the exact "firehose" scenario Josh is worried about, just arriving from a different direction.
The Positive
  • Josh is already treating hiring as his top priority and is actively interviewing — the intent to build a team is there, which is more than most solo attorneys at this stage can say
The Gap
  • Every case that currently requires Josh's personal attention is a case that keeps him from the 70-80% business-development role he says he wants
  • Without a hiring/onboarding blueprint, new hires ramp slower and risk repeating the same bottlenecks Josh has now
The 5,000-10,000-attorney vision Josh describes cannot be built by one person doing everything — it starts with the hiring and management systems that let him delegate for the first time.

Profit Plan Assessment

Revenue has grown from roughly $250K in 2024 to a projected $400K in 2026, but no cost-per-case, margin, or financial visibility metrics came up on the call. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • No profitability, margin, or cost-per-case visibility was discussed — growth so far has been revenue growth, not confirmed profit growth
  • LSA spend was paused specifically "to manage budget," indicating real budget sensitivity at this revenue stage
  • ~6 landlord-tenant calls a day are declined rather than monetized via referral fees — a standing revenue opportunity with no system to capture it
  • No stated average case value — a number the firm needs before it can responsibly plan ad spend or hiring against a $1M revenue goal
Blind Spot: Setting a $1M revenue goal without first knowing average case value or cost-per-case means Josh may hit the number and still not know if the firm actually made more money doing it.
The Positive
  • Revenue has already grown roughly 60% from 2024 to the 2026 projection — real momentum on a track record before any new marketing spend has even been added
The Gap
  • Without visibility into which practice areas are actually profitable, the firm cannot responsibly decide where to focus growth first
  • The landlord-tenant referral opportunity is likely-monetizable revenue sitting unrealized every single day
An empire is built on profit, not just revenue — a profit plan is what makes sure the growth Josh is chasing actually funds the vision he described.
