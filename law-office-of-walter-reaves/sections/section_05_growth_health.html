Pillar Health Scorecard

Lead Generation

  • Local Service Ads were paused post-merger — no active paid lead generation channel is running
  • The firm is not appearing above established competitors (Dunham & Jones, Dunnam & Dunnam) in DWI and general criminal defense search results
  • The blog has gone stale since June 2024, even though the firm's own drug crimes practice-area page shows the firm can rank when it invests in content
Rebuilding a predictable lead flow is the first step back toward the revenue this firm had before the merger.

Intake

  • Walter and one part-time assistant are the only two people fielding every incoming call
  • No documented follow-up process exists for leads who do not retain the firm on the first call
  • The firm is losing an estimated 5–6 cases a month — roughly $20,000–$24,000 in monthly revenue — because no one can answer fast enough
Every case lost to a slow answer is revenue the firm cannot afford to lose while it is trying to stabilize.

Self-Managing Team

  • The entire practice runs through two people: Walter and one part-time legal assistant
  • There is no one else positioned to keep the practice moving if Walter is unavailable
  • Every case decision, and most of intake, currently depends on Walter personally
Until someone besides Walter can carry part of the load, there is no path to more free time — only more hours.

Profit

  • Annual revenue was cut roughly in half by a failed merger, from about $600,000 to about $250,000
  • The current marketing budget (~$800/month) reflects a real, stated budget crisis — not a strategic choice
  • No profit plan or cost-per-case tracking exists to guide the rebuild after the merger
Stabilizing the practice starts with knowing the real numbers, not just feeling the pressure of the last two years.
