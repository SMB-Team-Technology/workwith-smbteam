Lead Generation

Trias Mathis PA generates nearly every new case through referral companies, billboards, and radio — spending $17K-$19K/month with no owned digital lead generation behind it. Across five practice areas and four Florida offices, the firm does not appear in organic search, paid search, or local map results anywhere in its own home market of Fort Myers.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • PageSpeed scores could not be pulled during this review — Google's PageSpeed Insights API returned rate-limit errors on every attempt. A live manual check is recommended before scaling paid traffic to this site, since speed directly affects Google Ads Quality Score
  • No video content on the homepage — for a firm whose bilingual, personal-relationship-driven brand is its biggest asset, there is no video of Larry or Joseph building trust before a prospect ever calls
  • No live chat — a visitor who isn't ready to call has no lower-commitment way to engage
  • Blog is hosted at a separate subdomain (blog.triasmathis.com) with no visible post date on the pages reviewed — post cadence and freshness could not be confirmed
Blind Spot: The site's own directory listings actively work against it — Avvo, FindLaw, and BBB all show conflicting addresses (Orlando, West Palm Beach, and Destin) instead of the actual Fort Myers headquarters, which can quietly suppress how much Google trusts the site for local searches, regardless of how well the site itself is built.
The Positive
  • Mobile responsive with a clear above-the-fold CTA ("Reach Out Now" plus a "FREE CONSULT? CALL TODAY" banner) and a "Free Case Evaluation" form visible without scrolling
  • Already has a real content asset most 2-year-old firms don't: 19 dedicated geo-targeted landing pages across 14 cities for Truck Accidents and 10 cities for Car Accidents, plus visible attorney bios for Joseph and Larry
The Gap
  • Without confirmed page speed, every dollar spent driving traffic here carries unknown conversion risk — slow load times lose mobile visitors before the form ever loads
  • No video and no live chat means visitors who are comparing firms have less reason to remember or trust Trias Mathis over a competitor's site
  • The 19-city landing page infrastructure is a real asset sitting mostly idle — it cannot produce leads until the authority and directory issues behind it are fixed
This website was built to open the door — but until it is verified and reinforced, it cannot be the tool that finally makes Trias Mathis independent of referral partners.

Local SEO

The Problem
  • GBP shows a 5.0 star rating per directory aggregation, but the live Google Maps listing and total review count could not be confirmed this pass — the firm's own /reviews/ page shows only 6 named written testimonials with no dates or star counts
  • Does not surface in proxy 3-pack searches for any of its five practice areas in Fort Myers, including "car accident lawyer," "workers compensation lawyer," "insurance claim denied lawyer," and "construction litigation attorney"
  • GBP primary category is listed as "General Practice" on at least one directory rather than Personal Injury — worth confirming on the live listing, since a mismatched category can suppress local ranking
Blind Spot: Larry has two conflicting FindLaw profiles — one correctly listing Fort Myers, one listing him as a West Palm Beach attorney — plus an Avvo profile tied to Orlando. Each of these dilutes the local authority Google assigns to the real Fort Myers office, working against every other SEO investment until they're corrected.
The Positive
  • The bilingual differentiator is real and provable — all 6 named client testimonials on the firm's own site are Hispanic surnames, and Larry is a native Spanish speaker, giving Trias Mathis a genuine angle competitors can't easily copy
The Gap
  • Every month invisible in the 3-pack is a month Farah & Farah, Viles & Beckman, and The Dellutri Law Group capture searchers actively looking for exactly what Trias Mathis offers
  • The firm's own 19-city landing page content cannot rank until directory consistency and review volume catch up to it
Viles & Beckman Accident & Injury Attorneys — 761 reviews at 4.8 stars and a "40+ years serving Florida" brand dominate the car accident and workers' comp searches where Trias Mathis does not appear at all.
Every practice-area search Trias Mathis loses today is a referral relationship it doesn't have to depend on tomorrow — once local SEO catches up to the content already built.

Google Ads

Ad Presence
  • This firm does not appear to be running Google search ads for personal injury, workers' comp, insurance claims, or construction litigation in Fort Myers — consistent with the transcript, which confirmed the firm discontinued a prior $2K-$3K/month campaign due to low lead quality and has not run paid search since
  • The prior campaign was never optimized around Quality Score, negative keywords, or dedicated landing pages — the same $2K-$3K/month, structured differently, could produce very different results
Competitor Ad Landscape
  • Viles & Beckman — dedicated "Car Accident Lawyer in Fort Myers | Free Consultations" PPC-style landing page
  • Goldstein Buckley Cechman Rice Purtz Smith & Smith (GBC Law) — dedicated "Fort Myers Car Accident Lawyers" landing page with a distinct tracking number (239-500-4878), a strong sign of active paid campaign infrastructure
  • Farah & Farah and Powell, Jackman, Stevens & Ricciardi — both run dedicated Fort Myers workers' comp landing pages
The Opportunity
  • "Car accident lawyer Fort Myers," "workers compensation lawyer Fort Myers," "insurance claim denied lawyer Fort Myers," and "construction litigation attorney Fort Myers" — all currently uncontested by Trias Mathis
  • Case-type specific terms like "hurricane damage claim lawyer" and "homeowners insurance claim attorney" — a stated firm strength that isn't showing up in search at all yet
  • Spanish-language campaigns — Larry's native bilingual capability is a validated differentiator the firm has never paid to amplify
  • Competitor-name and neighboring-city terms across the firm's other three offices (Destin, Miami, Jacksonville), where the firm already has a physical presence but no paid visibility
Blind Spot: The firm was pitched ChatGPT/AEO ads and, correctly, asked for a concrete cost-per-case ROI proposal before proceeding — this audit is that proposal.

Local Service Ads

LSA Presence
  • Trias Mathis's Google Screened / Local Service Ads status could not be verified live this pass — the LSA unit sits above the organic SERP and requires live browser rendering that was not available. Recommended as a live spot-check before finalizing scope.
  • No LSA review count could be found for the firm, consistent with its broader lack of a verifiable public review count at this early stage
Competitor LSA Landscape
  • Competitor LSA presence could not be confirmed live this pass either — but given the review volume Farah & Farah (578 reviews) and Viles & Beckman (761 reviews) have built, both are strong candidates for Google Screened status in this market and are worth a live spot-check
The Opportunity
  • Google Screened status puts Google's own verification badge next to the firm's name — meaningful trust-building for a firm still building its public review count
  • LSAs appear above everything else on Google — above paid ads, above the map pack, above all organic results — and you only pay when a real prospect calls you directly.
  • Pay-per-lead pricing is a lower-risk entry point for paid channels given the firm's earlier bad experience with pay-per-click Google Ads
