Intake Assessment

With referral volume already near capacity and a 4-5 person support staff absorbing intake alongside other duties, Trias Mathis has no defined process to prove new leads won't slip through once digital volume increases. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • No defined intake process was described on the call — a 4-5 person support staff currently absorbs it alongside other duties
  • No after-hours or overflow coverage was mentioned, despite the firm operating across four different markets (Fort Myers, Destin, Miami, Jacksonville)
  • No CRM or lead-tracking system was referenced — hard to know how many referral leads are being followed up on versus lost
  • No stated follow-up sequence for leads who don't sign on the first call
Blind Spot: Once digital lead volume is added on top of the current referral flow, the same intake gaps that go unnoticed today at low volume become the leaks that quietly cap how much of the new investment ever turns into cases.
The Positive
  • The firm is already handling close to 20 cases/month through referrals alone — proof intake fundamentals work well enough to operate at real volume, even without a defined process
The Gap
  • Every new digital lead added without a defined intake process is a lead that could be lost the same way inconsistent follow-up loses referral leads today
  • No after-hours coverage means leads calling outside business hours from four different markets have no guaranteed answer
  • Without a CRM, there is no way to see which leads convert and which fall through — making it hard to measure the return on the lead-gen investment this audit recommends
A firm that finally runs independent of referral partners still needs an intake process strong enough to catch every new lead that system produces — otherwise the investment in lead generation never reaches its full return.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • A "Free Case Evaluation" form sits above the fold on the homepage — a real intake asset already in place
  • No live chat is available, so a visitor unsure about calling has no lower-commitment way to start the conversation
  • No after-hours response process was described for form submissions that come in outside business hours

Team Assessment

At 7-8 people across two lawyers and 4-5 support staff, Trias Mathis has real team capacity — but growth decisions, including whether to invest in new marketing channels, still run through Larry personally. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"future-proof the business" / "control growth"
The Problem
  • No dedicated marketing, ops, or intake lead was described — those functions currently sit with Larry or are informally shared across support staff
  • Larry personally evaluated and rejected a proposed AI/AEO ad product pending a concrete ROI proposal — a sign he is still the one vetting every vendor and channel
  • Growth to date has depended on referral relationships Larry has personally built and maintained over nearly 20 years as an attorney — a dependency on him individually, not a system
  • No stated leadership structure beyond the two attorneys managing 4-5 support staff
Blind Spot: The instinct to personally vet every new vendor and channel is exactly what has kept the firm safe from bad spend so far — but it's also the same habit that will cap how big the firm can grow before Larry becomes the bottleneck.
The Positive
  • Larry and Joseph have already built a 7-8 person team and a four-office footprint in just two years — real proof this firm can scale operationally, not just legally
The Gap
  • Every new channel, vendor, or growth decision currently requires Larry's personal review — a ceiling on how fast the firm can move
  • No one besides Larry appears to own marketing or lead-gen decisions, meaning the "future-proof" goal he described still runs through him today
  • Team growth (7-8 people) has outpaced management structure — there is capacity without yet having self-management
The firm Larry described — one that runs independent of referral partners and boom-and-bust cycles — requires a team that can execute growth decisions without waiting on him to personally approve every one.

Profit Plan Assessment

No profit visibility or margin discussion came up on the call — the firm's spend on lead generation and its actual return could not be tied to any tracked cost-per-case figure. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • No cost-per-case or margin tracking was described for any of the firm's five practice areas
  • $17K-$19K/month is already going toward referral and traditional lead sources with no stated tracking of what that spend returns
  • Average case value (over $10,000 in attorney's fees) is known, but nothing was shared about the cost to acquire or service that case
  • No bookkeeper, controller, or financial reporting process was mentioned on the call
Blind Spot: Larry's own instinct — refusing to commit to a new marketing channel without "a concrete proposal with clear ROI projections" — shows he already knows he needs that visibility. That same standard just hasn't been applied yet to the $17K-$19K/month already being spent every month.
The Positive
  • Average case value above $10,000 in attorney's fees gives Trias Mathis strong underlying economics — profit visibility, not case value, is the missing piece
The Gap
  • Without cost-per-case tracking, there is no way to know which of the five practice areas is actually the most profitable to grow
  • $17K-$19K/month in spend with no ROI tracking means the firm cannot tell if that budget is working as hard as it could
  • Hitting the $2.5M revenue goal without a profit plan risks more revenue without more take-home for Larry and Joseph
Control over growth means knowing exactly what each dollar spent returns — not just growing revenue, but growing it in a way Larry can see, trust, and repeat.
