Intake Assessment

Filippov Law has no dedicated intake person, so inquiries reach you directly, and the firm's next stage depends on turning each good inquiry into a signed retainer. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • There is no dedicated intake person; you said there are no personal assistants and that you use AI agents for scheduling
  • The website inquiry form sits mid-page and in the footer, not above the fold, with the "Send An Inquiry" button as the only above-the-fold prompt
  • Office hours are Monday to Friday, 9 to 5, closed weekends, per the Google profile
  • A defined path from first inquiry to signed retainer was not described on the call
Blind Spot: The inquiries you get today are mostly one-time matters, so the real intake question for a flat-fee subscription model is how a business owner goes from a first call to a signed retainer without you handling every step.
The Positive
  • You already receive a steady stream of calls and inquiries because of your reputation
  • AI agents already handle scheduling, and the Google profile includes a booking link
The Gap
  • Retainer clients are worth far more than one-time matters, so an inquiry that is not followed up has a higher cost than it used to
  • Each inquiry that waits for your attention competes with the other work you are doing yourself
  • Without a defined first-call-to-retainer path, it is harder to tell which inquiries are the mid-sized clients you want
A simple intake path means new clients can start working with the firm without every step running through you.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The inquiry form sits mid-page under "Your Trusted Partners In Business Law Solutions" and again in the footer
  • The "Send An Inquiry" button above the fold links to the contact page
  • No live chat was observed on the site

Team Assessment

Filippov Law went from about ten employees to a bench of contractors hired as needed, and the work, client relationships and day-to-day decisions still run through Victoria. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
"clients kind of get used to you and they want you to do it because they like the way you do it."
The Problem
  • You described the firm as basically you plus people you hire as needed, rather than a standing team
  • Trusted contractors are asking for work, but you have very little to give them
  • You said you are running the firm rather than doing the legal work you would like to do
  • Clients expect to work with you personally, which keeps delivery tied to your time
Blind Spot: Your contractors asking for work means capacity already exists, but without defined roles and repeatable processes the work cannot move to them, so you keep doing it yourself.
The Positive
  • You have trusted contractors who are ready to take on more work
  • You have the time right now to build the infrastructure before bringing in new clients, as you said
The Gap
  • Every task that depends on you is a task you cannot hand off, which is the opposite of the firm running on its own
  • Idle contractor capacity is wasted while you work in the day-to-day
  • Without documented roles and processes, adding clients means adding to your own workload
Building the team and systems so the work moves without you is what makes room for the legal work you actually want to do and for being the face of the firm.

Profit Plan Assessment

Filippov Law's revenue has dropped sharply since the firm lost its largest client and several others were sold or restructured, and the flat-fee subscription model that is meant to rebuild it is still taking shape. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: RED
"I'm working on a model, like a flat fee subscription model."
The Problem
  • The firm relied heavily on one anchor client, and losing that client changed the firm's revenue base
  • Several remaining clients have been sold, restructured or are under financial pressure themselves
  • The flat-fee subscription model is still being built, so recurring revenue has not been established yet
  • No profit targets or margin plan were discussed on the call
Blind Spot: Mediation and arbitration cannot be sold on a flat fee, so as the subscription model grows the firm will need a clear plan for how those matters are priced alongside it.
The Positive
  • You are already moving toward recurring, predictable revenue with the flat-fee subscription model
  • You already use QuickBooks, so the financial data needed for a profit plan exists
The Gap
  • Without a recurring base, each month's revenue depends on whatever matters happen to come in
  • A subscription price that is not tied to the hours it takes can erode profit if the work is not systemized
  • Without a target number of retainer clients tied to a profit goal, it is hard to know when the firm has rebuilt enough
Predictable profit is what lets the firm run on its own while you spend your time on the work you want to do.
