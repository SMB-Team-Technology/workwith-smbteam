Intake Assessment

Jason Breit Law's public intake path is simple — a free-consultation booking button and a phone number — and a Client Services Director role appears on the team page, but how inquiries are answered, tracked, and followed up has not yet been reviewed. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Response times, call handling, and who answers new inquiries have not yet been reviewed with you
  • The homepage offers a booking button and a phone number but no short inquiry form for visitors who are not ready to book
  • Posted office hours are weekdays 9am–5pm, and how evening and weekend inquiries are handled is not yet known
  • No defined follow-up process for consultations that do not sign right away has been confirmed
The Positive
  • Free consultations by Zoom or in person make it easy for a family to take a first step
  • A dedicated Client Services Director role is listed on the team page, and fixed fees are explained openly on the pricing page
The Gap
  • An inquiry that waits until the next business day can end up with a competitor who answers sooner
  • Without tracked follow-up, it is hard to know how many consultations turn into signed plans
  • Without source tracking, the firm cannot tell which marketing channels are producing signed clients
Whatever growth means to you personally, it only counts when the inquiries already coming in are converted.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The homepage calls to action are a "Schedule a Free Consultation" button and the office phone number; a separate contact page exists
  • No live chat was detected on the site
  • On a phone, the homepage takes about 7.4 seconds to show its main content, so visitors wait before they can tap the booking button

Team Assessment

The website lists a founding attorney and a Client Services Director, and total team size, roles, and how work is delegated have not yet been reviewed. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • The firm's name, page title, attorney bio, and consultation offer are built around Jason personally, which can make client trust depend on him
  • Roles, decision-making authority, and accountability measures for the team have not yet been reviewed
  • It is not yet known whether the firm can run for a week without Jason
The Positive
  • A dedicated client services leadership role already exists on the team page
  • The website describes a defined way of working with clients ("How We Work"), a good starting point for documenting handoffs
The Gap
  • Without documented roles and handoffs, more consultations will land on the same few people
  • Every decision that routes through the founding attorney limits how far the firm can grow without adding to his workload
  • Time away from the firm is only an option when the work does not depend on one person
A team that runs on clear roles is what gives Jason the choice to step back when he decides to.

Profit Plan Assessment

No financial information was available for Jason Breit Law, so revenue, margins, and the cost of acquiring a client have not yet been reviewed. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Revenue, margins, and monthly financial reporting have not yet been reviewed with you
  • The cost of acquiring a signed client is unknown, so marketing return cannot yet be judged
  • Fixed fees put the risk of overruns on the firm, so margin depends on tracking the time each plan actually takes
The Positive
  • Published fixed fees mean clients know the price before work starts, which supports clean forecasting
  • Complimentary three-year plan reviews after completion create a regular touchpoint with past clients
The Gap
  • Without cost-per-client data, advertising spend cannot be measured against the value of a signed plan
  • Without time tracking per plan, it is hard to know which services deserve more marketing and which need repricing
  • Without a financial dashboard, growth decisions are made on instinct rather than numbers
Profit that is planned, not discovered, is what turns a growing practice into a valuable firm.
