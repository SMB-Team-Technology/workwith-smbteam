Here Is Where Christine Brown Sports Law Stands Today

Christine Brown Sports Law (Christine Brown & Partners) is a Connecticut-based firm launched in 2025 by an attorney with roughly 30 years of experience, representing college athletes, coaches, and teams on NIL, Title IX, NCAA eligibility, transfer portal, scholarship, and coaching employment matters. The firm already has a real team structure and a strong content and media presence — and the single most important finding is that its website, where every search and referral visitor ends up, takes far too long to load on a phone (a mobile performance score of 28 out of 100) to match that credibility.

Stage 6 is where the firm runs on its team and systems rather than on your personal involvement in every matter — and it is exactly where we want to take Christine Brown Sports Law.

Where You Are Today

Stage 4: Small Business Manager
  • A defined team is already in place — three attorneys, a COO, a finance manager, and two legal assistants, per the firm's public team page
  • Visibility is built on expertise-led content and media quotes — a newsletter of about 20 articles plus coverage in outlets like 9News and The Christian Science Monitor
  • The website scores 28/100 on mobile and 53/100 on desktop for performance, and intake runs through an external Clio Grow form rather than one embedded on the page
  • A national practice is served from a single Connecticut office, against specialist firms with keyword-focused page structures

Where You Need To Be

Stage 6: Law Firm Owner
  • The firm runs without you
  • Your income is no longer tied to your personal hours

The gap between where the firm is and where it is headed is smaller than it looks — the team, the credentials, and the reputation are already there — and closing it comes down to systems that let the team carry more of the work.
Overall Growth Stage Assessment
Christine Brown Sports Law
Christine Brown Sports Law looks like a Stage 4 firm: a young firm with a defined team and standout credentials that is still relying on its founder's expertise and reputation to carry the load. The most urgent limiting area appears to be the gap between that credibility and the systems behind it — a slow mobile website, an intake path that sends visitors off-site, and operations that likely still lean on the founder for decisions only she can make.
