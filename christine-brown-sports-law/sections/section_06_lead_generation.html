Lead Generation

Christine Brown Sports Law has built real visibility through expertise: a newsletter of about 20 articles and media quotes in outlets like 9News, WYFF, and The Christian Science Monitor. What is not yet visible is the lead-capture machinery behind that credibility — a fast website, topic-focused pages, and measurable paid channels — in a niche where specialist competitors already have it.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • The site loads slowly, especially on a phone: the largest piece of page content takes about 19 seconds to appear on mobile
  • PageSpeed scores (Lighthouse performance score): Mobile 28/100 — Desktop 53/100
  • No prominent "free consultation" button was visible above the fold — the header carries two phone numbers and a Contact Us menu item, and the page content below is a list of question-style prompts
  • Intake runs through a Clio Grow form that is linked rather than embedded above the fold, and a /contact-us address returned a "page not found" error when tested
  • No video was found on the homepage, so visitors cannot see or hear Christine before deciding to reach out
Blind Spot: A page that takes about 19 seconds to show its main content on a phone can lose a visitor before they ever read about Christine's 30 years of experience — and someone dealing with an urgent athletics or coaching-contract issue is unlikely to wait.
The Positive
  • A strong attorney bio: about 30 years of experience, admission to the Connecticut bar, the U.S. District Court (D. Conn.), the Second Circuit, and the U.S. Supreme Court, plus prior roles as Director of Legal Services and Title IX Coordinator at Fairfield University and Assistant City Attorney for Bridgeport
  • The site is mobile responsive and has an active "Newsletter & News" section with about 20 articles, plus nine client testimonials
The Gap
  • Visitors who leave before the page loads never see the credentials, the content, or the testimonials that set this firm apart
  • Without a clear consultation button, a visitor who is ready to act has to hunt for the next step
  • Without video, the firm misses the chance to build trust with athletes, parents, and coaches before the first conversation
A website that converts the credibility Christine has already earned is the first step toward a firm that grows without depending on her personal network.

Local SEO

The Problem
  • The firm's address is inconsistent across sources: the website lists 1700 East Putnam Avenue, Suite 210 in Old Greenwich, while a search listing shows Suite 400 and the 2025 launch release describes the firm as being in Shelton, Connecticut
  • Google Business Profile details and map-pack positions for searches like "title ix attorney connecticut" and "coaching employment lawyer connecticut" could not be verified in this review and should be checked live
  • No firm-specific listing surfaced in our search of legal directories such as Avvo, Justia, FindLaw, and Martindale
The Positive
  • The firm has earned trade and national press coverage, including Athletic Business and a PRWeb launch release, and has company profiles on ZoomInfo and LinkedIn
The Gap
  • Conflicting address details weaken trust signals for both Google and prospects trying to confirm the firm is who it says it is
  • For a national practice, the bigger local-search opportunity is topic-based visibility — dedicated pages by issue and by sport — rather than a traditional local map pack
Duffy Law, LLC — a New Haven-based Title IX firm operating since 2015 with a dedicated "Nationwide Title IX Athletics Attorney" page and a testimonials page, giving it a clearer topical footprint in Connecticut search.
Clean, consistent listings and topic-focused pages let the firm's reputation work for it around the clock, not only when Christine is in the room.

Google Ads

Ad Presence
  • Title IX and gender equity: this firm does not appear to be running Google search ads for terms like "title ix attorney connecticut" or "title ix lawyer for accused student athlete" — ads can vary by location and device, so this is not a confirmed absence
  • NIL, athlete rights, eligibility, and transfer portal: ads were not observed for terms like "nil attorney college athletes," "ncaa eligibility attorney," or "transfer portal lawyer"
  • Coaching employment: ads were not observed for terms like "college coach contract attorney" or "coach wrongful termination lawyer college"
  • A Google tag is installed on the site, which is consistent with analytics or conversion tracking but does not show whether ads are running
Competitor Ad Landscape
  • Duffy Law, LLC — ad activity was not observed, but its "Nationwide Title IX Athletics Attorney" page is the kind of dedicated destination paid search can point to
  • Athlete Defender — ad activity was not observed, but many keyword-targeted pages (athlete advocacy, transfers, scholarships, dismissals, coach representation) give it a ready landing page for almost any related search
  • Barry, Taylor & Levesque, LLC — ad activity was not observed; dedicated NCAA infractions pages put it in position to capture the same Connecticut searches
The Opportunity
  • Issue-based searches such as "title ix attorney," "nil attorney college athletes," "ncaa eligibility attorney," and "transfer portal lawyer"
  • Coaching employment searches such as "college coach contract attorney" and "coach wrongful termination lawyer college"
  • Urgency-driven searches that mirror the questions already on the homepage, like "accused of a Title IX violation," where someone needs a lawyer right now
  • Competitor-name and brand searches, so that people comparing Duffy Law or Athlete Defender, or searching for Christine Brown by name, find this firm first

Local Service Ads

LSA Presence
  • The firm was not observed in Local Service Ads, and no Google Screened badge was observed
  • Google's LSA categories are limited, and Title IX, NIL, and NCAA eligibility work are unlikely to be among them, so this is not expected to be a priority channel for this practice
Competitor LSA Landscape
  • No named competitors were observed holding Local Service Ads placements for the firm's practice areas
The Opportunity
  • Confirm which, if any, of the firm's practice areas qualify for an LSA category before putting budget behind it
  • LSAs appear above everything else on Google — above paid ads, above the map pack, above all organic results — and you only pay when a real prospect calls you directly.
  • Because most prospects for this practice search nationally by issue, paid search and topic-focused content are likely to matter more than LSAs
