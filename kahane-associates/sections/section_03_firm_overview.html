Here Is Where Kahane & Associates, P.A. Stands Today

Kahane & Associates, P.A. is a Sunrise, Florida default servicing firm that represents banks, lenders and servicers across Florida, with offices in Atlanta and Memphis and a team of roughly 80 people, about 12 of them attorneys. By the IT Director's own account, much of the firm's business comes from networking and relationships, so the most important finding is not about lead generation at all: the day-to-day work that follows each assignment, such as routing court notices, producing fees-and-costs reports and reconciling final bills, still runs on people, and it will not scale unless headcount does.

Stage 6 is a firm that grows without growing headcount, where no single role or person is a point of failure, and it is exactly where we want to take Kahane & Associates.

Where You Are Today

Stage 4: Small Business Manager
  • Court notices arrive in a shared mailbox and a dedicated person routes each one by hand into the case management system and out to the assigned attorney
  • Fees-and-costs reporting is handled by one person almost full-time, with no realistic backup when they are out
  • AI use today is minimal, and the push from partners to adopt it has landed on the IT department without a roadmap
  • Much of the firm's new business comes from relationships and networking rather than from a marketing program

Where You Need To Be

Stage 6: Law Firm Owner
  • The firm runs without you
  • Your income is no longer tied to your personal hours
  • Your time is yours to spend however you choose
  • This is where true freedom and true wealth become possible
The gap between a firm that has to add people to handle more volume and one that does not is a plan for where automation goes first, and that is exactly what this report lays out.
Overall Growth Stage Assessment
Kahane & Associates, P.A.
Kahane & Associates is operating at Stage 4: a real, established firm with a leadership structure and an institutional client base, but with a heavy reliance on manual effort to keep the work moving. The most urgent limiting area is the volume of repeatable administrative work, from notice routing to fees-and-costs reporting to final-billing reconciliation, that depends on specific people rather than on systems. Until that changes, every increase in case volume has to be matched by an increase in headcount, which works against the goal of scaling without scaling the team.
