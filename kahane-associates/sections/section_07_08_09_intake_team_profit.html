Intake Assessment

For Kahane & Associates, intake is less about consumer phone calls and more about the stream of court notices and client assignments entering the firm through a shared Exchange mailbox. Today that stream is routed by hand, and it scales only as fast as the firm can add people to route it. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
"Right now, it's not scaling. So, for example, the Notice Mailbox, being that we have a human traffic controller and they're routing things, as that grows, that position, the FTE. We need it to complete that task would have to grow."
The Problem
  • Court notices land in a shared Notice Mailbox and a dedicated person routes each one into the case management system and out to the assigned attorney
  • Client emails and pending-status reporting depend on that same routing step
  • Reports that arrive as unstructured Word documents are not in a format the firm's workflow can import directly
  • Work on external sites that have no API, such as clicking through pages, populating forms and filing cases, depends on staff time
Blind Spot: A routing role that has to grow with volume does more than add salary. It also caps how many new assignments the firm can comfortably take on from institutional clients before it has to hire.
The Positive
  • The firm runs its own case management system on SQL Server with a .NET front end, built with workflows and direct access to its data, which is a strong foundation for automation
  • The IT team arrived with specific, well-defined workflows to automate rather than a general interest in AI
The Gap
  • Every increase in notice volume means more human routing time, so growth and headcount move together
  • Staff time spent on routing and reformatting is time not spent on attorney-facing work
  • A time-sensitive notice flow that depends on a dedicated routing role leaves little room for absences
Routing court notices automatically means the firm can take on more assignments without taking on more headcount.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The contact form (first name, last name, company, email, phone and message) sits on a separate Contact page rather than on the homepage, and no explicit call to action appears above the fold
  • Including a company field is a sensible fit for an institutional audience, since it tells the firm which lender or servicer is reaching out
  • With a mobile speed score of 36/100, a visitor on a phone may never reach the form

Team Assessment

Kahane & Associates has roughly 80 people, including about 12 attorneys, across Florida, Georgia and Tennessee, yet key work still depends on specific individuals and the AI initiative has landed on the IT department. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"fees and costs is something that you have maybe one user do almost full-time, and for you to have a replacement for that user when they take a day off is just almost impossible."
The Problem
  • Fees-and-costs reporting is close to a full-time job for one person, with no realistic cover when they are out
  • The push to adopt AI has fallen on the IT department, which is being asked to find opportunities without a defined roadmap
  • Partners and attorneys keep asking about AI without specifics on where it should be applied
  • New technology decisions run through managing partners and department heads, so expectations need to be aligned before anything is rolled out
Blind Spot: The risk of a one-person process is not only a day off. When the knowledge of how a report gets built lives in one person's head, it cannot be handed off, checked or improved.
The Positive
  • A member of the accounting team has already offered to beta test a fees-and-costs automation
  • The IT team is thinking carefully about security, including keeping AI away from personally identifiable information in the case system
The Gap
  • Single-person dependencies mean one absence can stall a time-sensitive report
  • An IT team asked to lead AI on top of its day job moves slower than a team with a roadmap and dedicated support
  • Without a shared plan, leadership expectations and what is practical can drift apart
A firm where no single person is a point of failure is a firm that can grow without constant oversight.

Profit Plan Assessment

The call did not cover the firm's financials, so this assessment looks at the finance-side workflows that were discussed: fees-and-costs reporting and final-billing reconciliation. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"we advance a lot of costs and so on for the clients."
The Problem
  • The fees-and-costs report, which summarizes case history and estimates the next 10 to 20 days, is produced by hand by a near full-time role
  • Final billing requires reconciling unbilled billable tasks, attorney time entries and hearings, a report the team wants automated
  • Because the firm advances costs for its clients, the accuracy and timeliness of this reporting matter more
  • Profit planning and margin targets were not discussed on the call
Blind Spot: Billing leakage rarely shows up as a loss. Time entries or costs that slip through a manual final-billing review simply never get invoiced, so nobody sees what was missed.
The Positive
  • The firm's accounting runs on Aderant, an established, non-proprietary platform, which gives automation a defined system to work from
  • The accounting team is enthusiastic enough to volunteer as an early tester
The Gap
  • Manual reporting slows how quickly the firm can see where each file stands financially
  • Manual final-billing review leaves room for missed time and costs
  • Without faster, cleaner billing data, higher volume brings more work without a clear view of what it earns
Faster, cleaner billing data is what turns higher volume into higher profit rather than just more work.
