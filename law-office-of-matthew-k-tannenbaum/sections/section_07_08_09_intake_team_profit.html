Intake Assessment

An answering service screens calls before they reach the owner, so most unwanted matters never take up his time. What happens to a qualified lead after that first screen was not discussed on the call and appears to rest with him personally. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: RED
"I have the chaff being knocked out, I would say, eight out of ten times before they even get in touch with me."
The Problem
  • Beyond the answering service, there is no described process for following up with qualified leads, so every next step waits on the owner
  • No intake tracking or conversion reporting was described, so it is hard to see how many screened leads become signed clients
  • Website consultation requests appear to flow into the same owner-handled path as everything else
Blind Spot: A screen that works well for unwanted calls can also filter out good landlord matters. Tracking what gets screened out shows whether anything valuable is slipping through.
The Positive
  • Most unwanted calls are filtered out before they reach the owner
  • Tenant-side and difficult matters are referred out rather than consuming owner time, which keeps the practice selective
The Gap
  • Without a handoff beyond the answering service, each qualified lead still waits on the owner's schedule
  • Without tracking, there is no way to tell which lead sources produce the landlord matters the firm wants most
  • Capacity to say yes to work the firm currently turns away is limited by how much intake the owner handles himself
Every lead that is handled without you is time you get back — and it is how the practice stops depending on your Saturdays.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The homepage includes a consultation form and a "Schedule Your free Consultation" button, giving visitors a clear next step
  • The form sits in a consultation section on the homepage and was not confirmed to be above the fold, so some visitors may need to scroll to find it
  • It is worth confirming who responds to form submissions and how quickly, since they appear to land on the same owner-handled path

Team Assessment

The practice runs as a solo operation supported by an answering service, and the owner has said there is not the manpower to take on more of the work he is turning away. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"If I had the manpower, I probably would take tenants, but I just don't have the manpower."
The Problem
  • No associates or staff were described beyond the answering service, so drafting, research and case work all land on the owner
  • Tenant-side inquiries are declined for lack of capacity, which puts a ceiling on how far the practice can grow
  • The owner worked almost every Saturday until this year to keep up with the workload
  • AI tools are being learned and managed by the owner himself, which adds one more job to his plate
Blind Spot: Because the practice is selective and well run, it can feel like adding capacity means adding headaches. The right structure adds capacity without adding management burden for you.
The Positive
  • Weekend work has dropped sharply this year as AI took on more of the drafting
  • The owner is actively adopting new tools and is clearly open to handing work to systems
The Gap
  • Every matter still passes through one person, so time away from the desk means work waits
  • Work he would take on if he had the capacity goes elsewhere
  • Without guided AI implementation, the tools he has started using are not yet doing everything they could to lighten his load
A practice that carries more of the workload is what turns Saturdays back into your own time.

Profit Plan Assessment

Revenue, margins and profit targets were not discussed on the call, so this assessment reflects the firm's selective, high-service positioning and its capacity limits. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"It's medium volume, high level of service, but you pay for it…"
The Problem
  • No revenue, margin or profit targets were discussed, so there is no visible benchmark to plan against
  • Capacity, not demand, limits revenue today, since tenant-side work is declined for lack of manpower
  • Owner hours are the main production resource, so income scales with his time rather than with a system
  • Running out of AI usage while drafting several motions at once shows tool capacity is not yet planned around workload
Blind Spot: Without tracking owner hours by matter type, it is hard to see which matters pay the most for the time they take.
The Positive
  • The firm is positioned around high service rather than volume, and clients understand they pay for that level of attention
  • Marketing is described as working, so the firm is not spending to chase matters it does not want
The Gap
  • Without visible targets, growth decisions are made by feel rather than by a profit plan
  • Without time-per-matter tracking, the owner cannot tell where an extra hour of his time earns the most
  • Without capacity to take on more work, revenue stays tied to the owner's personal hours
The goal is a boutique practice where profit is planned and the workload no longer rests on you alone.
