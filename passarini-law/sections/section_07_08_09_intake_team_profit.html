Intake Assessment

Passarini Law has no documented intake process today — Andrea personally carries much of the client-facing work, and building a structured workflow from intake through off-boarding is the explicit reason this engagement exists. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • No documented intake process exists from first contact through off-boarding — everything currently runs on what's in Andrea's head
  • Andrea personally handles a large share of client-facing intake work across four practice areas, on top of her own casework
  • Operations are led only part-time by Andrea's son, who lacks a legal-operations background to build or run a formal process
  • The website's own contact form is not visible above the fold, adding friction before a lead even reaches anyone at the firm
Blind Spot: The firm has intentionally paused marketing to protect its roughly 10-case-a-month capacity — but without a defined intake process, it's unclear how much of that capacity is being used well versus lost to delay or inconsistency before a lead ever becomes a case.
The Positive
  • A four-person paralegal team (two in-house, two remote) already exists to support intake and casework — the missing piece is the process that tells them how
The Gap
  • Without a defined process, any future growth in caseload will hit the same bottleneck that exists today — Andrea in the middle of every file
  • There is currently no way to measure how many prospective clients are lost between first contact and signed engagement
  • Every hour spent improvising intake case-by-case is an hour that isn't going toward the retirement timeline Andrea is working against
A documented intake workflow is the most direct path to what Andrea actually wants — it's the mechanism that lets her delegate with confidence instead of by accident.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • No contact form is visible above the fold on the homepage — the primary path to reach the firm is consolidated near the footer
  • No live chat is present on the site
  • Contact information (phone, email, address, social links) is accurate once found, but only after a prospect scrolls past several sections

Team Assessment

Passarini Law depends entirely on Andrea for its day-to-day operation — she works close to 80 hours a week, and the person nominally leading operations does so only part-time and without a legal-operations background. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Andrea works close to 80 hours a week and remains the operational center of the firm
  • Operations are led only part-time by Andrea's son, a financial advisor by trade without deep legal-operations experience
  • No playbooks, scripts, or accountability structure currently guide the team's day-to-day work
  • The firm intentionally paused growth in new case volume rather than risk taking on more than the current team and structure can absorb
Blind Spot: Choosing to pause growth to protect the team is a sign of good judgment, not weakness — but it also means the firm's current ceiling isn't a market limit, it's an operations limit. Fixing operations is what raises that ceiling.
The Positive
  • Andrea has already built a five-person team — two in-house paralegals, two remote paralegals, and a family member helping lead operations — the foundation for delegation exists; it just doesn't have a system running through it yet
The Gap
  • Without documented systems, the team cannot absorb more volume even once marketing turns back on — capping growth twice over
  • Andrea cannot step away for a day, let alone begin executing the 5-year retirement plan she described, without someone else able to run the firm's core processes
  • Every process that exists only in Andrea's head is a process that disappears the moment she isn't available
This is the whole engagement: building the playbooks, scripts, and accountability that let the firm run without Andrea in the room — the precondition for both more free time now and a real retirement in five years.

Profit Plan Assessment

No annual revenue figure, close rate, or average case value was shared on the call, and no financial reporting exists beyond Clio's case management — Passarini Law is working toward a stated goal to double or triple revenue without a documented baseline to measure it against. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • No annual revenue figure was shared, and no baseline currently exists to measure progress toward "double or triple" against
  • No close rate or average case value was shared — the two inputs that turn case volume into a revenue plan
  • Clio manages case files, but no financial reporting cadence beyond that was mentioned
  • No cost-per-acquisition or marketing ROI tracking exists, though marketing itself is currently paused
Blind Spot: A five-year retirement plan and a revenue-doubling goal are being pursued as if they're the same project — but without financial visibility connecting the two, there's currently no way to know whether the path underway gets Andrea there in five years or in fifteen.
The Positive
  • The firm already uses Clio for case management, giving it a foundation of organized case data that could support financial reporting once that layer is added
The Gap
  • Without a revenue baseline, "double or triple" has no way to be tracked, planned around, or reported on
  • There is no current visibility into which of the firm's four practice areas is most profitable per hour of Andrea's time — a critical input for deciding where to focus growth
  • The 5-year retirement plan needs a number to work backward from, and that number doesn't exist yet
Every plan for retirement starts with knowing the number you're retiring on — building that visibility is what turns Andrea's five-year goal from a hope into a plan she can actually track.
