Intake Assessment

Every new lead at Allied Defense Law runs through Rebecca personally — the call, the text, the email, the follow-up — in between hearings and casework, and the leads she cannot reach quickly are the ones that get away. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: RED
"The time that I'm spending on intake is taking away from the time that I have for my cases"
The Problem
  • You handle every intake yourself — answering, texting, emailing, and logging each lead in MyCase between other work
  • After hours, calls go to voicemail; you told us that when you call back, some people have already hired the lawyer who answered
  • Follow-up is meant to be daily for about five days, but in practice it tends to happen once or twice a week
  • On LegalMatch leads, you estimated that 40–50% of the time the person does not answer when called
Blind Spot: Once a consultation actually happens, your hire rate is strong — you told us the real obstacle is getting it booked, not winning the client. That means speed to first contact, not sales skill, is the lever.
The Positive
  • You already have a clear intake routine — call, text and email, log it, send the representation agreement payment link — so there is a process to automate rather than invent
  • LegalMatch has delivered a good return when you are on top of calling, so the leads themselves are worth chasing
The Gap
  • You estimated losing 5–10 leads a month to slow or missed follow-up; at your roughly $4,000 average case, that is about $20,000–$40,000 a month in case value that never gets a chance to convert (arithmetic on your own figures, not a promise)
  • Every hour spent on intake is an hour not spent on the cases you already have
  • With no coverage outside your own hours, the firm cannot compete for the late-night calls that DUI work generates
Getting intake off your plate is how you take on more cases without hiring a team or adding hours to your week.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The website's contact form and any chat feature could not be reviewed in this pass, so whether a web inquiry triggers an instant response is still to be confirmed
  • Your company phone runs on Google Voice with no after-hours coverage — it goes straight to voicemail
  • A previous answering service could not be made to work with Google Voice, which is why after-hours coverage is still open

These findings are also referenced in the Website Audit section.

Team Assessment

Allied Defense Law is a one-person firm: Rebecca is the attorney, the receptionist, the admin, and the intake team, and the firm has no one else to hand work to. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
"I have no other attorneys, no paralegal, no receptionist, no admin, anything like that."
The Problem
  • Every task — intake, follow-up, scheduling, drafting — depends on you personally, with no one to cover when you are in court
  • Routine template work, such as motions to withdraw, is stacking up because there is no one else to draft it
  • A morning email pile needs triage before the real work of the day can start
  • You estimated that about 20 hours a week of help is what the firm needs
Blind Spot: You told us you would rather use AI than hire and then have to retrain someone who quits. That is a legitimate strategy — it means the capacity gap can be closed with systems instead of payroll, but only if those systems are actually built.
The Positive
  • You know exactly what you do not want — a bigger firm with more attorneys — which gives the plan a clear target
  • You are open to technology and have already opened an AI reporting account, so there is no need to convince you of the idea
The Gap
  • Your capacity is capped by your own hours: you told us you could take on more cases with help on the admin side
  • With no backup, a hearing or a day off means leads and tasks wait
  • Hiring would trade one problem for another — recruiting, training, and the risk of turnover you want to avoid
Replacing admin hours with systems is how you grow your caseload without growing a payroll.

Profit Plan Assessment

Allied Defense Law has grown substantially since it opened, and Rebecca would love to double revenue — without adding attorneys. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"My first year I made about a hundred, and this year I'm on track to make about 250."
The Problem
  • Margins, take-home, and the firm's cost to land each new client did not come up on the call, so the profit picture is still unclear
  • You could not say with certainty what LegalMatch costs each month, which means its return per signed case cannot yet be measured precisely
  • Growth today depends on your personal hours, so a higher caseload could mean more work for you rather than more profit
The Positive
  • Revenue has moved from about $100K in your first year toward about $250K this year — strong momentum for a firm not yet three years old
  • You said you see a good return on LegalMatch when you stay on top of it, so at least one lead source is already paying for itself
The Gap
  • Without knowing the cost of each signed case, you cannot tell which lead source deserves more or less of your attention
  • Doubling revenue without a plan for how the extra work gets done leads to burnout rather than wealth
Knowing your numbers is how doubling revenue becomes doubling what you actually keep.
