Intake Assessment

As the practice's only team member, Melissa personally handles every intake conversation across both locations, with no stated after-hours coverage or defined follow-up sequence. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • No delegated intake function — as the practice's sole operator, Melissa personally handles the calls and consultations generated by referrals, Ads, and SEO
  • No after-hours coverage confirmed beyond the website contact form — no live chat
  • No stated follow-up sequence for leads who don't sign on the first call
  • A previous business development hire ($80K/yr) was let go after producing only one client in four months, leaving intake back on Melissa alone
Blind Spot: The BD hire not working out doesn't necessarily mean delegation doesn't work for Dynasty — it may mean the hire had no documented process to plug into. Without a defined intake system, any future hire is set up to repeat that outcome.
The Positive
  • Dynasty already draws leads from three separate channels (referrals, Google Ads, Facebook Ads) — real demand exists, and the gap is on the conversion and follow-up side, not the top of the funnel
The Gap
  • Without a defined follow-up sequence, leads generated by the roughly $1,800/mo currently spent on Ads and SEO can go cold before Melissa gets back to them between cases
  • Every missed after-hours inquiry is a lead a competitor with live chat or LSA presence can pick up instead
  • No tracking of intake outcomes means there's no way to know which lead source actually converts best
A repeatable intake process is what turns "Melissa answers every call" into a system someone else could eventually run — which is exactly what standing up that first full-time hire requires.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • A contact form (name/email/phone/service inquiry) sits above the fold on the homepage, paired with the phone number — a genuine strength
  • No live chat present — after-hours visitors have no way to start a conversation
  • No stated automated confirmation or fast-response follow-up process for form submissions

Team Assessment

Every practice area, both locations, and every client conversation currently run through one person — Melissa. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • No employees currently on staff — the stated $60K/month goal exists specifically to reach the point where hiring a first full-time employee becomes possible
  • The one attempt to add headcount — an $80K/yr business development hire — was let go after producing only one client in four months
  • Two active locations (Gilbert and Mesa) are being run without any documented operational split or staffing plan for either address
  • No org structure, no manager layer, no accountability system beyond Melissa's own oversight
Blind Spot: The BD hire experience risks becoming a story Melissa tells herself about why hiring doesn't work — when the real issue was likely a missing system, not a missing person.
The Positive
  • Melissa has kept the practice running and growing toward a real revenue goal entirely solo — evidence of resilience and hands-on capability that a documented system can multiply rather than replace
The Gap
  • Every day spent managing marketing vendors, intake calls, and casework alone is a day not spent on higher-value work or personal time back
  • Two locations without a staffing plan means growth at either address is capped by Melissa's own physical presence
  • No accountability structure beyond Melissa means no one else is watching whether the plan to reach $30K, then $60K/month, is actually on track
Building a documented system now — one a future hire can actually step into — is what makes the next hiring attempt succeed where the last one didn't.

Profit Plan Assessment

Revenue fluctuates between $20K and $30K a month, and no profitability or margin data was shared on the call — meaning growth toward the stated $30K, then $60K/month goals is being pursued without a clear read on what each case actually nets. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • No profitability or margin data available for any of the four practice areas (family law, trusts & wills, guardianship & probate, business formation), despite each likely carrying a different effort-to-revenue ratio at the stated $1,200–$1,500 average case value
  • Monthly revenue fluctuates $20K–$30K with no stated plan for what's driving the swing month to month
  • Three separate vendors (self-managed Google Ads $1,000/mo, SEO $800/mo, hosting $200/mo) are being paid with no stated return-on-spend tracking across any of them
  • A previous $80K/yr business development hire was carried for four months before being let go — a real cost a clearer profit plan might have caught sooner
Blind Spot: Cutting the underperforming radio vendor ($5K/mo for 3 months) and the BD hire shows real financial discipline — but reactive cost-cutting after the fact is different from a profit plan that catches underperformance in month one, not month four.
The Positive
  • Melissa has already shown willingness to cut what isn't working — the radio campaign and the BD hire were both terminated rather than continued out of sunk cost
The Gap
  • Without knowing which practice area or channel is actually most profitable, every dollar of the roughly $2,000/mo currently going to Ads, SEO, and hosting is a bet made on incomplete information
  • Revenue swinging $20K–$30K month to month makes it difficult to plan confidently toward a full-time hire
A clear profit plan is what tells you whether $60,000 a month in revenue actually supports a full-time hire — or just feels like it should.
