Intake Assessment

Tomes Law Firm brings in a steady ~2 bankruptcy cases per week, but no defined intake process was described on the call — just staff Frances describes as reactive and hard to rely on. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • No defined intake process was discussed — staff has been described as lacking proactivity and follow-through
  • Staff coordinates over WhatsApp instead of a shared system, leaving Frances without visibility into how leads are actually being followed up
  • No after-hours coverage was stated — calls or form submissions outside business hours have no defined path
  • No CRM or lead-tracking system was mentioned on the call
Blind Spot: The ~2 bankruptcy cases/week Frances cited is a volume number, not a conversion number — no one has measured how many of the leads coming in each week are actually turning into signed cases versus falling through the cracks.
The Gap
  • Every lead that needs Frances's personal follow-up because staff isn't handling it consistently is time she is trying to buy back, not spend
  • After-hours leads with no coverage path likely reach a competitor who answers first
  • Without a lead-to-case conversion rate, there is no way to know how many opportunities are being missed each week
Every intake gap Frances has to personally catch is time she is trying to get back, not spend.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The homepage "Request a Call" form is above the fold and captures name, email, phone, service interest, and comments — a genuinely well-placed intake touchpoint
  • No live chat is currently present on the site
  • The "Free Video" opt-in doubles as a soft lead-capture point, but it is not clear whether it feeds into the same lead-handling process as the contact form

Team Assessment

Frances is personally managing a 7-person team through 60-hour weeks, after two separate attempts — a 2-year coaching engagement and a current part-time COO — have not yet produced a team that runs without her. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • A 7-person team (5 remote, 2 in-office) still requires Frances's constant, hands-on oversight
  • Staff has lacked proactivity and follow-through, and turnover has cost the firm roughly 30 people since 2020
  • Staff communicates over WhatsApp, a channel that excludes Frances from visibility into day-to-day operations
  • Two attempts to fix this — a 2-year How to Manage coaching engagement and the current 6-month local part-time COO — are ending without resolving the underlying issue
Blind Spot: Frances has already built a 180-page policy/procedure manual and implemented staff KPIs — the issue isn't a lack of systems on paper, it's that no one owns making sure the team actually follows them day to day.
The Positive
  • Frances has already invested seriously in trying to solve this — a 180-page policy/procedure manual and staff KPIs are already built, giving any new operational leader a real foundation to work from instead of starting at zero
The Gap
  • Every hour Frances spends managing staff directly is an hour she isn't practicing law, running the firm strategically, or living her life outside it
  • Continued turnover means the firm keeps re-training instead of building institutional knowledge
  • Without someone owning day-to-day accountability, the existing policy manual and KPIs cannot enforce themselves
Until Frances has a team that runs itself, more revenue just means more hours managing more people — never fewer.

Profit Plan Assessment

At roughly $650k in annual revenue, Tomes Law Firm has real top-line success — but no profit margin, cost-per-case, or profitability figure was discussed on the call, and even the revenue trend itself was described in a way that reads as inconsistent. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • No profit margin or profitability percentage was stated for the firm overall or for any of its 5 practice areas
  • The revenue figures given ($650k current vs. $250k "previously") were stated in a way that reads as an increase, not the described decrease — worth confirming directly with Frances rather than resolving silently
  • Average case value is known for bankruptcy only ($1,500 Ch7 / $4,750 Ch13) — tax resolution, debt defense, business startups, and wills/estates have no stated case value
  • No cost-per-acquisition or channel-level return data exists, since lead sources themselves were not defined on the call
Blind Spot: Confusion about which revenue figure is current versus historical is itself a signal — Frances may be making decisions around $650k in revenue without $650k in financial clarity.
The Gap
  • Without practice-area-level case values, Frances cannot know which of her 5 practice areas is actually most worth growing
  • Without a resolved revenue figure, near-term planning is built on a number that needs confirming first
  • No visibility into margin means growth could just mean more revenue at the same — or worse — take-home
A clear profit plan is what proves that stepping back from 60-hour weeks will not cost Frances the income she has already built.
