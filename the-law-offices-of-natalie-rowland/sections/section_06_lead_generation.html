Lead Generation

The Law Offices of Natalie Rowland has built its entire caseload — 15 to 20 active matters — on referrals alone, with no website presence, no reviews, and no paid marketing of any kind. As Natalie repositions the firm exclusively around high-asset family law, the online presence needed to support that positioning does not yet exist.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • The homepage is a near-blank single page showing only "Natalie R. Rowland" as visible text — no attorney bio, no practice area pages, and no calls to action
  • PageSpeed scores could not be captured live this session (5 attempts returned HTTP 429) — needs a live check before launch, but the current single-page stub strongly suggests significant technical debt regardless of the exact score
  • No contact form is currently live — Google's index still shows an older "Contact Us" page that now returns a 404
  • No attorney bio, credentials, or personal story is visible anywhere on the current site
Blind Spot: Google's search index still has an old bio page and an old "Contact Us" page cached from a prior version of the site — both now return 404. A prospective high-asset client doing due diligence could easily land on one of these dead pages, or worse, on a former firm's website where Natalie is still listed as a partner in a firm she has already left.
The Gap
  • Every prospective high-asset client who searches for Natalie by name right now lands on a page with no credibility signals at all
  • The site cannot support paid marketing yet — sending clicks to a blank page would waste every dollar spent
  • Stale directory listings and an old law-firm bio actively undermine the credibility a high-asset client is looking for
A rebuilt site that reflects the high-asset practice Natalie is building is the foundation everything else in this plan depends on.

Local SEO

The Problem
  • Google Business Profile currently has zero reviews, by Natalie's own account on the discovery call
  • The firm did not appear in organic results for any of three high-asset-family-law search terms tested, while several named competitors dominate all three
  • Three different office addresses are indexed across various directories for the same attorney, alongside a still-live bio on a former firm's website — a significant NAP inconsistency
Blind Spot: A high-asset client doing due diligence before hiring is exactly the type of prospect who will search your name and find the stale bio on a former firm's site, or one of three conflicting office addresses, before they ever reach your current site.
The Positive
  • Natalie already holds a 4.0/5.0 rating with 7 client reviews on Avvo — proof that clients who work with her are willing to vouch for her, even without any review-generation system in place
The Gap
  • Zero reviews on the platform that matters most (Google) means the credibility Natalie already has on Avvo is invisible to anyone searching Google directly
  • Not appearing in the local pack for any high-asset-divorce search means competitors are capturing every one of those searches by default
Naggiar & Sarif — maintains a dedicated high-asset-divorce landing page and appears prominently across every relevant search Natalie's ideal clients are running.
Building a real review base on Google is the fastest way to turn Natalie's existing 18-year reputation into visible credibility for the high-asset clients she wants to attract.

Google Ads

Ad Presence
  • This firm does not appear to be running Google search ads for high-asset family law terms — it also does not appear anywhere in organic results for the same terms, so paid placement is currently the only way to appear on the page at all
  • No prior paid advertising of any kind was mentioned on the discovery call — this would be a new channel for the firm
Competitor Ad Landscape
  • Naggiar & Sarif, KS Family Law, Kaye Lembeck Hitt & French, and Stern Edlin Graham — all maintain dedicated, heavily-optimized "high-asset divorce Atlanta" landing pages built specifically to capture this exact search intent, a strong signal of active SEO/PPC investment on these keywords
The Opportunity
  • "high-asset divorce attorney Atlanta" — the exact term the rebrand is built around
  • "Buckhead family law attorney" — the neighborhood where several top competitors concentrate their content
  • Neighboring high-income submarkets (Sandy Springs, Dunwoody) that are not yet saturated the way core Atlanta terms are
  • Conquesting on competitor firm names would put Natalie in front of prospects already comparing high-asset family law attorneys
Blind Spot: Because the firm has zero online presence today, Google Ads is not something to launch yet — without a rebuilt site and a review base to land on, paid clicks would be wasted the moment they arrive.

Local Service Ads

LSA Presence

  • This represents an open opportunity gap rather than a confirmed absence, and should be spot-checked with a live browser before the proposal call
Competitor LSA Landscape
  • Naggiar & Sarif — LSA placement and review count were not independently confirmed this session; flagged for a live follow-up check given their heavy SEO/content investment elsewhere
  • Stern Edlin Graham Family Law — LSA placement and review count were not independently confirmed this session; flagged for a live follow-up check for the same reason
The Opportunity
  • With a rebuilt site and a growing Google review base, Natalie could qualify for Google Screened status and begin appearing in Local Services placements this market has not yet confirmed is occupied
  • LSAs appear above everything else on Google — above paid ads, above the map pack, above all organic results — and you only pay when a real prospect calls you directly.
  • Pay-per-lead pricing means Natalie would only pay for a prospect who actually calls — a lower-risk way to test paid channels once the credibility foundation is in place
Blind Spot: LSA is pay-per-lead, not pay-per-click — even in a smaller market, a handful of the right high-asset leads could outperform flat-fee marketing spend. This channel is gated by review count and Google Screened status, both of which depend on the reputation-building work planned in Phase 1.
