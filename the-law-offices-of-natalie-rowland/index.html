Here Is Where The Law Offices of Natalie Rowland Stands Today

Natalie Rowland is an 18-year family law attorney who recently restarted her solo practice after a firm partnership ended, and is now repositioning entirely around high-asset family law under the incoming trade name “Rowland Family Law.” She has built her entire caseload — 15 to 20 active matters generating $22,000–$30,000 a month — on referrals alone, with a virtual assistant hired just three weeks ago as her only support. The single most important finding: her Google Business Profile carries zero reviews and her website is currently a single-page stub mid-rebuild, while three named local competitors already own the exact “high-asset divorce Atlanta” search terms she is trying to claim.

Stage 6 is the version of this firm where the systems run themselves and Natalie gets to choose exactly which high-asset clients and cases she takes — without ever becoming the “cookie-cutter” firm she has explicitly said she does not want to build.

Where You Are Today

Stage 3: Solo Practitioner
  • Every client relationship today comes through referrals — there is no other lead source in place
  • Natalie personally handles intake, casework, and client communication, with only a 3-week-old virtual assistant for support
  • Her Google Business Profile has zero reviews and her website is a single-page stub mid-rebuild, with no visibility in any of the searches her ideal high-asset clients are running
  • Revenue today is unpredictable month to month, and the $1M/yr goal requires a meaningfully higher average case value than her current caseload

Where You Need To Be

Stage 6: Law Firm Owner
  • The firm runs without you
  • Your income is no longer tied to your personal hours
  • Your time is yours to spend however you choose
  • This is where true freedom and true wealth become possible
Closing that gap starts with building the systems and online credibility to support the high-asset positioning Natalie is stepping into — not by taking on more casework, but by making the practice she already has run without demanding more of her time.

The Law Firm Growth Acceleration Model

We Help Law Firm Owners Get More Profit, More Personal Freedom, And Outstanding Results For Their Clients.

Most law firms try to grow by chasing more leads. But leads alone are not the problem — and leads alone are not the answer. Here is what actually works.

Lead Generation

Leads are to a law firm what gasoline is to a car. Without them, you go nowhere. But a steady, predictable flow of leads changes everything — you get to choose your cases instead of taking whatever comes in.

Intake

But all the leads in the world are worthless without a system to turn them into paying clients. Most firms are losing cases they already paid for simply because their follow-up is slow or inconsistent.

Self-Managing Team

Once clients are coming in, you need a team — otherwise you end up working 90-hour weeks and results suffer. A self-managing team is what gives you the freedom to step back.

Profit Plan

And once the team is in place, most owners look at their bank account and ask: where did all the money go? That is what a profit plan solves — clarity on what you earn, what you keep, and how to grow both.

All four have to work together. If one is missing, you cannot have more profit, more freedom, and outstanding results for your clients at the same time.

Intake Assessment

Every incoming call and email goes through Natalie personally, with no formal process behind it yet. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Natalie personally handles every intake call and inquiry — there is no delegation in place yet
  • The one team member who could eventually take on intake was hired only three weeks ago and is still being trained
  • No CRM or intake tracking system exists to show where inquiries come from or where they are lost
  • No after-hours coverage — a referral reaching out in the evening has nowhere to go until Natalie is personally available
Blind Spot: As the firm's target client shifts toward higher-asset cases, first impressions during intake matter more, not less — a delayed or informal response from a solo practice may not read as attentive, it may read as under-resourced.
The Positive
  • Natalie's personal involvement in every intake call today means no prospective client has ever fallen through the cracks due to a handoff error — every relationship starts with her directly
The Gap
  • Every hour Natalie spends on intake is an hour not spent on billable, high-asset case work — at $450+/hr, that is real revenue left on the table
  • Without a defined follow-up sequence, any referral who does not sign immediately has no structured path back toward becoming a client
  • As referral volume grows, there is no process in place to keep up without adding significant hours to Natalie's day
Delegating intake — backed by real SOPs for her assistant — is the first system Natalie needs so she can spend her time on the high-asset clients and cases she actually wants, not on answering every call herself.

Profit Plan Assessment

There is currently no documented profit plan behind the push to $1M/yr — just a monthly revenue range and an hourly rate Natalie has already identified as unsustainable for the clients she no longer wants to serve. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: RED
"unsustainable for clients earning <$100k/yr"
The Problem
  • No profit plan exists — revenue is described as a monthly range, not a documented target with a path to it
  • Her current fee structure ($450+/hr) is, by her own account, unsustainable for clients earning under $100k/yr — the stated reason for the shift to high-asset cases
  • Reaching the $1M/yr goal requires a materially higher average case value than her current 15–20 cases a month, and that gap has not yet been quantified
  • No tracking of cost per case, overhead, or take-home profit separate from gross revenue was discussed
Blind Spot: The case types generating your highest hourly billings today are not necessarily the ones that will get you to $1M/yr fastest — without cost-per-case-type visibility, more revenue does not automatically mean more profit.
The Gap
  • Without a profit plan, hitting $1M/yr in revenue does not guarantee any more actual take-home than today
  • The unsustainable hourly model already identified as a problem has not yet been replaced with a defined fee structure for high-asset cases
  • No visibility into which case types are most profitable makes it difficult to know which referrals to prioritize as the practice narrows
A profit plan built around the high-asset case values Natalie is targeting is what turns a revenue goal into real, predictable wealth — not just more billable hours.
