Intake Assessment

Every new client at CFS Attorneys is fielded personally by one of four attorneys already carrying a full caseload — there is no dedicated intake function, no tracking system, and no process to catch what a partner misses. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Partners personally handle every intake call and inquiry — there is no delegation, no intake coordinator, and no backup coverage
  • No CRM or intake tracking system was described — new client requests depend entirely on partner memory and available time
  • No after-hours or overflow coverage exists — a Friday-evening cannabis licensing inquiry has to wait for a partner to notice it
  • Recurring administrative drafting work (licensing and compliance documents) competes directly with intake and billable time for the same partner hours
Blind Spot: You may not realize how much administrative drafting work is quietly functioning as an intake bottleneck — every hour a partner spends on a licensing document is an hour a new inquiry sits unanswered.
The Gap
  • Every inquiry that comes in while a partner is in court, in a client meeting, or drafting paperwork risks going unanswered until they resurface
  • No process exists to prioritize a high-value cannabis licensing inquiry over a lower-value one — everything gets the same ad hoc treatment
  • Without a tracking system, there's no way to know how many inquiries the firm is actually losing before they ever become a client
An AI-run intake process is exactly the kind of modern, AI-forward capability Blake wants CFS Attorneys known for — and it starts by taking drafting and follow-up off partner plates, not by hiring more people.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • No confirmation of mobile responsiveness could be made this session, adding friction for anyone reaching out from a phone

Status: RED
The Problem
  • No administrative, marketing, or operations staff exists — all non-legal work falls to the attorneys themselves
  • No accountability structure or management layer beyond the partners
  • Blake Mensing's own compensation is processed by handwritten check, sometimes issued weeks late — a sign the firm's back office runs on an ad hoc basis
  • Blake's contract with the firm expires in October, and he is weighing a move to a more AI-forward firm — a retention risk tied directly to whether CFS modernizes its operating model before then
Blind Spot: Blake's willingness to consider leaving over how the firm operates — not over the work itself — is the clearest signal that CFS's lack of systems isn't just inefficient. It's now a retention risk for the very partner pushing hardest to modernize the firm.
The Gap
  • No partner can step away from the firm without leaving casework and admin work unattended
  • Blake's frustration with informal back-office processes is a flight risk that compounds every month nothing changes
  • Growth in caseload right now just means more hours for the same four people, not more capacity
Blake isn't asking for more marketing — he's asking for a firm that runs like the AI-forward practice he wants to be able to point to before October. Building that is what keeps him, and gives every partner real time back.

Profit Plan Assessment

No profitability tracking, revenue figures, or financial systems were discussed on the call — and Blake's own pay, processed by handwritten check and sometimes weeks late, suggests the firm's financial operations are as informal as its admin work. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • No revenue figures, profitability data, or financial visibility were shared or appear to exist in a structured form
  • Partner and of-counsel compensation is handled manually, with Blake's own pay described as unpredictable and sometimes weeks late
  • No dedicated bookkeeping, invoicing system, or financial dashboard was described
  • No marketing, coaching, or systems budget has ever been allocated — every dollar the firm makes goes through an informal process
Blind Spot: You may not realize that a firm running this informally on the back office almost never has a clean read on which practice area — cannabis regulatory work, M&A, or debt collection — actually makes the most money per hour billed.
The Gap
  • Without financial visibility, no one at the firm can say with confidence what growth is actually worth in take-home terms
  • Manual, delayed payroll erodes trust with the very partner most motivated to keep modernizing the firm
  • Every new referral adds more billable hours without a system to convert that into more predictable personal income for the partners
Building real financial visibility alongside the firm's new AI systems means growth finally shows up in partner pockets — not just in the caseload.
