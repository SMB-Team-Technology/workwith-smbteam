Here Is Where O'Brien Legal Stands Today

O'Brien Legal is a four-year-old estate planning, elder law, probate and trust administration firm in Moosic, PA, founded by Corey D. O'Brien and growing from a northeastern Pennsylvania practice to a statewide one, with revenue climbing from $500K in year one to a projected $1.6M this year. A broad, high-spend marketing program — seminars, direct mail, TV, billboards, Google and Meta — keeps interest coming in, but roughly ten disconnected systems mean Corey cannot see which channel drives clients or where inquiries fall through after the first call.

Stage 6 is where O'Brien Legal can reach all 50 states without Corey doubling headcount or carrying every recurring chore himself — and it is exactly where we are taking you.

Where You Are Today

Stage 4: Small Business Manager
  • Seminars, direct mail, TV, billboards, Google Ads and Meta Ads all feed the firm, with seminars the number one lead source
  • Roughly ten systems — Clio, Pipedrive, QuickBooks, phones, Zoom and more — that do not communicate with each other
  • After Answer Connect takes a call there is no systematic follow-up, and the firm cannot say what its close rate is on inbound calls
  • Corey still records about 30 videos per session and covers overflow casework while planning a move from statewide to all 50 states by the end of 2029

Where You Need To Be

Stage 6: Law Firm Owner
  • The firm runs without you
  • Your income is no longer tied to your personal hours
  • Your time is yours to spend however you choose
  • This is where true freedom and true wealth become possible
The gap between a firm that works hard to fill the pipeline and one that runs itself is mostly connection, follow-up and measurement — not more marketing — and that is what lets Corey grow without proportional hiring.
Overall Growth Stage Assessment
O'Brien Legal
O'Brien Legal is operating at Stage 4: a growing small business with several lead generation channels and a capable in-office team, but with Corey still deeply involved in the recurring work. The most urgent limiting gap is internal, not competitive — disconnected systems, no attribution and no consistent follow-up mean the firm cannot tell which of its roughly $70,000 a month in marketing is working or which inquiries are slipping away. Closing that gap is the path to the growth Corey described: scaling across states without a proportional increase in hiring.
