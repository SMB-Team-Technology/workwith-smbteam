Intake Assessment

O'Brien Legal's intake starts well — Answer Connect captures each caller's name, phone, email and reason for calling — but it stops there, with no consistent follow-up and no way to see which inquiries became clients. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
"I can't ask somebody, okay, what's our close rate on calls? Don't know."
The Problem
  • After Answer Connect captures a caller's details, there is no systematic follow-up
  • The firm cannot see which inbound calls became clients or which marketing drove them
  • Seminar registrants receive only a day-before confirmation call — no text messages and possibly no emails
  • Follow-up depends on busy people remembering, which Corey tied to a one-star and a two-star review in the same week
Blind Spot: A 70% close rate once prospects are in the room means the highest-leverage work is getting more of the people already interested into the room — through confirmations, reminders and post-seminar follow-up — rather than buying more registrations.
The Positive
  • Prospects who reach a meeting close at about 70%, by Corey's account
  • Every call is answered and its contact details and reason for calling are captured
The Gap
  • Seminars draw roughly 100 people and produce 13 to 25 meetings, so every additional person moved from registered to in the room converts at a high rate
  • Without close-rate tracking on calls, the firm cannot tell which vendors and channels are producing clients
  • Open follow-up loops show up publicly as reviews, adding to the cost of each missed touch
Follow-up that happens automatically is exactly the "one less thing" you described not having to worry about.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The homepage form asks for a name and email to download a free guide; no full intake or scheduling form was observed above the fold, so the primary route to a consultation is a phone call
  • No live chat was detected on the site
  • Mobile PageSpeed of 46 means the page that seminar, mailer and TV responders land on may load slowly when they try to reach out from a phone

Team Assessment

O'Brien Legal has a capable in-office team — two other attorneys and three client service coordinators every day, plus an intern and part-time U.S.-based drafters — but recurring chores like follow-up reminders and video recording still depend on people remembering or on Corey himself. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"it's a reminder problem. Like they just forgot because they have a lot going on."
The Problem
  • Follow-up, reminders and seminar confirmations rely on busy people remembering rather than on a system
  • About ten tools — Clio, Pipedrive, QuickBooks, two phone systems, Zoom and more — do not communicate with each other, so information has to be moved by people
  • You record about 30 videos per session, roughly five hours each, a few times a month, and you cover overflow casework when the team is stretched
  • The plan to add attorneys in every state means headcount grows with revenue unless more of the recurring work moves to systems
Blind Spot: You said you have a ton of bandwidth for strategic growth because you hired correctly. That is a real strength — but bandwidth spent on recording videos and policing follow-up is bandwidth not spent on the 50-state expansion.
The Positive
  • You describe your team as good people you hired correctly, with five in the office every day
  • Part-time, U.S.-based drafters working remotely give the firm flexible capacity for document production
The Gap
  • Each new state adds people, tools and follow-up work, so without systems the firm's costs rise as fast as its revenue
  • Hours spent recording and reviewing recurring work are hours taken from growth planning
  • With no connected system, no one place shows the full client journey from first call to signed engagement
The goal is a firm that reaches every state without you doing more recurring work and without hiring in proportion to every new client.

Profit Plan Assessment

O'Brien Legal's revenue has grown every year — $500K, $750K, $900K and a projected $1.6M — while marketing runs at about $70,000 a month across five or six outside firms, but there is no channel-level view of what that spend returns. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"I need to be able to not do a lot of hiring, right? Because I need to be able to pay people and I can't just put everything into growth and marketing"
The Problem
  • With about $70,000 a month in marketing, there is no close rate, cost per client or channel-level return behind it
  • Marketing is spread across five or six outside firms with no single report tying their results to signed clients
  • Revenue and marketing-return figures live in different places, so spend cannot be tied directly to signed clients
  • Entering each new state becomes a spending decision made without per-channel data
The Positive
  • Four years of growth, from $500K in year one to a projected $1.6M this year
  • Corey evaluates new investments on cost and does not want to overspend on growth tools
The Gap
  • Without channel-level return there is no way to move dollars from weaker channels to stronger ones
  • Growth funded by adding marketing and headcount in step leaves less room to invest in expansion
  • Attribution is the missing piece between the marketing spend and a confident growth plan
The goal is a growth plan that largely funds itself — expansion paid for by using what the firm already spends more intelligently, not by spending more.
