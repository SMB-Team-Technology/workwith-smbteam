Pillar Health Scorecard

Lead Generation

  • Seminars, direct mail, TV, billboards, social, Google Ads and Meta Ads all feed the firm, run through five or six outside marketing vendors
  • Seminars are the number one lead source, and attendance has grown from about 20–30 people to about 100 per event since a dedicated seminar-marketing firm came on board
  • With about $70,000 a month going into marketing, there is no single view of which channel produces signed clients
Knowing which channel earns its keep is how Corey grows toward all 50 states without simply buying more of everything.

Intake

  • Answer Connect captures each caller's name, phone, email and reason for calling, but there is no systematic follow-up after that
  • Seminar registrants receive only a day-before confirmation call — no text messages and possibly no emails
  • The firm's close rate on inbound calls is not tracked, so it cannot see which calls became clients
Every inquiry that goes quiet is growth Corey already paid for — fixing it adds clients without adding headcount or ad spend.

Self-Managing Team

  • Two attorneys and three client service coordinators work in the office every day, backed by part-time U.S.-based drafters and an intern
  • Corey describes missed follow-up as a reminder problem, not a people problem — the team needs a system, not more effort
  • Corey still records about 30 videos per session and covers overflow casework himself
Systems and AI agents that carry the recurring work are how the firm scales without Corey hiring in proportion to every new client.

Profit

  • Revenue has grown from $500K in year one to $750K, $900K and a projected $1.6M this year
  • Marketing spend of about $70,000 a month has no channel-level return or cost-per-client behind it
  • Corey wants to reinvest in growth but not put everything into marketing and hiring, so each new dollar needs to be placed on evidence
A growth plan that largely funds itself is what makes expansion into new states sustainable.
