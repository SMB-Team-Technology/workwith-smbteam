Intake Assessment

Hector flagged after-hours coverage and manual casework as two of the biggest drains on the firm's capacity — and both live squarely in intake. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • After-hours calls for criminal defense matters — often the most time-sensitive — go uncovered
  • Probate casework involves 27 of 39 steps that are still done manually, one file at a time
  • No automated connection between intake and the firm's practice management system — data has to be manually re-entered
  • No defined, systemized follow-up sequence was described for leads who don't retain immediately
Blind Spot: You may not realize how much of the 27-of-39-step probate process is genuinely repeatable — not case-specific judgment calls, but the same administrative steps every time. That's exactly the kind of work AI automation is built to absorb.
The Gap
  • Every after-hours call that goes to voicemail is a potential client one call away from a competitor who answers
  • Manual re-entry and manual case-prep steps mean the team's time is spent on repetitive work instead of on cases and clients
  • There's no way to know today exactly how much capacity is lost to manual steps — because it's never been measured
Automating the repeatable parts of intake and case prep is the fastest way to free up the capacity Hector needs to hit 3x — without adding headcount.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • No visible contact form on the homepage — the site relies entirely on phone-first calls-to-action
  • No live chat present
  • Combined with no after-hours phone coverage, prospects who visit outside business hours currently have no way to reach the firm at all

Team Assessment

Hector, as COO, is already carrying much of the day-to-day operational weight — but every new initiative, including the one discussed on this call, still has to clear H. Benjamin's personal approval before it moves forward. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Every new operational investment — including the one being evaluated here — requires H. Benjamin's personal sign-off before it can move forward
  • Hector is the sole driver of process improvements; there's no secondary layer between him and the attorneys handling casework
  • The firm's growth goal — 3x revenue in a year — hasn't been translated into a leadership or staffing plan
  • A prior vendor proposal (roughly $50k) was rejected because the firm found it was already doing that work in-house — real awareness, but not yet the tools to formalize it
Blind Spot: The instinct to say no to that $50k proposal wasn't wrong — the firm really was already doing that work. The blind spot is assuming the only alternative to an expensive outside vendor is doing everything manually forever. There's a middle path.
The Positive
  • Hector already functions as a real COO — someone actively driving operational improvement, not just a title
  • The firm has evaluated and rejected spend it judged genuinely redundant, showing real financial discipline
The Gap
  • Every decision that has to route through H. Benjamin personally is a decision that moves at the speed of his calendar, not the business's
  • Without a formal automation or leadership layer, hitting 3x means Hector personally scaling his own bandwidth — which doesn't scale
A firm that runs itself starts with removing Hector as the single point of failure for every operational improvement — not by adding more people, but by giving the team tools that don't need his direct hand on every task.

Profit Plan Assessment

Hector's goal is clear — 3x revenue within a year — but nothing about cost, margin, or what growth would actually take to execute came up on the call. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"3x revenue within one year"
The Problem
  • No annual revenue figure, case value, or close rate was discussed on the call — there's no financial baseline to plan against
  • The 3x growth goal doesn't yet have a plan behind it for what needs to change operationally to support 3x the volume
  • No mention of tracking cost-per-case or profitability by case type
  • Growth capacity today is capped by manual work, not by pipeline — the cases achievable per attorney-hour at current process won't simply triple on their own
Blind Spot: Growth and profit are not the same thing. If the same repetitive, manual steps are still happening three times over, the firm could hit the revenue goal and still not have more profit — or more time — to show for it.
The Positive
  • The firm has already shown real financial discipline once, walking away from a $50k proposal it judged as unnecessary spend rather than paying for redundant work
The Gap
  • Without a profit plan, there's no way to confirm whether 3x revenue would actually mean 3x take-home, or just 3x the workload
  • No visibility into what it currently costs — in time or dollars — to handle a case start-to-finish makes it hard to know what's actually driving profit
Hitting 3x on paper means nothing if the extra revenue is eaten by extra manual hours — a real profit plan has to be part of getting Hector the freedom he's actually after.
