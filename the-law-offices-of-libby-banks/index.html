Here Is Where The Law Office of Libby Banks Stands Today

The Law Office of Libby Banks is a Scottsdale estate planning firm — wills, trusts, special needs trusts, and non-contested probate and trust administration — serving higher-net-worth families across roughly ten core zip codes. With a 70% consult-to-signed rate, a strong referral-partner base, and a team of ten, the firm is running at about half of its capacity, and the owner's own words are that marketing and referral efforts are not growing volume the way the added attorney capacity was meant to.

Stage 6 is where the firm is profitable enough to run — and one day be handed over — without you at the center of it, on your own terms, and it is exactly where we are taking you.

Where You Are Today

Stage 4: Small Business Manager
  • Referral partners drive roughly half of new prospects, while search and social marketing are not producing the way they used to
  • The firm is running at about 50% of capacity — around six estate plans a week with room for fifteen
  • Two associate attorneys were added to scale the firm, but no business-development targets exist yet to turn their capacity into new clients
  • Marketing runs through several independent contractors, and tracing a new client back to its true source is still a manual dig

Where You Need To Be

Stage 6: Law Firm Owner
  • The firm runs without you
  • Your income is no longer tied to your personal hours
  • Your time is yours to spend however you choose
  • This is where true freedom and true wealth become possible
The gap between a firm at half capacity and a firm profitable enough to run without you is not about working harder — it is about turning the capacity you have already built into predictable, profitable growth, and that is exactly what this plan is for.

The Law Firm Growth Acceleration Model

We Help Law Firm Owners Get More Profit, More Personal Freedom, And Outstanding Results For Their Clients.

Most law firms try to grow by chasing more leads. But leads alone are not the problem — and leads alone are not the answer. Here is what actually works.

Lead Generation

Leads are to a law firm what gasoline is to a car. Without them, you go nowhere. But a steady, predictable flow of leads changes everything — you get to choose your cases instead of taking whatever comes in.

Intake

But all the leads in the world are worthless without a system to turn them into paying clients. Most firms are losing cases they already paid for simply because their follow-up is slow or inconsistent.

Self-Managing Team

Once clients are coming in, you need a team — otherwise you end up working 90-hour weeks and results suffer. A self-managing team is what gives you the freedom to step back.

Profit Plan

And once the team is in place, most owners look at their bank account and ask: where did all the money go? That is what a profit plan solves — clarity on what you earn, what you keep, and how to grow both.

All four have to work together. If one is missing, you cannot have more profit, more freedom, and outstanding results for your clients at the same time.

"I think our internet SEO marketing is falling short of what it used to be."
The Problem
  • The site's security layer blocked our automated review tools, so this assessment relies on indexed page titles and the third-party SEO data shown on our discovery call rather than a live page-by-page walkthrough

Blind Spot: A site can rank in Google and still be invisible to AI assistants. ChatGPT and Gemini pull from clear, FAQ-style answers more than from traditional blog posts, so the content that once earned rankings may not be the content that earns AI recommendations.
The Positive

The Gap
  • A site drawing roughly 430 visitors a month cannot carry a stronger paid-media program on its own — ads need pages built to turn clicks into consults
  • Not appearing when a prospect asks an AI assistant who the best estate planning attorney is means those prospects never reach your name
  • Dedicated pages for other nearby affluent cities were not seen in search results, and whether the site has FAQ-style answer content still needs to be confirmed

The firm has a real foundation — a dedicated intake person, a very high show rate, and roughly 70% of consults signing — but the way leads are tracked and the front desk is staffed still leave conversion and insight on the table. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
"The answer is yes, but I have to dig to get to it."
The Problem
  • Tracing a signed client back to the marketing that produced it takes manual digging, and the dashboard that would show it automatically is still being set up
  • When new clients are asked how they found the firm, they tend to remember only the last touch, so the source data you collect is incomplete by nature
  • The front-desk role is in replacement mode, a hand-off point where first impressions and calls can slip
  • About one in ten consults goes quiet before signing, and while the rest go on a follow-up drip, there is no stated target for how many come back

The Positive

  • About 95% of booked consults show up and roughly 70% sign, strong numbers for estate planning
The Gap
  • Every additional signed plan is worth about $4,500, so even a few points of conversion on existing consult volume compounds quickly
  • Without source-level tracking, ad dollars and referral-partner effort cannot be ranked by return — the very question behind why marketing has not grown volume more than it has
  • The consults that disappear are the cheapest leads to recover, because the firm has already paid to attract them
A tracked, repeatable intake process is what lets consult volume rise without everything routing back through you.

Profit Plan Assessment

Revenue has grown steadily and Libby works from a detailed budget, but the second attorney hire pushed payroll to roughly half of revenue, and the profit that makes a firm sellable has not followed yet. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"I have been focused on trying to get us more profitable. That's a huge issue to being able to … sell or to be able to step away is it's got to be profitable without … me."
The Problem
  • Payroll with benefits takes roughly half of revenue while the firm runs at about half of its capacity
  • Margin is still described in rough terms — a budgeted cushion that might be called profit — rather than a tracked, planned number
  • Marketing is held to about 9% of gross revenue, but without source-level tracking it is unclear which of those dollars produce the most signed plans
  • Estate plans are priced from about $3,000 for singles to $5,000–$7,000 for couples, and while you see room to raise prices, an increase has not been tested
Blind Spot: Because fees are collected 100% up front, cash can look healthy even when margin is thin — the cost of delivering each plan arrives over the weeks that follow. Planned margin, not cash in the bank, is the number that tells you what the firm is worth.
The Positive
  • Flat fees collected up front mean essentially no collections risk, and revenue has grown 15–20% a year for the last two to three years
  • A line-by-line annual budget and a weekly leadership meeting give the firm more financial discipline than most firms its size
The Gap
  • With the team at about half of capacity, additional signed plans can add revenue with relatively little added payroll — the fastest route to margin
  • A modest price increase falls almost directly to profit, because it adds revenue without adding cost to deliver
  • Without a financial leader focused on margin, the number that decides whether the firm can be sold stays fuzzy
Knowing and planning the profit number is what makes this a firm you can sell, hand over, or step back from on your own terms.
