Intake Assessment

The firm has a real foundation — a dedicated intake person, a very high show rate, and roughly 70% of consults signing — but the way leads are tracked and the front desk is staffed still leave conversion and insight on the table. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
"The answer is yes, but I have to dig to get to it."
The Problem
  • Tracing a signed client back to the marketing that produced it takes manual digging, and the dashboard that would show it automatically is still being set up
  • When new clients are asked how they found the firm, they tend to remember only the last touch, so the source data you collect is incomplete by nature
  • The front-desk role is in replacement mode, a hand-off point where first impressions and calls can slip
  • About one in ten consults goes quiet before signing, and while the rest go on a follow-up drip, there is no stated target for how many come back
Blind Spot: Free initial estate planning consults keep the calendar full, but a booked consult is not a signed plan. Tracking which sources produce signed plans — not just booked consults — shows where your best leads actually come from.
The Positive
  • A dedicated intake person, a legal answering service, and a follow-up drip give the firm a defined process rather than an owner answering every call
  • About 95% of booked consults show up and roughly 70% sign, strong numbers for estate planning
The Gap
  • Every additional signed plan is worth about $4,500, so even a few points of conversion on existing consult volume compounds quickly
  • Without source-level tracking, ad dollars and referral-partner effort cannot be ranked by return — the very question behind why marketing has not grown volume more than it has
  • The consults that disappear are the cheapest leads to recover, because the firm has already paid to attract them
A tracked, repeatable intake process is what lets consult volume rise without everything routing back through you.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

Team Assessment

The firm has grown to a team of ten with two associate attorneys, an intake person, paralegal support, and a team leader for administrative staff, and Libby is already taking Fridays off — real progress. But growth still leans on the owner's relationships, and business development has no targets. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"we decided to stretch and bring on that other attorney, but the business hasn't followed it the way we were hoping it would."
The Problem
  • The associate attorneys are expected to network through groups like BNI and the chamber of commerce, but there are no minimums, KPIs, or business-development targets
  • Referral relationships and LinkedIn outreach built by the owner still drive much of the new business, so growth is tied to her calendar
  • The firm is running at about half of capacity — roughly six estate plans a week against room for fifteen — so the team's cost is carried ahead of the revenue
  • A succession path to the associates is envisioned, but the timing is open and they are likely a couple of years from being ready
Blind Spot: You said you enjoy the work and do not want to step away completely. That is exactly why a self-managing firm matters: it turns stepping back into a choice you make, rather than an exit you are forced into.
The Positive
  • A weekly leadership meeting and a visible set of numbers give the team a rhythm many firms this size lack
  • A team leader supports administrative staff and the software and marketing stack, and in-house seminars keep hard costs low
The Gap
  • Without business-development targets, the associates' added capacity cannot be converted into revenue, and payroll keeps rising ahead of it
  • Until the associates can bring in their own clients, the firm's value to any future buyer — including them — still leans on the owner
  • Free time grows only when originating business is shared across the team instead of resting on one person's relationships
A firm whose attorneys generate their own business is one you can hand over, sell, or step back from without the revenue walking out the door with you.

Profit Plan Assessment

Revenue has grown steadily and Libby works from a detailed budget, but the second attorney hire pushed payroll to roughly half of revenue, and the profit that makes a firm sellable has not followed yet. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"I have been focused on trying to get us more profitable. That's a huge issue to being able to … sell or to be able to step away is it's got to be profitable without … me."
The Problem
  • Payroll with benefits takes roughly half of revenue while the firm runs at about half of its capacity
  • Margin is still described in rough terms — a budgeted cushion that might be called profit — rather than a tracked, planned number
  • Marketing is held to about 9% of gross revenue, but without source-level tracking it is unclear which of those dollars produce the most signed plans
  • Estate plans are priced from about $3,000 for singles to $5,000–$7,000 for couples, and while you see room to raise prices, an increase has not been tested
Blind Spot: Because fees are collected 100% up front, cash can look healthy even when margin is thin — the cost of delivering each plan arrives over the weeks that follow. Planned margin, not cash in the bank, is the number that tells you what the firm is worth.
The Positive
  • Flat fees collected up front mean essentially no collections risk, and revenue has grown 15–20% a year for the last two to three years
  • A line-by-line annual budget and a weekly leadership meeting give the firm more financial discipline than most firms its size
The Gap
  • With the team at about half of capacity, additional signed plans can add revenue with relatively little added payroll — the fastest route to margin
  • A modest price increase falls almost directly to profit, because it adds revenue without adding cost to deliver
  • Without a financial leader focused on margin, the number that decides whether the firm can be sold stays fuzzy
Knowing and planning the profit number is what makes this a firm you can sell, hand over, or step back from on your own terms.
