Here Is Where Erkan & Sullivan Stands Today

Erkan & Sullivan, P.C. is a two-partner criminal defense firm in Andover, Massachusetts built on a genuinely strong reputation — 154 five-star Google reviews and a landmark OUI case that entitled 27,000 people to new trials. But that reputation is nearly invisible online: the firm's current marketing vendor generates just 212 organic visitors a month, roughly a sixth of what direct competitors pull in, and the firm has never run a paid ad of any kind. The single most important finding is that Erkan & Sullivan's brand is already built — it just has no system turning that brand into a predictable flow of premium, high-margin cases.

Stage 6 is where a firm becomes selective — taking only the premium, high-margin cases that build the Erkan & Sullivan brand, while systems and a real team handle everything else — and that is exactly the market position you are building toward.

The Law Firm Growth Acceleration Model

We Help Law Firm Owners Get More Profit, More Personal Freedom, And Outstanding Results For Their Clients.

Most law firms try to grow by chasing more leads. But leads alone are not the problem — and leads alone are not the answer. Here is what actually works.

Lead Generation

Leads are to a law firm what gasoline is to a car. Without them, you go nowhere. But a steady, predictable flow of leads changes everything — you get to choose your cases instead of taking whatever comes in.

Intake

But all the leads in the world are worthless without a system to turn them into paying clients. Most firms are losing cases they already paid for simply because their follow-up is slow or inconsistent.

Self-Managing Team

Once clients are coming in, you need a team — otherwise you end up working 90-hour weeks and results suffer. A self-managing team is what gives you the freedom to step back.

Profit Plan

And once the team is in place, most owners look at their bank account and ask: where did all the money go? That is what a profit plan solves — clarity on what you earn, what you keep, and how to grow both.

All four have to work together. If one is missing, you cannot have more profit, more freedom, and outstanding results for your clients at the same time.

Profit Plan Assessment

Erkan & Sullivan's partners already think about profitability in specific terms — targeting $800/hour and pointing to clerk's hearings as a $2,000/hour opportunity — but no overall revenue baseline or case-mix tracking was discussed on the call. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"Target $800/hr for partners by focusing on high-margin work like clerk's hearings ($4k fee for 2 hrs work = $2k/hr)."
The Problem
  • No annual revenue figure was discussed or confirmed on the call — there is no clear financial baseline to measure growth against
  • Average case value (~$15,000) and effective hourly rates are known for specific case types, but there is no system tracking profitability by case type across the full caseload
  • The plan to add 25–30 cases a month has no stated connection yet to which case types those should be, risking a mix that increases revenue without increasing the profit-per-hour partners are targeting
  • No mention of tracking client acquisition cost, which will matter once paid marketing spend is introduced
Blind Spot: You may not realize that growing total case volume without a case-mix plan can actually pull partner effective hourly rate down, even while topline revenue goes up — volume and profitability are not the same goal.
The Positive
  • Partners already think in effective-hourly-rate terms for specific case types, like the $2,000/hr clerk's hearing example — a level of profit awareness many firms this size don't have yet
The Gap
  • Without a revenue baseline, it's difficult to know whether the firm's overall margin is actually improving as case value rises
  • Without case-mix tracking, the firm can't know today whether last month's caseload moved toward or away from the $800/hr target
  • Paid marketing, once launched, will need cost-per-case data layered on top of this to know if the return justifies the spend
$800/hr and a premium brand are both profit decisions — they get made deliberately, case by case, or they don't happen at all.
