Intake Assessment

Intake responsibility at Erkan & Sullivan currently sits with the two founding partners and admin support, with no defined process discussed on the call for how a surge in leads would be captured and converted. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • No defined intake process was discussed — inquiries appear to be handled by whoever has bandwidth among the partners and support staff
  • No after-hours coverage was mentioned, and the website has no live chat to capture visitors who reach out outside business hours
  • With a stated goal of adding 25–30 cases a month, there is no confirmed system in place to handle that volume without leads slipping through
  • No structured follow-up sequence for prospects who don't sign on the first call was discussed
Blind Spot: The growth plan calls for significantly more leads before it calls for a better system to catch them — without fixing that order, paid marketing will just generate more missed opportunities, not more signed cases.
The Gap
  • Every inquiry that isn't captured and followed up quickly is a $15,000–$30,000 case value walking to a competitor
  • Scaling to 25–30 cases a month without an intake system in place risks diluting client experience exactly when the firm is trying to build a premium brand
  • No after-hours coverage likely means losing prospects who search and call outside business hours to firms who do answer
A brand can only be as premium as the experience a prospect gets on their very first call — right now that experience isn't guaranteed.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • A contact form is present and reachable via navigation and multiple on-page CTAs, but its above-the-fold visibility could not be confirmed without a rendered browser session
  • No live chat is present on the site — visitors who want an immediate answer have no option beyond calling or filling out a form
  • The homepage CTA ("Request Your Consultation") is present above the fold, which is a genuine strength worth preserving in any redesign

These findings are also referenced in the Website Audit section.

Team Assessment

Erkan & Sullivan's growth plan depends on hiring a new associate to absorb additional case volume, but the two founding partners remain the firm's primary drivers of both casework and business development, with no defined structure yet for how work will be divided once that hire is made. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • No defined structure exists yet for dividing high-margin work, like clerk's hearings, between the founding partners and the incoming associate hire
  • Marketing, business development, and growth strategy currently run through the same two partners who also handle casework — there is no dedicated function for any of it
  • The support team has no clearly defined ownership of growth-related tasks based on what was discussed
  • There is no mention of a manager or lead attorney role that could absorb operational oversight as the firm scales toward 25–30 cases/month
Blind Spot: Hiring an associate solves for capacity, but not for structure — without a plan for how cases get routed and by what criteria, the new hire risks absorbing the same undifferentiated mix of work the partners are trying to move away from.
The Positive
  • The firm already has more team infrastructure in place than many firms this size — several named support staff are listed and profiled on the website, a foundation to build real structure on top of
The Gap
  • Without defined case-routing, the new associate hire could end up handling the same low-margin volume the partners want to shift away from — undermining the profitability goal, not advancing it
  • Partners tied up in every case, at every tier, limits how much of the high-end, judge-impressing brand work they can personally focus on
  • No dedicated marketing or growth function means every marketing decision competes directly with partners' billable time
Dominating a market takes more than more cases — it takes a team structured so the partners' time goes to the high-margin, brand-building work only they can do.

Profit Plan Assessment

Erkan & Sullivan's partners already think about profitability in specific terms — targeting $800/hour and pointing to clerk's hearings as a $2,000/hour opportunity — but no overall revenue baseline or case-mix tracking was discussed on the call. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"Target $800/hr for partners by focusing on high-margin work like clerk's hearings ($4k fee for 2 hrs work = $2k/hr)."
The Problem
  • No annual revenue figure was discussed or confirmed on the call — there is no clear financial baseline to measure growth against
  • Average case value (~$15,000) and effective hourly rates are known for specific case types, but there is no system tracking profitability by case type across the full caseload
  • The plan to add 25–30 cases a month has no stated connection yet to which case types those should be, risking a mix that increases revenue without increasing the profit-per-hour partners are targeting
  • No mention of tracking client acquisition cost, which will matter once paid marketing spend is introduced
Blind Spot: You may not realize that growing total case volume without a case-mix plan can actually pull partner effective hourly rate down, even while topline revenue goes up — volume and profitability are not the same goal.
The Positive
  • Partners already think in effective-hourly-rate terms for specific case types, like the $2,000/hr clerk's hearing example — a level of profit awareness many firms this size don't have yet
The Gap
  • Without a revenue baseline, it's difficult to know whether the firm's overall margin is actually improving as case value rises
  • Without case-mix tracking, the firm can't know today whether last month's caseload moved toward or away from the $800/hr target
  • Paid marketing, once launched, will need cost-per-case data layered on top of this to know if the return justifies the spend
$800/hr and a premium brand are both profit decisions — they get made deliberately, case by case, or they don't happen at all.
