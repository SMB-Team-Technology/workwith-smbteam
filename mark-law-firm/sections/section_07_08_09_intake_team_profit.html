Intake Assessment

Every call to Mark Law Firm lands on Michael’s own phone, and when he is in court, on another call or drafting, it goes unanswered with no system to catch it. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
"This past week alone, you know, I listened to my voicemail and there's like six or seven calls I get and there's no message there."
The Problem
  • You personally answer every call, with no receptionist, intake specialist or defined process behind you
  • Callers who reach voicemail often hang up without leaving a message, so you have no way to know who they were or to call them back
  • There is no system to track, follow up on or prioritize new inquiries, so an urgent will contest looks the same as a routine question
  • Calls that ring and ring with nobody answering, as one did during our demo, are a pattern you described yourself: “that happens too damn much”
Blind Spot: Someone searching for an attorney after a DWI stop or a family dispute over an estate rarely leaves a voicemail. They simply call the next firm on the list, and you never learn who you lost.
The Positive
  • You raised intake as a problem on our call, which means the biggest fix is one you are already motivated to make
  • A direct line to a 41-year trial attorney is a genuine selling point once every call is actually answered
The Gap
  • A single missed will contest can mean $50,000 to $60,000 in fees within 12 months, and even a simple DWI is $5,000 to $6,000
  • Six or seven silent calls in one week point to a steady stream of potential clients who never reach you
  • Every minute spent answering routine calls is time taken from the trial and drafting work that is billed at your full rate
Capturing every call is how you grow toward your $2 to $4 million goal without adding more hours to your own day.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The contact form (name, phone number and case details) sits mid-page, below the fold, so visitors have to scroll to find it
  • There is no live chat on the site so inquiries depend entirely on your phone being answered
  • The site has a single generic form for all practice areas, with nothing that routes will contests, DWI matters and injury cases differently

These findings are also referenced in the Website Audit section.

Team Assessment

Mark Law Firm is a team of one attorney with part-time typing help, so every call, complaint and follow-up task runs through Michael himself. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
"My team size is me, one attorney. I'm a solo. For 41 years, I've been around."
The Problem
  • You handle most of the work yourself: phone calls, placing calls and drafting complaints
  • With about 20 files you carry roughly 100 recurring tasks on your calendar, and you described being “overloaded with the tasks” rather than the number of files
  • Support is a part-time helper doing typing for about 10 hours a week, with no one trained to take on case work, scheduling or client follow-up
  • There is no delegation structure or accountability system, so if you step away the practice stops
Blind Spot: Hiring full-time staff at typical salaries is a big step, so the work stays with you. The cost is already being paid in hours you cannot bill and growth you cannot take on.
The Positive
  • You already use AI tools like Claude and have seen it save you roughly 20 hours on a single complaint, so you are open to technology that gives you leverage
  • You are not overloaded with case volume, only with tasks, so better support can make room for growth quickly
The Gap
  • Hours you could bill at $500 are spent on photocopies, envelopes and addresses that you described as $10-an-hour work
  • Any new cases you win today add to your personal workload rather than to a team that can handle them
  • Without trained support or a plan to hire it, you cannot grow beyond what one person can do in a day
Building the support around you is how your time moves to the work that is worth what your experience is worth.

Profit Plan Assessment

Michael has a clear income goal of $2 to $4 million a year, but we did not hear a financial plan, revenue figures or cost-per-case numbers on the call that show how the firm gets there. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"When we hang up, I have to go make photocopies, and I have to stuff a couple of envelopes… $10 an hour… I shouldn't be doing that."
The Problem
  • You described the goal clearly — “conservatively $2 to $4 million a year” — but also said, “I don’t know how to get there”
  • There is no revenue target broken down into cases, calls and marketing, so growth is not yet planned
  • You are cost sensitive and wary of adding subscriptions, which is wise, but without numbers it is hard to tell which investments pay back
  • Personal injury work has slowed, and the faster-closing municipal court and probate work you prefer has no system feeding it
Blind Spot: Your time bills at $500 an hour on some matters, yet a good part of it goes to tasks worth a fraction of that. Recovering even a few hours a week may be worth more than most marketing you could buy.
The Positive
  • You have already cut overhead meaningfully by closing the physical office and meeting clients in shared space
  • Your own figures show strong earning power per matter, from DWI work at $5,000 to $6,000 to probate cases billing $50,000 to $60,000
The Gap
  • Without a plan tying cases to revenue, there is no way to know how many more matters it takes to reach $2 million
  • Chasing the quicker, higher-fee cases you enjoy cannot be done on purpose until there is a steady flow of them
  • Every administrative hour you spend is an hour taken from billable work at your full rate
A profit plan turns your goal of being paid what your experience is worth from a hope into a number you can steer toward.
