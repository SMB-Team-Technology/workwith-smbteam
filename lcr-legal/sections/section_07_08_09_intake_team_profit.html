Intake Assessment

Intake at LCR Legal rests on a manual hand-off: once a client decides to move forward, the agreement, intake form and payment link go out through a paralegal the next day. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
"If the client says, yes, I want to move forward, my response is, well, I'll have my paralegal send you the rep agreement and intake form tomorrow. I don't like that. I want it to be sent right away. I want the payment link to be sent right away."
The Problem
  • The rep agreement, intake form and payment link are sent the day after a client says yes, not at the moment of the decision
  • Intake information lives across several tools — Clio for the case, Adobe for retainers and SharePoint for files — and you told us you are wary of putting all of your documents in Clio
  • Consultation calls are not recorded, so the details of what each prospect asked for are not captured automatically
  • The main contact form is on a separate Contact Us page, not on the first screen of the homepage
Blind Spot: Estate planning and probate are form-intensive, so every extra manual step is repeated on every matter. The same delay you see at sign-up shows up again when documents, notices and follow-ups are prepared.
The Positive
  • A live virtual assistant answers the firm's phones, so callers reach a person
  • You already know exactly what good intake looks like for your clients — an agreement and payment link sent right away — and the firm already has a case management system with intake tools in place
The Gap
  • Each day between “yes” and a signed agreement is a day a ready-to-hire client is waiting on a manual step
  • Paralegal time spent on repetitive hand-offs is billed by the hour, so it is time and money not spent on client work
  • Without a structured, captured first conversation, key details have to be gathered again later
Intake that responds the moment a client says yes lets you keep your personal touch while the paperwork takes care of itself.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The homepage’s only call to action on the first screen is a “Contact Us Today” button; the form and phone number are on the Contact Us page, with a second consultation form further down the homepage
  • No live chat or instant-response option was observed on the site, so a visitor who wants to start right away has to fill out a form or place a call
  • The Court-less Processes page lists mediation at $275 an hour with a two-hour minimum and a $1,625 dissolution package, but we found no comparable pricing or intake path for estate planning, probate or business law

These findings are also referenced in the Website Audit section.

Team Assessment

LCR Legal deliberately runs on contractors rather than employees, which keeps overhead flexible but leaves the owner as the connection point between every attorney, paralegal and deadline. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"I found that the easiest way to kind of run my firm is to do it with contractors versus actually having employees."
The Problem
  • Paralegal help is uneven by design: one core paralegal works about 30 hours a week and others are brought in as needed, so availability shifts month to month
  • You told us it is hard to find trusted paralegals who are meticulous, and a required publication step was missed on one matter, adding a 90-day wait
  • Paralegal work is bought by the hour or in monthly hour packages, so the output depends on the hours purchased
  • Phones are covered by a virtual assistant and social media by an outside company, so several outside parties each handle a piece of the client experience
Blind Spot: A contractor-based team cannot hold the firm's processes in its collective memory the way in-house employees do. The process has to live in a system — which is why automation matters more here than at a firm with a large staff.
The Positive
  • The contractor model keeps fixed payroll low and lets the firm flex up and down with the work
  • You have a clear picture of what you want automated and where the firm's repeatable work is
The Gap
  • When a step depends on one person remembering it, you remain the safety net for every deadline
  • Hunting for and re-briefing reliable paralegal help costs time that could go to clients
  • You are working to get out of the courtroom, but the estate, business and IP work that replaces it will need dependable support behind it
A team and a set of systems that do not need you checking behind them is what lets you leave litigation behind and keep your personal touch.

Profit Plan Assessment

LCR Legal is in the middle of a transition from litigation toward packaged estate planning, business law and IP work, and you described this year as a hard one. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"I really don't have a high ROI in a lot of different things. I'm in that transition phase."
The Problem
  • Whether this year ends as one of your best or one of your worst depends on large probate matters wrapping up by January
  • Fees come from several models at once — statutory-rate probate fees, flat packages, hourly mediation and client payment plans — which makes monthly revenue harder to predict
  • Some clients pay over time on payment plans, which spreads the collection of fees out
  • Paralegal support is paid by the hour or by monthly hour packages, so cost rises with every manual task
Blind Spot: The flat-fee packages you plan to offer only improve profit if the time to deliver them is controlled. A package that takes many hours of manual paperwork earns less than the same package delivered with automated drafting.
The Positive
  • You are clear-eyed about the transition and already moving toward packaged offers that can be priced and planned
  • Large probate matters are in the pipeline
The Gap
  • Revenue that rests on a few large matters makes it hard to plan hiring and spending with confidence
  • Hourly paralegal spend narrows the margin on any flat-fee work that still needs manual steps
  • Until packaged work becomes a steady stream, the move away from litigation carries income risk during the transition
Predictable packaged work with lower delivery cost lets you leave litigation behind without betting the year on a handful of matters.
