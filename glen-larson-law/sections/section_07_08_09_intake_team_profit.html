Intake Assessment

Intake at Glen Larson Law is staffed and covered after hours, but every new case still waits on your personal authorization and response speed is not consistent. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
"And then I have to make the decision, like, to authorize it."
The Problem
  • You personally authorize every case your intake team brings in — there is no one else with signing authority
  • Speed to respond and follow-up are not consistent from one lead to the next
  • RingCentral, Salesforce and the other tools do not communicate with each other, so information has to be moved by hand
  • Small process misses are reaching clients — such as new-client contracts that still carried the firm's old address
Blind Spot: You told us signing authority could be delegated to a trusted team lead with only a slight dip in performance. The real cost of waiting is not the dip — it is the hours you spend every week on decisions someone else could be trained to make.
The Positive
  • A dedicated intake team and after-hours coverage are already in place, along with some automated handling of web submissions
  • Once a client is signed, you report the firm's closing rate is strong
The Gap
  • A lead that waits for authorization is a lead that can sign with another firm first
  • Disconnected tools mean missed steps that cost time and, on a contingency practice, delay fees
  • Your day is repeatedly interrupted by approvals that the process, not you, should be handling
Every authorization you no longer have to make is time you get back.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

Team Assessment

Glen Larson Law has a real organization — a pre-litigation department, a litigation team, a practice manager, a client experience team and outside finance support — but the managers underneath you have not yet been trained to run and optimize it, so the bottleneck stays with you. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
"The problem is also me, one of the observations is the bottleneck is actually me."
The Problem
  • Case authorization, cash transfers and even your calendar all run through you
  • Managers have not received the training you went through, and no one is responsible for making sure operations are optimized
  • Leaving for an extended stretch without new systems in place would put critical processes — including moving money between accounts — at risk
  • Your role is unclear: you are wearing many hats and an upper-level attorney hire has been open most of the year
Blind Spot: Your growth philosophy of staying around 25 people is a smart one — but it means the firm can only scale through management depth and systems, not headcount. That makes training the managers you already have the highest-leverage investment available.
The Positive
  • The structure already exists: defined departments, a practice manager, a customized Salesforce with dashboards and a bonus structure tied to performance
  • You are self-aware about where the bottleneck is and have set a clear goal of putting a plan in place within the next 90 days
The Gap
  • A fully offline two-week break by summer is not realistic while the firm depends on you for these decisions
  • Without a manager who owns optimization, tactical work stays messy and keeps consuming your time
  • Role clarity — knowing where your strengths belong in the firm — cannot come until others own the work you are carrying today
The time and freedom you told us you would take over a few points of margin depend on a team that can run without you.

Profit Plan Assessment

Glen Larson Law is a growing contingency-fee personal injury firm on pace to pass $3M this year, with financial metrics tracked in Salesforce and outside bookkeeping support — but growth has come in peaks and valleys rather than a steady, planned line. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"Super amazing month, crash."
The Problem
  • Results run in cycles — a standout month followed by a drop — which makes cash planning reactive
  • Financial metrics are tracked, but time is not, so the real cost of running each case and each department is hard to see
  • A cash reserve target exists, but it has not held steady through the swings
  • Margin and profit targets were not part of the conversation — the focus so far has been on revenue growth
Blind Spot: On a contingency-fee practice, a case that sits because of a missed process step is a fee that arrives months late. Operational gaps show up as profit timing problems long before they show up as a line item.
The Positive
  • Strong growth: from about $2.3M last year to a pace of more than $3M this year, with a clear 2027 target
  • You have a defined growth rhythm in mind — 8 to 10% a year is enough — and outside finance support already in place
The Gap
  • Without time tracking, you cannot see which parts of the operation cost the most to run
  • Without a monthly profit rhythm, strong months are not turned into a reliable reserve
  • Delayed cases and process misses quietly push revenue out and cost the firm in rework
A steadier, planned profit line is what lets you take a little less money for a lot more time.
