Lead Generation

Glen Larson Law has the lead flow it needs today — you told us you feel confident about your marketing systems, and case count is capped by team capacity, not demand. The opportunity here is protecting that flow with an owner for the vendors, a faster mobile site and a stronger review base, so it keeps working without pulling on your time.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • The mobile experience is slow — the homepage scored 42/100 for mobile performance in our lab test
  • PageSpeed scores: Mobile 42/100 — Desktop 80/100
  • The contact form sits in the middle-to-lower sections of the homepage; above the fold the only option is to pick up the phone
  • The testimonials page shows a feed of 5-star Google reviews, but the most recent dated testimonial we saw refers to a case from the end of 2023 and no overall rating or review total is displayed
Blind Spot: Most injured people search from a phone. A slow mobile page can lose a prospective client before they ever see the "Call Now" button — and you would never see that lost lead in your intake numbers.
The Positive
  • Strong content depth: dedicated Austin car accident and truck/18-wheeler pages, city pages, an actively updated blog and a Spanish-language homepage
  • A 24/7 call-now CTA, attorney bios and educational video all appear on the homepage, and the layout is mobile responsive
The Gap
  • A slow mobile page works against the content and rankings the firm has already built
  • Visitors who would rather submit a form than call — especially after hours — have to scroll to find one
  • Fresh, visible social proof is not carrying the weight it could for a firm with this many strong client reviews
A website that converts on its own is one more thing that works without Glen — and keeps your time free for the decisions only you can make.

Local SEO

The Problem
  • Based on what we could see, the firm appears to trail McMinn Law Firm, Wayne Wright Injury Lawyers and Shamieh Law on Google review volume — Google Business Profile details could not be loaded live during our research
  • Local map-pack positions for "car accident lawyer Austin TX" and "truck accident lawyer Austin TX" were not observed in this research
  • The firm moved offices about three months ago — directory listings we could not check (Avvo, Yelp, Justia, Chamber of Commerce, ReachAttorneys) should be audited for the old address, and the FindLaw profile shows no reviews
Blind Spot: An outdated address in even a few directories can quietly split your local ranking signals — and it is the same kind of detail that already showed up on new-client contracts.
The Positive
  • The firm's Austin car accident and truck/18-wheeler pages appear in organic results for the searches we ran, and the FindLaw listing matches the website's name, address and phone number
The Gap
  • Prospects comparing Austin PI firms see a much larger review base on competitor profiles than on this firm's
  • Address inconsistencies across directories after the move can hold back local visibility
McMinn Law Firm — appears alongside the firm in Austin car accident searches with a significantly larger Google review base, giving it the social proof edge when a prospect compares profiles.
Local visibility that is managed by someone else is local visibility that never lands on Glen's desk.

The Opportunity

  • Spanish-language terms, since the site already has a Spanish homepage and "Se Habla Español" displayed on the site

Blind Spot: Vendor-run campaigns need someone accountable for results. With the fractional CMO who managed the vendors now retired, no one is currently holding SEO and ad vendors to a standard — and you would only find out when lead quality slips.

Local Service Ads

LSA Presence
  • The Local Services Ads results could not be loaded in this research — whether the firm appears, and whether it is Google Screened, is not observed
  • LSA placement depends heavily on reviews and response speed, so both matter before an LSA investment makes sense
Competitor LSA Landscape
  • The firms holding Local Services Ads spots in Austin and their review counts were not observed
The Opportunity
  • A Google Screened Local Services Ads presence puts the firm in front of prospects at the exact moment they are ready to call
  • LSAs appear above everything else on Google — above paid ads, above the map pack, above all organic results — and you only pay when a real prospect calls you directly.
  • A larger review base is what moves a firm up inside Local Services Ads — so reviews and LSAs reinforce each other
Blind Spot: Local Services Ads reward review volume and fast response. Those are both areas where Glen Larson Law has room to grow — response speed is not yet consistent — so LSA only pays off once those pieces are tightened.
