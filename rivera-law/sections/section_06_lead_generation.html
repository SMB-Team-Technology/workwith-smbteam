Lead Generation

Rivera Law relies on a $125-per-week Google Ads budget, a per-referral relationship with Access Legal, and word of mouth — with no local SEO visibility to show for a three-practice-area, single-location firm in a competitive Chicago suburb. Across nine local searches spanning family law, immigration, and criminal defense, Rivera Law did not surface once, while named local competitors hold the visibility in every one of those searches.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • No contact form on the homepage — the site pushes phone and email only, with no low-friction way for a prospect to reach out after hours
  • PageSpeed scores could not be retrieved this pass (Google's public API returned a daily quota error on repeated attempts) — flagged to verify live before the proposal call rather than guessed at
  • No attorney bio, no video, and no blog on the site — nothing builds trust or credibility with a visitor beyond the homepage message
  • No geo-targeting pages exist for nearby cities like Hoffman Estates, Elgin, Palatine, or Arlington Heights, despite the firm serving that broader area
Blind Spot: Your homepage is entirely Spanish ("Bienvenido/a") with no English entry point — which is a real asset for your bilingual client base, but it also means any English-language searcher landing on your site sees no immediate confirmation you serve them too.
The Positive
  • The site is clean, professional, and clearly bilingual-first — a genuine reflection of the client base Rivera Law serves
  • A prominent above-the-fold phone number and free-consultation call to action make it easy for a ready-to-call visitor to take action
The Gap
  • Every visitor who isn't ready to call immediately — and doesn't want to fill out an email — has no way to convert, so those leads simply leave
  • Without attorney bios or credibility content, visitors comparing you against Francis Law Center or James G. Dimeas & Associates see far less reason to trust you before they ever pick up the phone
  • Missing geo pages mean the firm is invisible to searchers in surrounding towns who would otherwise become referral-quality clients
A website that converts around the clock is what lets you stop being the one who has to personally close every prospect who calls.

Local SEO

The Problem
  • Rivera Law did not surface organically in any of the nine local searches run across Family Law, Immigration, and Criminal Defense in Schaumburg
  • A NAP inconsistency exists live: the LawInfo directory lists a different phone number (773-368-1915) than the firm's actual line (847-348-0009), and no Avvo, Justia, or FindLaw profile could be found for the firm at all — three directories where every named competitor below has a visible presence
  • The firm's own Google review count could not be confirmed, even though the 4.7-star rating was corroborated across two sources — an open item to verify before the proposal call
Blind Spot: A "Rivera Law Firm, Inc." / "Rivera & Associates Law Firm" profile on BBB is tied to a Chicago address rather than Schaumburg — worth confirming whether that's a stale listing under an old address, since an unresolved duplicate can quietly confuse both prospects and Google's own local ranking signals.
The Positive
  • A 4.7-star rating is corroborated across two independent sources — a strong foundation to build visibility on, once it's properly claimed and consistent everywhere
The Gap
  • Without directory presence or local pack visibility, every practice area is ceding ground to competitors who show up first when prospects search
  • The missing Avvo, Justia, and FindLaw profiles mean lost referral-quality traffic from exactly the directories legal consumers use to vet a firm before calling
Sterling Lawyers, LLC — a dedicated Schaumburg office with 7,000+ reviews firm-wide and BBB A+ accreditation, consistently outranking Rivera Law for family law searches in its own market.
Fixing your local visibility means the reputation you've already earned finally starts working for you instead of staying invisible.

Blind Spot: Confirming whether your current $125/week is Search, LSA, or blended matters — you may be paying for underperforming clicks in one channel while missing out on pay-per-lead placement in the other.
