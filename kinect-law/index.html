Here Is Where Kinect Law Stands Today

Kinect Law, the Whitestone, NY intellectual property practice of the Law Offices of Albert Wai-Kit Chan, PLLC, handles trademark portfolios, patent counsel for biotech and pharma startups, and IP litigation through a four-partner team. The firm wants a steady stream of high-value B2B clients, but new business still depends on word-of-mouth and referrals, and a Google Business Profile that still carries an outdated name, no reviews, and no photos gives search very little to work with.

Stage 6 is where the pipeline of high-value B2B clients is predictable enough that the partnership stops depending on who refers them next — and it is exactly where we are taking you.

The Law Firm Growth Acceleration Model

We Help Law Firm Owners Get More Profit, More Personal Freedom, And Outstanding Results For Their Clients.

Most law firms try to grow by chasing more leads. But leads alone are not the problem — and leads alone are not the answer. Here is what actually works.

Lead Generation

Leads are to a law firm what gasoline is to a car. Without them, you go nowhere. But a steady, predictable flow of leads changes everything — you get to choose your cases instead of taking whatever comes in.

Intake

But all the leads in the world are worthless without a system to turn them into paying clients. Most firms are losing cases they already paid for simply because their follow-up is slow or inconsistent.

Self-Managing Team

Once clients are coming in, you need a team — otherwise you end up working 90-hour weeks and results suffer. A self-managing team is what gives you the freedom to step back.

Profit Plan

And once the team is in place, most owners look at their bank account and ask: where did all the money go? That is what a profit plan solves — clarity on what you earn, what you keep, and how to grow both.

All four have to work together. If one is missing, you cannot have more profit, more freedom, and outstanding results for your clients at the same time.

Lead Generation

Kinect Law's trademark, patent, and IP litigation work reaches new clients mostly through word-of-mouth and referrals, which makes the flow of new matters inconsistent. The firm's online presence is not yet doing the work of attracting high-value B2B clients: the Google Business Profile is outdated and the website has no pages built for the markets the partners want to serve.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • The homepage headline speaks to protecting intellectual property, but its only button, "Learn More," leads to a services page instead of inviting a call or consultation
  • PageSpeed scores: Mobile 61/100 — Desktop 94/100. The mobile largest-contentful-paint is 6.7 seconds, well past the point where visitors start to leave
  • There are no location-specific "Areas We Serve" pages, which limits visibility to Whitestone even though the firm wants Manhattan, Brooklyn, Nassau County, and Westchester clients
  • No blog, video, or client testimonials were observed, so a first-time visitor has little beyond the attorney bios to build confidence
The Positive
  • All four partners are listed with photos and titles, giving visitors a clear view of the team behind the work
  • Desktop performance is strong at 94/100, so the foundation is sound and mobile speed is the main fix
The Gap
  • A business owner who finds the site but sees no clear next step has no easy way to start a conversation
  • With no pages written for the surrounding markets, searches from Manhattan, Brooklyn, Nassau, and Westchester have nothing to land on
  • Slow mobile loading costs visitors before they ever read about the firm's patent and trademark experience
A website that turns search visitors into inquiries is the first step toward a pipeline that no longer depends on referrals.

Local SEO

The Problem
  • The Google Business Profile still shows the outdated name "Albert Chan" rather than Kinect Law, and is missing service categories
  • The profile has no reviews or photos, so it offers little proof of the firm's work when someone compares options on Google
  • Directory listings use different names ("Albert Chan," "Law Offices of Albert Wai-Kit Chan PLLC," "Kinect Law") and different suite numbers (Suite 604 on some, Suite 408 on the website), which weakens trust signals for search engines

Blind Spot: The firm has changed its name and suite over time, but older listings still carry the previous details. Search engines treat conflicting business information as a reason to trust a listing less, so cleaning it up can lift visibility before any new content is written.

The Gap

Intake Assessment

Kinect Law's inquiries have historically arrived through referrals, so the website has not had to do much converting, and how new inquiries are handled was not covered in detail on the discovery call. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • The only homepage button reads "Learn More" and leads to a services page, so there is no call or consultation action above the fold
  • Contact details are in the footer rather than on the page where a visitor decides to reach out
  • The main form on the site is a conflict-check questionnaire, which is built for existing relationships rather than for a new prospect asking a first question
  • The intake and follow-up process for inquiries from search was not established on the call, so it is an area to define before new traffic arrives
Blind Spot: Referred clients arrive already trusting the firm. Prospects who find Kinect Law on Google arrive as strangers comparing several firms, and the firm that responds first and makes the next step obvious usually wins the conversation.
The Positive
  • A conflict-check page already exists, which gives the firm a ready starting point for clearing new matters quickly
The Gap
  • A business owner who lands on the site and cannot see how to start a conversation will move on to the next firm
  • As search traffic grows, every inquiry without a clear path or fast reply is a high-value matter left on the table
  • Without a defined follow-up routine, the leads the firm works hard to earn can go cold
A clear path from search to first conversation turns new visibility into the steady, high-value client flow the partners are after.

Team Assessment

Kinect Law has four partners and additional attorneys, and the firm says it can take on more work and would hire if needed. What is missing is a clear owner and budget for growth, so marketing decisions wait on partner alignment. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • No one is named as the owner of marketing and client development, so it competes with billable work for partner attention
  • There is no marketing budget today, so any growth investment becomes a discussion across the partnership first
  • Several partners share leadership, which slows commitment to decisions that need a single point of accountability
  • The firm's exact team structure and roles beyond the four partners were not detailed on the call
Blind Spot: Having capacity for more work can hide the real constraint. Without someone accountable for bringing in the right clients, the extra capacity has nothing to fill it.
The Positive
  • The firm has capacity to handle more work and is open to hiring if demand grows
  • A strong bench of partners and attorneys across patents, trademarks, and litigation means the expertise is already in place
The Gap
  • Growth decisions that need group alignment move slowly, and every month of delay is a month of inconsistent client flow
  • Partners who carry client development on top of casework have less time for the work they would rather be doing
  • With no defined owner, follow-through on marketing depends on whoever has a free week
Giving growth a clear owner means the pipeline keeps moving without pulling every partner into every decision.

Profit Plan Assessment

Kinect Law has no formal budget or revenue projections, and cash flow is tight when clients pay slowly. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • There is no formal budget or revenue projection to plan around
  • Slow-paying clients leave cash flow tight, which makes any new investment feel heavier
  • There is no marketing budget, so there is no established way to judge what a client acquisition effort should cost or return
  • Without a target for what a new B2B matter is worth, it is hard to weigh marketing spend against the work it produces
Blind Spot: A marketing investment can feel like a "big nut" when there is no number to compare it to. A simple target for matters per month and revenue per matter turns the same figure from an expense into a decision.
The Positive
  • The partners know the kind of work they want: high-value B2B matters rather than high-volume, low-fee filings, which gives a clear basis for a plan
The Gap
  • Without projections, the firm cannot tell how much new business it needs to feel stable
  • Tight cash flow makes the timing of new client revenue more important and the cost of an inconsistent pipeline higher
  • With no numbers to anchor a growth decision, the partnership has to rely on judgment alone
A simple profit plan is what turns a steadier pipeline into the stable, predictable revenue the partners are after.
