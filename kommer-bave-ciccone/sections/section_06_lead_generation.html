Lead Generation

Kommer Bave & Ciccone LLP has built more than 40 years of reputation on referrals, but online it does not yet show up where divorce and custody clients search — the firm did not surface in any of the family-law or personal injury searches we ran for White Plains and Rockland County. Meanwhile, the Google Ads running today appear aimed at personal injury and wills and trusts, not the divorce, custody, and post-judgment enforcement work Rachel wants more of.

Website Audit

"I feel like I'm losing a lot of the market share or clients because of my less than stellar web page."
The Problem
  • The homepage headline, "Legal Insight. Trusted Advice.", names no practice area, city, or differentiator — a divorce client searching for help sees nothing about family law
  • PageSpeed scores (Lighthouse testing, the engine behind PageSpeed Insights): Mobile 44–67/100 across two runs — Desktop 71/100. Mobile load time (LCP) measured roughly 10 to 18 seconds
  • Family law is one of seven practice areas on a general-practice site, with no per-city or per-office pages for White Plains or New City, and post-judgment enforcement is not listed by name
  • Team photos and the attorney list are out of date, and the blog was last updated in March 2026 with elder-law news rather than family-law content
Blind Spot: The roughly 83 pages already on your site carry search value you have built over the years. A rebuild should reorganize that content by practice area and location rather than start from scratch.
The Positive
  • The site is mobile responsive and lists bios for all four attorneys
  • "Serving Clients Since 1979" and a clear call-to-action phone number sit above the fold, and the Family Law page already includes a free consultation form
The Gap
  • Visitors arriving from a divorce search see a generic general-practice homepage and no clear family-law-first path to a consultation
  • Slow mobile load times send away people searching from their phones, often at a stressful moment
  • Without location pages for White Plains and New City, Google has little reason to show the firm for Rockland searches
A website that matches the firm's real-world reputation is the first step toward marketing that works without taking more of Rachel's time.

Local SEO

The Problem
  • The Google Business Profile exists, but as shown on our call its primary category is not specific to family law — so Google did not surface the firm for "divorce attorney near me," even from the firm's own office address
  • The profile shows a building photo where an attorney headshot should be
  • The firm did not surface in the results returned for searches such as "divorce lawyer White Plains NY," "child custody attorney Rockland County NY," and "personal injury lawyer White Plains NY"
  • Search results indicate directory listings are inconsistent: some list a New Rochelle address for Rachel while the firm's site lists White Plains and New City, and several third-party profiles still show the old firm name, "Kommer Bave & Ollman, LLP"
Blind Spot: Rachel told us she has a large presence in Rockland, yet the New City office is listed "by appointment only" and the firm did not appear in Rockland searches — so Google has little local signal connecting the firm to Rockland clients.
The Positive
  • Rachel reports a high close rate once prospects come through the door, and the firm already has listings on FindLaw, Lawyers.com, and the Westchester Bar Association directory to clean up and build from
The Gap
  • Prospects searching for divorce and custody help near White Plains or New City are finding other firms first
  • Mixed addresses and old firm names across directories weaken the local trust signals Google relies on
Eisenberg Yellen LLP and the Law Offices of James J. Sexton — both New City divorce and family-law firms surfaced in our Rockland divorce searches, where Kommer Bave & Ciccone did not.
Showing up on the first page when someone searches for a divorce attorney near them means the firm's reputation does the selling — not Rachel's calendar.

Google Ads

Ad Presence
  • When Rachel reviewed the firm's Google Ads account on our call, she said she mostly sees personal injury campaigns. Exact headlines and descriptions were not captured.
  • Campaigns also appear to be running for wills and trusts. Those are real practice areas for the firm, but not the family-law work — divorce, custody, and post-judgment enforcement — that Rachel wants more of.
  • On a live "divorce attorney near me" search from the firm's office address during our call, the firm was not observed among the ads shown.
  • Rachel does not know how much of the roughly $2,900 monthly spend goes to ads versus fees, and performance is unknown.
Competitor Ad Landscape
  • Two advertisers held the sponsored positions on that live search, and Rachel's own assessment was that neither ad was strong.
  • Competitor ad copy and spend for Westchester and Rockland family-law terms could not be captured in our research; a live auction review at kickoff will show who is bidding on the terms that matter.
The Opportunity
  • Divorce, custody, and post-judgment enforcement searches tied to White Plains, New City, and Rockland County — the three case types Rachel wants most
  • Dedicated landing pages for each case type, so every click lands on a page about the exact problem the prospect searched for
  • Urgency searches such as emergency custody and enforcing a divorce judgment — high-intent moments when a prospect needs a lawyer right now
  • Keeping family-law campaigns and pages separate from personal injury protects the premium matrimonial brand Rachel is concerned about, while still leaving room to add personal injury carefully
Blind Spot: You may not realize how much of a combined ads-and-website budget can go to campaigns for services that are not your priority — every click on a wills or personal injury ad is a click that is not a divorce or custody consultation.

Local Service Ads

LSA Presence
  • We did not observe the firm in Local Service Ads. Rachel believes her current campaigns are pay-per-click rather than LSA.
  • Google Screened status and LSA review count were not observed — this is an opportunity gap, not a confirmed absence.
Competitor LSA Landscape
  • A divorce-focused firm in the White Plains area was reported on our call to be running Local Service Ads for divorce searches in the firm's market. Its review count could not be verified in our research.
  • No other competitor LSA activity could be verified in our research.
The Opportunity
  • Family-law LSAs are a natural first paid channel: pay-per-lead for divorce, custody, and post-judgment enforcement searches across Westchester and Rockland.
  • LSAs appear above everything else on Google — above paid ads, above the map pack, above all organic results — and you only pay when a real prospect calls you directly.
  • LSA performance leans on reviews and a clean Google Business Profile, so the profile fixes and a simple review process are part of the same plan.
Blind Spot: LSAs reward fast responses — a missed call is a lead handed to the next firm listed, so someone needs to be ready to answer promptly.

Facebook & Instagram Ads

Ad Presence
  • No active Facebook or Instagram ads were observed for the firm. Meta's ad library could not be queried directly in our research, so this should be confirmed live.
  • The firm maintains a Facebook page (KBCLLP), but we did not see paid social tied to family law.
Competitor Ad Landscape
  • Competitor Meta ad activity in the Westchester and Rockland family-law market could not be verified in our research.
  • A live Ad Library review at kickoff will show which firms are advertising, in what formats, and for how long.
The Opportunity
  • Retargeting — website visitors who did not convert are leaving with no follow-up
  • Video ads featuring the attorney — builds trust before a prospect ever calls
  • Educational content ads — capture leads earlier in the decision process
  • Family-law-only audiences and messaging, with Rockland-specific content that reflects Rachel's community expertise, kept separate from personal injury
