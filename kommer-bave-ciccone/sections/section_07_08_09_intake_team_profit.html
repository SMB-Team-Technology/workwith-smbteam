Intake Assessment

Rachel takes consultations herself between court appearances, and her close rate is strong once a prospect reaches her — the opportunity is getting more prospects that far and following up with those who do not sign right away. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
"I just need them to get one… Once get through the door, I'm good."
The Problem
  • You personally take consultations between court appearances, so intake competes with courtroom time
  • The homepage offers a phone number only — the consultation form sits on the Family Law page
  • The Family Law form notes that "a consultation fee may apply," which may give prospects comparing firms a reason to call someone else first
  • No structured follow-up, review request, or nurture process was described for prospects who do not sign right away
Blind Spot: Divorce prospects often reach out to several firms at once, so a slow or missed reply can hand the consultation to a competitor before you ever know it happened.
The Positive
  • You close at a high rate once a prospect gets through the door
  • Referral relationships, including rabbinical courts, send clients who already arrive trusting the firm
The Gap
  • Every consultation you take personally is time not spent in court or on briefs
  • Prospects who do not sign right away are not being nurtured, so leads the firm has already earned can slip away
  • Without a clear online path to book, some prospects who would have called never do
A consistent intake path means more of the clients you earn the chance to serve — without taking more of your time.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The homepage call to action is a phone number, (914) 633-7400; there is no contact form on the homepage
  • The Family Law page includes a "Free Consultation" form (name, email, phone, situation, contact preference), with a note that a consultation fee may apply when meeting with the attorney
  • No live chat was detected on the site, so there is no way to capture a visitor outside of a phone call or the Family Law page form

These findings are also referenced in the Website Audit section.

Team Assessment

Rachel is in court every day and fills the rest of her time with consultations and briefs, while the firm needs a personal injury hire and a paralegal right away. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"I need to sort of get my house in order before I start taking on additional people, although I do need a personal injury person and paralegal immediately."
The Problem
  • You are in court every day, and the rest of your time goes to consultations and writing briefs
  • A paralegal recently left the firm, and a personal injury hire and a paralegal are both needed immediately
  • You want to get the firm's house in order — roles, process, and systems — before taking on additional people, and no documented plan for that was described
  • Adding personal injury while protecting a premium matrimonial brand takes a team that can handle both without pulling you back in
Blind Spot: Adding people before the processes are in place can simply add more people who need your time. The order matters: process first, then hires.
The Positive
  • You already recognize that the firm's house needs to be in order before adding people — that instinct is the right one
  • The firm has a stable base of four listed attorneys and more than 40 years of continuity
The Gap
  • Every hour in court or in consultations is an hour not spent building the firm
  • Without documented roles and processes, each new hire takes more of your time to train before they take any off your plate
  • Hiring urgency can force decisions before the firm is ready for them
Until the firm can run without you in every room, your time stays the limiting factor on how far it can grow.

Profit Plan Assessment

Revenue and margin figures were not shared on the call, so this assessment reflects what Rachel told us about her case mix and her current marketing spend. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"So the matrimonials are more profitable than the run-of-the-mill family court, but there's a lot of crossover."
The Problem
  • Revenue, margins, and cost per new client were not discussed, so it is unclear which channels and case types are earning their keep
  • About $2,900 a month goes to Google Ads and website management combined, with no known split between ad spend and fees
  • The performance of the current advertising is unknown, so the return on that spend cannot be measured
  • Retainers vary widely by case type — from about $5,000 for a child support modification to $10,000–$25,000 for a divorce — so which cases marketing brings in matters as much as how many
Blind Spot: A lead for a divorce is not worth the same as a lead for a run-of-the-mill family court matter, so campaigns aimed at the wrong case types can fill the calendar with less profitable work.
The Positive
  • You know matrimonial work is the more profitable part of your practice
  • Divorce retainers in the $10,000–$25,000 range give the firm real room to invest in acquiring the right clients
The Gap
  • Spend without a tracked return makes it hard to decide how much to invest, or where
  • Marketing aimed at personal injury and wills and trusts may be pulling attention away from higher-value divorce and custody cases
  • Without a view of cost per consultation and cost per signed case, adding more spend is a guess
Knowing which cases and channels pay best is how the firm grows its profit without adding more hours to your week.
