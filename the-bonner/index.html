Here Is Where The Bonner Law Firm Stands Today

The Bonner Law Firm is a Houston personal injury practice that has served the community for more than 40 years, focused exclusively on car and truck accidents, slip and fall, and dog bite cases. Today, new clients come mainly from word-of-mouth, TV and radio, and billboards, with 40 to 50 active cases moving through the office each month. The most important finding is that a firm with this much history has no consistent, measurable digital system to turn its reputation into a steady flow of new clients.

Stage 6 is where the firm brings in steady, predictable new clients and runs on a team and systems rather than on your effort alone — and it is exactly where we want to take The Bonner Law Firm.

Where You Are Today

Stage 3: Solo Practitioner
  • Lead flow is inconsistent and depends on word-of-mouth plus TV, radio, and billboards
  • There is no formal intake process beyond an online sign-up form, and after-hours calls go to a recorded message
  • Case files are paper-based, which blocks digital marketing, review generation, and easy follow-up
  • A three-person team carries 40 to 50 active cases each month, with no systems that let the firm run without constant owner effort

Where You Need To Be

Stage 6: Law Firm Owner
  • The firm runs without you
  • Your income is no longer tied to your personal hours
  • Your time is yours to spend however you choose
  • This is where true freedom and true wealth become possible
The gap between where the firm is and where it wants to be is closable — it starts with a steady lead system, a defined intake process, and a team that does not depend on one person's effort.

The Law Firm Growth Acceleration Model

We Help Law Firm Owners Get More Profit, More Personal Freedom, And Outstanding Results For Their Clients.

Most law firms try to grow by chasing more leads. But leads alone are not the problem — and leads alone are not the answer. Here is what actually works.

Lead Generation

Leads are to a law firm what gasoline is to a car. Without them, you go nowhere. But a steady, predictable flow of leads changes everything — you get to choose your cases instead of taking whatever comes in.

Intake

But all the leads in the world are worthless without a system to turn them into paying clients. Most firms are losing cases they already paid for simply because their follow-up is slow or inconsistent.

Self-Managing Team

Once clients are coming in, you need a team — otherwise you end up working 90-hour weeks and results suffer. A self-managing team is what gives you the freedom to step back.

Profit Plan

And once the team is in place, most owners look at their bank account and ask: where did all the money go? That is what a profit plan solves — clarity on what you earn, what you keep, and how to grow both.

All four have to work together. If one is missing, you cannot have more profit, more freedom, and outstanding results for your clients at the same time.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • The contact form lives on a separate page rather than on the homepage, so a visitor who is not ready to call has no quick way to reach out
  • Page speed scores could not be captured at the time of this report — a live mobile and desktop speed test is recommended to confirm load times
  • The blog has only 16 posts and none display a publish date, so visitors and Google cannot tell whether the content is current; several posts are seasonal
  • No video was detected on the site, and no city-specific landing pages were observed for the surrounding Houston communities the firm serves
  • Site content is thinner than the leading Houston competitors in areas like attorney credentials and FAQs, as discussed on the call
Blind Spot: The homepage prominently features a Call Now button, but after hours that number reaches a recorded message. A visitor who finds the site on a Saturday night has no obvious way to submit their case, and the contact form is on a different page.
The Positive
  • The site is live, has a clear Call Now button and Contact Us button above the fold, and is set up for mobile devices
  • Practice areas are clearly laid out — 11 personal injury specialties from truck wrecks to wrongful death
  • An attorney bio and About page are in place, and a BBB accreditation badge builds trust
The Gap
  • A website that does not convert after hours or give visitors multiple ways to reach out loses cases the firm never knows about
  • Without fresh, dated content and local landing pages, Google has little reason to rank the firm above larger Houston competitors
  • Thin trust content makes it harder to win the visitor who is comparing three firms side by side
A website that turns every visitor into a conversation is the foundation of a steady, predictable flow of new clients — one that does not depend on word-of-mouth alone.

Intake Assessment

The Bonner Law Firm has no formal intake process today — remote sign-ups run through an online form, and calls after hours reach a recorded message. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • There is no formal intake process — no scripted questions, no defined follow-up, and no way to track a lead from first contact to signed case
  • Remote sign-ups depend on an online form with no structured process behind it
  • After-hours calls go to a recorded message, so weekend and late-night accident victims reach a voicemail instead of a person
  • Paper-based case files for 40 to 50 active cases each month make fast follow-up and consistent client communication harder
Blind Spot: Most serious accidents do not happen between nine and five. Competitors that answer 24/7 are likely winning the people who call The Bonner Law Firm on a weekend and reach a recorded message — and the firm never sees those lost calls.
The Positive
  • A receptionist is already in place, and an online sign-up option exists for clients who cannot come into the office
  • An established volume of 40 to 50 active cases a month shows there is real demand to convert better
The Gap
  • Every unanswered after-hours call is a case the firm already paid to attract — through billboards, TV, radio, and reputation — and never got to sign
  • Without a defined follow-up process, leads who do not sign on the first call quietly disappear
  • With no lead tracking, the firm cannot tell which marketing is producing signed cases and which is not
A firm with steady, predictable client flow starts with an intake process that catches every case — day or night — without relying on one person's effort.

Team Assessment

The Bonner Law Firm runs with a three-person team — the attorney, a chief paralegal, and a receptionist — carrying 40 to 50 active cases a month on mostly paper-based processes. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • With only three people, there is little room for anyone to step away without work slowing down
  • Case files live on paper, so information sits in people's heads and filing cabinets rather than in a system the whole team can use
  • The attorney and the chief paralegal prefer different ways of working — paper for perceived security versus a digital case system — which slows decisions on tools and process
  • There are no documented workflows for intake, follow-up, or case handoffs that would let the team run consistently without constant direction
Blind Spot: A practice that has served Houston for more than 40 years has a lot of value tied up in paper files and personal knowledge. That is hard to hand off, hard to grow, and hard to protect — whatever the firm's long-term plans are.
The Positive
  • The chief paralegal is already pushing for a modern case management system — a strong internal champion for change
  • The firm is open to testing new technology, including sharing sample handwritten intake notes for an AI demonstration
The Gap
  • When the team's work depends on paper and personal effort, the owner stays tied to the day-to-day
  • Adding 10 to 15 new clients each month without systems will stretch a three-person team past its limit
  • Without documented processes, hiring or delegating later means starting from scratch
A team that runs on clear systems is what lets the firm grow without growing the owner's workload.

Profit Plan Assessment

The Bonner Law Firm's stated goal is volume — adding 10 to 15 new clients a month — and the call did not cover profit margins, cost per case, or how marketing spend is tracked against results. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Marketing today runs through TV, radio, and billboards, which are difficult to tie to signed cases and return on spend
  • There is no lead tracking, so the cost to acquire a signed client is not measured
  • The growth goal is framed as a number of clients, not a target for profit or take-home
  • Paper-based files make it harder to see case status, costs, and collections at a glance
Blind Spot: Adding 10 to 15 clients a month without knowing what each signed case costs to acquire can mean more work and more spend without more profit. The number that matters is not leads — it is profit per signed case.
The Positive
  • An established monthly case volume gives the firm a real base to measure and improve from
The Gap
  • Without knowing which channels produce signed cases, the firm cannot put its next marketing dollar where it works hardest
  • Without a clear target for profit, growth is something that happens to the firm instead of something it plans
  • Without monthly financial reporting, it is difficult to decide how much to invest in growth with confidence
Knowing the numbers is what turns a steady flow of new clients into a firm that is strong on paper and in the bank.
