Intake Assessment

The Bonner Law Firm has no formal intake process today — remote sign-ups run through an online form, and calls after hours reach a recorded message. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • There is no formal intake process — no scripted questions, no defined follow-up, and no way to track a lead from first contact to signed case
  • Remote sign-ups depend on an online form with no structured process behind it
  • After-hours calls go to a recorded message, so weekend and late-night accident victims reach a voicemail instead of a person
  • Paper-based case files for 40 to 50 active cases each month make fast follow-up and consistent client communication harder
Blind Spot: Most serious accidents do not happen between nine and five. Competitors that answer 24/7 are likely winning the people who call The Bonner Law Firm on a weekend and reach a recorded message — and the firm never sees those lost calls.
The Positive
  • A receptionist is already in place, and an online sign-up option exists for clients who cannot come into the office
  • An established volume of 40 to 50 active cases a month shows there is real demand to convert better
The Gap
  • Every unanswered after-hours call is a case the firm already paid to attract — through billboards, TV, radio, and reputation — and never got to sign
  • Without a defined follow-up process, leads who do not sign on the first call quietly disappear
  • With no lead tracking, the firm cannot tell which marketing is producing signed cases and which is not
A firm with steady, predictable client flow starts with an intake process that catches every case — day or night — without relying on one person's effort.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The contact form sits on its own page rather than on the homepage, so a visitor must click through before they can reach out
  • The homepage offers a Call Now button, but after hours that call reaches a recorded message instead of a person
  • No after-hours path is visible on the site for someone who finds it late at night and wants to submit their case

Team Assessment

The Bonner Law Firm runs with a three-person team — the attorney, a chief paralegal, and a receptionist — carrying 40 to 50 active cases a month on mostly paper-based processes. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • With only three people, there is little room for anyone to step away without work slowing down
  • Case files live on paper, so information sits in people's heads and filing cabinets rather than in a system the whole team can use
  • The attorney and the chief paralegal prefer different ways of working — paper for perceived security versus a digital case system — which slows decisions on tools and process
  • There are no documented workflows for intake, follow-up, or case handoffs that would let the team run consistently without constant direction
Blind Spot: A practice that has served Houston for more than 40 years has a lot of value tied up in paper files and personal knowledge. That is hard to hand off, hard to grow, and hard to protect — whatever the firm's long-term plans are.
The Positive
  • The chief paralegal is already pushing for a modern case management system — a strong internal champion for change
  • The firm is open to testing new technology, including sharing sample handwritten intake notes for an AI demonstration
The Gap
  • When the team's work depends on paper and personal effort, the owner stays tied to the day-to-day
  • Adding 10 to 15 new clients each month without systems will stretch a three-person team past its limit
  • Without documented processes, hiring or delegating later means starting from scratch
A team that runs on clear systems is what lets the firm grow without growing the owner's workload.

Profit Plan Assessment

The Bonner Law Firm's stated goal is volume — adding 10 to 15 new clients a month — and the call did not cover profit margins, cost per case, or how marketing spend is tracked against results. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Marketing today runs through TV, radio, and billboards, which are difficult to tie to signed cases and return on spend
  • There is no lead tracking, so the cost to acquire a signed client is not measured
  • The growth goal is framed as a number of clients, not a target for profit or take-home
  • Paper-based files make it harder to see case status, costs, and collections at a glance
Blind Spot: Adding 10 to 15 clients a month without knowing what each signed case costs to acquire can mean more work and more spend without more profit. The number that matters is not leads — it is profit per signed case.
The Positive
  • An established monthly case volume gives the firm a real base to measure and improve from
The Gap
  • Without knowing which channels produce signed cases, the firm cannot put its next marketing dollar where it works hardest
  • Without a clear target for profit, growth is something that happens to the firm instead of something it plans
  • Without monthly financial reporting, it is difficult to decide how much to invest in growth with confidence
Knowing the numbers is what turns a steady flow of new clients into a firm that is strong on paper and in the bank.
