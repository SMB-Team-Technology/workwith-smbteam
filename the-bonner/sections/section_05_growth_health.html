Pillar Health Scorecard

Lead Generation

  • New clients come mainly from word-of-mouth, TV, radio, and billboards, and the lead flow is described as inconsistent
  • No digital acquisition system was described on the call, even though 40 to 50 active cases a month show real demand for the firm
  • Houston competitors show far larger review footprints and stronger website content than the firm's current online presence
A steady, predictable flow of new clients is what lets the firm stop relying on word-of-mouth and return to a strong, consistent pace.

Intake

  • There is no formal intake process — remote sign-ups run through an online form only
  • After-hours calls reach a recorded message, which can send weekend accident victims to competitors who answer around the clock
  • Paper-based case files make follow-up, review requests, and digital marketing harder to run
Every call that goes unanswered is a case the firm already paid to attract and never got to sign.

Self-Managing Team

  • A three-person team — attorney, chief paralegal, and receptionist — is handling 40 to 50 active cases a month
  • The attorney and the chief paralegal prefer different ways of working (paper versus a digital case system), which slows decisions on systems
  • As a 40-plus-year-old firm, there is a clear need for documented systems that make the practice easier to hand off over time
A team that runs on defined systems is what turns the firm from owner-dependent into something that works without constant owner effort.

Profit

  • Revenue, margins, and cost to acquire a client were not discussed, so profit visibility cannot yet be confirmed
  • Traditional media spend (TV, radio, billboards) is hard to tie to signed cases without lead tracking
  • Growing to 10 to 15 additional clients a month will require a plan for ad spend and return, not a guess
Knowing what each signed case costs to acquire is what makes growth planned instead of hoped for.
