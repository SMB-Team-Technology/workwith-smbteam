Intake Assessment

Davis Miles relies on free 15-minute consultations as its conversion step, and fewer than half of them are kept — a major leak between a paid lead and a signed retainer. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Fewer than half of the free 15-minute consultations are kept, so most booked time never turns into a conversation with an attorney
  • Unqualified “shopper” leads are reaching the consultation stage, which uses attorney time on people who were never likely to retain the firm
  • The attorney approach on consultations was named as a contributing factor, so results depend on how each conversation is handled
  • Lawmatics is not connected to the case management software, so you cannot trace a lead through to a signed case or measure true return on ad spend
Blind Spot: When unqualified leads show up as booked consultations, it can mean qualification is happening too late — after the attorney has already invested time — rather than before the calendar slot is offered.
The Positive
  • Lawmatics is already in place as a lead and intake platform, so the foundation for structured follow-up exists
  • The firm knows its own numbers: a 20% consultation-to-retainer conversion rate and a $5,000 average retainer, which makes the cost of every missed consultation measurable
The Gap
  • At a 20% conversion rate and a $5,000 average retainer, every consultation that is kept represents roughly $1,000 in expected revenue — and more than half are being lost before they start
  • Without lead-to-case tracking, there is no way to tell which channels produce signed clients and which only produce shoppers
  • Roughly $20,000 a month in ad spend is feeding a funnel that loses more than half its consultations at the last step
Tightening intake is one of the fastest, lowest-cost ways to move from $785,161 toward the $1,000,000 goal.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The primary “Schedule My Consultation” button is clear and sits above the fold, with a separate “I'm a LegalShield Member” path for members

Team Assessment

Davis Miles has a COO involved in key decisions and a multi-office, multi-attorney footprint, but consultation results vary with attorney approach, and staffing and accountability structure were not covered on this call. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Attorney approach on free consultations was named as a factor in the show rate, which points to inconsistent handling of the first conversation
  • Without lead-to-case tracking, neither the internal team nor the agency can be held to a clear cost-per-signed-case number
  • Who owns lead follow-up, consultation scheduling, and agency accountability was not discussed, so those responsibilities should be confirmed
Blind Spot: A show rate below 50% is easy to treat as a marketing problem, but how the first conversation is handled is a team skill that can be trained, scripted, and measured.
The Positive
  • A COO is part of the evaluation and review process, which signals leadership structure beyond the owner
  • Multiple attorneys have earned outside recognition, including Super Lawyers listings and The Best Lawyers in America 2027
The Gap
  • Reviewing, managing, and replacing marketing vendors pulls leadership time away from running the firm
  • Inconsistent consultation handling means signed cases depend on which attorney takes the call
  • Without shared numbers, there is no simple way for the team to see what is working and manage to it without the owner stepping in
A team that runs consultations and follow-up to a clear standard is what lets Davis Miles scale to $1,000,000 without everything routing back through leadership.

Profit Plan Assessment

Davis Miles projects $785,161 in Arizona family law revenue this year against a $1,000,000 goal and invests roughly $20,000 a month in Google plus Meta, but margin and profit figures were not discussed on the call. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Margin and profit figures were not shared on the call, so what each signed case earns after acquisition cost is not yet visible
  • Lawmatics is not connected to case management, so the return on ad spend cannot be calculated accurately
  • Leads have declined since March 2026 while spend has continued at roughly $20,000 a month
  • Meta spend is planned to double from about $75 to $150 a day before the return on current spend can be measured
Blind Spot: A $5,000 average retainer sounds like revenue, but the number that drives growth is cost per signed case — and until leads are tied to cases, that number is a guess.
The Positive
  • The firm has a clear, specific target — $1,000,000 in Arizona family law revenue — with a defined gap of about $215,000
  • Core unit economics are known: a $5,000 average retainer and a 20% consultation-to-retainer conversion rate
The Gap
  • Without knowing which of Google, LSA, and Meta produces signed cases, budget cannot be shifted toward what works
  • At a $5,000 average retainer, a $1,000,000 year requires roughly 16 to 17 signed cases a month, so every leak in the funnel directly delays the goal
  • Adding spend before the measurement gap is fixed risks buying more of the same results
A $1,000,000 year only counts when you can see what it cost to earn — growth that is planned, not discovered.
