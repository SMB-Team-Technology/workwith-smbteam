Lead Generation

Shield Law spent years building a lead pipeline almost entirely on organic search tied to its Brockton office — one that generated roughly 124 clicks and 30 new clients a month before an April 2026 move to Quincy broke it overnight. Today, the firm is running a single $1,500/mo Google Ads campaign through an outside agency, a budget far below what it would take to replace what was lost.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • PageSpeed mobile and desktop scores could not be captured live this pass (API quota limit hit on every attempt) — real numbers are being pulled manually and will be confirmed before the proposal call
  • Contact forms are scattered across multiple homepage sections ("Have you been a Victim?" and "Let's Work Together") rather than a single clear form above the fold
  • No live chat is present, so after-hours visitors have no way to start a conversation before calling
  • No city-specific landing pages exist for Quincy, Brockton, or Boston — practice-area pages list all four office addresses only in the footer
Blind Spot: Your site already lists Estate Planning and Family Law as practice areas — the infrastructure for the pivot Jonathan proposed already exists. It is the marketing spend and content strategy behind those pages that hasn't caught up yet.
The Positive
  • The site is mobile responsive with individual attorney bio pages for Nick Yousif, Matt McKenna, and Hunter Cook, plus an embedded video and a live Spanish-language page already in place
  • Clear above-the-fold calls to action ("Get Started," "Watch Video") are already on the homepage
The Gap
  • Without a single, prominent contact form, visitors who are ready to act have to hunt for how to reach the firm
  • Without city-specific landing pages, the site cannot rank distinctly for "Quincy," "Brockton," or "Boston" searches — exactly the geographic precision the firm needs post-move
  • Until PageSpeed is verified, every dollar spent driving new traffic to the site risks being partially wasted on a slow-loading experience
A faster, more focused website turns the traffic Nick is about to pay for into clients — not into visitors who bounce before they ever call.

Local SEO

The Problem
  • Organic clicks collapsed from ~124/mo to 10-11/mo after the April 2026 move — a roughly 91% drop
  • No confirmed Google Business Profile exists yet for the current Quincy address (218 Willard St #302); every directory checked instead still shows the old Brockton listing
  • The firm's ~122 five-star reviews are tied to the Brockton profile and have not transferred to Quincy, stripping away social proof that used to convert searchers into callers
  • Shield Law does not appear on page one for "bankruptcy lawyer Quincy MA," "consumer protection attorney Quincy MA," or "personal injury lawyer Quincy MA," while several named local competitors do
Blind Spot: Every one of the eight directories checked (Avvo, FindLaw, Lawyers.com, Martindale, SuperLawyers, LawInfo, Bay State Banner, plus a Yelp listing) still shows the old Brockton phone number. Every call routed to that old listing is a lead going to a number that isn't being worked the same way the new Quincy line is.
The Positive
  • The 122 five-star reviews still exist — they are an asset to be consolidated onto the new Quincy profile, not rebuilt from zero
The Gap
  • Every month the Quincy GBP stays unclaimed and reviews stay unconsolidated is a month of searches going to competitors instead of Shield Law
  • Without city-specific landing pages, the firm cannot distinctly compete for Quincy-based searches even once the GBP issue is fixed
Levin and Levin, LLP — a 1933-founded Quincy bankruptcy firm holding a 4.9-star Avvo rating and a 2025 Avvo Client's Choice Award, occupying exactly the local search territory Shield Law lost.
Rebuilding local search here isn't starting over — it's reclaiming ground Shield Law already earned once.

Google Ads

Ad Presence
  • Shield Law is currently running a $1,500/mo Google Ads campaign through Convert Marketing, started last month — a budget assessed as too small to produce consistent, meaningful visibility for competitive terms like bankruptcy and consumer protection
  • This firm does not appear to be running search ads for Estate Planning or Family Law — the practice areas identified as the fastest path back to predictable cash flow
Competitor Ad Landscape
  • Levin and Levin, LLP — established, SEO-optimized bankruptcy pages that would plausibly support a well-funded search campaign; live ad presence not confirmed this pass, but their organic dominance alone crowds out under-funded competitors
  • Bellotti Law Group, P.C. — a 10.0 "Superb" Avvo-rated personal injury firm with decades of local results, occupying the personal injury search space the firm's own site still lists
The Opportunity
  • "bankruptcy lawyer Quincy MA" and "consumer protection attorney Quincy MA" — the firm's core historical practice areas, currently invisible on page one
  • "estate planning attorney Quincy MA" and "uncontested divorce lawyer Quincy MA" — the higher-margin, faster-ROI pivot areas proposed on the call
  • Bidding on "Shield Law" and "Nick Yousif" by name — protecting against competitors capturing searchers who already know the firm
  • Spanish-language campaigns — the firm's Spanish-language site page already exists, but no Spanish-language ad presence was found
Blind Spot: At $1,500/mo, the current campaign is likely spending most of its budget just to stay visible for a handful of clicks — not enough volume to reliably replace the 20 new clients a month the move cost the firm.

The Opportunity

Blind Spot: LSA is one of the only channels here that isn't blocked by the NAP/GBP fragmentation problem — it can go live independent of fixing the Quincy Google Business Profile.
