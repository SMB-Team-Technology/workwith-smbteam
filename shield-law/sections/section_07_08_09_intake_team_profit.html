Intake Assessment

Nick personally answers every intake call, follow-up, and client communication himself — a workload that was already stretched thin before new client volume was cut in half. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Nick personally handles every intake call, follow-up, and client communication — no delegation in place
  • No CRM or intake tracking software identified to catch leads coming through the firm's now much smaller lead flow
  • No after-hours coverage identified — with organic traffic already down 91%, a missed call carries more weight than it used to
  • No defined follow-up sequence identified for leads who don't sign on the first call
Blind Spot: With new client volume already cut from 30/mo to 10-15/mo, every one of those remaining leads matters more than it did six months ago — and there's currently no system ensuring none of them slip through.
The Gap
  • Every unanswered or slow-followed-up lead is lost revenue at a moment when the firm can least afford to lose any client
  • Without a tracking system, there's no way to know which leads are falling through versus which simply aren't converting
  • Nick's personal bandwidth is now the ceiling on how many of the remaining leads actually become clients
Fixing intake means Nick keeps more of the leads he's already paying to generate — not more hours added to an already full week.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • Contact forms are placed in multiple homepage sections ("Have you been a Victim?" and "Let's Work Together") rather than one clear, prominent above-the-fold form
  • No live chat is present on the site — after-hours visitors have no way to start a conversation before calling
  • With traffic already down 91%, every visitor who lands on the site and can't quickly find how to reach the firm makes an already-thin lead flow thinner

These findings are also referenced in the Website Audit section.

Team Assessment

Shield Law has three attorneys on staff, but Nick still personally handles every piece of admin, follow-up, and client communication himself. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Nick personally manages admin, follow-up, and client communication for the entire firm, despite two other attorneys (Matt McKenna, Hunter Cook) on staff
  • No dedicated marketing or intake point of contact — Nick is the default owner of every function tied to growth
  • The firm's multilingual (Spanish) capability exists but isn't being deployed as an active growth lever
  • A second job in real estate is currently required to stabilize income — time that could otherwise go toward building team capacity
Blind Spot: The fact that Nick has two other attorneys on staff and still handles every intake call personally is the blind spot — the team capacity to delegate already exists, it just hasn't been organized around growth yet.
The Positive
  • Two other attorneys (Matt McKenna, Hunter Cook) are already on staff — the raw capacity to delegate exists, it doesn't need to be hired first
  • The team's multilingual Spanish capability is a genuine, ready-to-use asset for the niche opportunity identified on the call
The Gap
  • Every hour Nick spends on admin and follow-up is an hour he isn't practicing law, developing the firm's next practice areas, or spending on anything outside the firm
  • Without delegation, the firm's capacity is capped at what Nick personally has time for — regardless of how much lead flow improves
  • The second job isn't a permanent solution — it's a symptom of a firm that hasn't yet built the systems to run without him at the center of everything
Until admin and intake are actually delegated to the team already in place, Nick's second job doesn't go away — no matter how much the firm's lead flow recovers.

Shield Law's revenue has become highly volatile since the April move, with July 2026 projected at a low of $45k, and no financial benchmarks currently available for the firm's core practice areas. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Revenue is described as highly volatile since the move, with July 2026 projected at a low of $45k
  • Nick works a second job in real estate specifically to cover income gaps — a sign the firm's income isn't yet predictable enough to rely on alone
  • No average case value, close rate, or cost-per-acquisition figures were available for the firm's current bankruptcy and consumer protection work
  • No stated financial visibility system was identified for tracking margins across practice areas
Blind Spot: Working a second job to cover income gaps can start to feel normal — but it's a sign the firm's numbers aren't yet predictable enough to plan around, not a permanent condition.

A predictable $200k/month isn't just a bigger number — it's the number that finally lets Nick stop needing a second job to make ends meet.
