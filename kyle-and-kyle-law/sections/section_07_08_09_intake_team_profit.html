Intake Assessment

Kyle and Kyle Law has a defined intake process: an outsourced receptionist company answers every call and sets consultations, and a same-day follow-up covers people who do not schedule. The gap is the manual work between a consultation and an open case. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: RED
"it requires too much double work because then we still have to put it in our case management system before the case can be opened."
The Problem
  • Information has to be entered twice — once during onboarding and again in your case management system — before a case can be opened
  • Every new client arrives by phone, so intake depends on an outsourced receptionist company and one person following up with leads
  • Lead follow-up and rescheduling are still handled by hand, and automating them is on your own AI wish list
  • Loan modification applications contain client banking data, and there is no firm AI policy yet to govern how that data is handled
Blind Spot: A good process can hide a hidden cost: every manual step between a consultation and an open case is staff time spent on data entry instead of client work, and it multiplies with every new state.
The Positive
  • An outsourced receptionist company handles every incoming call and sets consultations, with a same-day follow-up process in place
  • Katherine says the process is working — "We're not losing people" — and dashboards already track consult-to-client percentages by attorney
The Gap
  • Double entry takes staff time on every new client, time that could go to serving the 500-plus clients the firm already has
  • Manual follow-up and rescheduling leave room for delays as volume grows with each new state
  • Without an AI policy, the firm cannot safely extend AI to the intake work that handles sensitive client data
Smoother intake means more of your team's time goes to clients instead of re-keying — and less of the business lands on your desk.

Team Assessment

Kyle and Kyle Law has about 15 people, including two additional attorneys, and both owners have moved out of casework — but running the business still falls to them. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"just the running the business pulls me away from it."
The Problem
  • Both owners no longer take cases, so they personally handle the business side of a 15-person firm
  • One person is hired just to review three people's emails, and email and calendaring are the first things Katherine wants AI to take on
  • Only 5 of the 15 people have Claude seats, and they mostly use it for one-off tasks
  • Team accountability and time entries are tracked by hand, and both are on your AI wish list
Blind Spot: Because the firm's systems are strong, it is easy to treat the owners' involvement as simply the cost of running a business. It is the single biggest brake on the multi-state expansion.
The Positive
  • Katherine describes "really good systems and processes" — which makes them far easier to hand off to automation
  • She is already saving about 45 minutes a day with AI, so the appetite and early results are there
The Gap
  • Time spent running the business is time not spent on the expansion into Indiana, Ohio, and beyond
  • Routine email and calendar work consumes a paid position that automation could help cover
  • With AI used by a third of the team, most of the firm's potential time savings are still untouched
A team that handles routine work on its own is what lets you make scaling your focus instead of running the business.

Profit Plan Assessment

Kyle and Kyle Law brought in about $1.9 million in 2025 and is over $2 million so far in 2026, built on flat monthly fees from more than 500 active clients. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"I compare our books to anybody. I think we have the best books… our business is running great on the financial side."
The Problem
  • The firm hit its original case-count goal and never reset the goal, so there is no revenue or profit target to plan against
  • The firm does not track how much revenue comes from going after fees, and some fees are written off
  • Katherine notes that standard tools are "not built for what we're doing in terms of flat monthly rates"
  • No profit margin or profit target was discussed on the call
Blind Spot: Strong books show where the money went. They do not show what a new state will cost to launch, which is the number that decides whether expansion adds wealth or just work.
The Positive
  • Katherine is confident in the firm's books and financial position, and revenue grew from about $1.9 million in 2025 to over $2 million so far in 2026
  • Flat monthly fees, with clients staying anywhere from six months to three years, give the firm a steady, predictable revenue base
The Gap
  • Without a current goal, it is hard to say how much growth the five-state plan needs to produce
  • Untracked fee recovery means the firm cannot tell whether that effort pays for itself
  • Dashboards already track consult conversions, but the same visibility has not been extended to fee and financial data
A clear target and visible numbers turn the five-state plan from an ambition into a funded decision.
