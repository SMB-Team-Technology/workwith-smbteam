Intake Assessment

With three law clerks supporting Rick across roughly 350 active files, intake responsibility is already spread across the team — but the firm's own website routes every inquiry off-site to a third-party form, with no live chat and no confirmed after-hours coverage. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • The homepage's only above-fold CTA sends prospects to an external JotForm, adding a step between a prospect's interest and an actual conversation with the firm
  • No live chat is available — a WhatsApp link is the only real-time contact option
  • No after-hours coverage was confirmed, despite operating across four locations and time-sensitive practice areas like immigration and family law
  • No defined follow-up sequence was identified for leads who do not sign or respond immediately
Blind Spot: You may not realize how many prospects abandon an off-site form before finishing it — every extra click between "I'm interested" and "I've reached your firm" is a chance to lose someone to a competitor who answers faster.
The Positive
  • Three law clerks provide real delegation capacity beyond Rick handling every file personally
The Gap
  • Every lead lost to a slow or off-site intake step is revenue the firm already earned the right to, with nothing to show for it
  • With no live chat or confirmed after-hours process, prospects searching outside business hours have no way to reach Rye Law before a competitor does
  • Without a tracked follow-up sequence, leads who don't convert immediately are likely never re-contacted
Fixing intake is the fastest path to the freedom Rick wants — it converts more of the clients he is already earning, without asking him to work any harder.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • No live chat is offered; a WhatsApp link is the sole real-time alternative
  • No visible after-hours contact option exists across any of the four office locations

Team Assessment

Rick has three law clerks and a bookkeeper supporting roughly 350 active files, but by his own account remains personally "entrenched" in the day-to-day running of the firm across four locations. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Rick remains personally involved in daily operations across all five practice areas and four locations
  • No office manager, associate attorney, or dedicated marketing/intake lead was identified
  • The firm's stability currently depends on Rick's continued day-to-day presence rather than a leadership structure beneath him
  • No documented processes or delegation authority were identified for the existing support staff
Blind Spot: The fact that Rick has built a stable team and a full caseload without ever stepping back is itself the blind spot — it means he has never had the chance to see what the firm looks like when it runs without him in the room every day.
The Positive
  • A stable four-person support team (three law clerks and a bookkeeper) is already in place — real capacity to build a leadership layer on top of, rather than starting from nothing
The Gap
  • Every day Rick stays entrenched in operations is a day the "scalable business that runs independently" he described stays a plan instead of a reality
  • Without a manager or lead beneath him, growth in caseload means more direct load on Rick, not less
  • The firm currently has no path for Rick to take real time away without the practice stalling
The personal freedom Rick says he wants starts with someone besides Rick being able to run the firm on a normal Tuesday.

Profit Plan Assessment

Rye Law is busy enough to carry roughly 350 active files across four offices, but roughly $50,000 CAD in unbilled receivables and $60,000 in year-end expenses are currently dictating the firm's financial decisions rather than the other way around. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Roughly $50,000 CAD in unbilled accounts receivable is currently sitting uncollected
  • Roughly $60,000 CAD in year-end expenses (taxes, prior investments) are being managed reactively rather than planned for in advance
  • No current revenue, close rate, or average case value figures were available to benchmark the firm's actual profitability
  • A stated $5,000/mo growth investment is being deferred specifically until cash flow clears — a sign profit and cash position aren't yet being planned around
Blind Spot: You may not realize that clearing the existing $50,000 in unbilled AR is itself a profit lever — collecting cash the firm has already earned, before any new marketing spend is even needed.
The Positive
  • The firm is clearly generating enough business — roughly 350 active files — to support real growth investment once cash flow allows
The Gap
  • Uncollected AR sitting on the books is revenue the firm has already earned but cannot yet use — effectively an interest-free loan to clients
  • Without current revenue or margin visibility, it isn't possible to know whether growth is building real wealth or just adding more work
  • Deferring growth investment until year-end expenses clear means competitors keep moving during the exact window Rye Law stands still
A firm generating real personal freedom for Rick needs profit that is planned around, not one where a five-figure AR balance decides when growth can start.
