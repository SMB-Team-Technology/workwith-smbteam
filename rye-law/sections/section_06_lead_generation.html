Lead Generation

Rye Law handles roughly 350 active files across five practice areas and four office locations with no paid advertising, no local pack presence, and no SEO strategy in any of its markets — every new client currently arrives through referrals alone, while named local competitors are actively running paid campaigns and building review profiles in the exact same searches.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • The site is built on GoDaddy's templated Website Builder platform (2025 copyright footer) — a recent build, but a generic template rather than a custom site designed to convert visitors into clients
  • PageSpeed mobile and desktop scores could not be pulled live this session (API quota limits) — flagged for a manual check at pagespeed.web.dev before the next call
  • The only above-fold call to action ("SEND US AN INQUIRY") routes off-site to an external JotForm instead of a native, trackable contact form
  • No blog and no dedicated landing pages exist for any of the five practice areas or four office locations
Blind Spot: Because inquiries route through a third-party form instead of a native one, Rye Law has no reliable way to see which source — referral, search, or social — is actually producing its next client.
The Positive
  • The site is mobile responsive, with a working mobile navigation menu and properly sized images
  • A dedicated attorney bio page highlights Rick's decade-plus in immigration and family law, including his Legal Aid panel work — genuine credibility that isn't being leveraged anywhere else
The Gap
  • Every visitor who wants to reach out is sent off-site to a third-party form — an extra step where interested prospects can drop off before ever becoming a lead
  • With five practice areas and four locations, there is no dedicated page to capture location- or practice-area-specific searches
  • No system exists to track which channel is actually driving new business, making every marketing decision a guess instead of a measurement
A website that can't track its own leads is a website that can't tell Rick where his freedom is actually going to come from.

Local SEO

The Problem
  • Rye Law's live Google star rating and review count could not be verified this session — a live pull is recommended before the next call
  • Rye Law's own Facebook page is indexed as "Not yet rated (0 Reviews)," while every named competitor researched carries a substantial, verified review footprint (57 to 868 reviews)
  • Rye Law did not appear in the local 3-pack across 15 searches run against its five practice areas
  • No presence was found on Avvo, Justia, or FindLaw — the three major legal directories — despite operating across four locations and five practice areas
Blind Spot: The firm's own contact details are inconsistent across its site and social profiles — the Contact page and About page list different office addresses, and Instagram lists "Kitchener" where the site lists "Cambridge." Inconsistent NAP details like these can quietly suppress local rankings even before a review-count gap is factored in.
The Positive
  • An active Instagram profile (@ryelawfirm, ~276 followers) has been posting into at least January 2026 — an existing audience to build on rather than starting from zero
The Gap
  • A thin or unverified review profile gives prospects nothing to build trust on before they call — while named competitors show up with real, visible social proof
  • No directory presence means Rye Law is invisible in exactly the places prospects research a lawyer before hiring one
Chaudhary Law Office — roughly 300 verified reviews at 4.8★ in the same North York immigration searches Rye Law does not appear in.
Every search where a competitor shows up and Rye Law doesn't is a client Rick has to work twice as hard to replace through referrals alone.

Google Ads

Ad Presence
  • Rye Law does not appear to be running Google Search ads for immigration, family, civil & commercial litigation, real estate, or business & corporate law — the firm may not be configured to bid on these terms, or may be getting outbid by established competitors
  • No paid presence was observed in any of the 15 searches run across the firm's five practice areas
Competitor Ad Landscape
  • Mamann Sandaluk LLP — a large immigration firm with 388–868 reviews across platforms holding strong presence in the exact immigration searches Rye Law is absent from
  • Russell Alexander Collaborative Family Lawyers — a multi-office family law firm with 1,625 reviews at 5.0★ dominating divorce and family law searches across the GTA
The Opportunity
  • "immigration lawyer North York," "family lawyer North York Toronto," "real estate lawyer North York" — the core practice-area-plus-location terms Rye Law is not currently capturing
  • "refugee protection lawyer Toronto," "civil litigation lawyer North York" — case-type-specific terms with less competition than broad city-wide searches
  • Bidding on competitor names like Chaudhary Law Office and Simple Divorce to intercept prospects who are already comparing firms
  • Mississauga and Cambridge/Kitchener geo-targeted terms — markets where Rye Law already has a physical office but no dedicated ad presence
Blind Spot: With five practice areas and four locations, Rye Law is effectively competing for visibility in five separate legal markets at once — with no plan for which one gets found first.

Local Service Ads

LSA Presence
  • Rye Law does not appear in Local Service Ads or Google Screened for any practice area
  • Google Local Services Ads for legal services currently has limited to no availability in the Canadian market — this is a non-factor across the board right now, not a gap unique to Rye Law
Competitor LSA Landscape
  • No local competitors were identified using LSA either, for the same reason — the channel is not currently active for legal services in this market
The Opportunity
  • If Google expands Local Services Ads / Google Screened to Canadian legal services, being the first firm in the market to claim it would be a meaningful, low-competition differentiator
  • LSAs appear above everything else on Google — above paid ads, above the map pack, above all organic results — and you only pay when a real prospect calls you directly
  • For now, this channel is correctly treated as a watch item rather than a current priority
