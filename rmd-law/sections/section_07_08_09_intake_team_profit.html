Intake Assessment

RMD Law tracks a real intake-to-signed conversion rate of about 28% on qualified leads, but there's no stated visibility into what happens before a lead reaches that stage — and no after-hours process for a firm running paid campaigns across nine locations and two time zones. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • No stated process exists for what happens to a lead between first contact and the point it becomes a "qualified lead" — the 28% conversion rate only measures the last step of the funnel
  • No after-hours or overflow coverage was described, despite paid campaigns running across nine offices spanning Pacific and Central time zones
  • New case signings dropped from roughly 50/month to 30/month with no described change in intake process to explain or counter it
  • No CRM or intake-tracking system was referenced on the call for a firm managing this much lead volume across this many markets
Blind Spot: A 28% qualified-lead conversion rate sounds solid in isolation — but without knowing how many raw leads never reach "qualified" in the first place, RMD Law can't tell whether the real leak is in intake or in lead quality itself.
The Positive
  • The firm already tracks a real conversion metric (28%) rather than operating with zero intake visibility — a genuine foundation to build a fuller funnel view on
The Gap
  • Every lead lost between first contact and "qualified" is a lead the $80,000/month budget already paid for
  • After-hours gaps in a two-time-zone, nine-location firm likely explain some share of the 5-to-20-signup swing seen in Yelp Ads performance — an intake issue, not only an ad-quality one
  • Without full-funnel visibility, it's impossible to tell whether the drop from 50 to 30 signings/month is a lead-generation problem, an intake problem, or both
Recovering signings by fixing intake — not by spending more — is the fastest route back to the 50+ cases/month pace Michael is chasing across every market RMD Law operates in.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • Contact form sits above the fold on the homepage with fields for case type, name, phone, and email — a solid baseline capture setup
  • A live chat option is present in the footer; response speed and staffing behind it were not confirmed on the call
  • No stated process for how contact-form submissions are routed or followed up once received

Team Assessment

RMD Law has real functional structure — an in-house CMO managing paid ads and a dedicated Marketing Director — but Michael is still the one driving channel-by-channel budget decisions personally, on a call about the firm's own marketing mix. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Michael is personally driving the channel-by-channel budget reallocation decisions discussed on this call, rather than a marketing leader owning that call independently
  • Whether the new employment law practice launches on a standalone site or inside rmdlaw.com is still an open decision sitting with ownership, not a delegated team
  • No stated leadership layer (COO, operations director) was described managing the operational side of running nine locations across two states
  • No stated delegation plan for intake, marketing, or new-market decisions as the firm expands into Seattle, Dallas, and Houston
Blind Spot: The fact that Michael is comfortable being the one making these calls personally is itself the blind spot — it means decisions that should scale independently of him, like channel mix and new-market entry, currently don't.
The Positive
  • A CMO and Marketing Director already exist in-house — real delegated marketing capacity most firms this size don't have, and a genuine foundation to build further independence on
The Gap
  • Every new market RMD Law enters — Austin already, Seattle, Dallas, and Houston next — currently routes its channel-mix and budget decisions back through Michael personally
  • Without a team empowered to make and own those calls, expansion adds to Michael's workload rather than freeing his time
  • The unresolved employment law site-structure decision is sitting in queue instead of moving forward, because it's waiting on ownership bandwidth
Scaling into four more markets while Michael is still the one deciding channel mix and practice-launch structure means growth adds hours to his week, not freedom — the opposite of what growth is supposed to buy him.

Profit Plan Assessment

No revenue figure, revenue goal, or profitability discussion was part of this call — RMD Law's own stated growth goal is expressed in case volume, not dollars, despite an $80,000/month marketing commitment. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • No annual revenue figure was stated or discussed on the call, despite supporting an $80,000/month marketing budget
  • The firm's growth goal is expressed entirely in case-volume terms (50+ signings/month) rather than revenue or margin terms
  • No cost-per-acquired-case figure was discussed relative to what each case is actually worth, across a mix that ranges from roughly $5,000–$8,000 (general PI) to $15,000–$50,000+ (truck accident)
  • No visibility was shared into whether the current $80,000/month spend produces a positive return once case value and time-to-settlement are factored in
Blind Spot: Without knowing profit per case type, RMD Law can't tell whether chasing more truck-accident cases specifically — its highest average case value — would grow profit faster than chasing case volume alone.
The Gap
  • Without a revenue or margin target, it's impossible to know if $80,000/month in spend is the right number, too little, or too much for the firm's actual profitability goals
  • Entering Austin, Seattle, Dallas, and Houston without case-level cost-to-acquire data risks scaling an unprofitable channel mix rather than a profitable one
  • A firm growing case volume without matching profit visibility risks becoming busier without becoming wealthier
Michael can't know if scaling into four new markets makes RMD Law more profitable — only busier — until the firm can see profit per case as clearly as it sees case count.
