Lead Generation

RMD Law spends more on marketing in a month — $80,000 — than many firms spend in a year, but that budget is concentrated in a single volatile channel (Yelp Ads) while the geo-targeted SEO pages the firm has already built for its expansion markets sit invisible in local search. The result is a firm with real scale and real spend that still can't explain why new case signings have slipped from 50 a month to 30.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • Homepage headline leads with "Top-Rated Personal Injury Law Firm in California" — strong positioning, but the new employment law practice area is not yet reflected anywhere on the site
  • PageSpeed scores: Mobile — pending manual verification; Desktop — pending manual verification. Automated pulls were blocked by an API quota limit across four attempts this cycle and must be confirmed by hand at pagespeed.web.dev before launch
  • Blog exists with recent posts (rideshare accidents, slip-and-fall claims), but exact publish dates aren't shown on listing pages — makes it hard to tell how active the content cadence really is
  • No video content observed anywhere on the site — for a firm this size, that's a missed trust-building opportunity before a prospect ever calls
Blind Spot: FindLaw shows three different phone numbers for RMD Law across a single directory profile — (949) 516-0687, (949) 326-5000, and a fax line listed as a third contact number. Inconsistent contact data like this quietly erodes the local SEO trust signals Google uses to rank your geo-pages.
The Positive
  • Six attorneys each have individual, credentialed bio pages (Aria Miran, John Rajaee, Nicole Dolle, Ryan Drakulich, Yen Quach, Shabnam Pourghorban)
  • Extensive geo-targeted landing pages already exist across multiple cities and practice-area combinations — a real content asset most firms this size haven't built yet
The Gap
  • Every visitor who lands on an unresolved mobile experience before the PageSpeed issue is fixed is a lead the $80,000/month budget already paid for and lost
  • No video presence means prospects comparing RMD Law against competitors' heavily-promoted messaging have less reason to pick up the phone
  • Paid traffic aimed at the new employment law practice has nowhere credible to land until that page exists
A website that converts what the firm is already paying to attract is the fastest way to fund the Austin, Seattle, Dallas, and Houston expansion without raising the marketing budget by a dollar.

Local SEO

The Problem
  • Live Google Business Profile review count could not be confirmed this cycle — aggregator sources ranged from 42 to 216 reviews; Yelp independently confirms 251 reviews / 44 photos for the Irvine office, which should be treated as the more reliable floor pending a manual GBP check
  • RMD Law appears in the local 3-pack only in Irvine, and only for "car accident lawyer" — not for "personal injury lawyer" or "truck accident attorney," even in its own home market
  • Zero 3-pack presence in Sacramento, San Diego, San Bernardino, or Austin — despite dedicated geo-landing pages already built for at least Sacramento
  • At least four different phone numbers are circulating for RMD Law across FindLaw, aggregator sites, and search snippets — a fixable consistency issue
Blind Spot: RMD Law is not present in Birdeye's public review-management index, unlike Demas Law Group and Adamson Ahdoot — both of which are actively out-ranking RMD Law in local search using structured review generation RMD Law isn't currently running.
The Positive
  • RMD Law's own Irvine review footprint (251 Yelp reviews) already exceeds every individually named Irvine-market competitor on raw volume — the credibility exists, it just isn't being extended into the expansion markets yet
The Gap
  • Every geo-landing page built for Sacramento, San Diego, and San Bernardino that isn't showing up in the local pack is SEO investment already made but not yet cashed in
  • A new market like Austin starts from zero local trust signal against an established statewide brand already dominating that market
Demas Law Group, P.C. — dominates Sacramento's local pack with roughly 668 aggregated reviews at 4.9★, while RMD Law's dedicated Sacramento geo-page doesn't surface at all for the same searches.
Turning existing SEO investment into 3-pack visibility in Sacramento, San Diego, and San Bernardino is how RMD Law wins case volume in markets it has already built pages for — without spending a single new ad dollar to do it.

Google Ads

  • Per the discovery call, RMD Law's paid ads are managed in-house by the CMO, and Google Search is currently deprioritized because personal injury keyword CPCs have been priced out to roughly $250–$300 — a stated business reason, not neglect. Employment law has no ad presence yet since the practice hasn't publicly launched

  • Aegis Law Firm, HKM Employment Attorneys, Eldessouky Law — named Orange County employment-law firms with an organic presence in the exact market RMD Law is preparing to enter
The Opportunity
  • "Truck accident attorney [city]" across Sacramento, San Diego, and San Bernardino — terms where RMD Law doesn't yet appear even organically
  • "Personal injury lawyer Austin TX" — an entirely open paid position while organic 3-pack visibility is built from zero
  • Competitor-name conquesting around Adamson Ahdoot and Alvandi Law Group in Irvine, where RMD Law's own review volume (251) already beats both
  • "Employment law attorney" + Orange County terms — an unclaimed paid position the moment the new practice area launches, ahead of Aegis Law Firm and HKM Employment Attorneys
Blind Spot: Google Search CPCs at $250–$300 for broad PI keywords mean high-competition head-on bidding will keep burning budget fast. The real opportunity is in the specific, lower-competition long-tail terms — city plus case type plus "near me" variants — the firm hasn't tested yet, not in outbidding the market leaders directly.

Local Service Ads

"[LSAs are] underperforming due to aged accounts and poor ranking, resulting in low-quality leads"
LSA Presence
  • RMD Law confirms on the call that it runs LSAs today, but describes the accounts as aged and poorly ranked — a known, active, but underperforming channel by the firm's own account
  • Google Screened status and current LSA review count could not be pulled via automated tools this cycle — recommend a live check from each target city before the proposal call
Competitor LSA Landscape
  • Named LSA competitors could not be confirmed this cycle (LSA units render dynamically and were not retrievable) — recommend a live, logged-out check from Irvine, Sacramento, San Diego, San Bernardino, and Austin before the proposal call
The Opportunity
  • Rebuilding the aged LSA account directly resets the "poor ranking" problem the firm itself flagged — this is a fix to an existing channel, not new spend
  • LSAs appear above everything else on Google — above paid ads, above the map pack, above all organic results — and you only pay when a real prospect calls you directly.
  • Google is reportedly consolidating standalone Local Services Ads into the main Ads platform starting around August 2026 — rebuilding the account now, ahead of that migration, avoids carrying the same aged, poorly-ranked history into the new system
Blind Spot: An LSA account with years of aged history and poor ranking doesn't fix itself by waiting — it needs an active rebuild, and the platform transition Google is rolling out this fall is a natural, lower-disruption window to do it in.
