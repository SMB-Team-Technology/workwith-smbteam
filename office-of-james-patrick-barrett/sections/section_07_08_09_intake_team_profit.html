Intake Assessment

The firm's website advertises a 24/7 phone line and a free consultation, but its contact form tells visitors to expect an attorney within 24 to 48 hours. This assessment is based on the public website only, since no intake process was reviewed with the firm. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • The contact form promises a reply within 24 to 48 hours, a long wait for someone who has just been injured and is choosing a lawyer
  • The form sits mid-page and lower down rather than above the fold, so a mobile visitor has to scroll to find it
  • No live-response option such as text-back or an instant call-back was seen on the site
  • How calls are answered after hours, and what happens to web form leads, still needs to be confirmed with the firm
Blind Spot: Prospects rarely contact only one firm. When the first firm to respond usually earns the case, a 24 to 48 hour promise can quietly send ready-to-hire callers to a competitor who answers in minutes.
The Positive
  • The phone line is advertised as available 24/7 with a free consultation, a strong promise to build on
  • The site separates a new-client number from an existing-client number, which helps route calls correctly
The Gap
  • Every web inquiry that waits a day or more is more likely to sign with a firm that responded first
  • A slow-loading mobile site and a slow-response promise compound each other, since both add friction for the same prospect
  • Without a tracked follow-up sequence, inquiries that do not sign right away may not be nurtured
Faster intake turns the leads the firm already earns into signed cases, and that growth comes from work the firm has already paid for.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The consultation form collects name, phone, email, case type, how the visitor heard about the firm, and a case description, which is a thorough and useful set of fields
  • The form's copy sets a 24 to 48 hour expectation, and it is positioned mid-page and again lower on the page rather than above the fold
  • No live chat or instant-response tool was detected in the page source. A click-to-call number is available above the fold

These findings are also referenced in the Website Audit section.

Team Assessment

The firm lists seven attorneys on its website, which suggests a team that goes well beyond a single owner, but how responsibilities are divided across intake, marketing and operations is not visible from the outside. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Who owns marketing results, intake performance and day-to-day operations is not visible from public information and needs to be confirmed
  • The firm's name appears in several forms across directories and on the site itself, a sign that brand details may not have a clear owner
  • No evidence was seen of defined performance metrics, such as response time or signed-case rate, that a manager could track
  • Whether the firm can run for a week or more without the managing attorney making routine decisions has not been confirmed
Blind Spot: In a firm with several attorneys, every lawyer can be busy with cases while no one owns the numbers that grow the firm. Growth work is easy to leave to whoever has time.
The Positive
  • Seven named attorneys with individual pages show real depth, and all are listed as graduates of the same law school
  • A long firm history of more than 35 years points to stability and institutional knowledge
The Gap
  • Without clear roles for marketing, intake and operations, leadership time that could go to cases and strategy is spent on routine decisions
  • Attorneys who carry growth tasks on top of casework have less time for the high-value work only they can do
  • Without a manager or system accountable for results, improvements depend on individual effort instead of a repeatable process
A team with defined roles and clear accountability lets the firm grow without depending on any one person's calendar.

Profit Plan Assessment

Financial information such as revenue, margins and cost per case was not available for this review, so this pillar is rated on limited information and should be confirmed with the firm. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Cost to acquire a signed case is not known, which makes it hard to decide how much to invest in marketing
  • Profit by case type has not been reviewed, so the firm cannot yet see which of its practice areas earn the most for the effort invested
  • Contingency-fee work, as the site's no-recovery, no-fee message indicates, pays when cases resolve, which makes a forward-looking plan important
  • Whether the firm tracks lead source, signed cases and return on marketing together has not been confirmed
Blind Spot: A large settlement can make a firm feel highly profitable in the month it arrives. Without tracking the cost of acquiring and working each case, it is hard to see which cases and channels actually build lasting profit.
The Positive
  • Featured results from $2.0M to $5.8M show the firm handles cases of real value
  • More than 10,000 cases handled, by the firm's own count, suggests a deep base of experience to learn from
The Gap
  • Without cost-per-case and return-on-spend numbers, marketing decisions are made by instinct rather than evidence
  • Investing in growth without a clear profit plan can add work without adding what the firm keeps
  • Without a view of how much to reinvest, the firm cannot know when to push growth and when to hold
A clear profit plan turns growth into wealth the firm can keep, because every dollar invested has a number behind it.
