Intake Assessment

BL Legal's referral-fed intake converts a healthy share of the leads it receives, but the firm's own assessment is that the process is slow and has never been tested against the volume of a real digital program. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
"it certainly is not a testament to a strong intake process."
The Problem
  • Moving a lead from first contact to a signed agreement can stretch over a week or two
  • The website tells prospects an attorney will reach out within 24 to 48 hours, a long window when people are calling several firms at once
  • After-hours coverage relies on an answering service rather than a team trained on the firm's case criteria
  • Reporting on lead volume and conversion is still being brought up to a standard leadership can fully rely on
Blind Spot: A conversion rate of roughly 40% on referred leads is not a benchmark for paid or organic leads. Those prospects are comparing several firms at once and are far less forgiving of slow follow-up.
The Positive
  • Referred leads convert at roughly 40% by the firm's own estimate, a strong base to build on
  • The firm's case criteria are selective, so intake can be built to screen for the high-value, litigation-focused cases it wants
  • Leadership is comfortable owning intake internally, which means new leads can be handed to a team that is ready to improve
The Gap
  • Every lead that waits days to sign is a lead that can sign with a faster firm, and at an average fee in the tens of thousands that is a costly delay
  • New leads from SEO and ads will arrive in a pattern the current process was never built to handle
  • Without lead source recorded on each signed case, marketing cannot be tied to results beyond the "how did you hear about us?" field
Fast, well-tracked intake is what turns new digital leads into the signed-case proof leadership wants to see.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The "Schedule Your Free Consultation" form sits mid-page and lower on the homepage rather than above the fold, while two phone numbers and a "Free Consultation" button are visible at the top
  • The form asks for name, phone, email, case type, how the visitor heard about the firm, a case description and SMS consent, which is a solid set of fields for screening
  • The form copy sets a 24 to 48 hour expectation for an attorney reply, and the only source question is "how did you hear about us?", which limits attribution to signed cases

These findings are also referenced in the Website Audit section.

Team Assessment

BL Legal has experienced attorneys and an operations leader in place, but the firm's marketing function is being built from scratch, with strategy, vendors and reporting concentrated in a small group of leaders. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
"They've brought me here to build their marketing strategy from the ground up."
The Problem
  • There is no established marketing function today, so strategy, budget and vendor decisions are all being set up at once
  • Marketing oversight sits with a small group of leaders who are also running the rest of the firm
  • Case flow depends on referral relationships that individual attorneys hold, which do not scale with the firm's structure
  • Reporting on leads, conversions and results is not yet consistent enough to manage the team against
Blind Spot: A strong vendor can generate leads, but without one clear internal owner of the handoff from marketing to intake, even good leads stall between the two.
The Positive
  • Seven attorneys and an operations leader give the firm real depth to build on
  • Leadership has committed to building marketing properly and has given its operations leader the authority to move quickly
The Gap
  • Without a staffed marketing function, every vendor result must be interpreted by someone already running operations
  • When growth rides on individual attorneys' networks, the firm's pipeline can change whenever those relationships do
  • Without clear owners and consistent reporting, leadership cannot see which efforts are working
A staffed marketing function lets the firm's digital push run without pulling leadership into every vendor decision.

Profit Plan Assessment

BL Legal is tracking toward roughly $10 million in revenue this year, but a meaningful share of recent growth came from lateral attorneys' books of business rather than a source the firm controls. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Part of the recent revenue growth came from lateral attorneys who brought their own cases, which is not a renewable source of new business
  • Fee sharing on lateral cases has not been tracked in detail, so the true return on that growth is hard to see
  • Marketing spend cannot yet be tied to signed cases, because attribution relies largely on a "how did you hear about us?" question
  • The average fee per case is lifted by a small number of large outcomes, so planning on the average can overstate what is predictable
Blind Spot: A high average fee can make every marketing channel look profitable on paper. The real test is cost per signed case, by case type.
The Positive
  • A litigation-focused, trial-ready practice produces an average fee per case that is well above what many firms see
  • Leadership is planning a dedicated marketing budget for the year ahead and wants to increase it as results are proven
The Gap
  • Without cost per signed case by case type, leadership cannot tell which dollars grow profit and which only add activity
  • Growth that depends on lateral hiring ends when hiring does, while a client-acquisition engine the firm owns keeps compounding
  • Without clear return figures, it is harder to make the case for expanding the budget when results arrive
Tying every marketing dollar to a signed case is what earns the budget room to grow and keeps BL Legal's lead as the market's digital pioneer.
