Here Is Where BGN Attorneys Stands Today

BGN Attorneys is a young Kent, WA personal injury firm founded by three former W-2 attorneys — Scott Boyce, Tyson Goss, and Dan Neal — now handling car accident, motorcycle, truck, slip-and-fall, and wrongful death cases at roughly 30 cases per month with a $15,000 average fee per case. Growth so far has come almost entirely from a high-touch referral network with medical providers and insurance agents, and the firm is now racing to build the hiring sequence and capacity structure needed to sustain 40–50 cases/month without slipping into the “settlement mill” model the partners have explicitly said they want to avoid.

Stage 6 is the point where Scott, Tyson, and Dan stop being the bottleneck in every file and finally get the self-managing firm they set out to build when they left their W-2 jobs behind.

Where You Are Today

Stage 4: Small Business Manager
  • Growth is driven almost entirely by a referral network built through direct outreach to medical providers and insurance agents — not a scalable marketing system
  • Local Service Ads are running through an outside agency, but that relationship is under review rather than owned in-house
  • All three partners are still hands-on in daily operations — litigation, HR/hiring/IT, and intake/ops — with no manager layer between them and the casework
  • Case volume is climbing (about 30/month toward a 40/month target) faster than the hiring and admin structure needed to support it

Where You Need To Be

Stage 6: Law Firm Owner
  • The firm runs without you
  • Your income is no longer tied to your personal hours
  • Your time is yours to spend however you choose
  • This is where true freedom and true wealth become possible
The gap between personally managing every case and building the self-managing firm you set out to create is not about working harder — it is about putting a hiring and capacity plan in place before growth outpaces the team.

The Law Firm Growth Acceleration Model

We Help Law Firm Owners Get More Profit, More Personal Freedom, And Outstanding Results For Their Clients.

Most law firms try to grow by chasing more leads. But leads alone are not the problem — and leads alone are not the answer. Here is what actually works.

Lead Generation

Leads are to a law firm what gasoline is to a car. Without them, you go nowhere. But a steady, predictable flow of leads changes everything — you get to choose your cases instead of taking whatever comes in.

Intake

But all the leads in the world are worthless without a system to turn them into paying clients. Most firms are losing cases they already paid for simply because their follow-up is slow or inconsistent.

Self-Managing Team

Once clients are coming in, you need a team — otherwise you end up working 90-hour weeks and results suffer. A self-managing team is what gives you the freedom to step back.

Profit Plan

And once the team is in place, most owners look at their bank account and ask: where did all the money go? That is what a profit plan solves — clarity on what you earn, what you keep, and how to grow both.

All four have to work together. If one is missing, you cannot have more profit, more freedom, and outstanding results for your clients at the same time.

Team Assessment

BGN Attorneys' three partners each personally own a distinct function of the firm — litigation, HR/hiring/IT, and intake/ops — with no layer of management between them and the day-to-day work. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • All three partners are individually responsible for a core function, so growth adds direct workload to each of them rather than to a scalable team layer
  • No stated org chart or manager role exists between the partners and staff
  • A "pre-legal" role and an additional pre-lit paralegal are being added reactively as volume grows, not against a documented capacity plan
  • Case volume is on track to grow from roughly 30 to 40–50/month, adding pressure to a structure that hasn't yet been tested at that scale
Blind Spot: 100% retention and zero churn to date may feel like proof the team structure is working — but a team that has never been tested under real strain hasn't yet revealed where it will break.
The Positive
  • 100% employee retention and no churn to date is a genuine sign of a healthy early culture
  • That retention gives the firm a real foundation to build a management layer on top of, rather than starting from scratch
The Gap
  • Owner dependency across all three partners means none of them can step away without the others absorbing their function
  • Hiring reactively, one role at a time as pressure builds, costs more in urgency and onboarding disruption than hiring against a plan
  • Without a manager layer, case volume growth will keep adding hours to the partners' weeks instead of the team's capacity
