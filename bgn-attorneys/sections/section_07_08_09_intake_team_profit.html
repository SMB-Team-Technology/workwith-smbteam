Intake Assessment

BGN Attorneys recently hired an intake specialist to keep pace with a referral pipeline that already converts at roughly a 90% meeting-to-referral rate, but a partner is still personally involved in day-to-day intake and case administration. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • A partner is still personally handling intake and case administration alongside litigation and ops responsibilities
  • No after-hours coverage or defined follow-up sequence was described for leads or referrals arriving outside business hours
  • No CRM or formal intake tracking system was mentioned on the call
  • An additional pre-lit paralegal is actively being hired, signaling the current team is already stretched at roughly 30 cases/month
Blind Spot: You may not realize how much of your own time is going into intake and case administration simply because there has never been a defined line between partner work and staff work — it grew organically as the firm did.
The Positive
  • A dedicated intake specialist has already been hired specifically to support this growth
  • The medical-provider and insurance-agent referral pipeline converts at roughly a 90% meeting-to-referral rate, so the top of the funnel is genuinely strong
The Gap
  • Every hour a partner spends on intake is an hour not spent on litigation strategy or business development
  • Without after-hours coverage, referrals and leads arriving evenings or weekends may go unanswered until the next business day
  • As case volume grows toward 40–50/month, the current informal intake process will not scale without documented handoffs
Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Team Assessment

BGN Attorneys' three partners each personally own a distinct function of the firm — litigation, HR/hiring/IT, and intake/ops — with no layer of management between them and the day-to-day work. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • All three partners are individually responsible for a core function, so growth adds direct workload to each of them rather than to a scalable team layer
  • No stated org chart or manager role exists between the partners and staff
  • A "pre-legal" role and an additional pre-lit paralegal are being added reactively as volume grows, not against a documented capacity plan
  • Case volume is on track to grow from roughly 30 to 40–50/month, adding pressure to a structure that hasn't yet been tested at that scale
Blind Spot: 100% retention and zero churn to date may feel like proof the team structure is working — but a team that has never been tested under real strain hasn't yet revealed where it will break.
The Positive
  • 100% employee retention and no churn to date is a genuine sign of a healthy early culture
  • That retention gives the firm a real foundation to build a management layer on top of, rather than starting from scratch
The Gap
  • Owner dependency across all three partners means none of them can step away without the others absorbing their function
  • Hiring reactively, one role at a time as pressure builds, costs more in urgency and onboarding disruption than hiring against a plan
  • Without a manager layer, case volume growth will keep adding hours to the partners' weeks instead of the team's capacity
A self-managing team — not more case volume — is what finally gives Scott, Tyson, and Dan the time back they left their W-2 jobs to build for themselves.

Profit Plan Assessment

Annual revenue, revenue goals, and fee structure were not part of this discovery call by design — the September 2 conversation is focused on coaching and capacity, not financial planning — but that also means there is currently no clear translation of case volume into a profit or take-home target. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • No annual revenue, revenue goal, or fee percentage has been discussed or tracked in a way that surfaced on this call
  • Personal comp targets exist ($350–400K in year two, $500K within three years) without a visible plan connecting case volume and case value to those numbers
  • The stated concern about over-hiring depressing owner take-home shows financial awareness, but no defined guardrail or formula backs it up yet
  • Average case value ($15,000) and case volume trajectory (30 toward 40–50/month) are known but have not been translated into a profit plan
Blind Spot: You may be measuring growth in cases closed rather than dollars kept — and those stop being the same thing the moment new hires start drawing salary.
The Positive
  • The stated caution about over-hiring shows the partners are already thinking about profit protection, not just growth for its own sake
The Gap
  • Without a defined profit plan, every new hire is a guess rather than a calculated trade-off against take-home pay
  • There is no stated per-case profitability view, so it is unclear whether the roughly $15,000 average case value is translating into the personal comp targets set
  • Comp targets ($350–400K year two, $500K within three years) currently exist as goals without a documented path connecting today's case volume to those numbers
Hitting 40 to 50 cases a month means little for personal take-home without a profit plan turning that volume into the $500K comp target the partners are working toward.
