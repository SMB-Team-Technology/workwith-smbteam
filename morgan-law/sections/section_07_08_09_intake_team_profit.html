Intake Assessment

Morgan Law gets roughly two to three new cases a month today, and the phone is answered on the first ring — a strong start. The intake process behind that first ring was not described, and it has to be ready for the 10 to 15 cases a month David is aiming for. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • No documented intake process or follow-up sequence was described for new inquiries
  • After-hours and weekend coverage was not discussed, so calls and web inquiries outside the office may go unanswered
  • Going from two to three cases a month to 10 to 15 multiplies inquiry volume, and intake capacity has not yet been planned for it
  • Prospects arriving from search or ChatGPT are ready to act now and will move to the next firm if the first contact is slow or unclear
Blind Spot: Paid traffic arrives with high intent and little patience. A strong first-ring phone answer only helps if the website form, after-hours inquiries, and follow-up are just as fast.
The Positive
  • Your phone system answers the first call on the first ring — many firms lose cases before anyone picks up
  • The website offers a free consultation and a 24/7 phone number, setting an expectation of quick access
The Gap
  • Every inquiry that is not followed up consistently is a case that can go to a competitor — and at your current volume, each one is a meaningful share of the month
  • Without a defined process, new marketing leads could be handled inconsistently as volume rises
  • Without tracking from first contact to signed case, you cannot tell which channels are producing cases at your target cost
Strong intake turns the effort you put into marketing into signed cases — so growth builds security for your family instead of just more phone calls.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The contact form is on a separate Contact page — no form was observed on the homepage above the fold
  • The homepage does offer a “Book My Free Consultation” button and a 24/7 phone number, which is a good starting point
  • Two different phone numbers appear on the site, which can confuse callers and make call tracking harder

These findings are also referenced in the Website Audit section.

Team Assessment

David has historically run his own marketing and still practices in both Idaho and Utah, while team size and roles were not described on the call. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Marketing, including PPC and Local Service Ads, has been run personally by the owner rather than by a dedicated person or partner
  • No dedicated marketing, intake, or operations role came up on the call
  • With a practice in Idaho and another in Utah, the owner is the common thread connecting both
  • Hiring is planned as a reaction to volume rather than designed ahead of it
Blind Spot: The best time to define roles is before 10 to 15 cases a month arrive. Hiring after volume spikes is how owners end up working more, not less.
The Positive
  • You are open to hiring more attorneys as volume grows rather than capping the firm
  • Decades of practice give you the experience to train and lead the people you bring on
The Gap
  • Every task that only the owner can do limits how many cases the firm can handle and how much time you can spend away
  • Without defined roles, adding cases can mean adding hours for you first
  • Managing marketing personally keeps time away from casework and growth decisions
A team that can absorb growth is what makes the next 20 years sustainable — for you and for the family you are providing for.

Profit Plan Assessment

Growth at Morgan Law is currently measured in cases per month, and David already knows the cost per signed case he can live with, but revenue and profit targets were not stated on the call. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"It was about $1,000 per case is what I was getting."
The Problem
  • Revenue and profit goals were not defined — the target is 10 to 15 cases a month, not a dollar figure
  • Past marketing spend, especially on organic, did not deliver results you could point to
  • No system was described for tracking cost per signed case across channels
  • On a contingency model, ad spend goes out months before fees come in, so cash flow needs to be planned alongside growth
Blind Spot: Cost per signed case is the right yardstick. Tracked by channel, it shows exactly where the next marketing dollar belongs — and where to stop spending.
The Positive
  • You already think in cost per signed case, with a clear ceiling of about $1,000 — a discipline many owners never develop
  • Your early-mover results show you can spot a channel that works and move on it quickly
The Gap
  • Without channel-level tracking, a campaign can look busy and still miss your cost-per-case target
  • Without a profit plan, growing from two to three cases to 10 to 15 may bring more work before it brings more take-home
  • Without clear targets, it is hard to know what to invest in the next level of marketing or hiring
A profit plan turns growth into wealth you can count on — the security you want for your family over the next 20 years.
