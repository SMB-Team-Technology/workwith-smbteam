Intake Assessment

Dallas intake today is informal and attorney-led, with no documented process to capture or follow up on leads — while Norman's tech-driven intake system proves the firm already knows how to build one that works. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Dallas intake is handled personally by attorneys, with no defined process or dedicated intake team
  • No CRM or intake tracking system exists in Dallas — leads and follow-up depend entirely on individual memory
  • Poor client selection is resulting in cases being taken on that later run out of funds, per the discovery call
  • Norman's proven tech-driven intake process — a strategy-and-contract system plus a dedicated closer — has never been replicated in Dallas, despite already being proven to work
Blind Spot: The exact intake system solving this problem already exists inside your own firm — it just lives in Norman, not Dallas.
The Positive
  • Norman's intake process shows the firm already has an internal, proven model for exactly this problem — it doesn't need to be invented, only replicated
The Gap
  • Every unprofitable case accepted without a screening process is revenue and time that could have gone to a better-fit client
  • Referrals with no structured follow-up are likely being lost to competitors who respond faster
  • Without intake data, there is no way to know how many leads Dallas is currently losing before they ever become clients

Team Assessment

Jon Velie is still the one handling Dallas litigation day to day, and Pam Velie's recent hire as Chief Growth Officer is the first real step toward building a team that can run without him. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Jon Velie personally carries Dallas casework, with no delegation structure in place for that office
  • Pam Velie was just hired as CGO — a new role still in its audit-and-build phase, not yet delivering operational relief
  • No documented org structure exists for Dallas specifically, even though the firm has 12+ named attorneys across both offices
  • The stated goal of expanding to New York and San Francisco requires Jon's time and attention — time he does not currently have
Blind Spot: Hiring Pam is necessary but not sufficient — without a defined system for her to build against, even a CGO can only audit the problem, not solve it alone.
The Positive
  • The firm has already taken the first real step toward a self-managing structure by bringing in a CGO specifically to build one
  • Norman's dedicated closer role shows the firm is capable of successfully delegating client-facing work once a system is defined
The Gap
  • Every hour Jon spends on Dallas casework is an hour he cannot spend leading firm-wide strategy or the planned expansion
  • Without a defined Dallas team structure, growth in that office will keep demanding more of Jon personally, not less
Real freedom for Jon starts when Dallas can run the way Norman already does — without him in every case.

Profit Plan Assessment

Velie Global doesn't yet know its own margins — profitability is described only as "unknown but low," with a stated goal of reaching 20–30% within 12 months. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • No financial tracking system currently shows what the firm actually keeps after expenses
  • Dallas revenue is growing ($700K in 2025, targeting $1M–$1.5M in 2026) with no visibility into whether that growth is profitable
  • Poor client selection in Dallas — cases accepted that later run out of funds — is a direct, unmeasured drain on margin
  • No fee-structure or cost-per-case data exists to show which practice areas or case types are actually the most profitable
Blind Spot: Growing Dallas revenue without fixing client selection first could mean working harder for the same — or worse — margin.
The Positive
  • The firm has already set a specific, measurable profitability target — 20–30% within 12 months — rather than treating profit as an afterthought
The Gap
  • Without financial visibility, it is impossible to know which of the firm's two offices — or which practice area — is actually funding the other
  • The 20–30% profitability goal has no baseline to measure progress against until tracking is put in place
A real profit plan turns Velie Global's growth goals from a hope into a number Jon can actually track.
