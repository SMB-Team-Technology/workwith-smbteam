Lead Generation

Velie Global Law Firm has built two very different lead generation stories under one roof: a Norman, OK immigration practice with real organic SEO equity built up over years, and a Dallas, TX litigation practice that has never had a formal lead generation system of any kind.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • The site is a client-rendered React single-page application that returns an essentially empty page before JavaScript loads — a real risk for search engine indexing and for any visitor on a slow connection
  • PageSpeed mobile and desktop scores could not be captured during this research pass and must be measured live before any website recommendation is finalized
  • The site has only one dedicated city landing page (/dallas) despite operating in two very different metro markets — no supporting pages exist for Plano, Frisco, McKinney, or Oklahoma City
  • Exact homepage headline, above-fold call-to-action copy, and mobile responsiveness could not be independently confirmed and should be checked in a live browser
Blind Spot: Because your site loads its content entirely through JavaScript, anyone who can't wait for the full page to load — search engine crawlers included — may see nothing at all on first contact.
The Positive
  • The immigration side of the site has genuine SEO depth — dozens of dedicated visa-type pages (H1B, L1, O1, the EB series, TN, K1–K4, and more) built out over time
  • The site already integrates a booking system and an AI voice widget for after-hours engagement — ahead of many firms in this respect
The Gap
  • Without confirmed PageSpeed performance, it is impossible to know how many mobile visitors are abandoning the site before it ever loads
  • The Dallas office effectively has no dedicated web presence to match its growth goals — a single generic city page cannot carry a $1M–$1.5M revenue target
  • Two related but separate web properties (a personal attorney portfolio site and a similarly-named legacy firm site) exist outside the main site, which likely splits search authority and confuses prospects searching for the firm by name
A website Jon can trust to represent both offices equally is part of what lets him stop personally vouching for the firm's credibility case by case.

Local SEO

The Problem
  • Google Business Profile figures reported by third-party aggregators could not be independently confirmed on Google and must be verified live before being used in any client conversation
  • A second, unrelated-looking business listing exists at a different Norman address under a similar name, creating a NAP (name/address/phone) consistency risk for anyone searching for the firm locally
  • The Dallas office has no independent local citation footprint of its own — consistent with the discovery call's description of Dallas operating purely on referrals with no formal marketing
Blind Spot: A prospective client searching your name today could land on three different web properties — each with different branding or address information — before ever finding the right one.
The Positive
  • The Norman office shows up prominently in organic and directory results for several immigration-related searches, reflecting real, earned SEO equity from years of content investment
The Gap
  • Every review a prospective client can't easily verify on Google is a reason to call a competitor whose reputation is easier to confirm
  • The lack of a distinct Dallas citation footprint means the office is functionally invisible in local search, regardless of how strong its referral reputation is
E. Vance Winningham & Associates — a 40+-year, immigration-exclusive firm in the same OKC metro with institutional press recognition that gives it a local search authority edge Velie's organic content alone has not yet closed.
Closing the local search gap in both markets means new clients find Velie Global on their own — without Jon or a referral source having to vouch for the firm first.

Google Ads

Ad Presence
  • No Google search ads were observed for the Norman office's immigration keywords — consistent with a firm that has invested in organic SEO but not paid search
  • No Google search ads were observed for the Dallas office's litigation or personal injury keywords — consistent with Dallas relying entirely on referrals with no formal marketing
Competitor Ad Landscape
  • Farzaneh & Patel, PLLC / Winningham & Associates — named local competitors in the Norman/OKC immigration market; their specific ad presence could not be confirmed live and should be checked before any campaign is proposed
  • GoSuits Trial Attorneys / DFW Injury Lawyers — show volume-marketing behavior (public client-count claims, active review-generation programs) consistent with active paid acquisition in the Dallas PI market, though their specific ad presence could not be confirmed live
The Opportunity
  • "immigration attorney Norman OK" and "immigration lawyer Oklahoma City" — direct, high-intent keywords the firm's own organic content already ranks for but has never backed with paid spend
  • "personal injury lawyer Dallas TX" and "business litigation attorney Plano TX" — the exact terms tied to the Dallas office's stated 2026 growth goal
  • Competitor-name campaigns against Winningham & Associates and GoSuits Trial Attorneys, capturing prospects already comparing firms
  • A dedicated campaign built around the South Asian community personal injury opportunity named on the discovery call
Blind Spot: Every day Dallas goes without paid search, a share of the exact PI searches tied to the South Asian community opportunity named on your call are going to a firm that shows up when Velie does not.

Local Service Ads

LSA Presence
  • No Local Service Ads or Google Screened badge were observed for either office — this may reflect the firm not having applied, or simply not yet being verified, and should be confirmed live
Competitor LSA Landscape
  • Norman/OKC and Dallas competitors — which specific local firms hold LSA placement in either market could not be confirmed live and should be checked before proposing an LSA campaign
The Opportunity
  • Google Screened certification would pair especially well with the credibility already built through 12+ named, individually-profiled attorneys across both offices
  • LSAs appear above everything else on Google — above paid ads, above the map pack, above all organic results — and you only pay when a real prospect calls you directly
  • For the Dallas office specifically, LSA pay-per-lead pricing removes the upfront risk of standing up a first paid channel for an office that has never run one
Blind Spot: Google Screened status functions as a trust signal independent of review count — valuable for the Dallas office, which has no independent review history of its own yet.
