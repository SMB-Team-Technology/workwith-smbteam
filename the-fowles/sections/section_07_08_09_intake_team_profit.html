Intake Assessment

Iris personally answers the phone and manages nearly every prospect conversation for The Fowles Law Firm, seven days a week, with no formal after-hours coverage and a CRM the firm has been locked out of for months. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • Iris — not a shared team or an after-hours service — personally covers intake calls around the clock, an unsustainable single point of failure as lead volume grows
  • No overflow answering service exists for after-hours or peak call volume, a gap the firm has flagged internally as a capacity-strain risk if lead volume increases as planned
  • The firm has been locked out of its Lawmatics CRM for roughly 2.5 months due to an unresolved 2FA issue, leaving no reliable system of record for lead tracking and follow-up
  • No defined, documented follow-up sequence exists for leads who do not sign immediately — follow-up currently depends on Iris's personal judgment and bandwidth
Blind Spot: Reaching the firm's own stated goal of ~18 retained clients/month requires roughly 40 additional qualified calls/month at a ~25% close rate — and that volume increase would hit an intake process that is already one person working without backup.
The Positive
  • Iris brings roughly 20 years of sales experience to every call, and the firm's own Lawmatics data shows a strong ~30–32% close rate once a lead actually reaches her — the underlying skill is already there
  • The recent move to Clio Manage for case management shows the firm is willing to invest in better systems once the value is clear
The Gap
  • Every after-hours call that goes unanswered is a potential $4,000–$6,500 case that may call a competitor instead
  • The CRM lockout means the firm cannot currently see, with confidence, how many leads are being lost to follow-up gaps
  • Scaling to 18 retained clients/month on today's single-person intake model risks burning out the firm's most experienced closer at the exact moment volume needs to increase
Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The homepage contact form is buried below the fold in a lower "Contact us now!" section, requiring visitors to scroll past significant content before finding a way to reach out
  • No live chat is present on the site, so visitors researching after-hours have no way to start a conversation before calling
  • With intake this dependent on Iris personally, a buried, form-only contact path adds unnecessary friction for the highest-intent visitors on the site

These findings are also referenced in the Website Audit section.

Team Assessment

Justin is the firm's only attorney, and the business can run without him for only about a week before issues start to surface — a sign that the systems needed to support real time away don't exist yet. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Justin is the only attorney in the firm, with no hiring process or defined path in place to bring on a second attorney
  • No documented KPIs exist for attorney performance, making it hard to know what "good" looks like as the team grows
  • The firm's own "vacation test" — about one week before problems surface — shows how tightly daily operations still depend on Justin personally
  • No leadership layer exists beyond Iris's informal ops role, so there is no one positioned to manage a growing caseload without him
Blind Spot: The fact that Iris already runs so much of the day-to-day may be masking how far the firm still is from a true leadership structure — her judgment is covering for a system that doesn't formally exist yet.
The Positive
  • Iris's roughly 20 years of sales experience and her willingness to run intake, client communications, and de facto operations gives Justin a real foundation to build a leadership layer on, rather than starting from zero
The Gap
  • Every case Justin personally has to review or approve is time he isn't spending with his two young children
  • Without documented hiring criteria or KPIs, adding a second attorney carries more risk and slows down growth
  • The firm's growth ceiling today is Justin's own personal capacity, not market demand — the walk-in client he signed mid-call shows the demand already exists
Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Profit Plan Assessment

The Fowles Law Firm tracks revenue closely — a $500K–$600K run rate this year with a clear per-case fee structure — but has no profit target distinct from that revenue number. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
The Problem
  • No documented profit target exists separate from the revenue goal — growth is currently measured in gross dollars, not take-home
  • The long-term goal of "$3M–$5M gross to net $1M profit" has no defined path connecting today's numbers to that outcome
  • No profitability data by case type has been tracked, so it isn't yet possible to know which case types are most worth prioritizing
  • The Lawmatics CRM lockout limits visibility into pipeline and collections data that would normally feed a profit plan
Blind Spot: Revenue growth without a profit plan can quietly increase Justin's workload without increasing what he actually takes home — the opposite of the freedom he's working toward.
The Positive
  • Justin has clear visibility into his own fee structure and case economics ($4,000–$6,500 per case, $5,000 working average) — the raw data for a real profit plan already exists, it just hasn't been assembled into one yet
The Gap
  • Without a profit target, Justin has no way to know whether hitting his $1M revenue goal in three years will actually translate into the income and freedom he's working toward
  • Growing revenue without a matching profit plan risks adding more casework and more hours, not more take-home pay
  • No case-type profitability tracking means decisions about new practice lines, like the transactional/estate line Justin is weighing, are being made without the numbers to back them
A defined profit plan is what turns Justin's revenue goal into an actual number of hours he can step back — not just a bigger number on a spreadsheet.
