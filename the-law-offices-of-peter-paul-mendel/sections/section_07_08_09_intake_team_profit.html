Intake Assessment

Every inquiry that reaches The Law Offices of Peter Paul Mendel is screened, qualified, and handled by Peter personally, with no process in place to filter out single-sided prospects before they consume his time. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating. This is where the biggest immediate ROI lives.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Peter personally handles every intake conversation — no delegation or screening step exists before an inquiry reaches him directly
  • No qualification process distinguishes a genuine multi-party mediation engagement from a one-sided prospect seeking representation
  • Past lead-generation efforts surfaced roughly 90% "looky-loos" — inquiries that were never viable mediation matters in the first place
  • No after-hours or overflow coverage of any kind, since there is no staff to provide it
Blind Spot: Because mediation requires buy-in from every party to a dispute, a generic legal intake process built for one-sided representation will never filter correctly here — a mediation-specific qualification script is a distinct discipline most solo mediators never build, and without one, lead volume alone will keep producing more "looky-loos," not more signed cases.
The Positive
  • Peter's existing referral relationships with attorneys already function as an informal qualification filter, since referring attorneys generally understand which disputes have real multi-party buy-in
The Gap
  • Every hour spent screening a non-viable inquiry is an hour not spent on the three mediations a week that would hit the $40,000/month goal
  • Without a repeatable intake process, adding any future lead volume — paid or referral — will not translate into more signed engagements
  • No process exists today that could be handed to a future hire, which keeps intake permanently tied to Peter personally
A fifty-mediator platform cannot run on Peter personally qualifying every inquiry — building that filter now is the first system a scaled firm will actually depend on.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The contact form is reached via a separate Contact Us page and a "Book Discovery Call" button, not positioned directly on the homepage
  • No live chat is present on the site
  • No after-hours capture mechanism exists beyond the standard contact form

Team Assessment

The Law Offices of Peter Paul Mendel is a solo operation — every case file, intake conversation, billing task, and collections follow-up runs through Peter personally, with no staff of any kind. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • Solo practice — no other staff or associate attorneys currently on payroll
  • No accountability structure or org chart exists, since there is no one else to hold accountable
  • Peter cannot step away from the practice for any period of time without every function — intake, mediation delivery, billing — stopping with him
  • The long-term vision of roughly fifty mediators has no operational scaffolding yet to support even a second person
Blind Spot: The AR collapse that ended the prior general practice happened specifically because there was no one dedicated to billing and collections follow-up — solving that structurally, not just by working harder, is what prevents it from recurring as the practice scales.
The Positive
  • Peter has already shown the operational discipline to walk away from a failing business model and rebuild around a stricter, safer one — the same discipline a hiring and delegation plan will require
The Gap
  • Owner dependency caps the practice at whatever Peter can personally handle — a hard ceiling well below three mediations a week, let alone fifty mediators
  • No one else currently exists who could take over billing, intake screening, or case coordination if Peter were unavailable
  • Every month without a hiring plan is a month further from the long-term platform vision described on the call
There is no version of a fifty-mediator platform that runs through one person's calendar — the first hire is the first real step toward the firm Peter has described.

Profit Plan Assessment

Current reported revenue (~$3,500/month) is coming from remaining legacy attorney work rather than the mediation practice itself, which has not yet become a stable, recurring income stream — and a prior general practice accumulated $500,000 in uncollected receivables with no billing discipline to stop it. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: RED
The Problem
  • A prior general practice left $500,000 in uncollected accounts receivable — a direct result of no dedicated billing and collections process
  • Current revenue is not yet separated or tracked by practice line — the mediation practice's actual case-by-case revenue is not distinct from legacy attorney income
  • No stated profit plan connects the $40,000/month short-term goal to an actual revenue and collections tracking system
  • Average case value for mediation has not been directly stated — it can only be back-calculated from the stated goal, which means Peter does not yet have a clear number to plan around
Blind Spot: Requiring payment upfront is the right instinct, but a policy alone did not prevent the last $500,000 in uncollected fees — without a system that actually tracks and enforces it case by case, the same exposure can quietly reappear as volume grows.
The Gap
  • Without case-by-case revenue tracking, Peter has no reliable way to know whether the $40,000/month goal is actually being hit
  • Without a collections system built into the new practice from day one, scaling toward fifty mediators multiplies the same AR risk instead of eliminating it
  • No visibility yet into which practice line — business/real estate mediation or divorce/family mediation — is actually more profitable per case
Eliminating the AR risk that ended the last practice is not a one-time decision — it is a system that has to be built now, before volume makes a mistake expensive again.
