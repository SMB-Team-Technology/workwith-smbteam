Lead Generation

Boeshaar Law already earns most of its leads through Google visibility and AI recommendations — about 60% by Bob's own estimate — with another 20–30% from referrals and only a "very nominal" paid ad budget. That is a strong organic foundation, but at roughly 25 leads a month it is a base to build on, not a system that scales.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • PageSpeed scores: Mobile 47/100 — Desktop 95/100 (Lighthouse lab test). The mobile experience is slow for the phone-first visitors searching for IRS help
  • The only contact form sits in the footer and asks for just a name and email — nothing about the tax problem or how urgent it is
  • Blog posts show no publication dates, so visitors and search engines cannot tell whether the IRS guidance is current
  • The navigation shows no city or neighborhood pages for Seattle-area searches beyond the main practice pages
Blind Spot: Tax rules and IRS procedures change every year, so undated articles can read as stale to a worried taxpayer — even when the advice is sound.
The Positive
  • A modern, mobile-responsive design with a clear headline — "Take Back Control From the IRS"
  • Two calls to action above the fold, including a link to schedule a free initial consultation
  • A full attorney bio highlighting 25+ years of experience, including 14 as an IRS attorney, plus nine tax-resolution practice areas listed on the site
  • A brand video on the site that Bob is pleased with
The Gap
  • A mobile score under 50 puts visitors at risk of leaving before the page loads — and most of them arrive on a phone
  • A footer-only form with two fields captures little about the case and gives the assistant less to work with before the first call
  • Without dated, regularly refreshed content, the site gives up ground to competitors who publish current guidance
A website that converts visitors on its own is one more lead source that works while you are not at your desk.

Local SEO

The Problem
  • On the discovery call, Washington Tax Services and Seattle Legal Services were showing above Boeshaar Law in the local results
  • The Google Business Profile lists weekday hours of 8:30 to 5:00 and shows the firm closed on weekends, while several competitors show later hours or 24 hours
  • The firm's review count appears small next to the top-ranked Seattle tax competitors
  • Directory listings appear to be inconsistent: the phone number on the website differs from the one in several directory listings, the firm name varies, and FindLaw shows what appears to be a second listing
Blind Spot: The firm's answering service picks up seven days a week, but Google tells weekend searchers the office is closed — so someone searching Saturday morning may choose a competitor who appears open.
The Positive
  • Google's AI results were recommending Boeshaar Law for "best tax attorney in Seattle" on the live call, and the firm also appears organically
  • The Business Profile is active, with updates and products visible, and the firm is listed on Avvo, Justia, FindLaw, Super Lawyers, Martindale-Hubbell, and other major directories
The Gap
  • Hours that read "closed" on weekends can cost the firm calls from people who are searching at the moment they are most motivated to act
  • A smaller review base gives competitors the edge when a prospect is comparing local tax attorneys side by side
  • Mismatched phone numbers and names across directories weaken the trust signals Google uses to rank the firm
Washington Tax Services Inc — ranks above Boeshaar Law in local results with a substantially larger review base and a business history dating back to 1989.
A stronger local presence means more of the right calls reach the team — not more hours spent chasing them.

Google Ads

Ad Presence
  • Bob has run paid ads in the past (platform not specified) and describes the current spend as "a very nominal amount" — just enough to keep the account active
  • Ad copy and keyword coverage for tax-resolution searches in Seattle were not observed in this review, so the firm may not be configured to show for the highest-intent searches
Competitor Ad Landscape
  • Washington Tax Services, Seattle Legal Services, and Kennedy Tax Solutions — the three firms most visible ahead of Boeshaar Law in search and local results, and the first to check in a live ad auction
  • Competitor ad copy was not captured in this review; a live check of the Seattle auction is the first step before any budget is committed
The Opportunity
  • Seattle-focused searches such as "tax attorney Seattle" and "IRS tax lawyer Seattle"
  • Service-specific searches such as "IRS offer in compromise attorney Seattle", matched to the firm's nine tax-resolution practice areas
  • Urgency searches from people facing an IRS levy, lien, or audit letter — the moment they are most ready to hire
  • Searches that include competitor firm names, and nearby Eastside and South Sound cities, which have no dedicated pages in the site navigation
Blind Spot: Capacity is the first constraint: with the firm already behind on client work, paid search is the lever to pull as a new associate comes on board, not before.

Local Service Ads

LSA Presence
  • Local Service Ads for the firm were not observed in this review — whether Boeshaar Law appears, and whether it is Google Screened, should be confirmed live
  • Not appearing in this space is an opportunity gap rather than a confirmed absence
Competitor LSA Landscape
  • No competitor LSA listings or review counts were captured in this review, so none are cited here
The Opportunity
  • Where Google offers Local Service Ads for tax-resolution work in the Seattle market, the format is worth testing — availability for this practice area should be confirmed first
  • LSAs appear above everything else on Google — above paid ads, above the map pack, above all organic results — and you only pay when a real prospect calls you directly.
  • Ranking in LSAs leans heavily on reviews, so the review work on the Google Business Profile builds toward this channel at the same time
