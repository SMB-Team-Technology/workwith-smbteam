Intake Assessment

Boeshaar Law has a real intake foundation — a legal assistant who screens calls, a follow-up sequence, and a CRM — but Bob still takes the sales call himself while the firm falls behind on client communication. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
"right now I'm really behind in client work."
The Problem
  • Bob personally takes the 15-minute sales call for every qualified lead, with a 30-minute call when the fit is strong
  • Of about 25 leads a month, roughly half are qualified and roughly half of those sign — around five to seven new clients a month in recent months
  • Email takes Bob two to three hours a day and the staff roughly two more, much of it substantive case work that competes with new-client follow-up
  • A letter was missed ahead of a deadline, a sign that incoming work is outrunning the firm's tracking
Blind Spot: As lead volume grows, the sales call on your calendar becomes the next bottleneck — more leads will not mean more signed clients unless someone besides you can take those calls.
The Positive
  • A legal assistant answers initial calls, screens them, and books the consultation, and an answering service covers the phones seven days a week
  • A seven-touch follow-up email sequence plus assistant calls is already in place, tracked in a CRM
  • Roughly half of qualified leads sign — a solid close rate for a flat-fee practice
The Gap
  • Every sales call Bob takes is an hour not spent on casework or on building the business
  • Slow replies and missed items put signed clients at risk as well as prospects who are still deciding
  • Weekend hours shown as "closed" on Google keep some searchers from ever reaching the answering service
Moving routine intake and follow-up off your calendar is how the firm starts running without you.

Website Contact Forms & Live Chat

  • The contact form sits in the footer and asks only for a name and email, so the team learns little about the tax problem before reaching out
  • Above the fold, a button links to schedule a free initial consultation — a clear way for ready visitors to book
  • No live chat was detected on the site, so a visitor with a question outside business hours has only the footer form or the booking link

Team Assessment

The firm is small — three people including Bob, plus a contract attorney — and the business side still depends on him for emails, decisions, and casework. Until this changes, the owner cannot have more free time — no matter how much revenue grows.

Status: RED
"I need to be, see myself, the business side is dependent on me. So I can take like a week off and everything runs fine and they don't need me."
The Problem
  • Bob estimates at least half of his roughly 40-hour week goes to work that could be delegated
  • Email responses take him two to three hours a day and the staff about two more
  • He is behind on client work, and he sees continuing this way as putting a ceiling on the firm's growth
  • There is no associate attorney yet — the hire is targeted for June — so casework and decisions still route back to the owner
Blind Spot: The ceiling is not demand — it is the hours of one attorney. Adding leads without adding capacity makes the backlog longer, not the firm bigger.
The Positive
  • A working team is already in place, with a legal assistant, a paralegal analyst, and a contract attorney
  • Bob has already built AI agents for emails and case evaluation letters, and the paralegal analyst uses AI as well
  • The goals are specific — a week off, then 30 days, and a roughly 10-hour workweek — and a hiring plan is already in motion
The Gap
  • A 40-hour week versus a 10-hour target is a 30-hour gap that has to be filled by people, process, and AI
  • Without defined roles and accountability, new hires will route decisions back to Bob rather than lifting them off his desk
  • Every hour spent on delegable work is an hour not spent setting the vision of the firm
A firm that runs without you is what turns a week off into 30 days and a full workweek into a quarter-time one.

Profit Plan Assessment

Boeshaar Law is on track for roughly $500,000 this year, mostly on flat fees, with a goal of $2 million gross in two to three years and $1 million by the end of 2027 as a first milestone. Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity. Until there is a clear profit plan, growth may just mean more work without more wealth.

Status: AMBER
"we have a lot of cash crunches, so I'd like to get those even out."
The Problem
  • Cash flow arrives unevenly — Bob describes recurring cash crunches he wants to smooth out
  • Growth from roughly $500,000 to $2 million is a fourfold increase that the current structure was not built to carry
  • New-client signings were weak early in the year and have varied from month to month since
  • No profit targets or financial plan were discussed on the call beyond the revenue goal
Blind Spot: On a flat-fee model, time spent on delegable work is a hidden cost — every hour Bob spends on email is an hour not spent on the work that brings in the next fee.
The Positive
  • A mostly flat-fee model gives clients price certainty and the firm a predictable fee per case
  • Revenue is growing steadily, and Bob has a clear, specific goal with an interim milestone
The Gap
  • Uneven cash flow adds stress to every month, which works against the less stressful life Bob is building toward
  • Without a profit plan, more revenue can arrive alongside more payroll and more complexity, not more take-home
  • Month-to-month swings in new clients make cash flow harder to predict
A profit plan is what lets growth lower your stress instead of adding to it.
