Here Is Where Boeshaar Law Stands Today

Bob Boeshaar spent 14 years as an IRS attorney before opening his own Seattle tax controversy practice 13 years ago, and he is on track for roughly $500,000 this year with a team of three plus a contract attorney. By his own estimate, about 60% of his leads now come from Google and AI recommendations — but with email, sales calls, and casework all routed through him, the firm's growth is capped by the hours in his own week.

Stage 6 is the stage where the business side runs without you — a week off first, 30 days eventually, and a workweek closer to 10 hours that leaves room for your family and your volunteer work — and it is exactly where we are taking you.

Where You Are Today

Stage 3: Solo Practitioner
  • Roughly 60% of new leads come from Google and AI recommendations and another 20–30% from referrals — about 25 leads a month, a volume you feel is not high enough
  • At least half of your roughly 40-hour week goes to work that could be delegated, and email alone takes you two to three hours a day
  • You are behind on client work, and a letter was missed ahead of a deadline
  • The business side depends on you: three people including you, a contract attorney, and no associate attorney yet

Where You Need To Be

Stage 6: Law Firm Owner
  • The firm runs without you
  • Your income is no longer tied to your personal hours
  • Your time is yours to spend however you choose
  • This is where true freedom and true wealth become possible
The gap between a firm that depends on you for everything and one that runs while you take 30 days off is closable — and it starts with getting your time back from the work that does not need you.
Overall Growth Stage Assessment
Boeshaar Law
Bob is operating at Stage 3 — a Solo Practitioner whose reputation, Google visibility, and AI-driven recommendations are working, but whose day is still filled with email, sales calls, and casework that only he handles. The most urgent limiting area is team capacity and delegation, not demand: he is already behind on client work, so more leads without more capacity would only deepen the backlog. That gap sits directly between today's roughly $500,000 and his goal of $2 million gross in two to three years — and the 10-hour workweek he wants.
