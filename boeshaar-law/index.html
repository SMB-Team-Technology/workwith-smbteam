Here Is Where Boeshaar Law Stands Today

Bob Boeshaar spent 14 years as an IRS attorney before opening his own Seattle tax controversy practice 13 years ago, and he is on track for roughly $500,000 this year with a team of three plus a contract attorney. By his own estimate, about 60% of his leads now come from Google and AI recommendations — but with email, sales calls, and casework all routed through him, the firm's growth is capped by the hours in his own week.

Stage 6 is the stage where the business side runs without you — a week off first, 30 days eventually, and a workweek closer to 10 hours that leaves room for your family and your volunteer work — and it is exactly where we are taking you.

Where You Are Today

Stage 3: Solo Practitioner
  • Roughly 60% of new leads come from Google and AI recommendations and another 20–30% from referrals — about 25 leads a month, a volume you feel is not high enough
  • At least half of your roughly 40-hour week goes to work that could be delegated, and email alone takes you two to three hours a day
  • You are behind on client work, and a letter was missed ahead of a deadline
  • The business side depends on you: three people including you, a contract attorney, and no associate attorney yet

Where You Need To Be

Stage 6: Law Firm Owner
  • The firm runs without you
  • Your income is no longer tied to your personal hours
  • Your time is yours to spend however you choose
  • This is where true freedom and true wealth become possible
The gap between a firm that depends on you for everything and one that runs while you take 30 days off is closable — and it starts with getting your time back from the work that does not need you.

The Law Firm Growth Acceleration Model

We Help Law Firm Owners Get More Profit, More Personal Freedom, And Outstanding Results For Their Clients.

Most law firms try to grow by chasing more leads. But leads alone are not the problem — and leads alone are not the answer. Here is what actually works.

Lead Generation

Leads are to a law firm what gasoline is to a car. Without them, you go nowhere. But a steady, predictable flow of leads changes everything — you get to choose your cases instead of taking whatever comes in.

Intake

But all the leads in the world are worthless without a system to turn them into paying clients. Most firms are losing cases they already paid for simply because their follow-up is slow or inconsistent.

Self-Managing Team

Once clients are coming in, you need a team — otherwise you end up working 90-hour weeks and results suffer. A self-managing team is what gives you the freedom to step back.

Profit Plan

And once the team is in place, most owners look at their bank account and ask: where did all the money go? That is what a profit plan solves — clarity on what you earn, what you keep, and how to grow both.

All four have to work together. If one is missing, you cannot have more profit, more freedom, and outstanding results for your clients at the same time.

Website Audit

The Problem
  • PageSpeed scores: Mobile 47/100 — Desktop 95/100 (Lighthouse lab test). The mobile experience is slow for the phone-first visitors searching for IRS help
  • The only contact form sits in the footer and asks for just a name and email — nothing about the tax problem or how urgent it is
  • Blog posts show no publication dates, so visitors and search engines cannot tell whether the IRS guidance is current
  • The navigation shows no city or neighborhood pages for Seattle-area searches beyond the main practice pages
Blind Spot: Tax rules and IRS procedures change every year, so undated articles can read as stale to a worried taxpayer — even when the advice is sound.
The Positive
  • A modern, mobile-responsive design with a clear headline — "Take Back Control From the IRS"
  • Two calls to action above the fold, including a link to schedule a free initial consultation
  • A full attorney bio highlighting 25+ years of experience, including 14 as an IRS attorney, plus nine tax-resolution practice areas listed on the site
  • A brand video on the site that Bob is pleased with
The Gap
  • A mobile score under 50 puts visitors at risk of leaving before the page loads — and most of them arrive on a phone
  • A footer-only form with two fields captures little about the case and gives the assistant less to work with before the first call
  • Without dated, regularly refreshed content, the site gives up ground to competitors who publish current guidance
A website that converts visitors on its own is one more lead source that works while you are not at your desk.

Intake Assessment

Boeshaar Law has a real intake foundation — a legal assistant who screens calls, a follow-up sequence, and a CRM — but Bob still takes the sales call himself while the firm falls behind on client communication. Fixing intake means converting more of the leads the firm is already generating — without spending another dollar on marketing.

Status: AMBER
"right now I'm really behind in client work."
The Problem
  • Bob personally takes the 15-minute sales call for every qualified lead, with a 30-minute call when the fit is strong
  • Of about 25 leads a month, roughly half are qualified and roughly half of those sign — around five to seven new clients a month in recent months
  • Email takes Bob two to three hours a day and the staff roughly two more, much of it substantive case work that competes with new-client follow-up
  • A letter was missed ahead of a deadline, a sign that incoming work is outrunning the firm's tracking
Blind Spot: As lead volume grows, the sales call on your calendar becomes the next bottleneck — more leads will not mean more signed clients unless someone besides you can take those calls.
The Positive
  • A legal assistant answers initial calls, screens them, and books the consultation, and an answering service covers the phones seven days a week
  • A seven-touch follow-up email sequence plus assistant calls is already in place, tracked in a CRM
  • Roughly half of qualified leads sign — a solid close rate for a flat-fee practice
The Gap
  • Every sales call Bob takes is an hour not spent on casework or on building the business
  • Slow replies and missed items put signed clients at risk as well as prospects who are still deciding
  • Weekend hours shown as "closed" on Google keep some searchers from ever reaching the answering service
Moving routine intake and follow-up off your calendar is how the firm starts running without you.
